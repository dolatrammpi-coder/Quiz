Cyber Security, Malware, Virus, Worm, Trojan, Spyware, Adware, and Ransomware (Digital Security 1)

Core Concepts of Digital Security, Malware, Password Security, and Cyber Security Measures

🔍

One-Liner Questions

What is the fundamental definition of Digital Security?
The process of protecting digital devices, networks, online accounts, and data from unauthorized access, cyber attacks, and data loss.
Tap to view answer
Which three core pillars make up the 'CIA Triad' of information security?
Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability.
Tap to view answer
What does 'Confidentiality' mean in the CIA Triad?
Ensuring that sensitive data can only be viewed and accessed by authorized individuals or systems.
Tap to view answer
What is meant by 'Integrity' under the CIA Triad?
Maintaining the accuracy and completeness of data, and ensuring it has not been tampered with or modified without authorization.
Tap to view answer
What is the primary objective of 'Availability' in the CIA Triad?
Providing continuous and uninterrupted access to information and systems for authorized users whenever needed.
Tap to view answer
What is the concept of 'Non-repudiation' in information security?
The principle that prevents the sender of a digital message or transaction from denying (repudiating) having sent it.
Tap to view answer
What is the difference between Authentication and Authorization?
Authentication verifies the user's identity (who you are), whereas Authorization determines which resources the user is permitted to access (what you can do).
Tap to view answer
How is 'Risk' calculated in the context of cyber security?
Risk = Threat × Vulnerability × Impact.
Tap to view answer
What does 'Vulnerability' mean in cyber security?
A flaw or weakness in software, hardware, or a security mechanism that an attacker can exploit to gain unauthorized entry.
Tap to view answer
What is an Exploit?
A piece of code or tool used to take advantage of a system's vulnerability to carry out an attack.
Tap to view answer
What is the definition of a 'Zero-Day Vulnerability'?
A security flaw in software that is exploited by attackers before the developer becomes aware of it or releases a patch.
Tap to view answer
What is meant by 'Attack Surface'?
All possible points or pathways in a system or network through which an unauthorized person can enter or extract data.
Tap to view answer
What is the primary basis of a Social Engineering attack?
Manipulating human psychology rather than exploiting technical flaws to obtain sensitive information.
Tap to view answer
What is the primary characteristic of a Phishing attack?
Impersonating a legitimate institution via email or fake websites to steal passwords or financial information.
Tap to view answer
How does Spear Phishing differ from ordinary phishing?
It is a customized attack targeted at a specific individual or organization rather than a broad general audience.
Tap to view answer
Whom does a Whaling attack target?
A phishing attack targeting high-profile executives of an organization, such as the CEO, CFO, or top management.
Tap to view answer
What is the difference between Vishing and Smishing?
Vishing is phishing conducted over voice phone calls, whereas Smishing is phishing conducted via SMS text messages.
Tap to view answer
What is the Baiting social engineering technique?
Infecting a system by luring a victim with an enticing offer (bait), such as a free download or a malware-infected USB flash drive.
Tap to view answer
What does Pretexting mean?
Fabricating a false scenario or story to gain the victim's trust and obtain private information.
Tap to view answer
What type of security breach is Tailgating (Piggybacking)?
A physical security breach where an unauthorized person enters a secure premises right behind an authorized employee.
Tap to view answer
What is done in the Shoulder Surfing technique?
Standing behind or secretly watching someone to directly observe the password or PIN they are entering.
Tap to view answer
What is the context of Dumpster Diving in cyber security?
Searching through trash bins or discarded documents to find sensitive passwords, printouts, or hardware information.
Tap to view answer
What is the main objective of Cyber Espionage?
Stealing confidential data, intellectual property, or strategic secrets from rival nations or competing companies.
Tap to view answer
What is the difference between the Dark Web and the Deep Web?
The Deep Web consists of non-public data not indexed by search engines (like email inboxes), whereas the Dark Web is an encrypted network accessed via special browsers (like Tor) where illicit activities often occur.
Tap to view answer
What is Information Warfare?
The strategic use of digital technologies to influence or disrupt a rival or enemy nation's information systems and public opinion.

One-Liner Questions

What is the full form and meaning of Malware?
Malicious Software; any harmful software designed to damage a computer system, data, or users.
Tap to view answer
What is the fundamental characteristic of a Computer Virus?
A program that attaches itself to a host file or executable program, creates copies of itself, and spreads without the user's permission.
Tap to view answer
Which is considered the world's first computer virus?
The 'Creeper Virus' discovered on ARPANET in 1971 (which displayed the message 'I'm the creeper, catch me if you can!').
Tap to view answer
Which was the first personal computer boot sector virus for MS-DOS?
The 'Brain Virus', created in 1986 by the Alvi brothers (Amjad and Basit Farooq Alvi) in Pakistan.
Tap to view answer
Which part of a computer does a Boot Sector Virus infect?
The Master Boot Record (MBR) or boot sector of a storage device, causing the virus to load when the operating system starts.
Tap to view answer
Which files does a File Infector Virus target?
Primarily executable files such as .exe, .com, and .bat.
Tap to view answer
Which types of applications does a Macro Virus affect?
It infects documents with embedded macro languages, such as Microsoft Word or Excel files.
Tap to view answer
Why is a Polymorphic Virus difficult to detect?
Because it changes its signature or encryption code every time it infects a new file.
Tap to view answer
How does a Stealth Virus evade antivirus software?
It intercepts operating system requests to hide changes in file size or modification timestamps.
Tap to view answer
What is the specialty of a Multipartite Virus?
It has the ability to infect both the boot sector and executable files simultaneously.
Tap to view answer
In what key way does a Computer Worm differ from a virus?
A worm does not require a host file or human intervention to spread; it self-replicates automatically across a network.
Tap to view answer
Which was the first famous worm to spread across the internet?
The 'Morris Worm', created in 1988 by Robert Tappan Morris.
Tap to view answer
What is the historical significance of the Stuxnet worm?
It was the world's first sophisticated cyberweapon, physically damaging SCADA and PLC controllers at Iran's Natanz nuclear facility.
Tap to view answer
How does a Trojan Horse operate?
It poses as useful or legitimate software while running a malicious payload in the background to open an unauthorized backdoor.
Tap to view answer
What is the danger of a Remote Access Trojan (RAT)?
It grants an attacker full administrative control over the victim's computer remotely.
Tap to view answer
What is the main objective of Ransomware?
To encrypt the victim's files and demand a ransom—typically in cryptocurrency—to unlock the data.
Tap to view answer
Which famous ransomware affected computers worldwide in 2017?
WannaCry Ransomware (which exploited the EternalBlue vulnerability in Windows).
Tap to view answer
How does Spyware cause harm?
It secretly monitors a user's internet activities, credentials, and personal data without consent and sends them to a third party.
Tap to view answer
What is a Keylogger?
A type of spyware (hardware or software) that records every keystroke pressed on a keyboard to steal passwords.
Tap to view answer
Which category of malware does 'Pegasus', developed by the NSO Group, belong to?
A highly sophisticated zero-click spyware that covertly monitors smartphones.
Tap to view answer
What is the difference between Adware and Malvertising?
Adware displays unwanted advertisements, whereas Malvertising is the technique of injecting malicious code into legitimate online advertising networks.
Tap to view answer
What is the primary function of a Rootkit?
To hide deep at the operating system's kernel level, keeping other malware and the attacker's presence completely invisible to antivirus software.
Tap to view answer
How is a Botnet formed?
By linking numerous malware-infected computers (zombies) to a central Command and Control (C2) server to form a massive network.
Tap to view answer
Who is called a Botmaster (Bot Herder)?
The cybercriminal who controls an infected botnet network and issues attack commands.
Tap to view answer
What is the main difference between Denial of Service (DoS) and Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS)?
A DoS attack originates from a single system, whereas a DDoS attack uses a botnet of thousands or millions of computers simultaneously to overload a target server.
Tap to view answer
When does a Logic Bomb activate?
When a specific condition in the system is met (such as an employee's name being removed or a specific event occurring).
Tap to view answer
What is a Time Bomb a specific type of?
It is a type of logic bomb that executes at a predetermined date or time.
Tap to view answer
Why is Fileless Malware different from traditional malware?
It does not save any files to the hard disk; instead, it runs directly in the system's RAM and abuses legitimate admin tools (like PowerShell).
Tap to view answer
How does Scareware deceive users?
It displays fake security alerts to frighten the victim into buying bogus antivirus software or downloading malware.
Tap to view answer
What does Drive-by Download mean?
The automatic background download of malware without any user click, triggered merely by visiting a compromised website.

ThreatKey CharacteristicSecurity Measure
Computer VirusView Answer: Attaches to a host file/program and replicates itself; requires human action to executeView Answer: Updated antivirus software, avoiding execution of unknown files
Computer WormView Answer: Self-replicates and spreads independently across a network without needing a host fileView Answer: Network firewall, OS security patch updates, port blocking
Trojan HorseView Answer: Disguises itself as legitimate software; opens a secret backdoor in the systemView Answer: Downloading software only from trusted sources, EDR tools
RansomwareView Answer: Encrypts data and demands a ransom in cryptocurrency to unlock itView Answer: Offline 3-2-1 data backups, strong endpoint protection
Spyware / KeyloggerView Answer: Secretly monitors personal credentials and keyboard inputView Answer: Anti-spyware, on-screen virtual keyboard, use of MFA
RootkitView Answer: Hides deep at the operating system (Kernel) level to grant unauthorized accessView Answer: Secure Boot, kernel-level integrity monitoring
Botnet / DDoS AttackView Answer: Overloads and crashes a server with traffic from thousands of zombie systemsView Answer: DDoS mitigation services (Cloudflare), rate limiting, firewall
PhishingView Answer: Steals user credentials using fake emails or websitesView Answer: User awareness, email authentication (DMARC/SPF/DKIM)
Zero-Day ExploitView Answer: Immediately attacks unknown and unpatched security vulnerabilitiesView Answer: Behavioral analysis (Heuristics), sandboxing, virtual patching
Man-in-the-Middle (MitM)View Answer: Secretly intercepts and modifies communication between two partiesView Answer: HTTPS / SSL-TLS encryption, use of a secure VPN

One-Liner Questions

What are the primary characteristics of a secure and strong password?
At least 12–16 characters long, containing a mix of uppercase letters, lowercase letters, numbers, and special characters (!@#$).
Tap to view answer
How does a Dictionary Attack work in password cracking?
Rapidly guessing passwords using a list of common dictionary words and their combinations.
Tap to view answer
What is the technique behind a Brute Force Attack?
Systematically trying every possible character combination one by one until the correct password is found.
Tap to view answer
What is used in a Rainbow Table Attack?
A massive precomputed hash table containing the hash values of previously calculated passwords.
Tap to view answer
What is the process of Password Salting?
Adding a random string (random data) to a password before hashing it so that rainbow table attacks are rendered ineffective.
Tap to view answer
What is the main benefit of a Password Manager?
Generating unique, complex passwords and storing them securely in an encrypted vault protected by a single master password.
Tap to view answer
Which three primary factors are used in Two-Factor / Multi-Factor Authentication (2FA / MFA)?
1. Something you know (password/PIN), 2. Something you have (OTP/smart card), 3. Something you are (biometrics).
Tap to view answer
What are examples of Biometric Authentication?
Fingerprint scan, iris scan, facial recognition, and voice recognition.
Tap to view answer
What does FAR (False Acceptance Rate) measure in biometric security?
The rate at which a system mistakenly accepts an unauthorized (wrong) person.
Tap to view answer
What does FRR (False Rejection Rate) mean in biometrics?
The percentage rate at which a system mistakenly rejects a legitimate and authorized user.
Tap to view answer
What is the benefit of Single Sign-On (SSO) technology?
Allowing a user to log in just once to gain access to multiple related systems.
Tap to view answer
What is the primary use of the OAuth (Open Authorization) protocol?
Granting third-party apps limited access rights without sharing the user's password (Secure Delegated Access).
Tap to view answer
What does the 'S' stand for in HTTPS used for secure web browsing?
Secure (Hypertext Transfer Protocol Secure), which encrypts data using SSL/TLS.
Tap to view answer
What does the 'padlock' icon in a web browser's address bar indicate?
That the communication connection between the website and the browser is encrypted and the SSL/TLS certificate is valid.
Tap to view answer
What is the role of a Certificate Authority (CA)?
A trusted organization that verifies the identity of websites and issues digital SSL/TLS certificates to them.
Tap to view answer
What is the difference between Session Cookies and Persistent Cookies?
Session cookies are deleted as soon as the browser is closed, whereas persistent cookies remain on the hard disk until a set expiration date.
Tap to view answer
What is the security risk of Third-Party Cookies?
Tracking a user's browsing patterns across different websites to build their digital profile.
Tap to view answer
What does Incognito (Private Browsing) mode hide and what does it not hide?
It does not save browsing history and cookies on the local device, but it does not hide activity from the ISP, network admin, or websites.
Tap to view answer
What is Email Spoofing?
Altering the email header to make the sender's address appear as that of another trusted person or institution.
Tap to view answer
What does SPF (Sender Policy Framework) do for email authentication?
It verifies whether an email was sent from a mail server with an authorized IP address for that domain.
Tap to view answer
What is the main function of DKIM (DomainKeys Identified Mail)?
Adding a cryptographic digital signature to an email so that tampering in transit can be detected.
Tap to view answer
What is the role of the DMARC protocol?
Defining policies to reject or quarantine emails when SPF and DKIM checks fail.
Tap to view answer
What is the full form and purpose of CAPTCHA?
Completely Automated Public Turing test to tell Computers and Humans Apart; it ensures human response to block automated bots.
Tap to view answer
What improvement does reCAPTCHA offer over traditional CAPTCHA?
It enhances user experience through image recognition, the 'I'm not a robot' checkbox, or invisible behavioral analysis.
Tap to view answer
What is an Evil Twin Wi-Fi attack?
An attacker creating a rogue hotspot with an identical SSID to a legitimate Wi-Fi network to intercept user data.

What is the 'Signature-based Detection' technique in antivirus software?
Comparing file code against a predefined database of known malware (Virus Signatures).
Tap to view answer
What is the benefit of 'Heuristic Analysis' in antivirus software?
Identifying unknown or new (Zero-Day) malware based on suspicious code behavior and structure.
Tap to view answer
How does the Sandboxing security technique work?
Running unknown or suspicious files in a safe, isolated virtual environment to examine their behavior.
Tap to view answer
What is the primary function of a Firewall?
Filtering and controlling inbound and outbound network traffic based on predefined security rules.
Tap to view answer
At which OSI layer does a Packet Filtering Firewall operate?
At the Network Layer and Transport Layer (by inspecting IP addresses and port numbers).
Tap to view answer
What is the key feature of a Stateful Inspection Firewall?
It does not just inspect individual packets, but also tracks the state and sessions of the entire network connection.
Tap to view answer
At which layer does an Application-Level Firewall (Proxy Firewall) operate?
At the Application Layer (Layer 7) of the OSI model, inspecting specific application protocols such as HTTP and FTP.
Tap to view answer
What additional features does a Next-Generation Firewall (NGFW) have compared to a traditional firewall?
Deep Packet Inspection (DPI), integrated IPS, TLS/SSL inspection, and application awareness.
Tap to view answer
What is the main difference between IDS (Intrusion Detection System) and IPS (Intrusion Prevention System)?
An IDS only monitors and sends alerts about security threats (passive), whereas an IPS actively stops and blocks threats (active).
Tap to view answer
What does Endpoint Detection and Response (EDR) technology provide?
Continuous monitoring, threat detection, and automated response capabilities on every endpoint device (laptops, mobiles) in an organization.

What is the '3-2-1 Backup Rule' for data backup?
Keep 3 copies of data, store them on 2 different types of media, and keep 1 copy off-site (cloud or another location).

What is the definition of an Air-Gapped System?
A highly sensitive computer or network that is kept completely physically disconnected from the internet or any external network.
