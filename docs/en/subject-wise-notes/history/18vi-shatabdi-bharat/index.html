Mughal Emperors Chronology
Later Mughal Emperors, Reigns, and Major Political Events
Historical overview of the decline of the Mughal Dynasty from 1707 to 1857 CE
Mughal Emperor & Reign Epithet & Title Major Historical Events & Decisions
Bahadur Shah I (1707–1712 CE) Shah-e-Bekhabar (Shah Alam I) Released Shahuji from Mughal captivity; friendly relations with Guru Gobind Singh; campaigns against Banda Bahadur; ascended the throne at age 63
Jahandar Shah (1712–1713 CE) Lampat Murkh (Foolish Libertine) (Influence of Zulfiqar Khan) Ijarah (revenue-farming) system introduced by Zulfiqar Khan; Jizya abolished; conferred the title 'Mirza Raja' on Jai Singh of Amer
Farrukhsiyar (1713–1719 CE) Ghrinit Kayar (Contemptible Coward) (Installed by the Sayyid Brothers) Execution of Banda Bahadur in 1716; Golden Farman (royal trade decree) granted to the British in 1717; blinded and killed by the Sayyid Brothers
Muhammad Shah (1719–1748 CE) Rangeela (Roshan Akhtar) Downfall of the Sayyid Brothers; Nadir Shah's invasion of Delhi in 1739; looting of the Peacock Throne (Takht-e-Taus) and Kohinoor; rise of independent states
Ahmad Shah (1748–1754 CE) Pleasure-seeking Ruler (Nawab Nazira Javed Khan) Initial invasions of Ahmad Shah Abdali; blinded and deposed by Wazir Imad-ul-Mulk
Alamgir II (1754–1759 CE) Aziz-ud-din (Puppet of Imad-ul-Mulk) Historic Battle of Plassey in 1757 CE; assassinated in 1759 at the behest of Wazir Imad-ul-Mulk
Shah Alam II (1759–1806 CE) Ali Gauhar (Beset by Prolonged Crises) Third Battle of Panipat (1761); defeat in Battle of Buxar (1764); Treaty of Allahabad (1765); became a British pensioner in 1803
Akbar II (1806–1837 CE) First Under British Protection (Pensioner Emperor) Conferred the title 'Raja' on Rammohun Roy and sent him to England; Mughal coinage discontinued (1835 CE)
Bahadur Shah II (1837–1857 CE) Bahadur Shah 'Zafar' (Last Mughal Emperor) Led the First War of Independence in 1857; arrested from Humayun's Tomb; exiled to Rangoon and died in 1862

Historical Analysis
Multidimensional Causes of the Decline of the Mughal Empire — Structural Analysis
Comparison of political, administrative, military, and economic factors of decline
Dimension / Factor of Decline Root Cause & Nature Historical Impact on the Mughal Empire
Jagirdari Crisis (Satish Chandra's Theory) Unchecked increase in the number of Mansabdars; acute shortage of fertile jagirs (Paibaqi) for distribution Violent scramble among nobles to grab jagirs; soldiers' salaries fell into arrears; military apparatus collapsed
Agrarian Discontent & Revolts (Irfan Habib's Theory) Land revenue demand reaching up to 50% of produce; ruthless exploitation of peasants by Ijarahdars and Mughals Violent peasant uprisings in the form of Jats, Satnamis, Sikhs, and Marathas; destruction of the empire's productive base
Aurangzeb's Policies (Religious & Deccan) Reimposition of Jizya; temple destruction; hostility with Rajputs; 25-year military entanglement of the emperor in the Deccan Permanent alienation of Rajputs; royal treasury drained by the 'Deccan Ulcer'; administrative vacuum in North India
Court Factionalism (Weak Successors) Mutual rivalry among Turani, Irani, Hindustani, and Afghan nobles; incompetent and pleasure-loving emperors (Rangeela, Jahandar) Sayyid Brothers became 'Kingmakers'; emperors reduced to mere puppets; provincial Subedars declared independence
Foreign Invasions (Nadir & Abdali) Relentless attacks by Nadir Shah (1739) and Abdali (1748–67); lack of modern artillery and military techniques Imperial military prestige shattered completely; Mughal coins discontinued; path cleared for the British East India Company
