Early Political Platforms
Major Pre-Congress Political Organizations
Key associations established in India and London from 1836 to 1885 CE
Organization and Year of Establishment Founders and Key Leaders Primary Objective and Sphere of Activity
Landholders' Society (1838 CE, Calcutta) Dwarkanath Tagore, Prasanna Kumar Tagore, Radhakanta Deb Protection of the interests of zamindars of Bengal, Bihar, and Odisha; first experiment in constitutional agitation
British Indian Association (1851 CE, Calcutta) Radhakanta Deb (President) and Debendranath Tagore (Secretary) First organized demand in the 1853 Charter for a separate legislature for Indians and recruitment to higher posts
East India Association (1866 CE, London) Dadabhai Naoroji (Grand Old Man) Building public opinion in the British Parliament and public on Indian grievances, exploitation, and administrative reforms
Poona Sarvajanik Sabha (1870 CE, Pune) M. G. Ranade, G. V. Joshi, S. H. Chiplunkar Mediation between the government and the public; official representation on Deccan peasant issues and famine relief
Indian Association (1876 CE, Calcutta) Surendranath Banerjea and Ananda Mohan Bose Political organization of the middle class; nationwide agitation for restoring the Civil Service age limit; precursor to Congress
Madras Mahajana Sabha (1884 CE, Madras) M. V. Raghavachari, G. Subramania Iyer, P. Ananda Charlu Mobilizing public opinion in South India for local self-government rights and tax reforms
Bombay Presidency Association (1885 CE, Bombay) Pherozeshah Mehta, Badruddin Tyabji, K. T. Telang Strong front in Bombay against Lord Lytton's reactionary policies and opponents of the Ilbert Bill

Sessions & Milestones
Important Early Sessions of the Indian National Congress
Historic sessions, presidents, and key resolutions from 1885 to 1905 CE
Session Year and Venue Elected President Major Historical Events and Resolutions
First Session (1885) Venue: Bombay Womesh Chandra Bonnerjee (W. C. Bonnerjee) Attendance of 72 delegates; 9 resolutions passed; demand for Civil Service examination reforms and reduction in expenditure
Second Session (1886) Venue: Calcutta Dadabhai Naoroji (First Parsi President) 436 delegates; formal merger of Surendranath Banerjea's National Conference with Congress; Garden Party hosted by Lord Dufferin
Third Session (1887) Venue: Madras Badruddin Tyabji (First Muslim President) 607 delegates; famous slogan 'Be a Congressman'; first speech delivered in Tamil; formation of the Subjects Committee
Fourth Session (1888) Venue: Allahabad George Yule (First European / British President) Ban on government officials attending Congress; Maharaja of Darbhanga purchased 'Lowther Castle' to host the session
Fifth Session (1889) Venue: Bombay Sir William Wedderburn (British MP) First participation of 10 women delegates; resolution passed fixing the minimum voting age at 21 years
Twelfth Session (1896) Venue: Calcutta Rahimtulla M. Sayani First singing of the national song 'Vande Mataram' composed by Bankim Chandra Chatterjee; Naoroji's Drain of Wealth theory accepted
Twenty-first Session (1905) Venue: Banaras Gopal Krishna Gokhale Strong condemnation of the Partition of Bengal (Curzon); first formal endorsement of the 'Swadeshi' and 'Boycott' movements

Prominent Moderate Leaders — Methodology, Titles, and Key Contributions
Key personalities who awakened national consciousness within constitutional limits
Moderate Leader and Period Titles and Methodology Major Contributions, Organizations, and Literature
Dadabhai Naoroji (1825–1917 CE) 'Grand Old Man of India'; first Indian member of the British Parliament (1892); constitutional agitation Drain of Wealth theory; 'Poverty and Un-British Rule in India'; thrice Congress President (1886, 1893, 1906)
Surendranath Banerjea (1848–1925 CE) 'Rashtraguru' and 'Uncrowned King'; father of the Civil Service agitation; eloquent orator Indian Association (1876); National Conference; 'The Bengalee' newspaper; autobiography: 'A Nation in Making'; President in 1895 and 1902
Gopal Krishna Gokhale (1866–1915 CE) Political guru of Gandhiji; incisive budget analyst; style of gentle and fact-based opposition Servants of India Society (1905); Banaras Session (1905) President; deposed before Welby Commission; role in 1909 Morley-Minto Reforms
Sir Pherozeshah Mehta (1845–1915 CE) 'Uncrowned King of Bombay'; jurist and constitutional expert; father of Bombay Municipal Corporation Bombay Presidency Association (1885); Calcutta Session (1890) President; founder of 'Bombay Chronicle' newspaper
Dinshaw Edulji Wacha (1844–1936 CE) Economic and commercial expert; early financial manager of the Congress General Secretary of Congress for many years; Calcutta Session (1901) President; sharp critic of reckless Indian Railway expenditure

Demands & Colonial Issues
Major Demands of the Early Congress and the British Government's Attitude
Issues related to constitutional, administrative, economic, and civil rights
Dimension / Area of Demand Major Demands of the Early Congress Attitude and Response of the British Government
Constitutional and Legislative Demands Expansion of Central and Provincial Legislative Councils; entry of elected Indian members; right to discuss and vote on the budget Passed Indian Councils Act, 1892; allowed only limited discussion on the budget, denying supplementary questions and voting rights
Administrative and Military Demands Simultaneous holding of ICS exam in India and England; raising age limit to 23 years; 50% reduction in military expenditure British Parliament passed a resolution for simultaneous exams in 1893 but never implemented it; flatly refused to cut military spending
Economic and Financial Demands Abolition of Salt Tax; capping land revenue rates; protection to Indian industries; immediate halt to the drain of wealth Complete disregard of demands; formed the Welby Commission in 1895 as an eyewash; continued colonial exploitation through free trade
Judicial and Civil Demands Separation of judiciary from the executive; expansion of jury system; repeal of the 1878 Arms Act and press restrictions Retained repressive laws; prosecuted leaders like Tilak for sedition (Section 124A) in 1897 with 18 months of rigorous imprisonment
