Historical Conflict
Tripartite Struggle — Major Phases, Participating Rulers, and Outcomes
Two-century chronology of the struggle for supremacy over Kannauj
Phase & Period of Struggle Participating Sides & Rulers Major Event & Decisive Outcome
First Phase (Late 8th Century) Vatsaraja (Pratihara), Dharmapala (Pala), Dhruva (Rashtrakuta) Vatsaraja defeated Dharmapala; Dhruva defeated both; Rashtrakuta return to the south enabled temporary Pala influence over Kannauj
Second Phase (Early 9th Century) Nagabhata II (Pratihara), Dharmapala (Pala), Govinda III (Rashtrakuta) Nagabhata II defeated Chakrayudha & Dharmapala to capture Kannauj; northern campaign of Govinda III; temporary setback to Pratiharas
Third Phase (Mid-9th Century) Mihira Bhoja (Pratihara), Devapala (Pala), Amoghavarsha (Rashtrakuta) Strong balance of power under Devapala; after Devapala, Mihira Bhoja established permanent and undisputed Pratihara supremacy over Kannauj
Fourth Phase & End (10th Century) Mahipala I (Pratihara), Indra III & Krishna III (Rashtrakuta) Devastating raid on Kannauj by Indra III; decline of all three great powers; independent rise of new Rajput feudatories in North India

Inscription & Provenance Associated Ruler / Dynasty Main Subject & Historical Significance

Munger Copper Plate (Munger, Bihar) Devapala (Pala Dynasty) Devapala's military conquests (Utkala, Kamarupa, Hunas) and clear evidence of making Munger the capital
Nalanda Copper Plate (Nalanda, Bihar) Devapala / Balaputradeva Construction of a vihara at Nalanda by the Shailendra king of Sumatra and grant of 5 villages by Devapala for its upkeep
Gwalior Prashasti (Gwalior, M.P.) Mihira Bhoja (Gurjara-Pratihara) Detailed genealogy of the Pratiharas, suppression of Arabs by Nagabhata I, and military achievements of Mihira Bhoja
Sanjan Copper Plate (Sanjan, Maharashtra) Amoghavarsha I (Rashtrakuta) Performance of Hiranyagarbha Yajna by Dantidurga at Ujjain and account of Rashtrakuta northern military campaigns
Ellora Cave Inscription (Ellora, Maharashtra) Dantidurga & Krishna I Rashtrakuta victory over the Chalukyas, establishment of independent power, and mention of cave temple construction

Administrative Element / Tier Pala & Gurjara-Pratihara System Rashtrakuta Imperial System
Territorial Administrative Division Bhukti (Province) -> Vishaya (District) -> Mandala/Vithi -> Grama Rashtra (Province) -> Vishaya (District) -> Bhukti (Cluster of villages) -> Grama
Provincial & Local Officials Uparika (Provincial head), Vishayapati (District head), Gramapati (Village head) Rashtrapati (Rashtra head), Vishayapati (Vishaya head), Gramakuta/Gavunda (Village head)

Royal Patronage & Religious Policy Palas: Mahayana-Vajrayana Buddhism; Pratiharas: Staunch Brahmanical (Vaishnava/Shaiva) faith Liberal religious harmony (equal patronage to Shaivism, Vaishnavism, and Digambara Jainism)
Feudal Influence & Decline Excessive feudal decentralization; decline due to Kaivarta rebellion and Ghaznavid invasions Growing power of Mahamandaleshwaras, constant wars with Cholas, and overthrow by Kalyani Chalukyas
