Major Chola Rulers — Reign and Historical Achievements
Overview of Prominent Rulers from Vijayalaya to Kulottunga
Ruler & Reign Titles & Major Centers Key Military, Administrative & Architectural Achievements
Vijayalaya (850–871 CE) Narakesari Capital: Tanjore Defeated the Muttaraiyars to capture Tanjore; re-established the Chola Empire; built the Nishumbhasudini Temple
Aditya I (871–907 CE) Kodandarama Capital: Tanjore Killed Pallava king Aparajita and annexed the Pallava kingdom; built numerous stone Shiva temples along the banks of the Kaveri
Parantaka I (907–955 CE) Maduraikonda Capital: Tanjore Defeated the Pandyas; gilded the roof of the Chidambaram temple with gold; issued the famous Uttaramerur inscriptions; defeated at the Battle of Takkolam
Rajaraja I (985–1014 CE) Mummudichola, Shivapadasekhara Capital: Tanjore Defeated Cheras and Pandyas; conquered Northern Sri Lanka (Mummudicholamandalam); conducted land survey; built the grand Brihadisvara Temple at Tanjore
Rajendra I (1014–1044 CE) Gangaikondachola, Mudikonda Capital: Gangaikondacholapuram Conquered all of Sri Lanka; Ganga Valley expedition; successful naval expedition against the Srivijaya Empire; built the Cholagangam lake
Rajadhiraja I (1044–1054 CE) Jayangonda, Kalyanapuramgonda Capital: Gangaikondacholapuram Clashed with the Chalukyas of Kalyani; attained martyrdom fighting on the battlefield of Koppam in 1054 CE (died on the battlefield)
Kulottunga I (1070–1120 CE) Sungamtavirta (Abolisher of Tolls) Capital: Gangaikondacholapuram Merged the Chola and Eastern Chalukya dynasties; conducted land re-survey; abolished commercial tolls and duties; sent a 72-member trade embassy to China in 1077 CE

Major Chola Temples — Patrons, Sites, and Architectural Features
Key Exemplars of Chola-Era Dravidian Temple Architecture
Temple & Location Builder / Patron Ruler Key Architectural & Artistic Features
Brihadisvara (Rajarajesvara) Temple (Tanjore, Tamil Nadu) Rajaraja I (1010 CE) Pinnacle of Dravidian style; 216-foot-high 13-storeyed Vimana; 80-ton monolithic stone capstone; massive monolithic Nandi
Brihadisvara Temple (Gangaikondacholapuram, Tamil Nadu) Rajendra I (1035 CE) Grandeur similar to Tanjore; graceful and curved contours in the Vimana; vast courtyard and intricate stone sculptural art
Airavatesvara Temple (Darasuram, Tamil Nadu) Rajaraja II (12th Century) Chariot-shaped mandapa with stone wheels and horses; miniature and exquisitely carved pillars
Kampaharesvara (Tribhuvanam) Temple (Tribhuvanam, Tamil Nadu) Kulottunga III (Late 12th Century) Last great exemplar of Later Chola architecture; Shiva depicted as Sarangadhara; towering gopurams and ornate sculptures
Koranganatha Temple (Srinivasanallur, Tamil Nadu) Parantaka I (Early 10th Century) Fine example of early Chola architecture; simple Vimana and graceful Ardhanarisvara sculptures on the walls
