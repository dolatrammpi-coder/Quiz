Comparative Features of Nirguna and Saguna Bhakti Traditions
Comparison of conception of God, mode of worship, and social outlook
Philosophical Basis / Element Nirguna Bhakti Tradition (Sant Poetry) Saguna Bhakti Tradition (Avatara Worship)
Fundamental Nature of God View Answer Formless, attribute-less, omnipresent, unborn, and transcendent Supreme Reality View Answer Embodied, with attributes, endowed with human emotions, playful Lord (Rama, Krishna)
Mode of Worship & Devotion View Answer Repetition of the Divine Name (nam-smaran), shabd-sadhana, inward meditation, and Guru's grace; rejection of idol worship View Answer Idol worship, temple rituals, kirtan, bhajan, singing of divine deeds (lila-gan), and conjugal/filial devotion
Caste System & Ritualism View Answer Fierce critique of caste distinctions, external ostentation, pilgrimages, fasts, and priestly dominance View Answer Religious tolerance and social harmony, while respectfully accepting the varna order
Prominent Representative Saints View Answer Kabirdas, Guru Nanak Dev, Dadu Dayal, Raidas, Malukdas View Answer Goswami Tulsidas, Surdas, Mirabai, Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, Vallabhacharya
Language of Expression View Answer Vernaculars, Sadhukkadi (Panchmel Khichdi), folk dialects, Ulatbansi View Answer Literary Awadhi, Brajbhasha, Maithili, Rajasthani, and Bengali

Sufi Silsila & Founder Principal Centers & Saints in India Key Philosophical Ideas & Features
Chishti Silsila (Khwaja Abu Ishaq Shami) View Answer Ajmer, Delhi, Punjab, Badaun Moinuddin Chishti, Bakhtiyar Kaki, Farid, Auliya View Answer Simple living, strong support for Sama (spiritual music), complete distance from state power and royal patronage, religious harmony
Suhrawardi Silsila (Shaikh Shihabuddin Suhrawardi) View Answer Multan, Uch, Sindh Shaikh Bahauddin Zakariya, Ruknuddin View Answer Affluent and grand lifestyle, acceptance of state offices and wealth, prohibition of music (Sama), strict adherence to Sharia
Qadiri Silsila (Shaikh Abdul Qadir Jilani) View Answer Punjab, Sindh, Deccan Shah Niamatullah, Miyan Mir, Mullah Shah View Answer Moderate approach, association of Dara Shikoh; Miyan Mir laid the foundation stone of the Golden Temple at Amritsar
Naqshbandi Silsila (Khwaja Bahauddin Naqshband) View Answer Delhi, Sirhind (Punjab) Khwaja Baqi Billah, Shaikh Ahmad Sirhindi View Answer Most orthodox and rigid order; vehement opposition to music and Sama; Wahdat-ul-Shuhud; supported by Aurangzeb
Shattari Silsila (Shah Abdullah Shattari) View Answer Jaunpur, Malwa, Gwalior Shaikh Muhammad Ghaus View Answer Deep synthesis of Yogic practices and Hindu philosophy; Shaikh Muhammad Ghaus was Tansen's guru; intense spiritual trance
Firdausi Silsila (Shaikh Badruddin Samarqandi) View Answer Bihar (Maner & Rajgir) Shaikh Sharfuddin Yahya Maneri View Answer Branch of the Suhrawardi order; most popular in Bihar; spiritual instruction through Maktubat (letters)
