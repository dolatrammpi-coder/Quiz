Company-Era Laws
Major Acts Under Company Rule — 1773 to 1853
Comparative framework of administrative and legislative evolution of the East India Company
Act and Year Contemporary Governor-General Key Constitutional Provisions and Impact
Regulating Act (1773 CE) Warren Hastings (First Governor-General of Bengal) Governor-General and 4-member Council; Supreme Court established at Calcutta in 1774; ban on private trade and gifts
Pitt's India Act (1784 CE) Warren Hastings Separation of commercial and political functions; Board of Control (6 members) constituted; beginning of Dual Control system
Charter Act (1793 CE) Sir John Shore Company's trade privileges extended for 20 years; salaries of the Board of Control charged on Indian revenues
Charter Act (1813 CE) Lord Minto I / Hastings Company's trade monopoly ended (except tea and trade with China); provision of Rs. 1 lakh for education; permission to Christian missionaries
Charter Act (1833 CE) Lord William Bentinck (First Governor-General of India) Commercial rights completely abolished; GG of Bengal became 'Governor-General of India'; Law Member added; Macaulay Law Commission
Charter Act (1853 CE) Lord Dalhousie Executive and legislative functions separated; 6-member Legislative Council (Mini-Parliament); provision for open competitive exam for Civil Services

Crown Rule
Major Acts Under the British Crown — 1858 to 1935
Comparative overview of constitutional reforms under direct British imperial control
Act and Year Contemporary Viceroy Key Constitutional Reforms and Features
Government of India Act (1858 CE) Lord Canning (First Viceroy) Company rule ended; direct Crown control; new post of Secretary of State (with 15-member Council); abolition of dual system
Indian Councils Act (1861 CE) Lord Canning Beginning of representative institutions; three Indians nominated; Portfolio system recognized; ordinance power (6 months)
Indian Councils Act (1892 CE) Lord Lansdowne Principle of indirect election; limited right to discuss budget and ask questions (no supplementary questions or voting rights)
Morley-Minto Reforms (1909 CE) Lord Minto II (Lord Morley: Secretary of State) Separate communal electorate for Muslims; first Indian in Viceroy's Council (S. P. Sinha); right to ask supplementary questions
Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms (1919 CE) Lord Chelmsford (Edwin Montagu: Secretary of State) Dyarchy in provinces (Reserved and Transferred); bicameral legislature at the Centre; direct elections; separate electorates for Sikhs and Christians
Government of India Act (1935 CE) Lord Willingdon / Linlithgow All-India Federation; provincial autonomy (dyarchy ended in provinces); dyarchy at the Centre; three lists; Federal Court (1937)

Historical Chronology
Chronological Table of Constitutional Development from 1773 to 1947
Milestones from the Regulating Act to the Indian Independence Act
Name and Year of Act Contemporary Historical Context Most Significant Historical Change
Regulating Act (1773 CE) Corruption and financial crisis in the Company Post of Governor-General of Bengal; Supreme Court at Calcutta in 1774; foundation of Parliamentary control
Pitt's India Act (1784 CE) Rectification of defects of the 1773 Act Formation of Board of Control; clear division of commercial and political functions; dual system
Charter Act (1813 CE) Napoleon's Continental System Company's trade monopoly ended (except tea and China); Rs. 1 lakh annually for education
Charter Act (1833 CE) Liberal industrial reforms in Britain Company's commercial role ended; 'Governor-General of India'; Macaulay Law Commission
Charter Act (1853 CE) Final review of Company rule Separation of executive and legislature; 6 legislative councillors; open competition for Civil Services
Government of India Act (1858 CE) First War of Independence of 1857 Company rule ended; governance transferred to British Crown; Secretary of State post and 'Viceroy' designation
Indian Councils Act (1861 CE) Demand to associate Indians with governance Beginning of representative institutions; provincial autonomy (decentralization); recognition of Portfolio system
Indian Councils Act (1892 CE) Persistent demands of the Congress (1885) Principle of indirect election; expansion of Legislative Councils; right to debate the budget
Morley-Minto Reforms (1909 CE) Swadeshi Movement and militant nationalism Separate communal electorate for Muslims; first Indian in Viceroy's Council (S. P. Sinha)
Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms (1919 CE) First World War and Home Rule Movement Dyarchy in provinces; bicameral system at the Centre; Public Service Commission; direct elections
Government of India Act (1935 CE) Simon Report and three Round Table Conferences Provincial autonomy; dyarchy at the Centre; Federal Court; separation of Burma; basis of the present Constitution
Indian Independence Act (1947 CE) Mountbatten Plan (3 June 1947) Two sovereign Dominions of India and Pakistan on 15 August 1947; complete end of British sovereignty

Comparative Framework
Constitutional Reforms of 1909, 1919, and 1935 — Comparative Analysis
Comparative study of representation, provincial structure, elections, and central framework
Constitutional Dimension Reforms of 1909 (Morley-Minto) Position in the Acts of 1919 and 1935
Expansion of Communal Electorate Introduction of separate electorate only for Muslims (foundation of communalism) 1919: Extended to Sikhs, Christians, Anglo-Indians, and Europeans; 1935: Separate electorates extended to Dalits, women, and labour
Administrative Structure of Provincial Governance Numerical majority of non-official members in provinces, but real power rested with the Governor 1919: Dyarchy in provinces (Reserved and Transferred); 1935: Provincial dyarchy abolished, full 'Provincial Autonomy'
Nature and Election of Central Legislature Unicameral Central Council (60 members); official majority retained; indirect selection 1919: Bicameral legislature at the Centre and direct elections; 1935: Proposal for All-India Federation and dyarchy at the Centre
Franchise and Financial Powers Extremely narrow property-based franchise; permission to ask supplementary questions on the budget 1919: Voting rights to women and separation of budgets; 1935: Franchise extended to ~14% of population and 3 legislative lists

Stages of Constitution-Making
Key Events in Constitution-Making — From the First Sitting to Enforcement
Summary of the historical chronology of making the Indian Constitution from 1946 to 1950
Date / Period Event in Constitution-Making Historic Decision and Impact
9 December 1946 (First Sitting) Inaugural session of the Constituent Assembly Central Hall of Parliament House Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha became temporary President; 207 members attended; complete boycott by the Muslim League
11 & 13 December 1946 (Permanent President & Objectives) Dr. Rajendra Prasad permanent President; Objectives Resolution moved by Nehru H. C. Mookerjee elected Vice-President; ideological framework of an independent sovereign republic presented; adopted on 22 January 1947
22 July 1947 (National Flag) Adoption of the National Flag Tricolour (saffron, white, green) featuring the Sarnath Ashoka Chakra instead of the charkha accepted as the National Flag
29 August 1947 (Drafting Committee) 7-member Drafting Committee under Dr. B. R. Ambedkar's chairmanship Reviewed B. N. Rau's draft and prepared the official legal draft of the new Constitution; debated for 114 days
26 November 1949 (Adoption of Constitution) Constitution passed and adopted (Constitution Day) Signed by 284 members; 16 articles including citizenship and elections came into immediate effect; 395 Articles and 8 Schedules adopted
24 January 1950 (Final Sitting) 12th Session of the Constituent Assembly Dr. Rajendra Prasad elected first President; 'Jana Gana Mana' declared National Anthem and 'Vande Mataram' National Song; final signatures of members
26 January 1950 (Enforcement of Constitution) India proclaimed a Republic (Republic Day) Acts of 1935 and 1947 repealed; India became a fully Sovereign Democratic Republic; Constitution came into full force
