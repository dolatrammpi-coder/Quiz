Reform Programmes
Objectives, Ideologies, and Social Programmes of Major Reform Movements
Ideological foundation, instruments of social reform, and key impacts
Movement / Organization Core Ideological and Philosophical Basis Major Social Reform Programmes
Brahmo Samaj Advaita philosophy of the Upanishads, rationalism, human dignity, respect for moral truths of all religions Legal abolition of Sati; strong attack on polygamy, child marriage, and caste untouchability; support for widow marriage
Arya Samaj 'Back to the Vedas'; Vedas are divine knowledge and infallible truth; rejection of Puranas and idol worship Shuddhi Movement (reconversion); DAV Schools and Gurukul education; inter-caste marriage; opposition to child marriage
Prarthana Samaj Tradition of medieval Marathi Bhakti saints (Tukaram, Namdev), monotheistic prayer, and spiritual purification Eradication of caste system and untouchability; widow remarriage and orphanages; spread of education via night schools
Ramakrishna Mission Neo-Vedanta philosophy; 'Yato Mat Tato Path' (as many faiths, so many paths); 'Jiva is Shiva' (service to man is worship of God) Free relief work during famines, epidemics, and floods; establishment of modern hospitals, libraries, and Ramakrishna Mission schools
Satyashodhak Samaj Rejection of Brahminical scriptures and priestly exploitation; liberation of Shudras and Ati-Shudras and human equality Marriage ceremonies without priests; India's first girls' schools for untouchables and girls (Pune); peasant rights

Legal Reforms
Major British Acts Related to Social Reforms — Years and Provisions
Laws against Sati, female infanticide, widow remarriage, and child marriage
Name and Year of Act Concerned Governor-General / Viceroy Key Legal Provisions and Social Reform
Prohibition of Female Infanticide (1795 and 1804 CE) Sir John Shore and Lord Wellesley Bengal Regulations XXI (1795) and III (1804); declared killing of female infants a crime punishable equally as ordinary murder
Abolition of Sati Act (1829 CE) Lord William Bentinck (Regulation XVII) Through Raja Rammohan Roy's efforts; declared Sati illegal and culpable homicide; enforced first in Bengal, then Madras-Bombay in 1830
Abolition of Slavery (1843 CE) Lord Ellenborough (based on Charter Act of 1833) Act V (1843) declared slavery illegal in India; buying/selling of slaves and bonded labour prohibited
Religious Disabilities Act (Lex Loci Act, 1850) Lord Dalhousie A person could not be deprived of inheritance of ancestral property even upon converting to another religion
Hindu Widow Remarriage Act (1856 CE) Lord Canning (Act XV) Through Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar's tireless efforts; gave full legal recognition to widow remarriage and legitimized children born from it
Age of Consent Act (1891) Lord Lansdowne Through B. M. Malabari's efforts; prohibited marriage of girls below 12 years of age; Bal Gangadhar Tilak strongly opposed it
Sharda Act (Child Marriage Restraint, 1929) Lord Irwin (Enforced: 1 April 1930) Through Dr. Harbilas Sharda's efforts; legally fixed minimum marriageable age at 14 years for girls and 18 years for boys
