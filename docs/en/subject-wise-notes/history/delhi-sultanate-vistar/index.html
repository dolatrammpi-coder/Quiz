Ibrahim Lodi (Lodi Dynasty) 1517–1526 CE Last of Lodi Dynasty Suppressed Afghan nobles; Gwalior conquest; defeated by Rana Sanga at Battle of Khatoli; killed at Panipat in 1526

Ambitious Experiments
Major Schemes of Muhammad bin Tughlaq — Objectives & Outcomes
Analysis of the five most controversial policy experiments of the Sultanate
Scheme / Experiment Objective & Implementation Outcome & Historical Impact
Taxation Increase in Doab (1326 CE) 10–20% tax hike to raise additional revenue from the fertile Ganga-Yamuna Doab Severe famine the same year; widespread peasant rebellion; scheme had to be withdrawn; heavy loss of life
Transfer of Capital (1327 CE) Direct administrative control over wealthy South Indian states; renamed Devagiri as 'Daulatabad' Delhi residents forced into a 1,500 km march on foot; discontent in the South; capital shifted back to Delhi in 1335
Token Currency (1329–30 CE) Counter global silver shortage; circulated bronze/copper coins at par with silver Tanka Zero state control; large-scale counterfeiting; coins redeemed from treasury by paying silver Tankas
Khurasan Expedition (1332–33 CE) Plan to conquer Khurasan and Iraq in Central Asia exploiting Mongol instability Paid 1 year's advance salary to 3.7 lakh soldiers; expedition cancelled due to Central Asian treaty; huge financial loss
Qarachil Expedition (1333 CE) Check Chinese incursion and subdue rebellious hill chiefs of the Kumaon frontier After initial victory, army perished due to severe mountain rains and plague; only a few soldiers returned alive

Decline of the Sultanate
Major Rulers of the Lodi Dynasty & Events of the Battle of Panipat
Chronology from Afghan kingship to the establishment of Mughal power in 1526
Lodi Ruler & Period Major Political Developments Historical Outcome & End of Sultanate
Bahlul Lodi (1451–1489 CE) Principle of Afghan equality ('Masnad-i-Ali'); victory over Sharqi dynasty of Jaunpur; Bahluli coin Foundation of the first Afghan empire; partial restoration of Delhi Sultanate's lost prestige and territorial control
Sikandar Lodi (1489–1517 CE) Founded Agra in 1504 (capital in 1506); Gaz-i-Sikandari; Gulrukhi poems; religious orthodoxy Administrative centralization; economic prosperity by abolishing transit duties; control over Afghan nobles
Ibrahim Lodi (1517–1526 CE) Suppression of Afghan nobles; conspiracy of Daulat Khan and Alam Khan; defeat at Battle of Khatoli; invitation to Babur Sultanate weakened by internal strife; betrayal by Afghan chieftains; opened the path for invasion of North India
First Battle of Panipat (21 April 1526) Babur vs. Ibrahim Lodi; epoch-making first use of Tulughma tactics and gunpowder artillery Ibrahim Lodi killed on the battlefield; 320-year-old Delhi Sultanate ended; Mughal Empire established in India
