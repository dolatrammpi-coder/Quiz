Comparative Revision
Comparative Features of the Palaeolithic, Mesolithic, and Neolithic Ages
Subsistence and technological development
Aspect Palaeolithic and Mesolithic Neolithic
Main Economy Hunting, gathering, fishing; early animal domestication in the Mesolithic More systematic development of agriculture and animal husbandry
Tools Large stone tools; microliths in the Mesolithic Diverse tools including ground and polished stone implements
Settlement Primarily mobile; some relatively stable settlements in the Mesolithic Expansion of permanent or relatively stable settlements
Agriculture Not a primary basis; transitional experiments in some regions Agriculture became an important foundation of life
Animal Husbandry Early tendencies in the Mesolithic Expansion of systematic animal husbandry
Pottery Generally not a major feature Use increased in several Neolithic communities
Technology Stone-knapping and micro-tool manufacturing techniques Development of grinding, polishing, and agricultural tools
Way of Life Predominantly hunter-gatherer Transition toward a food-producing economy
Social Organization Small mobile bands predominant More organized communities due to permanent settlements
Relationship with Environment High dependence on natural resources Tendency toward more planned use of the environment
