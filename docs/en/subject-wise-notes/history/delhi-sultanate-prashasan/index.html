Central Administration
Central Departments of the Delhi Sultanate — Functions & Concerned Sultans
Major Sultanate-era Diwans, responsibilities, and founding rulers
Central Department (Diwan) Concerned Officer & Major Functions Founding / Reorganizing Sultan
Diwan-i-Wizarat (Finance Department) Wazir; financial control of the entire state, land revenue assessment, and expenditure supervision Existed from the early Sultans; reached its zenith during the Tughlaq period
Diwan-i-Arz (Military Department) Ariz-i-Mamalik; recruitment of soldiers, supplies, inspection, Dagh-Huliya, and salaries Ghiyasuddin Balban (strengthened by Alauddin Khilji)
Diwan-i-Insha (Correspondence Department) Dabir-i-Mamalik; drafting royal firmans and maintaining confidential state correspondence Continuously effective from the early Slave Dynasty
Diwan-i-Risalat (Foreign / Appeals Department) Sadr / Muhtasib; relations with foreign envoys, diplomatic letters, and public grievances Functional from the early Sultanate period
Diwan-i-Mustakhraj (Revenue Arrears Department) Mustakhraj officer; strict recovery of revenue arrears from Iqtadars and peasants Alauddin Khilji (1296–1316 CE)
Diwan-i-Kohi (Agriculture Department) Amir-i-Kohi; bringing barren land under cultivation, Sondhar loans, and crop rotation Muhammad bin Tughlaq (1325–1351 CE)
Diwan-i-Bandagan (Department of Slaves) Naib-i-Bandagan; maintenance, salaries, and craft training of 1.8 lakh slaves Firuz Shah Tughlaq (1351–1388 CE)

Major Taxes of the Sultanate Period — Nature, Basis & Concerned Classes
Comparative classification of Sharia-sanctioned and non-Sharia taxes
Name of Tax Nature, Rate & Basis Concerned Taxpayer Class / Sector
Ushr Religious land revenue; 1/10 (10%) on naturally irrigated land and 1/20 (5%) on artificially irrigated land Only Muslim landowners and their authorized agricultural land
Kharaj General land revenue; 1/5 to 1/3 of produce (50% during Alauddin's reign) Non-Muslim Hindu peasantry and all non-Muslim agricultural land
Khums Tax on war booty looted from the enemy or underground buried treasure (1/5 or 4/5) Soldiers and royal treasury (Alauddin and MBT took 80% for the state)
Jizya Protection and military-service exemption tax; annual rates of 48, 24, and 12 Dirhams Non-Muslim subjects (Zimmis); Firuz Tughlaq imposed it on Brahmins as well
Zakat Mandatory Islamic religious tax; 2.5% (1/40th) on annual savings/wealth Only affluent Muslim citizens; proceeds spent exclusively for poor Muslims
Haqq-i-Sharb / Abwab Haqq-i-Sharb (10% irrigation tax); Abwab (24 local cesses like Chari, Ghari, Muqaddami) Peasants irrigating from canals (Haqq-i-Sharb); general public (Abwab)

Major Architecture/Monuments of the Sultanate Period, Builders & Features
Details of historic monuments from the Slave Dynasty to the Lodi period
Monument & Location Builder Sultan & Period Major Architectural & Artistic Features
Quwwat-ul-Islam Mosque (Mehrauli, Delhi) Qutbuddin Aibak (1192–1198 CE) First Turkish mosque in India; reuse of ornate stone pillars from 27 Hindu-Jain temples
Qutb Minar (Mehrauli, Delhi) Aibak (started), Iltutmish (completed), Firuz Tughlaq (5th storey) 72.5-metre-high 5-storey red sandstone minaret; projecting carved balconies
Adhai Din Ka Jhonpra (Ajmer, Rajasthan) Qutbuddin Aibak (1199 CE) Conversion of a Sanskrit college built by Vigraharaja IV; ornate arched screen wall
Sultan Garhi Tomb (Malikpur, Delhi) Shamsuddin Iltutmish (1231 CE) First Islamic tomb in India; underground octagonal chamber and fortress-like structure
Tomb of Balban (Mehrauli, Delhi) Ghiyasuddin Balban (1287 CE) First scientific construction of a True Arch in the Indian subcontinent
Alai Darwaza (Qutb Complex, Delhi) Alauddin Khilji (1311 CE) Horseshoe arch, perforated lattice windows, marble ornamentation; 'Gem of the Sultanate'
Tomb of Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq (Tughlaqabad, Delhi) Ghiyasuddin / MBT (1325 CE) Roman-style battered/sloping walls (Salami technique); white marble dome over red sandstone
Tomb of Sikandar Lodi (Lodi Garden, Delhi) Ibrahim Lodi (1517–18 CE) First use of the Double Dome in India; built in the center of a Charbagh-style garden
