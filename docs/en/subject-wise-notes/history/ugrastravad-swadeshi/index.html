Historical Chronology
Major Events from the Partition of Bengal (1905) to the Surat Split (1907)
Chronological account of the turning points in the Indian freedom struggle
Date / Year & Event Associated Leaders & Major Centers Historical Outcome & Impact
19 or 20 July 1905 (Announcement of Partition) Lord Curzon & Andrew Fraser; Shimla & Calcutta Official announcement of the Partition of Bengal; unprecedented discontent and wave of protest across Bengal
7 August 1905 (Launch of Swadeshi) Town Hall (Calcutta); Surendranath Banerjea, Ananda Mohan Bose Formal proclamation of the Swadeshi Movement; historic Boycott Resolution against foreign goods adopted
16 October 1905 (Partition Effective) All over Bengal; Rabindranath Tagore, Ramendrasundar Trivedi Observance of Day of Mourning and Raksha Bandhan Day; singing of 'Amar Sonar Bangla'; foundation of Federation Hall
December 1905 (Benares Congress Session) Benares; President: Gopal Krishna Gokhale Strong condemnation of Curzon's policies and partition; formal support for Swadeshi and Boycott
October–December 1906 (Foundation of Muslim League) Shimla & Dhaka; Aga Khan, Nawab Salimullah Shimla Deputation; formation of All-India Muslim League at Dhaka on 30 December 1906; demand for separate electorates
December 1906 (Calcutta Congress Session) Calcutta; President: Dadabhai Naoroji First demand for 'Swaraj' from the Congress platform; 4 resolutions passed on Swaraj, Swadeshi, Boycott, and National Education
26 December 1907 (Surat Congress Split) Surat (Banks of Tapti); President: Dr. Rash Behari Ghosh Historic clash between Moderates and Extremists; Congress split into two factions; Extremists expelled; 6 years imprisonment for Tilak

Dimensions of the Movement
Core Programmes of the Swadeshi Movement — Boycott, Education, and Self-Reliance
Four major constructive and negative programmes that shook the foundation of British rule
Programme of the Movement Specific Activities & Major Organizations Historical Objective & Mass Impact
Boycott of Foreign Goods (Boycott) Renunciation of Manchester cloth, Liverpool salt, foreign sugar, shoes, and utensils; bonfires of foreign clothes Direct blow to British colonial trade; unprecedented revival of Indian handloom and cottage industries
Promotion of Indigenous Goods (Swadeshi Enterprise) Swadeshi handloom textiles, soap and match factories; Bengal Chemicals; V. O. C. Pillai's Steam Navigation Company Establishment of economic self-reliance; unprecedented growth of Indian capitalists and artisans
National Education Movement (National Education) Dawn Society (1902); National Council of Education (1906); Bengal National College; Bengal Technical Institute Boycott of government educational institutions; spread of patriotic, scientific, and technical education in the mother tongue
Self-Reliance & Constructive Work (Atmashakti) Arbitration courts by Swadesh Bandhab Samiti; boycott of social evils; organization of folk festivals and fairs Revival of villages; awakening of self-respect and moral strength; making the masses fearless and organized
