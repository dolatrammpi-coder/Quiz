Formation of the Swaraj Party
Swaraj Party — Founders, Objectives, Strategy, and Major Achievements
Politics of constitutional obstruction within the legislatures from 1923 to 1926
Component / Dimension Founders & Nature Key Strategy & Achievements
Establishment & Leadership 1 January 1923 (Allahabad); Founders: C. R. Das (President) and Motilal Nehru (Secretary) Formed as an internal wing within the Congress; aimed to enter legislative councils and wreck dyarchy from within
1923 Election Results Remarkable victory in 42 out of 101 elected seats in the Central Legislative Assembly Clear absolute majority in the Central Provinces; largest single party in Bengal; satisfactory performance in Bombay and UP
Legislative Achievements in the Central Assembly Formed a joint front called the 'Nationalist Party' with Jinnah (Independent) and Malaviya Rejected the 1924 Budget; passed resolutions to repeal repressive laws; led to the appointment of the Muddiman Committee
Election of Vithalbhai Patel Became the first elected Indian Speaker of the Central Legislative Assembly on 24 August 1925 Defeated official candidate T. Graham; defeated the Public Safety Bill in 1928 with his casting vote
Split & Decline After Das's death in 1925, the 'Responsivists' (Lala Lajpat Rai, Kelkar, Jayakar) broke away Acceptance of ministerial office by S. B. Tambe; defeat in the 1926 elections; re-merged into the Civil Disobedience Movement in 1930

Historical Chronology
Chronology of Major Events from 1923 to 1929
From the foundation of the Swaraj Party to the Purna Swaraj resolution at Lahore
Year & Chronology Historical Event Key Details & Impact
1 January 1923 Establishment of the Swaraj Party (Allahabad) Formed by C. R. Das and Motilal Nehru; aimed to enter legislative councils and create constitutional deadlock for the government
November 1923 Central & Provincial Legislative Elections Notable success for the Swarajists; 42 seats in the Central Legislative Assembly; absolute majority in Central Provinces; largest party in Bengal
24 August 1925 Election of Vithalbhai Patel as Speaker Became the first Indian President (Speaker) of the Central Legislative Assembly; defeated the British candidate by 2 votes; established legislative supremacy
8 November 1927 Formal Announcement of the Simon Commission Appointed by British Prime Minister Baldwin; 7-member all-white commission; constituted 2 years ahead of schedule to review the Act of 1919
3 February 1928 Arrival of the Simon Commission in India (Bombay) Nationwide hartal with black-flag demonstrations; slogans of 'Simon Go Back'; Madras Congress (1927) had resolved on complete boycott
10 August 1928 Presentation of the Nehru Report (Lucknow) First Indian-drafted constitutional framework prepared by the Motilal Nehru Committee; demanded Dominion Status and 19 Fundamental Rights
30 October 1928 Anti-Simon Lathi Charge in Lahore Lala Lajpat Rai severely injured by Saunders' lathi blows; attained martyrdom on 17 November; assassination of Saunders by Bhagat Singh (17 December)
March 1929 Announcement of Jinnah's 14 Points (Delhi) Muslim League's 14-point communal demands following rejection of the Nehru Report; insistence on separate Muslim electorates
31 December 1929 'Purna Swaraj' Resolution at the Lahore Congress Tricolour hoisted by Jawaharlal Nehru on the banks of the Ravi; decision to celebrate 26 January 1930 as the first Independence Day

Constitutional Deadlock
Simon Commission, Nehru Report, and Jinnah's 14 Points — Comparative Overview
Analysis of India's future constitutional framework and communal demands from 1927 to 1929
Document / Commission & Year Background & Key Provisions Response & Political Outcome
Simon Commission (November 1927 / May 1930) 7-member all-white commission to review the 1919 Act; recommended abolition of dyarchy in provinces and introduction of responsible government Nationwide boycott over exclusion of Indians; martyrdom of Lalaji; formed the basis for the Round Table Conferences and the Act of 1935
Nehru Report (10 August 1928) All-Parties Committee formed on Birkenhead's challenge; Dominion Status, 19 Fundamental Rights, joint electorates, linguistic provinces Youth (Nehru-Bose) demanded Purna Swaraj; disagreement from Muslim League and Hindu Mahasabha; 1-year ultimatum at Calcutta
Jinnah's 14 Points (March 1929 CE) 14-point charter of demands presented at the League's Delhi session opposing the Nehru Report's abolition of separate electorates Weak centre with residuary powers to provinces; 1/3rd Muslim reservation; rejected by Congress; deepened the communal divide

Historic Sessions
Lahore Session of 1929 — President, Purna Swaraj Resolution, and Decisions
Epochal turning point in the Indian freedom struggle — from Dominion Status to Complete Independence
Aspect of the Lahore Session Specific Decisions & Resolutions Historical Significance & Impact
Presidency & Leadership December 1929, Lahore (banks of the Ravi); President: Pandit Jawaharlal Nehru (at Gandhiji's insistence) Rise of young leadership; Nehru declared himself a socialist and republican, infusing new energy into the movement
Purna Swaraj Resolution Historic resolution for 'Purna Swaraj' (Complete Independence) adopted at midnight on 31 December 1929 Nehru Report's demand for 'Dominion Status' lapsed; complete independence became the sole declared national goal of the Congress
Hoisting of the Tricolour Flag At 12 midnight on 31 December 1929, Pandit Nehru hoisted the tricolour of independent India on the banks of the River Ravi Skies echoed with slogans of 'Inquilab Zindabad' and 'Vande Mataram'; formal declaration of the end of British servitude
Independence Day on 26 January Congress Working Committee decided to celebrate 26 January every year as 'Purna Swadhinata Diwas' (Complete Independence Day) First Independence Day celebrated across the country on 26 January 1930; the Constitution came into force on this same date in 1950
Authorization of Future Movement Order for complete boycott of legislative councils; Gandhiji entrusted with supreme authority to launch the 'Civil Disobedience Movement' Gandhiji's 11-point demands in January 1930; upon rejection, launch of the Dandi March on 12 March 1930
