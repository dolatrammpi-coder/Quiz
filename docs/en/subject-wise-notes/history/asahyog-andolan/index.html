Historical Chronology
Chronology of Major Events from 1919 to 1922
Sequence of events from the Rowlatt Act to Chauri Chaura and the withdrawal of the Non-Cooperation Movement
Year & Date Major Historical Event Immediate Outcome & Impact
March 1919 Passage of the Rowlatt Act (Black Act) Powers of arrest without lawyer, appeal, or plea (Bina Vakil, Bina Appeal, Bina Dalil); Gandhiji's call for the first nationwide hartal on 6 April
13 April 1919 Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (Amritsar) Massacre of 1,000+ civilians by General Dyer; Martial Law in Punjab; Rabindranath Tagore renounced his 'Knighthood'
23–24 November 1919 All India Khilafat Conference (Delhi) Gandhiji unanimously elected President; historic platform for Hindu-Muslim unity formed; decision to boycott British goods
1 August 1920 Formal Launch of the Non-Cooperation Movement Demise of Bal Gangadhar Tilak early in the morning; nationwide hartal; Gandhiji returned his 'Kaisar-i-Hind' medal
4–9 September 1920 Special Session of Congress at Calcutta Under Lala Lajpat Rai's presidentship, the historic Non-Cooperation resolution moved by Gandhiji was passed by majority
December 1920 Annual Session of Congress at Nagpur C. Vijayaraghavachariar as President; main resolution moved by C. R. Das; new Congress constitution and 4-anna membership approved
31 March 1921 Vijayawada Congress Working Committee Meeting National targets fixed to collect Rs. 1 crore in the Tilak Swaraj Fund and distribute 20 lakh charkhas
17 November 1921 Arrival of the Prince of Wales in India Nationwide complete hartal and protests with black flags; fierce demonstrations in Bombay; public bonfires of foreign cloth
4 or 5 February 1922 Chauri Chaura Violent Incident (Gorakhpur) Crowd provoked by police firing set the police station ablaze; 22 policemen burned to death; Gandhiji deeply distressed
12 February 1922 Bardoli Resolution (Withdrawal of the Movement) Formal announcement by Gandhiji suspending the Non-Cooperation Movement; resentment among leaders; Gandhiji arrested on 10 March

Religio-Political Synthesis
Khilafat Movement — Key Leaders, Objectives, Congress Alignment, and Impact
Coordination of the Indian national platform in support of the Turkish Caliph after World War I
Dimension / Aspect of the Movement Key Leaders & Core Objectives Congress Stance & Political Impact
Core Motivation & Objective Protection of the Turkish Caliph's sacred places (Jazirat-ul-Arab) and annulment of the humiliating Treaty of Sevres Anti-British outrage among Indian Muslims; Gandhiji viewed it as a joint anti-imperialist front
Prominent Leaders Maulana Muhammad Ali, Shaukat Ali (Ali Brothers), Abul Kalam Azad, Hakim Ajmal Khan, Hasrat Mohani Gandhiji became President of the All India Khilafat Conference; Tilak, Nehru, and C. R. Das later extended full support
Coordination with Congress Integrating the Khilafat Movement as a core pillar of the Non-Cooperation Movement to wage a united struggle Approved at the September 1920 Calcutta Session; a historic zenith of Hindu-Muslim unity was achieved
Decline & Impact of the Movement Abolition of the Caliphate in 1924 by Mustafa Kemal Pasha; secular modernization of Turkey Core basis of the movement ended; Moplah Rebellion in Malabar heightened communal tension; Muslim masses entered active politics

Movement Outline
Major Programs, Measures, and Participation of Social Groups in the Non-Cooperation Movement
Two-tier structure of boycott and constructive programs along with mass participation
Program / Nature of Policy Main Objectives & Adopted Measures Participation of Various Social Classes
Renunciation of Titles & Offices (Negative Aspect) Rejecting the moral legitimacy of British rule; returning 'Kaisar-i-Hind' and Rai Bahadur titles Upper-middle class, intellectuals, and eminent citizens renounced honorary offices and titles
Boycott of Educational Institutions (Negative & Positive) Boycotting government schools and colleges; establishing parallel national educational institutions Thousands of students left government colleges; enrolled in Kashi, Bihar, Gujarat Vidyapiths, and Jamia
Boycott of Law Courts (Judicial Reform) Paralyzing the British judicial system; establishing village panchayats to settle disputes Top lawyers such as C. R. Das, Motilal Nehru, Rajendra Prasad, and Patel gave up legal practice
Boycott of Foreign Cloth (Economic Blow) Public bonfires of foreign cloth; crippling Manchester imports and promoting indigenous Khadi Widespread participation by women, merchants, and the general public; Khadi and Gandhi cap became national attire
Picketing of Liquor & Toddy Shops (Social Purification) Damaging government excise revenue; freeing workers and families from intoxication Women picketed liquor shops; toddy palm trees were cut down; sharp decline in government revenue
Rural & Peasant Upsurge (Regional Expansion) Ending zamindari exploitation, forced labor (begar), and heavy rent; no-tax movements in Awadh, Guntur, and Bijolia Direct participation of lakhs of peasants and tribals; rise of Baba Ramchandra, Madari Pasi, and Alluri Raju

Turning Points & Outcomes
Chauri Chaura Incident and Withdrawal of the Non-Cooperation Movement — Decision and Impact
The incident of 4 February 1922, Gandhiji's moral decision, and its impact on Indian politics
Historical Event / Aspect Sequence of Events & Gandhiji's Decision Immediate & Long-Term Political Impact
Chauri Chaura Incident (4/5 February 1922) At Chauri Chaura in Gorakhpur district, a crowd provoked by police lathi-charge and firing surrounded the police station; 22 policemen died in the fire Gandhiji believed the country was not yet ready for a completely non-violent movement; the government would have gained a moral pretext for brutal repression
Bardoli Resolution (12 February 1922) Emergency meeting of the Congress Working Committee at Bardoli; resolution passed suspending Civil Disobedience and Non-Cooperation with immediate effect Peasants instructed to pay land revenue; Congress directed toward constructive work like Khadi, prohibition, national education, and removal of untouchability
Reaction of Nationalist Leaders Sharp and anguished criticism by Subhas Chandra Bose, C. R. Das, Motilal Nehru, and Lala Lajpat Rai; widespread disappointment among the masses Censure motion moved by Dr. Moonje; sudden withdrawal created deep ideological differences within top Congress leadership
British Repression & Gandhiji's Arrest (10 March 1922) Disarray among the public following withdrawal; British government arrested Gandhiji from Sabarmati and tried him for sedition (Section 124A) 6 years' imprisonment sentenced by Judge Broomfield; released in 1924 due to ill health; political vacuum gave rise to the Swaraj Party and revolutionary groups
