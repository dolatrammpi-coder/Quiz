Institutional Consolidation
Major Measures & Institutions for Consolidation of Power by Iltutmish
Administrative steps that provided stability to the Delhi Sultanate
Institutional Measure / Policy Objective & Administrative Nature Contemporary & Historical Impact
Receipt of Mansur from Caliph (1229 CE) Independent legitimate authority by obtaining the patent of 'Sultan-i-Azam' from the Caliph of Baghdad The Sultanate gained legal recognition in the Islamic world; claims of rivals ended
Turkan-i-Chihalgani (Chalisa Dal) Supreme military and administrative core group of 40 loyal Turkish slaves Consolidated royal power, though it later became a center of anti-Sultan conspiracies
Organization of Iqta System Assignment of land revenue territories (Iqta) to officers in place of cash salaries Checked feudal decentralization, maintained a regular army, and ensured revenue collection
Pure Arabic Coinage System Silver Tanka (175 grains) and copper Jital; mint name inscribed on coins Established monetary sovereignty of the Sultanate; greatly facilitated trade and commerce
Astute Mongol Diplomacy (1221 CE) Politely refused asylum to Mangabarni; kept Genghis Khan neutral Saved the nascent Delhi Sultanate from catastrophic Mongol bloodshed and devastation

Shamsuddin Kayumars (1290 CE)
Statecraft Policy
Balban's Theory of Kingship & Major Administrative Measures
Establishment of an autocratic monarchy based on Iranian traditions
Kingship Theory / Policy Implementation & Administrative Nature Objective & Historical Outcome
Niyabat-i-Khudai & Zill-i-Ilahi The king is God's deputy and His shadow; the Sultan is absolute and divine Restored the prestige of the Sultan's office; instilled awe and reverence among nobles and commoners
Sijda, Pabos & Nauroz Festival Kneeling before the Sultan, kissing his feet, and grand celebration of the Persian New Year Strict court discipline; complete elimination of Turkish nobles' claims to equality
Blood and Iron Policy Ruthless execution of rebels, enslaving women and children, and clearing forests Suppressed rebellions of Mewatis, Katehar, and Doab; established law and order in Delhi
Destruction of Turkan-i-Chihalgani Eliminated prominent Chalisa nobles (Sher Khan, etc.) through poison and punishment Ended factionalism among nobles; complete centralization of power in the Sultan's hands
Diwan-i-Ariz & Espionage System Military department independent of Wazir (Imad-ul-Mulk) and dense network of spies (Barid) Improved military efficiency; swift and impartial intelligence on officials' activities

Major Administrative, Military, and Economic Reforms of Alauddin Khilji
Epoch-making reforms enacted to establish a centralized military monarchy
Reform Area / Scheme Key Rules & Departments Objective & Impact
4 Anti-Rebellion Ordinances Confiscation of grant lands, spy network, prohibition of liquor, and complete ban on social gatherings of nobles Eradicated noble conspiracies; supreme consolidation of the Sultan's personal authority
Military Reforms & Cash Salary Cash salary (234 Tanka), large permanent central army, Dagh (horse branding) and Huliya (soldier roll) systems Ended middlemen and fake musters in the army; decisive military victories over Mongols and Indian states

Separation of Religion and Politics Rejected religious interference of the Ulema; placed state interest above Sharia Initiated purely secular pragmatic politics; absolute supremacy of the Sultan
