Details of the January–February 1937 election results and ministries across 11 British provinces
Province & Total Seats Election Results & Party Position Government, Premier & Key Facts
Madras (Total 215 seats) Landslide Congress victory with 159 seats (absolute majority); crushing defeat for the Justice Party C. Rajagopalachari became Premier; enforced prohibition and temple entry legislation
United Provinces (UP) (Total 228 seats) Congress won 134 seats (clear majority); Muslim League won only 29 seats Govind Ballabh Pant became Premier; Rafi Ahmad Kidwai Finance Minister; Tenancy Act passed
Bihar (Total 152 seats) Congress won 98 seats (clear majority); independents and other parties trailed Sri Krishna Sinha became Premier; Anugrah Narayan Sinha Finance Minister; peasant debt relief
Central Provinces & Berar (Total 112 seats) Congress won 70 seats (clear majority); independents defeated Dr. N. B. Khare (later Pt. Ravishankar Shukla) became Premier; educational reforms
Odisha (Total 60 seats) Congress won 36 seats (clear majority); rajas and zamindars defeated Stable government under Harekrushna Mahatab and Biswanath Das; tenancy reforms
Bombay (Total 175 seats) Congress won 86 seats (near majority); formed government with support of independents B. G. Kher became Premier; K. M. Munshi Home Minister; Industrial Disputes Act
Assam (Total 108 seats) Congress emerged as the single largest party with 33 seats; initially Saadulla government In September 1938, a Congress coalition government was formed under Gopinath Bordoloi
North-West Frontier Province (NWFP) (Total 50 seats) Congress emerged as the single largest party with 19 seats; influence of Khudai Khidmatgar Congress coalition government formed under Dr. Khan Sahib (brother of Frontier Gandhi)
Bengal (Total 250 seats) Krishak Praja Party 36, Muslim League 38, Congress 54 (single largest party) Fazlul Huq (Krishak Praja Party) formed a coalition government with the Muslim League
Punjab (Total 175 seats) Unionist Party won 89 seats (clear majority); Congress 18, League 1 Non-Congress Unionist government formed under Sir Sikandar Hayat Khan
Sind (Total 60 seats) Sind United Party emerged as the largest party; party instability Non-Congress government led by Ghulam Hussain Hidayatullah, later Allah Bakhsh

Major Policies, Achievements, and Background of Resignation of Congress Ministries
28 months of rule, public welfare reforms, and collective resignation over World War II
Administrative / Policy Area Major Policies & Achievements of Congress Ministries Background & Impact of the 1939 Resignation
Civil Liberties & Release of Political Prisoners Lifted government restrictions on the press; repealed emergency powers; lifted the ban on Hindustan Seva Dal; secured the release of Kakori and other political prisoners Clash with Governors over prisoner release; Premiers of UP and Bihar threatened resignation; Governors eventually relented
Agrarian & Land Reforms (Tenancy Laws) Passed Tenancy Acts; rent remissions; legal ceiling on moneylenders' interest; ban on evictions; restored confiscated lands to peasants Stiff opposition from zamindars; pressure from Kisan Sabhas for more radical reforms; paved the way for social security
Social Reforms & Education (Prohibition & Wardha) Enforced prohibition (liquor ban) in Madras and Bombay; temple entry law for Dalits in Madras; launched Mahatma Gandhi's Wardha Basic Education Scheme Sharp decline in government excise revenue, but improvement in public health; concrete legal steps against untouchability
Labour & Industrial Policies Improved working conditions of workers; Bombay Industrial Disputes Act (1938); minimum wage and arbitration committee Opposition to certain provisions by Communists and leftists, yet successful in establishing industrial peace
Mass Resignation of 1939 (World War II Crisis) World War II began on 1 September 1939; Viceroy Linlithgow declared India a belligerent nation without consultation; denied assurance of independence Resignation from 8 provinces on 22 October 1939 on CWC directives; Muslim League observed 'Day of Deliverance' on 22 December

Historical Chronology
Chronology of Major Events Related to Haripura, Tripuri, and Forward Bloc
Account of Subhas Chandra Bose and the ideological conflict within Congress from 1938 to 1940
Session / Event & Year Associated Leaders & Background Major Decisions, Crisis & Political Outcomes
Haripura Congress Session (February 1938, Gujarat) President: Subhas Chandra Bose; Pandit Nehru, Sardar Patel Subhas elected unopposed as President; National Planning Committee formed (Nehru Chairman); moral support to movements in Princely States
Tripuri Congress Election (29 January 1939) Subhas Chandra Bose vs. Dr. Pattabhi Sitaramayya Subhas re-elected President by defeating Gandhi-backed Sitaramayya by 203 votes; Gandhi termed it his 'personal defeat'
Tripuri Session & Pant Resolution (March 1939, Jabalpur) Subhas Bose (severely ill), Govind Ballabh Pant, Gandhiji Pant Resolution passed: 'Working Committee be constituted according to Gandhiji's wishes'; Gandhiji's non-cooperation created a severe organizational deadlock
Resignation of Subhas Bose (29 April 1939, Calcutta) Subhas Chandra Bose and Congress Working Committee Following failure to resolve the deadlock, Subhas resigned as President; Dr. Rajendra Prasad became the new President for the remainder of 1939
Formation of Forward Bloc (3 May 1939) Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose and Left-wing Nationalists Establishment of a progressive left-wing party within the Congress; given an all-India form in Bombay on 22 June 1939
Disciplinary Action (August 1939) Congress Working Committee vs. Subhas Chandra Bose For protesting against Provincial Congress Committee decisions, Bose was disqualified from holding any elected Congress office for 3 years
