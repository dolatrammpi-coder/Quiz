Warfare & Strategy
Babur's Military Tactics — Artillery, Tulughma & Use in Major Battles
Successful tactical implementation of Central Asian warfare methods in India
Military Technique / Strategy Origin & Nature Use in Major Battles & Tactical Advantage
Tulughma System (Flanking Envelopment) View Answer Central Asian battle formation learned from the Uzbeks; dividing the army into right and left flanking units to suddenly encircle the enemy from the rear View Answer Panipat (1526) & Khanwa (1527); encircled and disoriented the vast traditional armies of Lodi and Sanga from all sides
Ottoman (Rumi) Method (Artillery Arrangement) View Answer Technique adopted from the Ottoman Turks; lashing carts (bullock/camel carts) together with leather thongs and positioning cannons in the gaps View Answer Panipat & Khanwa; provided secure cover for gunners (Ustad Ali and Mustafa); gunpowder fire caused stampedes among enemy war elephants
Mounted Archers (Mobile Cavalry) View Answer Turko-Mongol cavalry tradition; shooting arrows accurately in any direction while riding at full gallop View Answer All four major battles; shattered enemy vanguard lines; high mobility neutralized slow, heavy war elephants
Mining & Trench Warfare (Siege Engineering) View Answer Persian and Turkish siegecraft; digging explosive mines beneath fort walls and constructing protective trenches View Answer Chanderi Fort (1528) & Bhira (1519); carved a path up the Chanderi hill in a single night to capture the fort ramparts

Subject / Dimension Details Recorded in the Baburnama Historical Significance & Remarks
Original Language & Composition View Answer Turkish language (Chagatai Turkic); original title: 'Tuzuk-i-Baburi' View Answer One of the finest autobiographies in world literature; testimony to Babur's candor
Persian & Other Translations View Answer Abdur Rahim Khan-i-Khanan (during Akbar's reign); Payanda Khan; English translation: Mrs. Beveridge (1905) View Answer Most translated text of the Mughal period; titled 'Baburnama' in Persian

Battle & Year Location & Adversary Strategic Outcome & Historical Impact
Siege of Kalinjar (1531 CE) View Answer Kalinjar Fort (Bundelkhand) Adversary: Prataparudra Deva Chandela View Answer Humayun's first military expedition; could not capture the fort; lifted the siege after concluding a treaty and accepting tribute
Battle of Dauharia (1532 CE) View Answer Dauraha (Banks of the Gomti, Jaunpur) Adversary: Mahmud Lodi and the Afghans View Answer Resounding Mughal victory; Mahmud Lodi defeated and fled; dealt a severe blow to Afghan power

Battle of Kannauj (Bilgram) (17 May 1540) View Answer Bilgram (Kannauj, U.P.) Adversary: Sher Shah Suri View Answer Humayun decisively defeated; Sur dynasty established over Agra and Delhi; 15-year exile for Humayun
Battle of Machhiwara (15 May 1555) View Answer Ludhiana (Banks of the Sutlej, Punjab) Adversary: Nasib Khan and Tatar Khan View Answer Victory under Mughal commander Bairam Khan; Mughal control re-established over Punjab
Battle of Sirhind (22 June 1555) View Answer Sirhind (Punjab) Adversary: Sikandar Shah Suri View Answer Decisive victory by Bairam Khan and Humayun; Humayun re-enthroned in Delhi on 23 July 1555
