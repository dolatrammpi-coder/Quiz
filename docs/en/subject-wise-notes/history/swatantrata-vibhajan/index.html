Historic Plans
Major Pre-Independence Plans and Missions — Comparative Table
Proposals presented between 1942 and 1947 to resolve the constitutional deadlock
Plan / Mission & Year Proponent & British Representative Key Provisions & Outcome
Cripps Mission (March 1942) Sir Stafford Cripps (Member of British War Cabinet) Post-war Dominion Status and Constituent Assembly; option for provinces to secede; rejected by both Congress and the League
C. R. Formula (March 1944) C. Rajagopalachari (Backed by Mahatma Gandhi) Plebiscite by adult franchise in Muslim-majority border areas; rejected by Jinnah insisting on full Pakistan
Wavell Plan (June 1945) Lord Wavell (Then Viceroy) Indianization of the Viceroy's Executive Council (50% Hindu, 50% Muslim); failed at the Simla Conference due to Jinnah's veto
Cabinet Mission (March–May 1946) Pethick-Lawrence, Stafford Cripps, A. V. Alexander Rejected the demand for Pakistan; three-tier federation (Groups A, B, C); election of Constituent Assembly; formation of Interim Government
Mountbatten Plan (3 June 1947) Lord Mountbatten (Last British Viceroy) Partition of India into two sovereign Dominions (15 August 1947); partition of Punjab and Bengal; freedom of princely states

Chronology of Major Political Events of 1946–1947
Decisive sequence of events from the arrival of the Cabinet Mission to India's complete independence
Date & Year Major Historical Event Immediate & Long-Term Outcome
24 March 1946 Arrival of the Cabinet Mission in Delhi Three-tier federation plan presented on 16 May; demand for Pakistan rejected; paved the way for Constituent Assembly elections
16 August 1946 Muslim League's Direct Action Day Severe communal riots in Calcutta and Noakhali; killing of thousands of innocents; inevitability of partition increased
2 September 1946 Formal Formation of the Interim Government Government formed under Nehru's leadership; League joined on 26 October and began creating internal deadlock
9 December 1946 First Meeting of the Constituent Assembly (Delhi) Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha as temporary President; boycotted by the Muslim League; Rajendra Prasad elected permanent President on 11 December
20 February 1947 Declaration by British Prime Minister Attlee Resolve to transfer power before 30 June 1948; Lord Mountbatten appointed Viceroy in place of Wavell

18 July 1947 Indian Independence Act, 1947 Passed by the British Parliament with Royal Assent; legal basis for the independence of India and Pakistan on 15 August
14–15 August 1947 Independence Day of Pakistan and India Pakistan became independent on 14 August and India on 15 August; 200 years of British colonial rule ended

Constitutional Provisions
Key Provisions of the Indian Independence Act, 1947
Main clauses of the legal document approved by the British Parliament on 18 July 1947
Dimension of Provision Legal Clauses & Arrangements of the Act Constitutional & Administrative Impact
Establishment of Two Sovereign Dominions On 15 August 1947, two fully sovereign Dominions named India and Pakistan would be established Legislative control of the British Parliament over India ended completely; rule of the British Crown ended
Abolition of Viceroy & Secretary of State Offices of the Viceroy and Secretary of State for India abolished; each Dominion to have a Governor-General The Governor-General became merely a constitutional head; he would act strictly on the advice of the Indian Cabinet
Sovereignty of Constituent Assemblies Constituent Assemblies of both nations empowered as legislatures to make full laws and repeal British laws Constituent Assemblies became sovereign parliaments; gained authority to legislate until India's constitution was completed
End of Paramountcy over Princely States British Paramountcy over princely states lapsed; they were free to join India or Pakistan or remain independent Challenge of integrating 565 princely states into India arose; resolved by Patel and Menon through skilled diplomacy
Transitional Governance System Until a new constitution was framed, both nations would be governed by an adapted form of the 'Government of India Act, 1935' Special privileges and discretionary powers of the Governor-General were abolished; full responsible government established

Impact of Partition
Major Administrative, Economic, and Human Consequences of the Partition of India
Account of displacement, financial division, and security challenges arising from the 1947 Partition
Dimension of Partition Immediate Administrative & Demographic Nature Long-Term Historical & Geopolitical Impact
Demographic & Humanitarian Crisis Cross-border displacement of 1.5 crore people; 5 to 10 lakh innocents killed in communal violence; brutal atrocities against women Largest refugee crisis in history; massive relief camps in Delhi, Punjab, and Bengal; separate ministry for refugee rehabilitation
Physical Division of Administration & Army Division of soldiers on religious lines (64% to India, 36% to Pakistan); division of arms, ammunition, railway wagons, office tables, chairs, and even books Administrative machinery disrupted; migration of senior Muslim officers to Pakistan; formation of new armies for border security
Economic & Agricultural Structural Fragmentation Raw jute-producing areas went to East Pakistan, while jute mills remained in India (Calcutta); 31% of canal-irrigated agricultural land went to Pakistan Severe crisis of foodgrains and raw materials (jute, cotton) in India; canal water dispute (dragged on until the 1960 Indus Waters Treaty)
Division of Financial Assets Out of undivided India's total cash balances, 75 crore rupees were to go to Pakistan (20 crore given first, 55 crore after Gandhi's fast) RBI continued managing central banking and currency for Pakistan for some time; complex division of debts and liabilities
Geopolitical & Permanent Border Disputes Borders hastily drawn by Radcliffe in 5 weeks; tribal invasion of Kashmir; disputes over Sylhet in Assam and Chittagong Hill Tracts in Bengal Three major wars between India and Pakistan (1947–48, 1965, 1971); birth of permanent military tension and nuclear rivalry in South Asia
