Sangam Assemblies
The Three Sangam Assemblies — Venues, Presidents & Literature
Traditional Accounts, Patronage Venues & Historical Evidence
Sangam Assembly Venue & President / Patron Key Literature & Historical Details
First Sangam (Madurai) Madurai | Sage Agastya | 89 Pandya Kings No extant text available; believed to have been submerged in the sea.
Second Sangam (Kapatapuram) Kapatapuram | Agastya & Tolkappiyar | 59 Pandya Kings 'Tolkappiyam' (grammar text) is the sole surviving work.
Third Sangam (North Madurai) Northern Madurai | Nakkirar | 49 Pandya Kings Most of the currently extant Sangam literature belongs to this assembly.

Sangam Literature
Major Sangam Texts and Epics
Authors, Subject Matter & Historical Significance
Sangam Text / Epic Author & Tradition Subject Matter & Historical Significance
Tolkappiyam Tolkappiyar (Second Sangam) Tamil grammar, poetics, and social norms.
Tirukkural (Kural) Tiruvalluvar Bible of Tamil literature; exposition of Dharma, Artha, and Kama.
Silappadikaram Ilango Adigal (Chera Prince) Story of Kovalan and Kannagi; basis of the Pattini cult.
Manimekalai Sittalai Sattanar (Buddhist Merchant) Buddhist life of Manimekalai, daughter of Kovalan and Madhavi.
Jivaka Chintamani Tiruttakkadevar (Jain Monk) Story of Prince Jivaka; famously known as the 'Book of Marriages'.

Society, Economy, and Administration in the Sangam Age
Administrative Bodies, Social Classes & Economic Aspects
Social / Economic Aspect Key Institution / Class Important Historical Facts
Administrative Institutions Manram, Aimperungulu, Enperayam Checked royal absolutism and administered the judicial system.
Social Class Structure Arasar (Rulers), Vellalar (Peasants), Kadaisiyar (Agricultural Laborers) Occupational and geographical division rather than a rigid Varna system.
Civilization & Status of Women Fire-pit Sati (Tippaydal), Scholar-poetess Avvaiyar Women received high education, though the life of widows was harsh.
Agriculture & Industry Paddy as main crop; Uraiyur as cotton textile center 'Ma' and 'Veli' as land measurement units; Kaveri Valley was the most fertile region.
