Functional Classification of Key Posts in the Mauryan Administrative Bureaucracy
Officer / Post Department / Rank Category Key Functions and Responsibilities
Samaharta Revenue Department (Tirtha) Collected revenue, prepared the budget, and maintained records of income and expenditure

Vyavaharika Civil Court (Tirtha) Chief judge of the Dharmasthiya (civil) court

Senapati Military Department (Tirtha) Commander-in-chief of the entire Mauryan army and war strategist
Yuvaraja Royal Dynasty (Tirtha) Crown prince and administrative associate to the king
Sitadhyaksha Agriculture Department (Adhyaksha) Managed crown agricultural lands (Sita) and state agricultural production
Akaradhyaksha Mining Department (Adhyaksha) Controlled and supervised mines and mineral resources
Lakshanadhyaksha Mint Department (Adhyaksha) Chief officer in charge of minting coins (currency)
Pautavadhyaksha Weights and Measures (Adhyaksha) Inspected and regulated standards of weights and measures
Kupyadhyaksha Forest Department (Adhyaksha) Oversaw forests and forest produce (timber and resources)
Shulkadhyaksha Commercial Taxes (Adhyaksha) Chief collector of tolls, customs duties, and trade taxes

Administrative Structure
Principal Organs of Mauryan Administration: Central, Provincial, Local, Municipal, and Military
Comparative Structure of the Five Administrative Tiers of Mauryan Governance
Administrative Organ / Tier Key Officials / Governing Body Salient Features and System
Central Administration Emperor, Mantrinah, 18 Tirthas, and 26 Adhyakshas Highly centralized; appointments via Upadha Parikshana; the king was supreme
Provincial Administration Kumara / Aryaputra, Vishayapati, Rajjuka 5 provinces (Chakras); limited provincial autonomy; royal family control
Local and Rural Sthaniya, Dronamukha, Gopa, Gramika Groupings of 800/400/100/10 villages; Grama (village) was the smallest autonomous unit
Municipal Administration Nagaraka, Astynomoi (6 boards / 30 members) 6 committees for industries, foreigners, births/deaths, trade, and tax collection
Military Administration Senapati, Nayaka, War Council (6 boards) Separate boards for infantry, cavalry, elephants, chariots, navy, and transport/commissariat

Revenue System
Major Mauryan Taxes/Revenue Systems — Bhaga, Bali, Hiranya, etc. — and Their Nature
Analytical Overview of Mauryan Taxation System, Tax Types, and Evidence
Tax / Revenue Name Basis and Nature Evidence and Historical Source
Bhaga Principal land revenue tax (1/6th to 1/4th of produce) Arthashastra, Ashoka's Rummindei Pillar Edict
Bali Religious/tribute tax (exempted by Ashoka in Lumbini) Rummindei Pillar Edict, Arthashastra
Hiranya Tax collected in cash (in lieu of grain) Kautilya's Arthashastra
Udakabhaga Irrigation tax (1/5th to 1/3rd share, for state canals) Arthashastra (Kshetradhyaksha and canal references)
Pranaya Emergency benevolence tax (special levy on subjects) Arthashastra (to replenish treasury during crises)
Vishti Forced / unpaid labor Arthashastra and Junagarh Inscription
Shulka Toll/customs duty on trade goods and frontier checkpoints Arthashastra (Shulkadhyaksha details)
Vartani Road cess (levied for roads and transit security) Arthashastra (Sarthavaha and road management)
