Administrative & Military Reforms
Major Administrative, Land Revenue, and Military Reforms of Sher Shah Suri
Reorganization of Welfare-Oriented and Military Monarchy in Medieval India
Reform Area / Subject Administrative Structure & Departments Historical Objective & Impact
Central Administration (4 Major Diwans) Diwan-i-Wizarat (Finance), Diwan-i-Ariz (Military), Diwan-i-Insha (Correspondence), and Diwan-i-Risalat (Foreign/Religious Affairs) Complete centralization of power in the Sultan's hands; ministers functioned like secretaries enabling swift decision-making
Local Administration (Sarkar & Pargana) Divided into 47 Sarkars (districts); Shiqdar-i-Shiqdaran (law & order) & Munsif-i-Munsifan (justice); Pargana headed by Shiqdar, Amin, Fotadar End of feudal anarchy; balance across administrative units and swift local justice for the people
Military Reforms (Dagh & Chehra) Direct recruitment of soldiers, cash salaries, branding of horses (Dagh), descriptive roll of soldiers (Chehra/Huliya), and regular inspections Revival of Alauddin Khilji's system; elimination of intermediary nobles and creation of a disciplined standing central army
Espionage & Judicial System Barid (intelligence) network via dak chowkis; Qazi and Mir-Adl; impartial personal justice by the Sultan Advance intelligence on crimes and rebellions; policy of 'no leniency in justice for any relative or noble'

Land Revenue Administration
Sher Shah's Land Revenue System — Measurement, Classification, and Assessment
Forerunner of the Ryotwari and Zabti Systems
Revenue Element / Term Process & Structure Associated Officials & Impact
Land Measurement (Sikandari Gaz) Used the 32-digit (39-inch) Sikandari Gaz instead of hemp rope; standard Bigha (60×60 yards) Amin (surveyor) and Patwari; accurate area recorded through actual land measurement
Land Classification (Based on Productivity) Land divided into three categories: Good (Uttam), Middling (Madhyam), and Bad (Nikrishta); average produce calculated Munsif and Qanungo; standard state produce rate fixed by averaging the three categories
Land Revenue Rate (Rai System) State share fixed at 1/3rd (33.3%) of average produce; only 1/4th in Multan Shiqdar and Amil; payable in both cash and kind, though cash was given top priority
Patta & Qabuliyat (Legal Contract) Patta: written title deed of land and tax given by state to peasant; Qabuliyat: written deed of acceptance signed by peasant Ended exploitation by intermediaries (Khuts, Muqaddams); established direct legal link between peasant and state
Jaribana & Mahasilana (Survey & Collection Fees) Jaribana (surveyor's fee): 2.5%; Mahasilana (tax collection fee): 5% additional cess Collected in cash from peasants to maintain revenue staff; complete tax remission during famines

Roads & Communication System
Sher Shah's Roads, Sarais, and Postal System — Commercial & Military Importance
Network of Four National Highways and 1,700 Sarais Connecting the Empire
Road / Institution Extent & Structure Administrative, Commercial & Postal Importance
Sadak-i-Azam (Grand Trunk Road) From Sonargaon (Bengal) via Agra, Delhi, Lahore up to Attock (Indus River) (~1,500 kos / 2,500 km) Main artery of the empire; rapid movement of troops; direct commercial link from Bengal to the North-West Frontier
Agra to Burhanpur Road (Deccan Highway) From capital Agra via Dholpur, Gwalior, Narwar up to Burhanpur (Khandesh border) Strategic military route connecting North India with Malwa and commercial centres of South India
Agra to Jodhpur-Chittor Road (Rajputana Highway) From Agra via Fatehpur Sikri, Jaipur, Jodhpur up to Chittorgarh Fort Tight control over major Rajputana forts and rulers, and security for Gujarat trade
Lahore to Multan Road (North-Western Route) From Punjab's capital Lahore to the south-western trading city of Multan Protection of Balochistan, Sindh, and Kandahar trade caravans and monitoring of frontier outposts
Sarais & Dak Chowkis (1,700 Sarais) Brick-built sarai every 2 kos (approx. 6 km); separate food and water arrangements for Hindus and Muslims; 2 relay horses Called 'arteries of the empire' by Abbas Khan Sarwani; couriers carried mail hundreds of miles in 24 hours

Currency Reforms
Currency System of Sher Shah's Reign — Rupiya, Dam, and Their Economic Role
Abolition of Mixed-Metal Coins and Introduction of Pure Bi-Metallic Currency
Coin Name / Metal Weight (Grains) & Exchange Rate Economic & Commercial Significance
Silver Rupiya (Standard Silver Coin) 178 grains (173 grains pure silver); round and square shapes Foundational measure of value in the empire; remained the standard currency until the British period (1835)
Copper Dam (Daily Exchange Coin) 380 grains pure copper; exchange ratio of 1 : 64 with the Rupiya Primary medium for everyday transactions, wage payments, and local haats and bazaars
Gold Mohur (Ashrafi) 167 to 169 grains pure gold; high-value store of wealth Used for large international trade settlements, royal gifts, nazrana, and treasury reserves
Copper Subdivisions (Adhela, Paula, Damri) Subdivisions of Dam: Adhela (1/2), Paula (1/4), and Damri (1/8 Dam) Facilitated very small daily purchases; effectively checked inflation
23 Royal Mints (Bilingual Inscription) Kalima, Sultan's name, title, and mint name in Arabic; 'Sri Sher Shah' in Devanagari script Completely curbed currency counterfeiting; ensured wide acceptance among Hindu and Muslim subjects and boosted trade

Architectural Heritage
Major Architectural Monuments Related to Sher Shah Suri, Locations, and Features
Finest Transitional Specimens of Indo-Islamic Architecture
Monument & Location Builder & Architect Key Architectural Features & Purpose
Tomb of Sher Shah (Sasaram, Bihar) Sher Shah Suri (Completed by: Islam Shah) Architect: Aliwal Khan Octagonal mausoleum built on a 30-foot-high square plinth in the middle of a vast artificial lake; grand dome in red sandstone; called 'superior to the Taj Mahal in masculine grandeur'
Qila-i-Kuhna Mosque (Purana Qila, Delhi) Sher Shah Suri (1542 CE) Mosque of red sandstone and white marble with five arched entrances; horseshoe arches and exquisite calligraphy; forerunner of Mughal style
Purana Qila (Shergarh) (Yamuna Bank, Delhi) Sher Shah Suri (1540–1545 CE) Fortified royal citadel with massive ramparts built after demolishing Humayun's Dinpanah; three grand gateways (Bara Darwaza, Humayun Gate, Talaqi Gate)
New Fort of Rohtasgarh (Jhelum, Punjab, Pakistan) Sher Shah Suri Supervisor: Todar Mal Massive strategic hill fort with a 4-km perimeter built to check rebellious Ghakkar tribes and potential Mughal counter-attacks on the North-West Frontier
Sher Mandal (Purana Qila, Delhi) Sher Shah Suri (1541 CE) Two-storeyed octagonal pavilion built of red sandstone; solar observatory and pleasure pavilion; later Humayun's library from whose stairs he fell to his death
