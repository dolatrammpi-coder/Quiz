Phases of the Movement
Major Phases of the Quit India Movement — 1942 to 1944
Gradual evolution of the mass revolution following the arrest of top leadership
Phase & Timeline Regions & Key Leaders Main Activities & Mass Resistance
First Phase (August 1942) [Urban & Spontaneous] View Answer Bombay, Calcutta, Delhi, Ahmedabad, Patna; Youth, students, workers, and ordinary citizens View Answer Top leadership arrested under Operation Zero Hour; massive strikes and demonstrations in cities, attacks on post-telegraph offices and railway stations; severe police firing
Second Phase (Mid-August 1942) [Rural & Militant Upsurge] View Answer Eastern Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Midnapore (Bengal), Odisha, Maharashtra; Peasants, students, and local leaders View Answer Communication networks completely disrupted; capture of government buildings, police stations, and treasuries; establishment of parallel governments in Ballia, Tamluk, and Satara
Third Phase (September 1942–1944) [Underground Revolutionary Resistance] View Answer Bombay, Nepal border, Gujarat, and Karnataka; JP, Aruna Asaf Ali, Lohia, Usha Mehta View Answer Guerrilla attacks by Azad Dasta; operation of secret Congress Radio; pamphlet distribution; Gandhiji's 21-day fast in 1943; release in 1944

Parallel Government & Location Key Leaders & Duration Major Works, Institutions & Features

Tamluk Jatiya Sarkar (Midnapore, Bengal) View Answer Satish Chandra Samanta, Sushil Dhara December 1942 to September 1944 (long-lasting) View Answer 'Vidyut Vahini' armed wing; Bhagini Sena; cyclone relief work; 73-year-old Matangini Hazra ('Gandhi Buri') martyred holding the tricolour

Historical Chronology
Chronology of Major Events of the INA and Azad Hind Government
From Netaji's Great Escape in 1941 to the Red Fort Trials of 1945
Year & Date Historical Event Key Details & Impact
16–17 January 1941 View Answer Netaji's Great Escape (Calcutta) View Answer Secret escape from his Elgin Road residence disguised as Ziauddin Pathan; travelled via Peshawar and Kabul to Moscow and Berlin
1 September 1942 View Answer Formation of the 1st Division of the INA View Answer First formal formation of the Azad Hind Fauj by Captain Mohan Singh in Malaya with 16,300 prisoners of war
2 July 1943 View Answer Netaji's Arrival in Singapore View Answer Reached Singapore from Tokyo by submarine; Rash Behari Bose formally handed over command of the INA and the League
21 October 1943 View Answer Establishment of the Provisional Government of Azad Hind View Answer Formed at Cathay Hall in Singapore; slogan 'Dilli Chalo'; diplomatic recognition by 9 Axis/allied nations

18 March 1944 View Answer Tricolour Hoisted at Moirang (Manipur) View Answer Under Colonel Shaukat Malik, the INA entered the Indian mainland and hoisted the national tricolour for the first time
18 August 1945 View Answer Netaji's Reported Plane Crash View Answer Announcement of Netaji's martyrdom in a plane crash at Taihoku (Taiwan); mystery around his death persists even today
November 1945 View Answer Commencement of Historic Red Fort Trials View Answer Treason trial against Sahgal, Dhillon, and Shah Nawaz; nationwide mass upsurge; sentences remitted by Wavell

Red Fort Trials — Principal Accused, Defence Counsel & Outcome
Details of the historic court-martial held at Delhi's Red Fort in November 1945
Trial Aspect / Accused Military Officers & Counsel Involved British Verdict & Historical Impact
Principal Accused (Three Military Officers) View Answer Col. Prem Kumar Sahgal (Hindu), Col. Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon (Sikh), Maj. Gen. Shah Nawaz Khan (Muslim) View Answer Charged with waging war against the British King (treason); the shared unity of the three officers sparked unprecedented communal harmony nationwide
Congress Defence Committee (Legal Defence Team) View Answer Bhulabhai Desai (Chief Counsel), Pandit Jawaharlal Nehru, Sir Tej Bahadur Sapru, Dr. Kailash Nath Katju, Asaf Ali View Answer Argued under international law: 'A subject nation has full right to wage war for its freedom'; funds raised nationwide for the defence fund
Court Verdict & Outcome (Wavell's Prerogative) View Answer The court-martial found all three guilty and pronounced the death sentence (hanging) View Answer Seeing the fierce nationwide mass agitation and discontent among Indian soldiers, Viceroy Lord Wavell completely remitted the death sentences using his special prerogative
