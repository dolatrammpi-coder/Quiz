Administrative Comparison
Comparative Features of the Administrative and Political Policies of Jahangir and Shah Jahan
Analysis of Mansabdari, Religious Outlook, Deccan Policy, and Justice System
Administrative / Policy Area Jahangir's Administrative Policy (1605–1627) Shah Jahan's Administrative Policy (1628–1658)
Mansabdari System Introduction of the 'Du-Aspa and Sih-Aspa' system; doubling or tripling a mansabdar's Sawar rank and allowances without increasing Zat rank Introduction of the 'Month-Scale' (Mahana ratio: 8-month, 6-month salary) system; made actual military maintenance practical
Religious Outlook Liberal tolerance; followed Akbar's Sulh-i-Kul; celebrated Raksha Bandhan and Shivratri, though ordered cow slaughter at Kangra Initial orthodoxy; abolished Sijda and Pabos and introduced 'Chahar Taslim'; banned construction of new Hindu temples
Deccan & Regional Policy Partial success due to Malik Ambar's guerrilla warfare; concluded the historic honourable treaty with Mewar in 1615 Aggressive integration of the Deccan; complete annexation of Ahmadnagar in 1636; made Bijapur and Golconda tributaries; Aurangzeb appointed Viceroy of the Deccan
Justice & Court Personal accessibility of justice via 'Zanjir-i-Adl'; impartiality in justice; preservation of Persian court traditions Strictness in justice; personally dispensed justice on Wednesdays; peak display of grandeur at the Peacock Throne and Shahjahanabad court
Art & Architectural Orientation Highest priority to painting among fine arts; zenith of natural beauty, bird studies, and portraiture Highest priority to grand architecture (white marble); construction of monumental mosques, palaces, and the Taj Mahal

Painter & Specialization Major Works & Subjects Title & Honour Conferred by Jahangir

Mughal Architecture
Major Architecture/Monuments of Shah Jahan's Reign, Location, Period, and Features
Golden Age of Mughal Architecture — White Marble, Symmetry, and Grandeur
Monument & Location Construction Period & Chief Architect Key Architectural & Artistic Features
Taj Mahal (Agra, banks of Yamuna) 1631–1648 CE (22 years) Chief Architect: Ustad Ahmad Lahori Built of pure Makrana white marble; massive bulbous double dome; four corner minarets; intricate pietra dura inlay; UNESCO World Heritage Site
Red Fort (Lal Qila) (Delhi, banks of Yamuna) 1638–1648 CE (10 years) Patron: Shah Jahan; Architects: Hamid & Ahmad Octagonal fort built of red sandstone; Diwan-i-Am, Diwan-i-Khas, Rang Mahal; Lahori and Delhi Gates; symbol of India's sovereignty
Jama Masjid (Delhi, opposite Red Fort) 1650–1656 CE (6 years) Patron: Shah Jahan Largest mosque in India at the time; 3 massive marble domes on a high plinth, 2 tall minarets, and grand red sandstone staircases
Peacock Throne (Takht-i-Taus, Delhi) 1628–1635 CE (7 years) Artisan: Bebadal Khan Installed in Diwan-i-Khas; throne inlaid with pure gold, diamonds, and gems; two dancing peacocks on canopy; set with the world-famous Koh-i-Noor diamond
Moti Masjid (Agra Fort) 1648–1654 CE (6 years) Patron: Shah Jahan Serene, sacred, and elegant mosque built entirely of milky white marble inside Agra Fort; 3 arched domes
City of Shahjahanabad (7th City of Delhi) 1638–1648 CE Patron: Shah Jahan Planned walled city built to shift the capital from Agra to Delhi; Chandni Chowk market (designed by Jahanara Begum)

Wars of Succession
Major Battles and Sequence of Events in Shah Jahan's War of Succession
Fierce Civil War Fought Among Mughal Princes from 1657 to 1659 CE
Battle & Date Opposing Sides & Leadership Strategic Outcome & Historical Impact
Battle of Bahadurpur (14 February 1658) Imperial Army (Sulaiman Shikoh & Jai Singh) vs. Shah Shuja (Subedar of Bengal) Resounding victory for Sulaiman Shikoh; Shuja defeated and fled back to Bengal; Dara's eastern front secured
Battle of Dharmat (15 April 1658) Imperial Army (Jaswant Singh & Qasim Khan) vs. Combined Army of Aurangzeb & Murad Bakhsh Jaswant Singh defeated due to Qasim Khan's betrayal; Jodhpur's Hadi Rani closed the fort gates; Aurangzeb marched toward Agra
Battle of Samugarh (29 May 1658) Dara Shikoh (Imperial Army) vs. Aurangzeb & Murad Bakhsh Decisive battle; Dara dismounted his elephant and fled; complete victory for Aurangzeb; Agra Fort captured and Shah Jahan imprisoned
Battle of Khajwa (5 January 1659) Aurangzeb's Army vs. Shah Shuja (near Allahabad) Aurangzeb victorious despite Jaswant Singh plundering his camp; Shuja fled to Arakan (Myanmar) where he was killed
Battle of Deorai Valley (12–14 April 1659) Dara Shikoh vs. Aurangzeb (near Ajmer) Final battle of succession; Dara decisively defeated; betrayed by Malik Jiwan; Dara executed in Delhi
