Rulers & Titles
Prominent Satavahana Rulers and Their Important Titles/Epithets
Chronology of Rulers, Titles, and Major Achievements
Ruler and Chronology Title & Metronymic Major Historical Achievements
Simuka (Founder) Sindhuka / Shishuka Established Satavahana power and ended the Kanva dynasty.
Satakarni I Dakshinapathapati, Apratihatachakra Performed two Ashvamedha sacrifices; mentioned in the Nanaghat inscription.
King Hala (17th Ruler) Kavipriya / Litterateur Composed the Prakrit text 'Gathasaptashati' and patronized scholars.
Gautamiputra Satakarni (23rd Ruler) Trisamudratoyapitavahana, Ekabrahmana Destroyed the Saka ruler Nahapana of the Kshaharata dynasty; described in the Nashik Prashasti.
Vasishthiputra Pulumavi (24th Ruler) Dakshinapatheshvara, Navanagaraswami Made Dhanyakataka his capital and expanded the Amaravati Stupa.
Yajna Sri Satakarni (27th Ruler) Samudrapriya / Maritime King Revived Roman trade; depicted a ship motif on his coins.

Historical Evidence
Important Satavahana-Era Inscriptions and Historical Evidence
Inscriptions, Issuing Rulers, and Key Subjects of Evidence
Inscription & Site Ruler / Issuer Main Subject & Historical Evidence
Nanaghat Inscription (Pune) Naganika (Wife of Satakarni I) Earliest epigraphic evidence of land grants; describes Satakarni I's Vedic sacrifices.
Nashik Cave Inscription I Kanha (Krishna) Earliest cave inscription evidence of the Satavahana period.
Nashik Prashasti (Cave III) Gautami Balashri (Mother of Satakarni) Describes Gautamiputra's conquests and the annihilation of Kshaharata Nahapana.
Karle Cave Inscription Gautamiputra & Pulumavi Evidence of granting tax-free land/villages to Buddhist monks.
Myakadoni Inscription (Bellary) Pulumavi IV Feudal administration and rural governance under the Mahasenapati.
Junagarh Inscription (Girnar) Saka Ruler Rudradaman I Mentions Satavahana-Saka conflicts and matrimonial alliances.

Economic Life & Trade
Satavahana Economy, Trade Routes, and Foreign Commerce
Major Ports, Trade Commodities, and Foreign Accounts
Port / Trade Centre Major Commodities & Industries Roman Trade & Historical Evidence
Bharuch (Bhrigukachchha / Barygaza) Cotton textiles, muslin, gems, medicinal herbs Foremost international port on the western coast.
Sopara and Kalyan Silk, spices, iron implements, agate (onyx) Major ports on the Konkan coast; arrival of Roman ships.
Arikamedu and Ghantasala Beads, pottery (Arretine ware), wine amphorae Famous Roman trading centres on the eastern (Coromandel/Andhra) coast.
Pratishthana and Tagara Cotton cloth, muslin, oil, leather goods Inland market towns from which goods were transported to Bharuch.
Periplus of the Erythraean Sea Italian wine, copper, lead, gold coins Describes the inflow of Roman gold into India and Satavahana trade.
Pliny's 'Naturalis Historia' Drain of gold from the Roman Empire Lamented India's annual drain of 50 million sesterces worth of Roman gold.
