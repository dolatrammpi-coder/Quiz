Administrative Structure
Vijayanagara Administration — Nayankara and Ayagar Systems & Local Governance
Details of central military feudalism and autonomous rural self-governance
Administrative System / Institution Nature & Functioning Administrative & Social Impact
Nayankara System (Military Feudalism) Grant of 'Amaram' land to military commanders (Nayakas) for salary and troop maintenance; maintaining a fixed contingent mandatory Backbone of the empire's vast military strength; later independence of Nayakas led to the empire's decline
Ayagar System (Rural Self-Governance) Council of 12 hereditary officials in every village; tax-free 'Manyam' land allotted as remuneration Complete autonomy at the village level; land sale and justice impossible without the consent of Ayagars
Provincial Structure (Mandalam to Grama) Mandalam (Province) -> Nadu/Kottam (District) -> Sthala/Sima (Sub-district) -> Grama (Basic unit) Harmonious coordination between centralized royal control and traditional local institutions
Athavane Department (Revenue Administration) Assessment of land revenue (Shishta); tax at 1/6th of produce; scientific classification of land into 4 categories Strong financial condition of the treasury; heavy financial allocation for irrigation works and dam construction

Battle of Talikota (1565 CE) — Belligerents, Events & Historical Impact
Analytical overview of the Battle of Rakshasa-Tangadi
Phase / Dimension of Battle Details of the Battle of Talikota (1565) Historical Impact & Consequences
Belligerents & Leadership Vijayanagara (Rama Raya, Tirumala, Venkatadri) vs. Joint Confederacy (Bijapur, Ahmadnagar, Golconda, Bidar) Berar remained neutral; confederacy forged through matrimonial alliance between Adil Shah and Husain Nizam Shah
Strategic Events Battle on the banks of the Krishna on 23 January 1565; fierce use of artillery; defection of the Gilani commanders Rama Raya captured and executed; stampede in the Vijayanagara army; over 100,000 soldiers killed
Destruction of Hampi Victorious armies continuously looted, burned, and desecrated idols in Hampi for 5 months The magnificent political and cultural metropolis of South India turned into ruins forever
Long-Term Impact Brief rule by Aravidu dynasty from Penukonda and Chandragiri; Mysore, Tanjore, and Madurai became independent End of Hindu imperial sovereignty in South India; paved the way for the expansion of Deccan Sultanates
