Historic March
Dandi March — Starting Point, Route, Dates, and Major Halts
Account of the Historic Salt Satyagraha March from 12 March to 6 April 1930
Date & Stage Place & Distance Covered Key Events & Historical Significance
12 March 1930 (Day of Departure) Sabarmati Ashram (Ahmedabad) Starting Point March began at 6:30 AM with 78 followers; chanting of 'Raghupati Raghav Raja Ram'; huge crowd gathered for farewell
13–20 March 1930 (Initial Halts) Aslali, Nadiad & Anand Kheda District Grand welcome by villagers; hundreds of government patwaris and village headmen resigned en masse; widespread promotion of Khadi
21–29 March 1930 (Mid-route Halts) Ras, Jambusar & Bharuch Crossing the Narmada River Crossed the Mahisagar River by boat; Pandit Jawaharlal Nehru met Gandhiji at Jambusar; unprecedented participation of women
1–4 April 1930 (Surat Region) Surat & Navsari South Gujarat Pledge to boycott foreign cloth and liquor; live global coverage of the march by American journalist Webb Miller
5 April 1930 (Arrival at Dandi) Dandi Coast Navsari District Reached Dandi after completing the 240-mile (385 km) march; special address on self-purification and non-violence at the evening prayer meeting
6 April 1930 (Breaking the Salt Law) Dandi Coast 6:30 AM Gandhiji broke British law by picking up a handful of natural salt from the shore; formal nationwide launch of the Civil Disobedience Movement

Major Regional Leaders and Activities of the Civil Disobedience Movement
Salt and No-Tax Struggles from Tamil Nadu to the North-West Frontier and Assam
Region / Province Prominent Leaders Key Activities & Nature of Mass Resistance
Tamil Nadu (Tanjore Coast) C. Rajagopalachari ('Rajaji') 150-mile Salt March from Trichinopoly to Vedaranyam (April 1930); broke the Salt Law on the Vedaranyam coast
Kerala (Malabar Coast) K. Kelappan ('Kerala Gandhi') & K. P. Kesava Menon Historic march from Calicut to Payyanur; civil disobedience by making coastal salt; clarion call for temple entry
North-West Frontier (NWFP - Peshawar) Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan ('Frontier Gandhi') 'Khudai Khidmatgar' (Red Shirt Movement); non-violent Pashtun Satyagraha; popular control over Peshawar
Odisha (Cuttack & Balasore) Gopabandhu Choudhury & Rama Devi Breaking of Salt Law at Inchudi (Balasore) coast; boycott of foreign cloth in Cuttack and Puri
Bihar (Saran, Champaran, Patna) Dr. Rajendra Prasad & Ambika Kant Sinha Making saltpetre and salt from saline water; Anti-Chowkidari Tax movement; Satyagraha at Nakhas Pond (Patna)
Assam & Nagaland (Northeast India) Rani Gaidinliu (13-year-old Naga Heroine) 'Heraka Movement'; boycott of British taxes; fierce student protest in Assam against the Cunningham Circular
Central Provinces & Maharashtra (Forest Regions) M. S. Aney & Local Gond Tribals 'Forest Satyagraha'; grazing cattle and cutting timber in defiance of reserved forest laws
United Provinces (UP) (Allahabad & Rae Bareli) Jawaharlal Nehru & Purushottam Das Tandon 'No-Rent and No-Tax' campaign by peasants; call to withhold rent payments to zamindars

Round Table Conferences
Three Round Table Conferences — Years, Venue, Indian Representation, and Outcomes
Account of Constitutional Conferences Held at St. James's Palace, London (1930–1932)
Conference & Period British PM & Indian Representation Key Deliberations & Outcomes
First Round Table Conference (12 Nov 1930 – 19 Jan 1931) Venue: London PM: Ramsay MacDonald; Total 89 delegates; Congress absent; Ambedkar (Depressed Classes), Sapru (Liberals), Aga Khan & Jinnah (League) Deliberations on All-India Federation and provincial autonomy; fruitless without Congress; Gandhi released via Sapru-Jayakar mediation
Second Round Table Conference (7 Sep 1931 – 1 Dec 1931) Venue: London PM: Ramsay MacDonald; Sole Congress representative: Mahatma Gandhi (via SS Rajputana); Sarojini Naidu, Madan Mohan Malaviya, Ambedkar Communal deadlock; Ambedkar demanded separate electorates for Dalits; opposed by Gandhiji; conference ended in complete failure; Civil Disobedience resumed upon return
Third Round Table Conference (17 Nov 1932 – 24 Dec 1932) Venue: London PM: Ramsay MacDonald; Only 46 delegates; Congress and British Labour Party absent; Ambedkar and Sapru attended Finalized outline of future constitution; White Paper published in 1933; formed the basis of the Government of India Act, 1935

Comparative Overview of Major Political and Constitutional Agreements (1931–1932)
Agreement / Event & Year Key Parties & Leaders Involved Major Provisions & Historical Outcome
Gandhi-Irwin Pact (5 March 1931, Delhi) Mahatma Gandhi (Congress) vs. Lord Irwin (Viceroy) Civil Disobedience suspended; release of non-violent prisoners only; permission to make coastal salt; demand for police inquiry rejected; Congress participation in Second RTC agreed
Karachi Congress Session (29 March 1931, Karachi) President: Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel; Gandhiji, Nehru, Subhas Endorsed Gandhi-Irwin Pact; passed resolution on 'Fundamental Rights and National Economic Programme' drafted by Nehru; anguish over Bhagat Singh's execution

Poona Pact (24 September 1932, Pune) Dr. B. R. Ambedkar & Pandit Madan Mohan Malaviya (for Gandhiji) Separate electorates for Dalits abandoned; reserved seats in provincial legislatures increased from 71 to 147 under joint electorates; 18% reservation in Central Assembly
Final Withdrawal of Movement (April 1934) Mahatma Gandhi & Congress Working Committee Shifted from mass to individual satyagraha in May 1933; completely withdrawn on 7 April 1934; Congress pivoted toward elections under the 1935 Act
