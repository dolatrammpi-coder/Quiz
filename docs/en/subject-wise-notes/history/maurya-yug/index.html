Mahavamsa and Dipavamsa Sinhalese Buddhist Chronicles Ashoka's conversion to Buddhism and propagation of Buddhism in Sri Lanka
Junagarh Inscription Rudradaman (Rock Inscription) Construction of Sudarshana Lake and provincial governors of Chandragupta and Ashoka

Epigraphic Evidence
Ashoka's 14 Major Rock Edicts and Their Core Themes
Epigraphic Classification of the Political and Moral Messages of the Mauryan Empire
Rock Edict Main Theme / Message Location and Historical Significance
1st Rock Edict Prohibition of animal sacrifice and festive gatherings Girnar, Dhauli, etc.; compassion toward all living beings
2nd Rock Edict Medical care for humans and animals; mention of Cholas and Pandyas Shahbazgarhi, etc.; concept of a welfare state
3rd Rock Edict Appointment of Yuktas, Rajjukas, and Pradeshikas 12th regnal year; quinquennial (5-year) inspection tours
4th Rock Edict Proclamation of Dhammaghosha in place of Bherighosha Propagation of moral values and non-violence
5th Rock Edict First mention of the appointment of Dhamma Mahamatras 13th regnal year; social welfare
6th Rock Edict Self-control and administrative promptness Emperor always accessible for public welfare
7th & 8th Rock Edicts Tolerance among all sects and Dhamma Yatras Mention of pilgrimage to Bodh Gaya
9th Rock Edict Explanation of true gifts and true ceremonial conduct Condemnation of meaningless rituals
10th Rock Edict Condemnation of fame and glory; supremacy of Dhamma Emphasis on the moral upliftment of subjects
11th Rock Edict Explanation of Dhammadana and its superiority Material gifts considered secondary to Dhamma
12th Rock Edict Religious tolerance and appointment of Ithijhakha Mahamatras Emphasis on growth of the essence of all religions
13th Rock Edict Account of the Kalinga War and 5 Greek kings Longest rock edict; Ashoka's change of heart
14th Rock Edict Exhortation to subjects to lead a righteous life Purpose behind engraving the inscriptions

Principle / Objective of Dhamma Key Provisions and Practical Teachings Associated Epigraphic Evidence
Non-Violence and Compassion Ban on animal sacrifice, renunciation of violence, restriction on killing birds/animals in the royal kitchen 1st and 5th Rock Edicts, Delhi-Topra Pillar Edict

Historical Chronology
Chronological Table of Major Events from Chandragupta Maurya to Ashoka
Timeline of the Rise, Expansion, and Transformative Events of the Mauryan Empire
Year / Approximate Period Historical Event Historical Context and Impact
322 BCE Foundation of the Mauryan dynasty by Chandragupta Maurya Slaying of Dhana Nanda; first pan-Indian empire with Chanakya's aid
305 BCE Chandragupta–Seleucus war and treaty Seleucus defeated; 4 provinces merged into the Mauryan Empire, matrimonial alliance
298 BCE Death of Chandragupta and accession of Bindusara Renounced life via Sallekhana at Shravanabelagola; Bindusara became king
273 BCE Death of Bindusara and war of succession Ashoka gained control of power; 4-year interregnum
269 BCE Formal coronation of Emperor Ashoka Ascended the throne of Magadha; assumed the title Devanampiya
261 BCE Kalinga War (8th regnal year) Massive bloodshed; Ashoka renounced war and proclaimed Dhammaghosha
256 BCE Appointment of Dhamma Mahamatras (13th regnal year) Creation of a new administrative post for Dhamma propagation and social welfare
250 BCE Convening of the Third Buddhist Council Held at Pataliputra; compilation of Abhidhamma Pitaka and foreign missions
232 BCE Death of Emperor Ashoka End of a 37-year reign; division of the empire between Kunala and Dasharatha
185 BCE Assassination of the last ruler Brihadratha Killed by Pushyamitra Shunga; foundation of Shunga dynasty and end of Mauryan Empire
