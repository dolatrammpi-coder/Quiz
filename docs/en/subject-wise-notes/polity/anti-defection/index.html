Defection: Constitutional Problem & Background

Disqualification on Grounds of Defection

How many primary grounds of disqualification exist under the Tenth Schedule?
Two primary grounds — voluntary giving up of membership and violation of whip.
Tap to view answer
What does 'voluntary giving up of membership' mean?
A member voluntarily leaving their political party.
Tap to view answer
Does disqualification apply only when one is a member of a party?
Yes, to a member of a registered political party.
Tap to view answer
Is leaving a party without a formal resignation letter a ground for disqualification?
Yes, it can also be inferred from conduct.
Tap to view answer
Who decides the question of disqualification?
The Speaker/Chairman of the concerned House.
Tap to view answer
Is the decision on disqualification subject to judicial review?
Yes, on limited grounds.
Tap to view answer
Are disqualification proceedings initiated suo motu automatically?
No, upon a petition/complaint.
Tap to view answer
Whose decision is final on disqualification?
That of the Speaker/Chairman.
Tap to view answer
Is the decision on disqualification political in nature?
It is a quasi-judicial decision.
Tap to view answer
What happens to a member after disqualification?
Their membership of the House is terminated.
Tap to view answer
Can a disqualified member contest elections again?
Yes, there is no legal bar.
Tap to view answer
Can a disqualified member continue as a Minister?
No, not without retaining membership.

Party Whip and Voting-Related Disqualification

What does 'Party Whip' mean?
A voting directive issued by a party to its members.
Tap to view answer
What happens upon defiance of a whip?
The member may be declared disqualified.
Tap to view answer
Is a whip limited only to voting?
No, it also applies to abstention from voting.
Tap to view answer
Does disqualification occur only upon changing parties?
No, also upon violating a whip.
Tap to view answer
Is violation of a whip recognised only inside the House?
Yes, in the proceedings of the House.
Tap to view answer
Does voting against a whip necessarily lead to disqualification?
Yes, if the party files a complaint and does not condone it.
Tap to view answer
Is absence also a violation of a whip?
Yes, if the party has directed members to remain present.
Tap to view answer
Does the violation of a whip need to be proved?
Yes, before the Speaker.
Tap to view answer
Does a whip exist only for the ruling party?
No, for all registered political parties.

Is a member issued a warning upon violating a whip?
The procedure depends on the Speaker.

Rules for Independent & Nominated Members

Who is an Independent Member?
One who does not contest an election as a candidate of any political party.
Tap to view answer
Can an Independent Member join a political party after election?
No, joining a party leads to disqualification.
Tap to view answer
Which paragraph applies to an Independent Member?
Paragraph 2(1)(b) (Paragraph 2(2)).
Tap to view answer
Who is a Nominated Member?
A member nominated by the President or Governor.
Tap to view answer
Is a Nominated Member allowed to join a party?
Up to 6 months from taking their seat.
Tap to view answer
What happens if a Nominated Member joins a party after 6 months?
Declared disqualified.
Tap to view answer
Is a Nominated Member affiliated with any party at the time of appointment?
Not affiliated with any party at the time of appointment.
Tap to view answer
Does a whip apply to a Nominated Member?
Yes, if they join a party within 6 months.
Tap to view answer
Does a whip apply to an Independent Member?
No.
Tap to view answer
Can a Nominated Member become a Minister?
Yes, provided their membership continues.

Provisions Related to Merger & Split

What is the meaning of Merger?
The merging of one political party with another political party.
Tap to view answer
What is the condition for a valid merger under the Anti-Defection Law?
Agreement of at least 2/3 of the legislature party members.
Tap to view answer
Is a merger by 1/3 of members valid?
No.
Tap to view answer
Are members disqualified after a valid merger?
No.
Tap to view answer
What other exception exists besides a merger?
No exemption for any other reason (except Presiding Officer exemption).
Tap to view answer
What is the meaning of a Split?
Division of a political party into two factions.
Tap to view answer
Is a split exempted under the Anti-Defection Law?
No, the exemption was removed after the 91st Amendment.
Tap to view answer
Which Amendment removed the exemption for a split?
The 91st Constitutional Amendment (2003).
Tap to view answer
Is a split recognised after the 91st Amendment?
No.
Tap to view answer
Is a member considered a defector in case of a merger?
No, if 2/3 of the members agree to the merger.
Tap to view answer
How is a merger proved?
Through a written resolution of the party.
Tap to view answer
Is prior permission of the Speaker required for a merger?
No.
