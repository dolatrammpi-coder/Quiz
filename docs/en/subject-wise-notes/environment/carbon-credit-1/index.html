Carbon Credit: Meaning, Market, and CDM

A carbon credit is a measurable instrument linked to climate change mitigation and reducing greenhouse gas emissions. Generally, one credit represents the reduction or removal of one metric ton of carbon dioxide equivalent (CO₂e). For exams, it is important to distinguish clearly between carbon credits, carbon taxes, emissions trading, the Kyoto Protocol, and the Clean Development Mechanism (CDM).

Carbon Credits and Carbon Markets

In carbon markets, credits or units representing emission reductions or removals can be traded. In compliance markets, units are used to meet targets under a regulatory framework; in voluntary markets, organizations or individuals can purchase credits to meet voluntary climate goals. The quality of a credit depends on its baseline, measurement, additionality, and verification.

  • One carbon credit is generally equal to 1 ton of CO₂e.
  • A carbon tax imposes a price or tax on emissions; a credit market relies on trading units.
  • Reliable monitoring and verification are essential for genuine emission reductions.

Kyoto Protocol and CDM

The Kyoto Protocol established emission reduction commitments and flexible mechanisms for certain countries. Under the Clean Development Mechanism (CDM), eligible projects in developing countries could earn certified emission reduction units by reducing or removing emissions. Joint Implementation (JI) and International Emissions Trading were also mechanisms under the Kyoto framework; do not use these terms interchangeably.

  • CDM: Emission reduction through projects in a developing country.
  • JI: Project-based cooperation between eligible countries under the Kyoto framework.
  • Emissions trading: Buying and selling of assigned or permitted emission units.

Criteria for a Credible Credit

A credit claim is credible only when the reduction is real, measurable, verified, and additional to what would have occurred in a business-as-usual scenario without the project. Permanence, prevention of double counting, and the project's social and environmental impacts must also be evaluated. Simply buying credits is no substitute for cutting an organization's own emissions; reducing direct emissions first and considering credible credits for residual emissions is the better sequence.

  • Additionality: The reduction would not have occurred automatically under normal conditions.
  • MRV: Measurement, Reporting and Verification.
  • Double counting and temporary or unverified reductions undermine credit credibility.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much emission is one carbon credit equal to?

By standard definition, one metric ton of carbon dioxide equivalent (1 tCO₂e).

What is the main difference between a carbon tax and a carbon credit market?

A carbon tax sets a direct price or tax on emissions, whereas a credit market involves trading units linked to emission reductions or allowances.

What is the full form of CDM?

Clean Development Mechanism; it was a project-based mechanism under the Kyoto Protocol.
