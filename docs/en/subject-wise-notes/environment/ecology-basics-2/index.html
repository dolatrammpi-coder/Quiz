Principles of Ecology and Key Concepts

Ecological principles help us understand organisms' habitats, ecological roles, tolerance limits, adaptations, competition, and the impact of limiting resources. In exams, it is useful to clearly distinguish and illustrate terms such as habitat, niche, limiting factor, ecological tolerance, adaptation, and succession with examples.

Habitat and Ecological Niche

A habitat is the physical place or environment where an organism lives, whereas an ecological niche is the broader description of its role in that environment, including resource use, activity patterns, and interactions with other organisms. Species living in the same habitat can occupy different niches. This distinction is crucial in questions involving habitat destruction, competition, and species coexistence.

  • Habitat = Where an organism lives.
  • Niche = How an organism lives and what role it plays in the ecosystem.
  • A niche also encompasses resource utilization and interactions with other species.

Tolerance, Adaptation, and Limiting Factors

Every species survives best within a specific range of temperature, moisture, salinity, light, or other environmental factors. Conditions outside this tolerance range can inhibit growth and reproduction. Adaptations may be structural, physiological, or behavioral. A limiting factor is any factor whose deficiency or excess restricts a biological process or population growth.

  • Adaptations can enhance survival and reproductive success.
  • A limiting factor can be a resource shortage or an unfavorable environmental condition.
  • Tolerance ranges vary across different species.

Ecological Succession and Balance

In ecological succession, community structure changes over time. Primary succession can begin on a site lacking developed soil or an existing community, whereas secondary succession can occur where soil and some organic remnants persist after a disturbance. Ecological balance does not mean a static state, but rather a dynamic equilibrium shaped by ongoing interactions and change.

  • Primary succession: Begins on a virtually lifeless or soil-less surface.
  • Secondary succession: Soil or some biological base remains after a disturbance.
  • Fire, floods, climate change, and human land use can alter ecological communities.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a habitat and a niche?

A habitat is the physical dwelling place of an organism, while a niche is the comprehensive description of its resource use, environmental requirements, and ecological role.

What is a limiting factor?

An environmental factor whose scarcity or excess restricts the growth, reproduction, or distribution of an organism.

What is the main difference between primary and secondary succession?

Primary succession begins on a new or nearly soil-less site, whereas secondary succession starts where soil or some biological base remains after a disturbance.
