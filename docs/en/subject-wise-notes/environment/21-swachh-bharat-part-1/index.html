Swachh Bharat Mission 2014: ODF, Twin-Pit Toilets & Objectives of SBM

The first phase of the Swachh Bharat Mission focused on addressing open defecation, expanding access to toilets, and driving sanitation behaviour change. In this part, study the 2 October 2014 launch, predecessor rural sanitation programmes, SBM-G/SBM-U, the ODF concept, twin-pit technology, Swachhagrahis, and sanitation surveys.

Key Topics Covered in This Chapter

  • Background of Swachh Bharat Mission: Programme evolution from CRSP, Total Sanitation Campaign, and Nirmal Bharat Abhiyan to SBM.
  • ODF and Twin-Pit Toilets: The ODF concept, working of twin pits, safe faecal sludge management, and compost utilization.
  • Behaviour Change and Institutional Role: IEC/BCC, Swachhagrahis, the 'Darwaza Band' campaign, and village-level participation.
  • Assessing Sanitation Impact: Context of assessments like NARSS and WHO/UNICEF, and care in interpreting statistical achievements.

Frequently Asked Questions by Students

When was the Swachh Bharat Mission launched? The Swachh Bharat Mission was launched on 2 October 2014. It is useful to study its rural and urban components alongside their distinct administrative frameworks and targets.

What is a twin-pit toilet? A twin-pit toilet uses two pits alternately. Once one pit fills up, it is sealed while the second pit is used; proper design and safe operation are essential.
