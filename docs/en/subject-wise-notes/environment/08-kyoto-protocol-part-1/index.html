Kyoto Protocol 1997: Objectives, Commitment Periods & India's Stand

The Kyoto Protocol is an international agreement developed under the UNFCCC to combat climate change. In this part, revise its adoption at COP-3, entry into force, Annex I/II and Non-Annex I Parties, targeted greenhouse gases, the first and second commitment periods, and the CBDR-RC principle.

Key Topics Covered in This Chapter

  • Historical Background & UNFCCC: Adoption of the Kyoto Protocol, entry into force, and connection to climate negotiations.
  • Annexes & Greenhouse Gases: Gases listed in Annex A and classification of Annex I, Annex II, and Non-Annex I Parties.
  • Commitment Periods: Reference to the first period (2008–2012) and the second period / Doha Amendment (2013–2020).
  • CBDR-RC & India's Stand: Principle of Common But Differentiated Responsibilities and Respective Capabilities and the roles of different countries.

Frequently Asked Questions by Students

When was the Kyoto Protocol adopted and when did it enter into force? It was adopted on 11 December 1997 at COP-3 and entered into force on 16 February 2005.

What does the CBDR-RC principle mean? It means that while everyone shares the responsibility to tackle climate change, responsibilities differ according to historical contributions and respective capabilities.
