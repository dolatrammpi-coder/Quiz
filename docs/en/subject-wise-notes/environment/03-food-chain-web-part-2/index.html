Food Web and Biomagnification Notes: DDT, Mercury and Minamata

This part clarifies the difference between a food web, keystone species, bioaccumulation, and biomagnification. Connect the concepts with examples such as DDT, mercury, Minamata disease, and the increasing concentration of pollutants along the food chain.

What to Study in This Chapter?

  • Food Web and Keystone Species: Interconnection of multiple food chains in an ecosystem and role of key species.
  • Bioaccumulation vs. Biomagnification: Accumulation of pollutants in an organism over time and increase in concentration at higher trophic levels.
  • DDT, Mercury and Environmental Examples: Context of examples related to Minamata, endosulfan, vultures, and dolphins.
  • International Conventions and Bioremediation: Stockholm and Minamata Conventions, radioactive magnification, and basic concepts of bioremediation.

Important Points for Competitive Exams

Pay special attention to the definitions and differences between bioaccumulation and biomagnification. Understand the examples of DDT and mercury in relation to trophic levels; also revise the objectives of conventions and pollutant-related facts.

Test yourself: What is the difference between bioaccumulation and biomagnification? Which pollutant is associated with Minamata disease? What is a food web?
