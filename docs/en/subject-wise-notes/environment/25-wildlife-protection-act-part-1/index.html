Wildlife Protection Act 1972: Sections 9/11, Protected Areas & NBWL

The Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 is India's primary legal framework for conserving wild animals and specified plants. In this part, study the constitutional basis, prohibition of hunting and exceptions, protected areas including National Parks and Sanctuaries, and the roles of the National Board for Wildlife (NBWL), CZA, NTCA, and WCCB.

Key Topics Covered in This Chapter

  • The Act & Constitutional Basis: The 1972 legislation, constitutional provisions after the 42nd Amendment, and fundamental duties of citizens.
  • Sections 9 & 11: Understanding the prohibition of hunting alongside the specific statutory exceptions and permissions.
  • Categories of Protected Areas: Differences among National Parks, Wildlife Sanctuaries, Conservation Reserves, and Community Reserves.
  • Institutions & Conservation Strategies: Roles of NBWL/SBWL, Central Zoo Authority, NTCA, and the Wildlife Crime Control Bureau.

Frequently Asked Questions by Students

What is Section 9 of the Wildlife Protection Act 1972 related to? Section 9 generally deals with the prohibition of hunting of specified wild animals; for exceptions and permissions, the corresponding sections of the Act (such as Section 11) should also be studied.

What is the difference between a National Park and a Wildlife Sanctuary? Both are protected areas, but there are legal differences in permitted activities, settlement of rights, and management regimes. Compare their statutory provisions carefully for exams.
