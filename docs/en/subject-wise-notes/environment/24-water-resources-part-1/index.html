India's Water Budget, Falkenmark Index, Groundwater, and Water Bodies Census

Studying water resources requires understanding the distinction between global water distribution, total available versus utilizable water in India, per capita availability, and groundwater status. This part covers questions on the Falkenmark water-stress indicator, CGWB/CGWA, aquifers, the Water Bodies Census, virtual water, water footprint, and the hydrological cycle.

Key Topics Covered in This Chapter

  • Global Water Distribution and India's Water Budget: Ratio of saline to freshwater, and the difference between total availability and utilizable resources.
  • Falkenmark Water Stress Index: Assessing water stress from per capita annual water availability and limitations of the index.
  • Groundwater and Institutional Monitoring: Aquifers, CGWB, CGWA, and groundwater extraction categories.
  • Water Bodies, Virtual Water, and Hydrological Cycle: Water Bodies Census, water footprint, hydrological cycle, e-flow, and Glacial Lake Outburst Flood (GLOF).

Frequently Asked Student Questions

What does the Falkenmark Index indicate? It is an indicator used to assess water stress or scarcity based on per capita annual water availability; by itself, it does not capture the complete picture of local distribution, quality, or seasonal availability.

What are groundwater and aquifers? Groundwater is water present in saturated soil or rock layers beneath the ground; an aquifer is a geological formation capable of storing and transmitting significant quantities of water.
