Paris Agreement 2015: 1.5°C Goal, NDCs, and Global Stocktake

The Paris Agreement is the primary framework for global action on climate change under the UNFCCC. In this part, study its adoption at COP21, date of entry into force, temperature targets, Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs), the five-year progress cycle, the Global Stocktake, and the role of key Articles.

Key Topics Covered in This Chapter

  • Adoption and Temperature Goal: COP21, 2015; entry into force and targets related to keeping warming well below 2°C and pursuing 1.5°C.
  • NDCs and Five-Year Cycle: Nationally Determined Contributions, countries' self-set targets, and the mechanism for raising ambition.
  • Global Stocktake and Transparency: Assessment of global progress, the Enhanced Transparency Framework, and review of climate pledges.
  • Comparison of Kyoto and Paris: Differences in target-setting, participation, national contributions, and compliance/transparency frameworks.

Frequently Asked Questions by Students

What is the 1.5°C goal of the Paris Agreement? The Agreement sets a goal to hold global average temperature rise well below 2°C above pre-industrial levels and to pursue efforts to limit it to 1.5°C.

What is an NDC? A Nationally Determined Contribution, through which a country submits its own climate action targets and measures.
