What is the Greenhouse Effect? Radiation, GWP and Climate Feedback Notes

Energy exchange between the Earth's surface and the atmosphere plays a crucial role in the greenhouse effect. In these Environment Notes, understand short-wave solar radiation, long-wave infrared radiation, CO₂ forcing, Global Warming Potential (GWP) of methane and N₂O, water vapour feedback, the dual role of ozone, and the effects of aerosols.

Main Topics Covered in This Chapter

  • Process of Greenhouse Effect: Solar radiation, outgoing infrared radiation from Earth, and heat absorption by greenhouse gases.
  • GWP and Major Gases: Concept of climate impact potential of CO₂, methane, N₂O, and fluorinated gases.
  • Forcing, Feedback and Albedo: Water vapour feedback, surface reflectivity (albedo), aerosols, and radiative balance.
  • Greenhouse Effect vs. Global Warming: Difference between natural greenhouse effect, anthropogenic warming, and scientific monitoring.

Frequently Asked Questions by Students

What is the greenhouse effect? Certain atmospheric gases absorb part of the outgoing infrared radiation from the Earth, keeping the surface and lower atmosphere warm.

What is the difference between the greenhouse effect and global warming? The greenhouse effect is a natural process; the rise in average global temperature due to increased greenhouse gases from human activities is called global warming.
