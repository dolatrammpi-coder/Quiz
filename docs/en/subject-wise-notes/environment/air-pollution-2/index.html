Effects and Control of Air Pollution

The effects of air pollution, its difference from the greenhouse effect, ozone layer depletion, and pollution control measures are frequently asked together. Keep these concepts distinct: not every air pollutant is a greenhouse gas, and ground-level ozone and stratospheric ozone play very different roles.

Effects on Human Health

  • PM2.5 and other particulates can affect respiratory and cardiovascular health. Risk depends on pollutant concentration, duration of exposure, age, and pre-existing health conditions.
  • Carbon monoxide impairs the oxygen-carrying capacity of blood. Sulphur dioxide and nitrogen dioxide can worsen respiratory irritation; children, the elderly, and sensitive individuals face heightened risk.

Effects on Environment, Visibility, and Climate

  • Particulate matter and certain gaseous pollutants can reduce visibility. Sulphur and nitrogen compounds, after atmospheric reactions, contribute to acid deposition, affecting water, soil, and vegetation.
  • Carbon dioxide and methane are greenhouse gases contributing to long-term climate change. Air quality and climate change are interlinked, though their measurement methods and key indicators are not identical.

Ground-Level Ozone vs. Ozone Layer Depletion

  • Ozone near the ground is a secondary air pollutant formed from NOx and VOCs in sunlight; it can harm respiratory health and plants.
  • Stratospheric ozone helps absorb harmful ultraviolet rays from the sun. Ozone-depleting substances like CFCs can thin stratospheric ozone; this should not be confused with ordinary air pollution haze.

Major Pollution Control Measures

  • Cleaner vehicle technologies and fuels, public transport, compliance with emission norms, appropriate control devices in industrial chimneys, dust management, and avoiding waste burning reduce local air pollution.
  • At the household level, clean cooking fuels and adequate ventilation are vital. Monitoring stations indicate pollution levels; effective policy also requires source-specific action, compliance, and regional coordination.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which organs are most affected by air pollution?

The respiratory system and cardiovascular system are primarily affected; the impact depends on the pollutant, concentration, and duration of exposure.

Are ground-level ozone and the ozone layer the same thing?

Both are ozone molecules, but their location and role differ. Ground-level ozone is a pollutant, whereas stratospheric ozone protects against UV radiation.

What are simple measures to control air pollution?

Avoiding waste burning, adopting clean fuels, using public transport, maintaining vehicles, and controlling construction dust are effective measures.
