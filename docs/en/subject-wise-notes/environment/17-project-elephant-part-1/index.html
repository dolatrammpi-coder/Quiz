Project Elephant 1992: Objectives, Elephant Corridors, and Conservation Status

Project Elephant is a flagship programme dedicated to the conservation of Asian elephants and their habitats in India. This part covers the objectives of the programme, the need to mitigate human-elephant conflict, the biological profile of the Asian elephant, conservation references under IUCN/CITES and Indian law, elephant corridors, and the MIKE monitoring programme.

Key Topics Covered in This Chapter

  • Objectives of Project Elephant: Goals related to elephant habitat conservation, reducing human-elephant conflict, and welfare of captive elephants.
  • Conservation Status of the Asian Elephant: Identification of Elephas maximus and its distinct statuses under the IUCN Red List, CITES, and the Wildlife (Protection) Act.
  • Why Are Elephant Corridors Important?: Connecting habitats, seasonal movement, and barriers caused by human settlements and infrastructure.
  • MIKE and Public Awareness Campaigns: Initiatives such as Monitoring the Illegal Killing of Elephants, Gaj Yatra, and Haathi Mere Saathi.

Frequently Asked Questions by Students

Why was Project Elephant launched? The primary objective of this programme is to support the conservation of elephants and their habitats, protect elephant corridors, and promote measures to reduce human-elephant conflict.

What is an elephant corridor? An elephant corridor is a strip of land that enables the movement of elephants between separate habitats. Obstructions in corridors can increase conflict and habitat fragmentation.
