What is Plastic Pollution? Types of Plastics, Resin Codes, and Microplastics

Plastic can persist in the environment for a long time and break down into tiny particles that reach soil, rivers, and oceans. In this part, study synthetic polymers, the difference between thermoplastics and thermosetting plastics, Resin Identification Codes 1–7, primary and secondary microplastics, nanoplastics, and sources of marine plastic debris.

Key Topics Covered in This Chapter

  • Thermoplastics vs. Thermosetting Plastics: Differences in behaviour upon heating, remoulding, and recyclability.
  • Resin Identification Codes 1–7: PET, HDPE, PVC, LDPE, PP, PS, and OTHER codes along with their common uses.
  • Primary and Secondary Microplastics: Sources of microbeads, synthetic fibres, and particles formed from the breakdown of larger plastics.
  • Marine Plastic Pollution: Ghost nets, oceanic garbage patches, ingestion/entanglement, and impacts on marine life.

Frequently Asked Questions by Students

What are microplastics? Microplastics generally refer to plastic particles smaller than 5 millimetres. They are studied as primary particles released directly from sources and secondary particles formed from the fragmentation of larger plastic items.

What do Plastic Resin Codes 1 to 7 indicate? They are used to identify the resin category of a plastic—such as PET, HDPE, PVC, LDPE, PP, PS, and OTHER. Having a code on an item does not automatically guarantee that it will be recycled by local waste systems.
