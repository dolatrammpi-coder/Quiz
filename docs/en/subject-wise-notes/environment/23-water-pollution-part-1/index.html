Water Pollution: DO, BOD, COD, Eutrophication, and Biomagnification

Understanding water pollution requires knowing the relationship between pollution sources and water quality indicators. In this part, study point-source and non-point-source pollution, dissolved oxygen (DO), biochemical oxygen demand (BOD), chemical oxygen demand (COD), eutrophication, biomagnification, and water-borne diseases with examples.

Key Topics Covered in This Chapter

  • Point and Non-Point Sources: Difference between identifiable sources like industrial outfalls/pipes and diffused sources like agricultural runoff.
  • DO, BOD, and COD: Meaning of dissolved oxygen and oxygen demand indicators, and their use in assessing water quality.
  • Eutrophication and Biomagnification: Algal growth from excess nutrients, depletion of dissolved oxygen, and the process of increasing pollutant concentration across food chains.
  • Toxicity and Water-Borne Diseases: Differences in causes of Minamata, Itai-Itai, Blue Baby Syndrome, arsenic/fluoride toxicity, and infectious diseases.

Frequently Asked Questions by Students

What is the difference between BOD and COD? BOD indicates the oxygen used by microorganisms in decomposing organic matter; COD represents the oxygen equivalent demand through chemical oxidation.

Why can eutrophication cause fish mortality? Excess nutrients can trigger excessive algal growth. When algae die and decompose, dissolved oxygen in the water can drop, putting stress on fish and other aquatic organisms.
