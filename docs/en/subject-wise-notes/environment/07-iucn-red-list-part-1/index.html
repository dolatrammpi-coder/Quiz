Section 2 — Definition of Threatened, Five Scientific Assessment Criteria (A to E) & Commissions

Five criteria (Criteria A, B, C, D, and E)

9 Categories & 5 Assessment Criteria of the IUCN Red List — English Notes

The IUCN Red List is the premier scientific reference for assessing global species extinction risk. In this chapter, systematically study the founding and headquarters of IUCN, its nine categories, the definition of Threatened, the five assessment criteria (A to E), expert commissions, and how it differs from the Red Data Book.

Key Topics Covered in This Chapter

  • Introduction to IUCN & Red List: Full form, establishment, headquarters, and objective of the Red List.
  • Understanding the Nine Categories: Meanings of EX, EW, CR, EN, VU, NT, LC, DD, and NE, and identifying Threatened categories.
  • Five Assessment Criteria (A–E): Scientific thresholds for population decline, geographic range, small population size, and extinction probability.
  • Scientific Listing vs. Legal Protection: Difference between IUCN risk assessment and national wildlife laws / the Red Data Book.

Frequently Asked Questions by Students

What are the nine categories of the IUCN Red List? Extinct, Extinct in the Wild, Critically Endangered, Endangered, Vulnerable, Near Threatened, Least Concern, Data Deficient, and Not Evaluated.

Does listing on the IUCN Red List automatically confer legal protection? No. The Red List is a scientific assessment of risk; legal protection is determined by the laws and notifications of the respective country.
