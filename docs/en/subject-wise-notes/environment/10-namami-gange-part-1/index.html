Namami Gange Programme Notes: History, 8 Pillars & Ganga Conservation

The Namami Gange Programme is a flagship initiative for pollution abatement and rejuvenation of the Ganga river. In this part, study the Ganga Action Plan, NGRBA background, programme objectives and pillars, sources of sewage and industrial effluents, institutional governance structure, ecological flow, and aquatic biodiversity of the Ganga.

Key Topics Covered in This Chapter

  • Historical Background of Ganga Conservation: Context of Ganga Action Plan-I/II, NGRBA, and the launch of the Namami Gange Programme.
  • Programme Pillars & Pollution Sources: Sewage, industrial effluents, grossly polluting industries, and key river pollution control measures.
  • Institutional Governance Structure: Roles of the National Ganga Council, NMCG, and related state/district-level bodies.
  • Ecological Flow & Biodiversity: Conservation topics related to e-flow, Ganges Dolphin, Gharial, turtles, otters, and fish.

Frequently Asked Questions by Students

What is the main objective of the Namami Gange Programme? Its primary objective is to reduce pollution in the Ganga river, conserve riverine ecology, and coordinate activities related to the rejuvenation of the river.

What does e-flow mean in the Ganga? Ecological flow refers to the minimum water flow required in a river to sustain its ecosystem and essential ecological functions.
