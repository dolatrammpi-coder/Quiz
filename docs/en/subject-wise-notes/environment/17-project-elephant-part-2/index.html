Human–Elephant Conflict, Elephant Reserves, Gajraj & RE-HAB — Notes

Elephant conservation is not limited to habitats alone; it also encompasses preventing railway accidents, reducing crop damage, early warning systems, captive elephant welfare, and curbing illegal ivory trade. This part focuses on Elephant Reserves, the Gajraj system, RE-HAB bee fencing, radio collaring, and captive elephant management.

Key Topics Covered in This Chapter

  • Elephant Reserves and Their Nature: Understanding the conservation and administrative significance of Elephant Reserves and how their legal status differs from Tiger Reserves.
  • Measures to Mitigate Human–Elephant Conflict: Safety along railway tracks, warning alerts, crop protection, and monitoring elephant movement.
  • Gajraj and RE-HAB: Detection systems for railway safety and using honeybees to reduce human-elephant conflict.
  • Captive Elephants and Ivory Trade: Microchipping and management, rescue protocols, and international trade controls under CITES.

Common Student Questions

What are the main causes of human–elephant conflict? Habitat fragmentation, obstruction of traditional elephant corridors, availability of agricultural crops, and clashes with human settlements and railway lines are major contributing factors.

What is Project RE-HAB? RE-HAB stands for Reducing Elephant-Human Attacks using Bees. It is an initiative to mitigate conflict between elephants and people through bee fencing and bee-based deterrents.
