Major Biomes and Vegetation of India

India hosts diverse natural vegetation—including tropical rainforests, moist and dry deciduous forests, thorny scrub, desert regions, and montane vegetation—shaped by climate and topography. To identify a region's biome, examine rainfall, temperature, altitude, and dominant vegetation together.

Tropical Evergreen and Rainforests

  • Evergreen forests develop in regions with heavy rainfall and relatively warm conditions. In India, they occur in the humid Western Ghats and parts of the Northeast, as well as in the Andaman and Nicobar Islands.
  • Dense tree cover, multiple forest strata, and high biodiversity are notable features of these forests. Variations in rainfall and altitude can alter vegetation structure within the same region.

Moist and Dry Deciduous Forests

  • Deciduous forests shed leaves during unfavorable seasons to minimize water loss. Moist deciduous forests occur in wetter areas, whereas dry deciduous forests are found in regions with lower rainfall.
  • Teak and Sal are familiar tree examples associated with Indian deciduous forests, though their actual distribution varies with region and local conditions. In exams, prioritize the relationship between forest type and rainfall.

Thorny Vegetation and Desert Regions

  • In regions with low rainfall and high evaporation, thorny shrubs, small leaves, spines, or deep roots serve as adaptations to water scarcity. The arid regions of western India and the Thar Desert exemplify this vegetation.
  • Deserts are not limited to sand dunes; they are defined by low precipitation and water-balance conditions. Grazing pressure, groundwater extraction, and land degradation increase the vulnerability of arid regions.

Montane and Altitudinal Vegetation

  • In the Himalayas, vegetation changes with altitude and temperature: broad-leaved forests at lower elevations give way to coniferous forests higher up, and alpine grasslands above the tree line.
  • Slope, aspect, rainfall, and local climate also influence vegetation in mountainous areas. Because climate change can shift species distributions by elevation, conserving montane biodiversity is vital.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where are evergreen forests found in India?

Evergreen forests are found in the humid parts of the Western Ghats, parts of Northeast India, and the Andaman and Nicobar Islands.

Why do deciduous forests shed their leaves?

Many deciduous plants shed their leaves during dry or unfavorable seasons to reduce water loss; this adaptation varies with local species and climate.

Which adaptation is common in the vegetation of the Thar region?

Adaptations such as thorns or small leaves, water storage, and deep roots help plants cope with water scarcity.
