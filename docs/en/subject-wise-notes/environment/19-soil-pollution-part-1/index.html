What is Soil Pollution? Heavy Metals, Pesticides, and Soil Pollutants

Soil pollution becomes a major concern when chemicals, heavy metals, or wastes degrade soil properties, organisms, and usability. In this part, study the difference between soil contamination and pollution, agrochemicals, industrial/mining wastes, fly ash, landfill leachate, e-waste, salinity, and sources of emerging pollutants.

Key Topics Covered in This Chapter

  • Soil Contamination vs. Soil Pollution: The distinction between the mere presence of a substance in soil and harmful changes caused by it.
  • Agrochemicals and Pesticides: Imbalanced use of Urea/DAP, NPK ratio, and insecticides/herbicides/fungicides.
  • Heavy Metals and Industrial Waste: Sources of Lead, Cadmium, Arsenic, Mercury, and Chromium along with impacts of mining and fly ash.
  • Leachate, Salinity, and Emerging Pollutants: Landfill leachate, e-waste, plastics, oil spills, pharmaceuticals, and impacts of waterlogging/salinity.

Frequently Asked Questions by Students

What is the difference between soil contamination and soil pollution? Contamination refers to the presence of a substance in soil; it is termed pollution when its concentration or nature adversely impacts soil, organisms, or land uses.

Where do heavy metals in soil come from? Industrial effluents, mining, e-waste, and certain agricultural inputs can be sources of heavy metals. They persist in soil and pose a risk of entering crops and groundwater.
