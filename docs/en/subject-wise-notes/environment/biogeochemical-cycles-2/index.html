Major Biogeochemical Cycles

Ecological Significance of Carbon, Nitrogen, Phosphorus, Sulfur, and Water Cycles

One-Liner Questions

Why is a nutrient cycle referred to as a 'biogeochemical cycle'?
Because nutrients circulate continuously among living organisms (bio), geological rocks/soil (geo), and the chemical environment.
Tap to view answer
What is the fundamental difference between a perfect cycle and an imperfect cycle?
Gaseous cycles are rapid and relatively perfect, whereas sedimentary cycles are slower and imperfect because nutrients get locked in sediments.
Tap to view answer
Which type of biogeochemical cycles are the carbon and nitrogen cycles classified as?
Gaseous cycles, as their primary reservoirs are the atmosphere and the oceans.
Tap to view answer
How are the phosphorus and sulfur cycles primarily classified?
Phosphorus is a purely sedimentary cycle, whereas sulfur is a mixed cycle involving both sedimentary and gaseous phases.
Tap to view answer
What acts as the primary bridge between biotic and abiotic components in the carbon cycle?
Photosynthesis, which converts inorganic CO2 into organic sugars.
Tap to view answer
What is the greatest natural barrier for most living organisms in the nitrogen cycle?
The inability of plants to directly absorb inert atmospheric N2, making nitrogen fixation essential.
Tap to view answer
What is the main natural reservoir of phosphorus?
Phosphate-bearing sedimentary rocks in the Earth's crust.
Tap to view answer
What is the most distinctive physical feature of the phosphorus cycle that sets it apart from other cycles?
It has no significant gaseous or atmospheric component.

What is the primary biological role of phosphorus in living organisms?
Formation of nucleic acids (DNA, RNA), ATP (energy carrier), cell membranes (phospholipids), and bones.

In which ionic forms do plants primarily absorb phosphorus from the soil?
As orthophosphate ions (H2PO4⁻ and HPO4²⁻).

By which natural process is phosphorus released from rocks into the soil?
Chemical and physical weathering, along with dissolution by rainwater.

Why is the phosphorus cycle considered extremely slow in aquatic systems?
Because phosphates buried in deep marine sediments take millions of years to return to the surface through geological uplift.

The phosphate- and nitrogen-rich excrement of seabirds (and bats), valued as a rich natural fertilizer.

Phosphorus and Sulfur Cycles

The phosphorus and sulfur cycles, their natural reservoirs, and the processes by which nutrients reach living organisms form an essential part of biogeochemical cycles. Unlike the carbon and nitrogen cycles, the phosphorus cycle lacks a major gaseous atmospheric phase under normal conditions—a distinction frequently tested in exams.

Stages of the Phosphorus Cycle

  • Phosphorus primarily exists as phosphates in rocks and sediments. Weathering releases phosphates into soil and water, where plants can absorb them; through food chains, it passes to other organisms.
  • Decomposition and excretion return phosphates back to soil or water. Some phosphate accumulates in reservoirs and marine sediments; geological processes can make it available again over long timescales.

Key Processes of the Sulfur Cycle

  • Sulfur is found in various compounds across rocks, seawater, organisms, soil, and the atmosphere. Volcanoes, marine emissions, and biological processes contribute to natural sulfur fluxes.
  • Combustion of fossil fuels can emit sulfur dioxide. Through atmospheric chemical reactions and deposition, sulfur compounds can return to land and water.

Differences Among Phosphorus, Nitrogen, and Carbon Cycles

  • In the carbon cycle, atmospheric CO2, photosynthesis, respiration, and combustion are key; in the nitrogen cycle, fixation, nitrification, and denitrification are major processes.
  • Most of the phosphorus cycle operates between rocks, soil, water, and sediments and generally lacks a major gaseous phase. Remember each cycle's name alongside its main reservoir and return process.

Human Activities and Nutrient Imbalance

  • Excessive phosphates entering water bodies from fertilizers and wastewater can cause rapid algal blooms. Subsequent decomposition of organic matter can deplete dissolved oxygen and harm aquatic organisms.
  • SO2 emissions from sulfur-rich fuels can contribute to acid deposition. Wastewater treatment, reducing nutrient runoff, and adopting clean energy help reduce human pressure on these cycles.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where are the major reservoirs in the phosphorus cycle?

Rocks, soil, water, and sediments are the primary reservoirs; weathering plays a key role in making phosphates available to organisms.

Which biogeochemical cycle generally lacks a major gaseous phase?

The phosphorus cycle lacks a major atmospheric gaseous phase under normal conditions; its flow is primarily linked to rocks, soil, water, and sediments.

What can be the impact of excess phosphate in water?

Excess nutrients can trigger eutrophication and algal growth; a drop in dissolved oxygen during decomposition can severely affect aquatic life.
