What is Noise Pollution? Decibels, Sources, and Health Effects

To understand noise pollution, it is essential to know the fundamentals of sound intensity, the decibel scale, frequency, and its effects on humans and wildlife. This part covers dB/dB(A), audible range, infrasonic and ultrasonic waves, sonic boom, NIHL, tinnitus, effects on sleep and health, and basic concepts of noise control.

Key Topics Covered in This Chapter

  • Decibel and Sound Measurement: Core facts related to dB and dB(A), logarithmic scale, and sound intensity.
  • Frequency and Sound Waves: Human audible range, infrasonic and ultrasonic waves, and sonic boom.
  • Sources and Effects of Noise Pollution: Traffic, industry, and other sources; hearing loss (NIHL), tinnitus, insomnia, and stress.
  • Wildlife and Noise Management: Impact on marine life, reference to WHO guidelines, and the Green Muffler concept.

Common Student Questions

Why is the decibel scale called logarithmic? The decibel scale expressing sound levels is logarithmic, so equal numerical increments do not represent equal proportional increases in sound energy.

What health effects can noise pollution cause? Prolonged exposure to loud noise can affect hearing ability, sleep, stress levels, and cardiovascular health outcomes such as blood pressure.
