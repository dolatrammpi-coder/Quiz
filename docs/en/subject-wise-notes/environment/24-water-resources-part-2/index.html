National Water Policy 2012, Traditional Water Harvesting, and River Water Disputes

Water management in India encompasses traditional rainwater harvesting, irrigation efficiency, drinking water schemes, groundwater recharge, and inter-state river water sharing. In this part, study the National Water Policy 2012, systems like Tanka, Johad, Kul, and Ahar-Pyne, Jal Jeevan Mission, Atal Bhujal Yojana, Article 262, and the Ken-Betwa Link Project.

Key Topics Covered in This Chapter

  • National Water Policy and Traditional Systems: Managing water as a resource and regional uses of Tanka, Johad, Kul, Ahar-Pyne, Bamboo Drip, and Zabo.
  • Jal Jeevan Mission and Groundwater Schemes: Household tap connection targets, Atal Bhujal Yojana, PMKSY, Catch the Rain, and water-conservation initiatives.
  • Article 262 and River Water Disputes: Inter-State River Water Disputes Act, Cauvery/Krishna/Mahadayi disputes, and the Indus Waters Treaty.
  • Ken-Betwa and River Linking Projects: Objectives of river interlinking, potential socio-ecological impacts, and movements such as Narmada and Tehri.

Frequently Asked Student Questions

What does Article 262 relate to? Article 262 of the Indian Constitution empowers Parliament to enact laws for the adjudication of disputes relating to the waters of inter-state rivers or river valleys.

What are Tanka and Johad? A Tanka is a traditional rainwater harvesting structure used in arid regions, while a Johad is a local pond or water-impounding structure that helps arrest rainwater and recharge groundwater.
