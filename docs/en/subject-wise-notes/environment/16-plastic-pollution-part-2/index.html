Plastic Waste Management Rules 2016, SUP Ban, and EPR — English Notes

Proper waste collection, producer responsibility, recycling, and safe alternatives are crucial to solving plastic pollution. In this part, understand the Plastic Waste Management Rules, single-use plastic (SUP), Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR), bioplastics, the use of plastic in road construction, and the Global Plastic Treaty process.

Key Topics Covered in This Chapter

  • Ecological and Health Impacts of Plastic Waste: Ingestion and entanglement by marine life, and issues linked to substances like BPA and phthalates.
  • PWM Rules and SUP Ban: Plastic Waste Management Rules 2016, banned items, and carry bag thickness standards—verify against the latest notifications.
  • Role of EPR: Extended responsibility of producers, importers, and brand owners, and the categories of plastic packaging.
  • Alternatives and Recycling: Bioplastics like PLA/PHA, jute/cloth/glass, Prof. Vasudevan's road-building technique, and the circular economy.

Frequently Asked Questions by Students

What does EPR mean? Extended Producer Responsibility. In the context of plastic packaging, collection, recycling, or other specified obligations under the rules may apply to relevant producers, importers, and brand owners.

Can all bioplastics be composted at home? No. Some materials require specific industrial composting conditions; 'bio-based', 'biodegradable', and 'compostable' are distinct properties. Reading labels and standards carefully is essential.
