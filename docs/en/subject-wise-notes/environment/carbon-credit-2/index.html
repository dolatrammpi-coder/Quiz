Carbon Credits and Carbon Market in India

The aim of India's carbon market is to create incentives for industries and other eligible entities to reduce emissions. While understanding India's carbon credit trading system, it is important to grasp its legal basis, institutional roles, compliance and voluntary markets, carbon credit certificates, and the difference between trading and actual emission reductions. These notes focus on enduring core concepts rather than the details of current regulations.

Core Concept of the Indian Carbon Market

Under the Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS), India's framework has been developed in phases. Within this structure, prescribed performance and emission norms may apply to emission-intensive sectors, while another pathway may involve credits from project-based emission reductions or removals. Covered sectors, targets, and procedures are determined by official notifications and updated regulations.

  • The full form of CCTS is Carbon Credit Trading Scheme.
  • Do not treat compliance pathways and offset/project-based pathways as identical.
  • Official notifications are decisive for eligible sectors, targets, and timelines.

Institutional Framework and Measurement

A carbon market is credible only when emission data are measured using a uniform methodology, reported transparently, and independently verified. Rulemaking, market administration, registry management, exchange trading, and verification may be handled by separate bodies. Therefore, rather than calling any single institution the 'sole regulator' without context, one should understand its specific function.

  • A clear emission boundary or baseline is necessary for measurement and reporting.
  • Verification improves data quality and confidence in credits.
  • Unit identification and tracking in a registry help prevent double counting.

Opportunities and Safeguards for India

Clean technologies, energy efficiency, renewable energy, industrial process improvements, and measurable carbon removal projects can all contribute to lowering emissions. However, market benefits are sustainable only when the emission baseline is clear, improvements are genuine, and social and environmental safeguards are followed. A carbon market should be viewed as a complement to direct energy savings, clean production, and long-term climate policy.

  • Energy efficiency and clean energy create opportunities to cut emissions.
  • Weak baselines and double counting degrade credit quality.
  • Market rules can evolve over time; refer to updated official sources for exams.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the full form of CCTS?

Carbon Credit Trading Scheme.

Why is verification necessary in a carbon market?

It helps ensure that reported emission reductions are measurable, certified, and free from double counting.

Is buying carbon credits a complete substitute for cutting one's own emissions?

No. Energy efficiency, clean energy, and direct emission reductions are the primary measures; credible credits can serve as a complementary tool for residual emissions.
