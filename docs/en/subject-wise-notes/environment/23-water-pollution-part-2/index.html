Water Act 1974, CPCB Water Quality Classes, and STP/ETP/CETP

India's institutional framework for water pollution control is anchored in the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974 and regulatory bodies like CPCB and SPCB. In this part, study regulatory powers, CPCB water quality classes A–E, stages of sewage treatment, STP/ETP/CETP, Zero Liquid Discharge, thermal pollution, and drinking water quality standards.

Key Topics Covered in This Chapter

  • Water Act and Pollution Control Boards: Roles of CPCB/SPCB, Consent to Establish/Operate (CTE/CTO), and applicable statutory provisions.
  • CPCB Water Quality Classes A–E: Water classification based on Designated Best Use and key quality indicators.
  • STP, ETP, CETP, and ZLD: Objectives of sewage, industrial effluent, and common treatment plants; primary, secondary, and tertiary treatment.
  • Thermal Pollution and Drinking Water Standards: Ecological impacts of heated discharge water and BIS drinking water standards in practical context.

Frequently Asked Student Questions

What is the difference between STP and ETP? STP is primarily designed for treating sewage and domestic wastewater, whereas ETP treats industrial wastewater based on its specific chemical composition. CETP is a common facility capable of treating effluent from multiple industrial units.

Why is it necessary to reduce BOD? When wastewater contains high levels of organic matter, microorganisms consume more dissolved oxygen to decompose it. Treating this load reduces the risk of oxygen depletion in receiving water bodies.
