Soil Pollution Remediation: Bioremediation, Phytoremediation & Soil Health Card

Impacts of soil pollution can reach soil microorganisms, crop production, food security, and groundwater. This part features Q&As on bioremediation, the difference between bioaugmentation and biostimulation, phytoremediation, hyperaccumulator plants, landfarming/biopiles, Soil Health Card, organic farming, and integrated pest management.

Key Topics Covered in This Chapter

  • Ecological Impacts of Soil Pollution: Loss of soil organisms, disruption of nutrient cycles, pollutant uptake by crops, and groundwater leaching.
  • Bioremediation and Its Types: Breakdown of pollutants by microorganisms, and the difference between bioaugmentation and biostimulation.
  • Phytoremediation: Techniques for extracting, stabilizing, or treating pollutants through plants and their roots.
  • Soil Health and Sustainable Agriculture: Soil Health Card parameters, organic/natural farming, IPM, compost, and soil reclamation measures.

Frequently Asked Questions by Students

What is Bioremediation? Bioremediation attempts to break down or render less harmful certain pollutants present in media like soil/water using microorganisms or their biological activity.

What is the difference between bioaugmentation and biostimulation? In bioaugmentation, beneficial microorganisms are added; in biostimulation, nutrients or favorable conditions are provided to boost the activity of pre-existing microorganisms.
