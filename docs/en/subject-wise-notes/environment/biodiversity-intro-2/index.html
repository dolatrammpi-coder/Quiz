Threats to Biodiversity and Conservation

When studying threats to biodiversity, extinction risk, and conservation measures, it is essential to distinguish between local extinction, global extinction, and the status of threatened species. A decline in a species' population does not automatically equate to global extinction, though it can signal escalating risk.

Major Threats Affecting Biodiversity

  • Habitat destruction and fragmentation reduce opportunities for species to find shelter, food, breed, and migrate. Activities such as roads, agricultural expansion, urbanization, mining, and dams can alter habitats.
  • Overhunting or harvesting, illegal trade, pollution, invasive alien species, and climate change also impact biodiversity. These pressures can act together, requiring cause-specific management.

Concepts of Extinction and Risk

  • Global extinction occurs when no living member of a species remains. Local extinction means a species has disappeared from a specific area while still existing in other regions.
  • The IUCN Red List is a global system for assessing species' extinction risk. When memorizing categories, distinguish Critically Endangered, Endangered, and Vulnerable as separate threat levels; classification does not depend solely on population count.

Major Types of Conservation

  • In-situ conservation protects organisms within their natural habitats; National Parks, Wildlife Sanctuaries, and Biosphere Reserves are key examples.
  • Ex-situ conservation involves protection outside natural habitats, such as zoos, botanical gardens, seed banks, and gene banks. These methods are not merely alternatives to one another, but are often complementary.

Steps to Make Conservation Effective

  • Protecting habitats and wildlife corridors, habitat restoration, pollution control, curbing illegal trade, and scientific monitoring can help reduce extinction risk.
  • Involving local communities in decision-making and addressing livelihood needs makes conservation sustainable. Species-specific data and long-term assessment are essential in conservation planning.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between local extinction and global extinction?

In local extinction, a species disappears from a specific region while surviving elsewhere; in global extinction, no living individual of the species remains anywhere.

What are examples of In-situ and Ex-situ conservation?

National Parks are examples of In-situ conservation; seed banks and zoos can serve as examples of Ex-situ conservation.

What does the IUCN Red List indicate?

It evaluates the extinction risk of species and provides information on their conservation status.
