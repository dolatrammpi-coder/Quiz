Ecology: Definition, History, and Branches

Ecology is the scientific study of the relationships of organisms with one another and with their physical environment. It covers the distribution and abundance of organisms, resource use, adaptation, and the flow of energy and nutrients. Competitive exams frequently ask about the origin of the term, Ernst Haeckel, levels of ecological organization, and the major branches of ecology.

Definition and Historical Development

The term 'Ecology' was coined by German biologist Ernst Haeckel in 1866. It derives from the Greek words oikos (home or habitat) and logos (study). Modern ecology is not limited to listing organisms; it analyzes the interactions among organisms, habitats, resources, competition, symbiosis, and environmental change.

  • Oikos: home or habitat; Logos: study.
  • Ecology encompasses interactions between both biotic and abiotic components.
  • Environmental science is a broader field; ecology is its core scientific foundation.

Major Levels of Study

Ecology is studied across distinct levels of organization. Starting from an individual organism's relationship with its environment, the study expands to populations (organisms of a single species), communities (multiple species), ecosystems (living and abiotic components), biomes, and ultimately the biosphere. This hierarchy reflects progression from smaller to broader levels of organization.

  • Organism → Population → Community → Ecosystem → Biome → Biosphere.
  • A population is a group of organisms of the same species sharing a given area and time.
  • A community comprises populations of different species.

Major Branches and Applications

Autecology focuses on the relationship of a single organism or species with its environment, whereas synecology examines interactions among multiple species within a community. Population ecology studies population size, density, and growth; ecosystem ecology examines energy flow and nutrient cycling. These concepts are applied in conservation, pollution control, agriculture, and natural resource management.

  • Autecology: Relationship between a single species or organism and its environment.
  • Synecology: Relationships among communities and multiple species.
  • Population ecology: Study of population size, density, growth, and regulation.
