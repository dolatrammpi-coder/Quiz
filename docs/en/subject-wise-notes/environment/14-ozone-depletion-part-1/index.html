How Does the Ozone Layer Deplete? Dobson Unit, ODS and UV Rays

The stratospheric ozone layer absorbs a portion of the harmful ultraviolet radiation coming from the Sun. In this part, study the discovery of ozone, Chapman Cycle, Dobson Unit (DU), UV-A/UV-B/UV-C, Ozone Depleting Substances (ODS), chlorine/bromine radicals, ODP, and the health and ecological impacts of ozone depletion.

Key Topics Covered in This Chapter

  • Ozone Layer and Chapman Cycle: Formation/breakdown of ozone, location in the stratosphere, and natural chemical cycle.
  • Dobson Unit and UV Radiation: Measurement of total ozone, concept of ozone hole, and differences among UV-A, UV-B, and UV-C.
  • ODS and ODP: CFCs, HCFCs, halons, carbon tetrachloride, methyl bromide, and Ozone Depletion Potential.
  • Health and Ecological Impacts: Potential effects of increased UV-B, eye/skin damage, and impact on phytoplankton and biological systems.

Common Student Questions

What does the Dobson Unit (DU) measure? The Dobson Unit is the unit used to express the total amount of ozone present in a column of the atmosphere.

How do CFCs damage the ozone layer? When CFCs break down under UV radiation in the stratosphere, the released chlorine radicals can destroy ozone molecules through catalytic cycles.
