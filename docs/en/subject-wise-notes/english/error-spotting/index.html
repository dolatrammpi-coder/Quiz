Quick Reference
Major Grammatical Error Categories, Rules & Corrections
6. Error Spotting
Error TypeRuleCorrect Example
Subject-Verb AgreementIntervening prepositional phrases do not affect subject numberThe quality of apples was poor.
Correlative ConjunctionsNearest subject determines verb number in either...orEither he or his brothers were present.
Parenthetical ConnectorsFirst subject governs verb with as well asThe leader, as well as followers, was arrested.
Indefinite PronounsEach, every, either, neither take singular verbsEach of the boys has arrived.
Uncountable NounsNouns like advice and furniture never take plural suffixAll the luggage was checked.
Hyphenated Compound NounsNoun acting as adjective remains singularA five-rupee note was found.
Tense Sequence with SincePresent perfect main clause paired with simple past clauseFive years have passed since he came.
Habitual Action in Continuous TensePresent habits require simple present tenseHe goes for a walk daily.
Conditional Clause ModalsNever use modal auxiliaries inside if-clausesIf it rains, we will stop.
Correlative PairingHardly and scarcely pair with when, not thanHardly had I arrived when it rained.
Comparative ConjunctionsNo sooner pairs with than, not then/whenNo sooner did he arrive than cheers rose.
Negative Word PairingLest governs should with no negative wordsWalk carefully lest you should fall.
Degree RedundancyAvoid double comparatives and superlativesThis is much better than that.
Latin ComparativesSenior, junior, superior take preposition toHe is senior to me.
Adjective vs AdverbAction verbs are modified by adverbsHe spoke extremely politely to us.
Dangling ModifiersParticipial phrases must clearly attach to logical subjectsWalking down the road, I saw a tiger.

Frequently Repeated Competitive Exam Error Traps
6. Error Spotting
Error TypeRuleCorrect Example
Superfluous PairingThe reason why takes that, never becauseThe reason why he failed is that he slept.
Double NegativeAvoid negative words with unless and untilUnless you study, you will fail.
Redundant PrepositionsOrder, describe, enter take no direct prepositionHe entered the hall quietly.
Fixed PrepositionAbstain, refrain, prevent require preposition fromHe abstained from alcohol completely.
Idiomatic PrepositionManage difficulties uses cope with, never cope upShe could cope with the pressure.
Possessive before GerundNouns and pronouns preceding gerunds require possessive caseI insisted on his going there.
Relative Pronoun InversionOne of the plus plural noun plus who takes plural verbOne of the men who were present.
Parallel StructureCoordinating conjunctions connect identical grammatical formsShe likes painting, dancing, and singing.
Subjunctive WereUnreal conditions use were across all personsIf I were you, I would agree.
Article OmissionZero article with institutions visited for primary purposeHe went to hospital for surgery.
Pronoun OrderNormal polite personal pronoun order is 2-3-1You, he, and I must attend.
Pronoun Order (Fault)Confessing errors or negative acts uses order 1-2-3I, you, and he are guilty.
