One-Liner Questions

Into which three main types are the continuous physical movements of ocean water classified?
Waves, Tides, and Ocean Currents

In deep-water Ocean Waves, what is the actual motion of individual water particles as a wave passes?
Water particles move in nearly stationary circular orbital paths, while only the wave energy propagates forward horizontally

What is the lowest depressed valley between two wave crests called?

What is the horizontal distance between two successive wave crests or two successive troughs called?

What is the vertical distance between the crest and the trough of a wave called?
Wave Height (H)
Tap to view answer
What is the time interval taken for two successive wave crests to pass a fixed point called?
Wave Period (T)

What is the uninterrupted distance of open sea over which the wind blows constantly in one direction to generate waves called?
Fetch (the longer the fetch, the larger the waves)

When a deep-water wave enters shallow coastal water, how do its speed, wavelength, and height change?
Bottom friction reduces wave speed and wavelength, while wave height increases sharply

When a steepening wave in shallow water becomes unstable and its crest curls over and collapses into foam on the shore, what is it called?

What are the long-wavelength destructive sea waves generated by submarine earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, or underwater landslides called?

What are the speed and wave height characteristics of Tsunami waves in the deep open ocean?
Jet-like speed (700–800 km/h), but a very small wave height of less than 1 metre in deep water

What is the periodic, rhythmic rise and fall of sea level once or twice a day caused by the gravitational pull of the Moon and Sun and Earth's centrifugal force called?

What is the periodic rise of sea water and its advance toward the coast called?

What is the falling of sea level and the retreat of water back toward the sea between two high tides called?

How many times stronger is the tide-generating gravitational force of the Moon compared to that of the Sun?
Approximately 2.17 times stronger (because the Moon is much closer to the Earth than the Sun)

What is the scientific reason why two high-tide bulges occur simultaneously on opposite sides of the Earth?
One bulge forms on the side facing the Moon due to direct gravitational attraction, and the opposite bulge forms on the antipodal side due to centrifugal force of the Earth–Moon barycentre rotation

What is the position of Syzygy when the Earth lies directly between the Sun and the Moon (on Full Moon day) called?

What are the exceptionally high tides (~20% higher than normal) occurring on New Moon and Full Moon days called?
Spring Tides

What is the primary physical cause of Spring Tides?
The combined gravitational pull of the Sun and the Moon acting in the same straight line (Syzygy)

Which tides occur on the seventh or eighth day (First and Third Quarter moons) of each lunar fortnight?

What causes Neap Tides (~20% lower than normal) during the first and third quarters of the Moon?
The Sun and the Moon are positioned at right angles (90° Quadrature) relative to the Earth, partially counteracting each other's gravitational pull

What is the average time interval between two successive high tides at a given coastal location (semi-diurnal cycle)?
12 hours 26 minutes (full lunar tidal day of 24 hours 52 minutes)

Why does high tide occur approximately 50–52 minutes later each day (or 26 minutes later per half-cycle)?
Because as the Earth rotates on its axis, the Moon also advances in the same direction along its orbit around the Earth, requiring the Earth to rotate an extra ~12.2° (52 minutes) to catch up

What is the continuous, directed horizontal movement of a massive volume of ocean water along a definite path called?
Ocean Current
Tap to view answer
Which are the four Primary Forces that initiate and govern the movement of Ocean Currents?
Heating by solar energy, Prevailing planetary winds (frictional drag), Gravity, and the Coriolis Force
Tap to view answer
Which Secondary Factors modify the direction and speed of Ocean Currents?
Differences in water density (temperature and salinity), configuration of coastlines, and submarine bottom relief
Tap to view answer
Due to intense solar heating and thermal expansion at the Equator, how much higher is the mean sea level near the Equator compared to middle latitudes?
Approximately 8 centimetres higher (creating a very gentle poleward gravity slope)
Tap to view answer
Which is the single most important direct driving force controlling the direction of surface Ocean Currents?
Prevailing Planetary Winds (Trade Winds and Westerlies exerting frictional drag on the sea surface)
Tap to view answer
By pushing tropical surface waters from east to west, which major currents do the Trade Winds generate?
The North Equatorial Current and South Equatorial Current
Tap to view answer
By driving mid-latitude surface waters from west to east, which currents do the Westerlies generate?
The West Wind Drift (North Atlantic Drift, North Pacific Drift, and Antarctic Circumpolar Current)
Tap to view answer
In which directions does the Coriolis Force deflect ocean currents in the Northern and Southern Hemispheres respectively?
To the right in the Northern Hemisphere and to the left in the Southern Hemisphere

What is the progressive clockwise (in N. Hemisphere) spiralling change in current direction and decrease in speed with depth caused by wind drag and Coriolis force called?

Tap to view answer
According to Ekman Transport theory, at what angle to the prevailing wind direction does the net transport of the upper wind-driven water layer occur?
At 90° to the right of the wind direction in the Northern Hemisphere, and 90° to the left in the Southern Hemisphere
Tap to view answer
What are the large, basin-wide circular loops of rotating ocean surface currents bounded by continents and driven by wind and Coriolis force called?

In which direction do subtropical Oceanic Gyres rotate in the Northern Hemisphere and Southern Hemisphere respectively?
Clockwise in the Northern Hemisphere and Counter-clockwise (Anticlockwise) in the Southern Hemisphere
Tap to view answer
How does differences in Salinity affect the vertical and horizontal movement of ocean water?
More saline water is denser and sinks below less saline water, while lighter, less saline water flows on the surface toward areas of higher salinity

How does the highly saline, dense water of the Mediterranean Sea flow across the sill of the Strait of Gibraltar into the Atlantic Ocean?

What is the slow, global-scale deep-ocean overturning circulation driven by density differences in temperature (thermo) and salinity (haline) called?

When offshore or coast-parallel winds push warm surface water away from a coast and cold, nutrient-rich water rises from below to replace it, what is the process called?

Why do coastal Upwelling zones support some of the world's richest commercial fisheries?
Because upwelling brings nitrates and phosphates from the deep sea to the sunlit surface zone, fuelling massive blooms of Phytoplankton

When surface water converges or piles up against a coast and sinks downward, what is the process called?
