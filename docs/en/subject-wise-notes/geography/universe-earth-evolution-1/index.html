Quick Reference
Important Facts & Contributions
Universe Earth Evolution 1
Theory / ConceptCore IdeaAssociated Facts
Big Bang Theory
View Answer
Origin of space, time, and matter from a cataclysmic explosion of an extremely dense and hot point (Singularity)
View Answer
Propounded by Georges Lemaître (1927); evidenced by Edwin Hubble's Redshift and CMBR radiation
Steady State Theory
View Answer
The universe has neither a beginning nor an end; continuous spontaneous creation of new matter in empty spaces as it expands
View Answer
Fred Hoyle, Hermann Bondi, and Thomas Gold (1948); invalidated after the discovery of CMBR
Pulsating Universe Theory
View Answer
The universe exists in a continuous cyclical sequence of expansion and contraction (Big Crunch)
View Answer
Dr. Allan Sandage; based on the balance between gravitational pull and expansion force
Nebular Hypothesis
View Answer
Formation of planets from rings separated due to cooling and rotation of a hot, rotating gaseous nebula
View Answer
Presented by Immanuel Kant (1755), modified by Pierre-Simon Laplace (1796)
Planetesimal Hypothesis
View Answer
Condensation of planetesimals from tidal bulges raised by a massive wandering star passing close to the Sun
View Answer
T.C. Chamberlin and F.R. Moulton (1905); categorized as a binary-star hypothesis
Tidal Hypothesis
View Answer
A cigar-shaped filament broke away from the proto-Sun due to the gravitational pull of an approaching star and cooled into planets
View Answer
Propounded by Sir James Jeans (1919), modified by Harold Jeffreys (1929)
Interstellar Dust Hypothesis
View Answer
The Sun attracted wandering interstellar dust and gas particles in space and accreted them into a saucer-shaped disc
View Answer
Russian scientist Otto Schmidt (1943); accurately explains differences in the chemical composition of planets
Electromagnetic Theory
View Answer
Dominance of solar magnetic fields and plasma forces alongside gravity in the origin of the Solar System
View Answer
Hannes Alfvén (1942); successfully explains the angular momentum distribution between the Sun and planets
