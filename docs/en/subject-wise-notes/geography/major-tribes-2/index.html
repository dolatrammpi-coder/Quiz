Tribe / GroupDistributionKey Features
Eskimo (Inuit / Inupiaq)
View Answer
Arctic Tundra region (Canada, Alaska, Greenland, and Eastern Siberia)
View Answer
Mongoloid race; dome-shaped snow houses 'Igloo' and summer skin tents 'Tupik'; seal hunting via 'Maupok' and 'Iturpuk' methods; harpoon, kayak boat, and sledge
Pygmy (Mbuti, Twa, Baka)
View Answer
Congo Basin of Central Africa (Ituri Rainforest, Gabon, Uganda, Cameroon)
View Answer
Negrito race; shortest stature in the world (average height under 1.5 metres); hunters and honey gatherers; beehive-like leaf huts on trees; Silent Trade with Bantu farmers
Bushman (San)
View Answer
Kalahari Desert of Southern Africa (Botswana, Namibia, South Africa)
View Answer
Negrito–Khoisan group; click language; water storage in ostrich eggshells; termites regarded as 'Bushman's Rice'; hunting with poisoned arrows; semi-circular windbreak 'Scherm'
Maasai
View Answer
East African Savanna grasslands (Kenya, northern Tanzania)
View Answer
Semi-nomadic pastoralists; mixed diet of cattle blood and milk; thorn-fenced circular village 'Kraal'; mud-and-dung huts 'Manyatta'; warrior class 'Moran'
Kirghiz
View Answer
Central Asian Steppe and Tian Shan / Pamir mountains (Kyrgyzstan)
View Answer
Mongoloid herders; seasonal migration (Transhumance) to high mountains in summer and valleys in winter; portable circular felt tent 'Yurt'; fermented mare's milk 'Koumiss'
Bedouin
View Answer
Arabian Desert (Hamada, Nafud, Rub' al Khali) and northern Sahara
View Answer
Caucasoid nomadic camel and sheep herders; camel- and goat-hair tents 'Khaima' (House of Hair); caravan travel; strong tradition of hospitality
Tuareg
View Answer
Western and central Sahara Desert (Mali, Niger, Algeria, Libya)
View Answer
Caucasoid–Berber camel herders and caravan traders; called 'Blue Men of the Sahara' due to blue robes and male face-veiling (Tagelmust)
