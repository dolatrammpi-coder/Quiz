Geological Time Scale and Evolution of the Earth - Part 2 (Universe Earth Evolution 2)

Motions of the Earth, Latitude & Longitude, Eclipses, and Equinoxes

🔍

One-Liner Questions

In which direction does the Earth rotate on its axis?
From West to East (Counter-clockwise).
Tap to view answer
What is the duration of one Sidereal Day on Earth?
23 hours 56 minutes 4 seconds.
Tap to view answer
What is the duration of one standard Solar Day?
Exactly 24 hours.
Tap to view answer
Why is a Solar Day approximately 4 minutes longer than a Sidereal Day?
Because due to its orbital revolution, the Earth has to rotate approximately 1° extra to face the Sun directly again.
Tap to view answer
What is the linear velocity of the Earth's rotation at the Equator?
Approximately 1,670 km per hour (zero at the poles).
Tap to view answer
What are the major geographical phenomena caused by the Earth's rotation?
The regular cycle of day and night and the generation of the Coriolis force.
Tap to view answer
What is the definition of the Circle of Illumination?
The imaginary circle on the globe that divides day from night.
Tap to view answer
What angle does the Earth's axis make with its orbital plane?
An angle of 66.5° (an axial tilt of 23.5° from the perpendicular).
Tap to view answer
In what type of orbit does the Earth revolve around the Sun?
In an Elliptical Orbit.
Tap to view answer
What is the average orbital speed of the Earth revolving around the Sun?
Approximately 29.8 km per second (about 107,000 km/hour).
Tap to view answer
What is the exact time taken by the Earth to complete one full revolution around the Sun?
365 days 5 hours 48 minutes 46 seconds (~365¼ days).
Tap to view answer
Why does a Leap Year have 366 days?
Because the extra ~6 hours from each year are added together to form one full day every 4 years.
Tap to view answer
What are the two main causes of the change of seasons on Earth?
The Earth's revolution around the Sun and its 23.5° axial tilt.
Tap to view answer
When does the astronomical position of Perihelion occur?
On 3 January (closest distance to the Sun — approximately 147 million km).
Tap to view answer
On which date does the astronomical position of Aphelion occur?
On 4 July (farthest distance from the Sun — approximately 152 million km).
Tap to view answer
How much more solar radiation (in percentage) does the Earth receive at Perihelion compared to Aphelion?
Approximately 7% more solar energy.
Tap to view answer
Towards which star does the Earth's rotational axis currently point?
Towards the Pole Star (Polaris).
Tap to view answer
What is the slow top-like wobble of the Earth's rotational axis called?
Axial Precession.
Tap to view answer
What is the duration of one complete Precession Cycle of the Earth?
Approximately 26,000 years (25,772 years).
Tap to view answer
What is the fundamental cause of the origin of the Coriolis Force?
Rotation of the Earth on its axis.
Tap to view answer
According to Ferrel's Law, in which direction does the Coriolis force deflect winds?
To the right in the Northern Hemisphere and to the left in the Southern Hemisphere.
Tap to view answer
What is the magnitude of the Coriolis force at the Equator and at the Poles?
Zero at the Equator and maximum at the Poles.
Tap to view answer
Why is the Earth flattened at the poles and bulging at the equator instead of being a perfect sphere?
Due to the centrifugal force generated by rotation (forming a Geoid / Oblate Spheroid).
Tap to view answer
What is the difference between the equatorial diameter/radius and polar radius of the Earth?
A difference of approximately 21 kilometres in radius (6,378 km vs. 6,357 km).
Tap to view answer
Why is the value of gravitational acceleration lower at the Equator than at the Poles?
Due to greater distance from the Earth's centre and maximum centrifugal force at the Equator.
Tap to view answer
What would happen if the Earth's axial tilt became zero?
There would be no change of seasons, and day and night would remain equal throughout the year.
Tap to view answer
What is the main reason for 6 months of daylight and 6 months of darkness at the Poles?
The Earth's axial tilt and its revolution around the Sun.
Tap to view answer
What is the imaginary astronomical line joining the positions of Aphelion and Perihelion called?
Line of Apsides (Apside Line).
Tap to view answer
What primarily governs the daily frequency of ocean tides?
The Earth's rotation and the relative position of the Moon.
Tap to view answer
What is the time interval between two successive high tides at a given place?
12 hours 26 minutes (due to the Moon's forward motion in its orbit).
Tap to view answer
What long-term change is occurring in the Earth's rotational speed due to tidal friction?
The rotational speed is gradually slowing down (causing a minute increase in the length of a day).
Tap to view answer
What is the difference between a Sidereal Year and a Tropical (Solar) Year?
A Sidereal Year is measured relative to fixed stars (and is about 20 minutes longer).
Tap to view answer
Why are seasons opposite to each other in the Northern and Southern Hemispheres?
Because due to axial tilt, only one hemisphere tilts towards the Sun at a given time.
Tap to view answer
What would happen if the Earth started rotating in reverse (from East to West) on its axis?
The Sun would rise in the West and the direction of Coriolis deflection would be reversed.
Tap to view answer
What is the orbital plane of the Earth called in astronomy?
Ecliptic Plane.

What is the latitudinal value of the Equator?
0° latitude (the Great Circle dividing the Earth into Northern and Southern Hemispheres).

181 (including 0° and 90° on both sides).
Tap to view answer
What is the total number of latitude lines (circles) drawn on a globe?
179 lines (since the two poles are points, not circles).
Tap to view answer
What is the approximate average ground distance of 1 degree of latitude?
Approximately 111 kilometres (111.13 km).
Tap to view answer
What is the latitudinal value of the Tropic of Cancer?
23.5° North latitude (23°30' N).
Tap to view answer
What is the latitudinal value of the Tropic of Capricorn?
23.5° South latitude (23°30' S).
Tap to view answer
What are the latitudinal values of the Arctic Circle and Antarctic Circle respectively?
66.5° N (Arctic Circle) and 66.5° S (Antarctic Circle).
Tap to view answer
Between which latitudes are the Horse Latitudes located?
Between 30° and 35° latitudes in both hemispheres (Subtropical High-Pressure Belt).

Where does the Doldrums (Equatorial Calm Belt) extend?
Between 5° North and 5° South latitudes on either side of the Equator.

What are Lines of Longitude (Meridians)?
Semi-circular vertical lines connecting the North Pole to the South Pole.

What is the total number of meridians of longitude on Earth?
360 meridians of longitude.

What is the longitudinal value of the Prime Meridian?
0° longitude (the meridian passing through the Royal Observatory at Greenwich, London).
Tap to view answer
What is the distance between 1 degree of longitude at the Equator?
Approximately 111.32 kilometres (decreasing towards the poles to zero).
Tap to view answer
What is the geographical area between two consecutive meridians of longitude called?

Tap to view answer
What is the intersecting network of lines of latitude and longitude called?
Geographic Grid (Graticule).

How much time does the Earth take to rotate through 1° of longitude on its axis?
4 minutes (15° longitude = 1 hour).

How does local time change as one moves eastward from Greenwich?
Time advances (4 minutes are added for each degree of longitude).

How is local time affected as one moves westward from Greenwich?
Time lags behind (4 minutes are subtracted for each degree of longitude).

By how much is Indian Standard Time (IST) ahead of Greenwich Mean Time (GMT)?
5 hours 30 minutes ahead (+5:30).

What is the approximate time difference between the easternmost (Kibithu) and westernmost (Guhar Moti) extremities of India?
Approximately 2 hours (due to a longitudinal extent of about 30°).

To avoid confusion of having two different calendar dates within the same country or island group.

One-Liner Questions

What is the meaning of the astronomical alignment 'Syzygy'?
The alignment of the Sun, the Moon, and the Earth in a straight line.
Tap to view answer
When does the astronomical position of Conjunction occur?
When the Moon lies between the Sun and the Earth (New Moon / Amavasya).
Tap to view answer
When does the astronomical position of Opposition occur?
When the Earth comes between the Sun and the Moon (Full Moon / Purnima).
Tap to view answer
Under what astronomical condition does a Solar Eclipse occur?
When the Moon comes between the Sun and the Earth, blocking sunlight from reaching Earth.
Tap to view answer
On which lunar day can a Solar Eclipse always occur?
Only on a New Moon day (Amavasya).
Tap to view answer
Under what condition does a Lunar Eclipse occur?
When the Earth comes between the Sun and the Moon and casts its shadow on the Moon.
Tap to view answer
On which lunar day does a Lunar Eclipse always occur?
Only on a Full Moon day (Purnima).
Tap to view answer
Why does an eclipse not occur on every New Moon and Full Moon?
Because the Moon's orbital plane is inclined at 5.14° (about 5°) to the Earth's orbital plane.
Tap to view answer
What is the dark, central inner shadow cast during an eclipse called?
Umbra.
Tap to view answer
What is the lighter, partial outer shadow surrounding the Umbra called?
Penumbra.
Tap to view answer
When does an Annular Solar Eclipse occur?
When the Moon is near Apogee (farthest from Earth) and only the bright outer ring of the Sun remains visible.
Tap to view answer
When is the 'Diamond Ring' phenomenon observed?
At the exact moments just before the beginning and just after the end of totality during a Total Solar Eclipse.
Tap to view answer
What is the physical cause of the formation of 'Baily's Beads'?
Sunlight shining through the rugged valleys and craters along the Moon's limb.
Tap to view answer
What can be the maximum possible duration of a Total Solar Eclipse?
Approximately 7 minutes 32 seconds.
Tap to view answer
What can be the maximum duration of a Total Lunar Eclipse?
Approximately 1 hour 47 minutes (because the Earth's shadow is much larger than the Moon).
Tap to view answer
Why does the Moon appear reddish during a 'Blood Moon' (Total Lunar Eclipse)?
Because only long-wavelength red light refracted and scattered through Earth's atmosphere reaches the Moon.
Tap to view answer
What are the minimum and maximum total number of eclipses (solar and lunar combined) in a calendar year?
Minimum 2 and maximum 7 eclipses.

What is the duration of the 'Saros Cycle' governing the repetition of eclipses?
18 years 11 days 8 hours (approximately 6,585.3 days).
