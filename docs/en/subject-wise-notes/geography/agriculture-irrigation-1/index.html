Characteristics of Indian Agriculture, Farming Systems, Cropping Seasons, Major Food & Cash Crops, and Agricultural Revolutions

Important Facts & Contributions
Agriculture Irrigation 1
Agricultural Concept / TypeKey CharacteristicExamples
Kharif Cropping Season
View Answer
Sowing at the onset of the South-West Monsoon (June–July) and harvesting at the beginning of winter (September–October); requires high temperature and high humidity
View Answer
Paddy (Rice), Maize, Jowar, Bajra, Cotton, Jute, Groundnut, Soybean, Arhar (Pigeon Pea), Urad, Moong
Rabi Cropping Season
View Answer
Sowing in winter (October–December) and harvesting in summer (March–April); requires low temperature for germination and dry, sunny weather during ripening
View Answer
Wheat, Barley, Gram (Chickpea), Pea, Mustard, Toria, Linseed, Lentil (Masoor), Potato
Zaid Cropping Season
View Answer
Short-duration dry summer crops grown between Rabi and Kharif from March to June; completely dependent on assured irrigation facilities
View Answer
Watermelon, Muskmelon, Cucumber, Kakdi, Okra (Bhindi), Bitter Gourd, and various seasonal fodder crops
Primitive Subsistence / Jhum Farming
View Answer
Clearing forests by cutting and burning trees and using the ash as fertilizer (Slash and Burn); based on primitive tools and family labour
View Answer
'Jhum' in North-East India, 'Bewar/Dahiya' in Madhya Pradesh, 'Podu/Penda' in Andhra Pradesh
Intensive Subsistence Agriculture
View Answer
Maximizing output on small landholdings in densely populated regions through chemical fertilizers, high-yielding seeds, and intensive irrigation
View Answer
Ganga-Brahmaputra Plains, Krishna-Godavari Delta (rice-dominated intensive farming)
Plantation Agriculture
View Answer
Cultivation of a single cash crop over a large estate with heavy capital investment, scientific management, and direct linkage to processing industries
View Answer
Tea in Assam and West Bengal, Coffee in Karnataka, Rubber and Spice plantations in Kerala
Mixed Farming
View Answer
Integrated operation of crop production alongside animal husbandry (dairy, poultry, sheep and goat rearing); ensures continuity in income and employment while minimizing risk
View Answer
Irrigated agricultural regions of Punjab, Haryana, Western Uttar Pradesh, and Gujarat
Zero Budget Natural Farming (ZBNF)
View Answer
Complete exclusion of chemical fertilizers and pesticides, using bio-formulations based on indigenous cow dung and cow urine
View Answer
Subhash Palekar Model (Bijamrit, Jeevamrit, Acchadan/Mulching, and Waaphasa)

Important Facts & Contributions
Agriculture Irrigation 1
Agricultural Concept / TypeKey CharacteristicExamples
Green Revolution
View Answer
Rapid surge in foodgrain production through a package of High-Yielding Variety (HYV) dwarf seeds, chemical fertilizers, pesticides, and assured canal-tubewell irrigation
View Answer
Record increase in wheat and rice production (Punjab, Haryana, and Western Uttar Pradesh)
White Revolution (Operation Flood)
View Answer
Unprecedented growth in milk production through a three-tier cooperative dairy structure, scientific animal breeding, and cold-chain systems
View Answer
Anand (Amul) Model, launched in 1970 by the National Dairy Development Board (NDDB)
Yellow Revolution
View Answer
Technology mission and expansion of hybrid seeds to achieve self-reliance in the production of edible oils and oilseed crops
View Answer
Self-reliance campaign in Mustard, Soybean, Groundnut, and Sunflower production
Blue Revolution
View Answer
Scientific harnessing of marine and inland water resources for multidimensional growth in fish and aquatic production and exports
View Answer
Inland Aquaculture and shrimp production; Andhra Pradesh and West Bengal are leading states
Golden Revolution
View Answer
Integrated development programme to boost the production of horticulture crops, fruits (especially apples), vegetables, plantation crops, and honey
View Answer
National Horticulture Mission (NHM), Sweet Revolution (beekeeping and honey promotion)
Rainbow & Evergreen Revolution
View Answer
Sustainable and holistic development of agriculture, forestry, animal husbandry, and aquaculture with ecological balance (sustainable livelihood security)
View Answer
Emphasis by Dr. M.S. Swaminathan on sustainable and eco-friendly agricultural technologies
