Structure of the Atmosphere - Part 1 (Atmosphere Structure 1)

Composition of the Atmosphere, Troposphere, Stratosphere, and Structure & Importance of the Ozone Layer

🔍
Quick Reference
Important Facts & Contributions
Atmosphere Structure 1
Component / LayerKey CharacteristicImportance
Nitrogen (N₂ — 78.08%)
View Answer
Most abundant gas by volume in dry air; colourless, odourless, and chemically inert under normal conditions
View Answer
Basis of amino acid and protein synthesis in plants; dilutes oxygen to prevent rapid spontaneous combustion
Oxygen (O₂ — 20.95%)
View Answer
Chemically active life-sustaining gas; most concentrated up to 16 km altitude and negligible above 120 km
View Answer
Essential for cellular respiration in all aerobic organisms and indispensable for oxidation and fuel combustion
Carbon Dioxide (CO₂ — 0.04%)
View Answer
Heaviest major variable gas in the atmosphere; transparent to incoming shortwave solar radiation but opaque to outgoing longwave terrestrial radiation
View Answer
Maintains Earth's average surface temperature (~15°C) via the natural greenhouse effect; primary raw material for plant photosynthesis
Water Vapour
View Answer
Highly variable gas ranging from 0% to 4% by volume; 90% of it is confined within the lowest 5 km of the troposphere
View Answer
Acts as Earth's thermal blanket; sole source of clouds, rainfall, dew, frost, fog, and latent heat driving weather storms
Dust Particles & Aerosols
View Answer
Microscopic solid and liquid particles of sea salt, fine soil, pollen, smoke, soot, and meteoric ash suspended in the air
View Answer
Serve as hygroscopic nuclei for water vapour condensation and cloud formation; cause Rayleigh scattering producing the blue sky and red sunsets
Troposphere
View Answer
Lowest layer averaging 13 km height (8 km at poles, 18 km at the equator); contains ~75–80% of total atmospheric mass
View Answer
Arena of all weather and climatic phenomena (rain, cyclones, thunderstorms); exhibits the Normal Lapse Rate (6.5°C/km)
Stratosphere
View Answer
Extends from the tropopause up to 50 km; devoid of vertical convection, storm clouds, and dust; isothermal in lower part, then warms with altitude
View Answer
Provides calm, turbulence-free horizontal flying conditions ideal for commercial and jet aircraft in its lower zone
Ozonosphere (Ozone Layer)
View Answer
Concentrated between 15 and 35 km in the stratosphere (peak density at 20–25 km); formed via photochemical action of UV rays on oxygen
View Answer
Absorbs lethal solar Ultraviolet-C (UV-C) and Ultraviolet-B (UV-B) radiation, shielding the terrestrial biosphere

Important Facts & Contributions
Atmosphere Structure 1
Component / LayerKey CharacteristicImportance
Tropopause
View Answer
1.5 to 2 km thick transition boundary between the Troposphere and Stratosphere where temperature stops decreasing with height
View Answer
Temperature is ~-80°C over the Equator and ~-45°C over the Poles; acts as a lid capping vertical weather convection
Nacreous Clouds (Mother-of-Pearl)
View Answer
Rare Polar Stratospheric Clouds (PSCs) formed in the lower stratosphere at extremely low temperatures (~-80°C)
View Answer
Provide ice crystal surfaces that catalyze chlorine activation reactions responsible for Antarctic springtime ozone depletion
Stratopause
View Answer
Upper boundary of the Stratosphere at ~50 km altitude where temperature peaks near 0°C (freezing point of water)
View Answer
Thermal transition zone dividing the Stratosphere from the overlying Mesosphere
Ozone Hole
View Answer
Severe springtime (September–October) thinning of stratospheric ozone below 220 Dobson Units (DU) over Antarctica
View Answer
Caused by CFCs, halons, and the Polar Vortex; regulated globally under the 1987 Montreal Protocol and 2016 Kigali Amendment
