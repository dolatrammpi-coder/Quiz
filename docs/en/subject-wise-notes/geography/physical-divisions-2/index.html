Physical Divisions of India - Part 2 (Coastal Plains and Islands) (Physical Divisions 2)

Peninsular Plateau, Coastal Plains and Islands

🔍

One-Liner Questions

What are the geological origin and structural nature of the Peninsular Plateau?
It is a part of ancient Gondwanaland, composed primarily of extremely hard and stable Pre-Cambrian Archaean gneiss, schist, and granite rocks.
Tap to view answer
What are the geographical shape and general slope of the Peninsular Plateau?
It is triangular in shape; the northern part slopes towards the north and north-east (towards the Ganga valley), while the southern part slopes from west to east (towards the Bay of Bengal).
Tap to view answer
What is the average elevation of the Peninsular Plateau of India above sea level?
Approximately 600 to 900 metres.
Tap to view answer
Which physical landforms demarcate the northern boundary of the Peninsular Plateau?
Kutch, the Aravalli Range, the Delhi Ridge, and the Rajmahal Hills in the east.
Tap to view answer
What are the geographical location and geological structure of the Malwa Plateau?
It lies between the Aravalli and Vindhyan mountain ranges and is covered by Cretaceous basaltic lava flows.
Tap to view answer
What is the distinctive landform created by the Chambal River and its tributaries on the Malwa Plateau called?
Ravines or Badland Topography (Utkhat Bhumi), resulting from intense gully erosion.
Tap to view answer
Of which rocks is the Bundelkhand Upland composed and what is its geographical location?
It is composed of ancient 'Bundelkhand Gneiss' (granite) and extends south of the Yamuna River across the border of Uttar Pradesh and Madhya Pradesh.
Tap to view answer
Between which two major river basins does the Baghelkhand Plateau act as a water divide?
Between the Son River basin and the Mahanadi River basin.
Tap to view answer
Why is the Chota Nagpur Plateau called the 'Ruhr of India'?
Due to its extraordinary abundance of vast mineral reserves such as coal, iron ore, bauxite, mica, and copper.
Tap to view answer
Which is the highest mountain peak of the Chota Nagpur Plateau?
Parasnath Peak (1,366 metres), which is also a premier Jain pilgrimage centre (Sammed Shikhar).
Tap to view answer
What are the distinctive 'Patlands' (Pat Bhumi) of the Chota Nagpur Plateau?
High, flat-topped mesa plateaus capped with bauxite and laterite, such as Netarhat Pat and Jamir/Jasidih Pat.
Tap to view answer
Into which two major physical units does the Damodar River rift valley divide the Chota Nagpur Plateau?
The Hazaribagh and Koderma Plateau in the north and the Ranchi Plateau in the south.
Tap to view answer
What type of drainage pattern do the major rivers originating from the Ranchi Plateau form?
Radial Drainage Pattern, wherein rivers like the Subarnarekha and South Koel flow outward in different directions.
Tap to view answer
Geologically, of which structural division is the Meghalaya (Shillong) Plateau an integral part?
An eastern outlier of the Peninsular Plateau itself, rather than the Himalayan mountain chain.
Tap to view answer
By which geological process was the Malda Gap (Garo-Rajmahal Gap) formed?
By crustal subsidence due to down-faulting, which was subsequently filled up by alluvial sediments of the Ganga and Brahmaputra rivers.
Tap to view answer
What is the correct West-to-East geographical sequence of the three major hill ranges of the Meghalaya Plateau?
Garo Hills, Khasi Hills, and Jaintia Hills.
Tap to view answer
Which is the highest peak of the Meghalaya Plateau and in which hill range is it situated?
Shillong Peak (1,961 metres), situated in the central part of the Khasi Hills.
Tap to view answer
Of which geological unit are the Mikir Hills and the Karbi Anglong Plateau in Assam an extension?
The north-eastern extension of the Meghalaya (Shillong) Plateau and the Peninsular Block.
Tap to view answer
In which geological period and by which process was the Deccan Trap formed?
During the late Cretaceous period through quiet fissure basaltic eruptions that deposited lava layer upon layer.
Tap to view answer
Which major soil type has developed from the weathering of basaltic lava rocks in the Deccan Trap?
Black Soil (Regur Soil), renowned for its high moisture-retaining capacity and suitability for cotton cultivation.
Tap to view answer
Into which two major topographic sub-units is the Karnataka Plateau divided?
Malnad (forested hilly region adjoining the Western Ghats) and Maidan (rolling upland plain stretching eastward).
Tap to view answer
Which is the highest peak of the Baba Budan Hills on the Karnataka Plateau?
Mullayanagiri (approximately 1,930 metres), the highest point in Karnataka.
Tap to view answer
Which major rivers divide the Telangana-Rayalaseema plateau region into two natural basins?
The Godavari River (northern basin) and the Krishna River (southern basin).
Tap to view answer
Across which states does the Dandakaranya Plateau extend?
Across contiguous parts of Odisha, Chhattisgarh (Bastar region), Maharashtra, and Andhra Pradesh.
Tap to view answer
For which rare and strategic mineral is the Bastar Plateau of Chhattisgarh famous as India's sole primary producing region?
Tin ore (Cassiterite).

One-Liner Questions

What type of mountain range is the Aravalli Range and in which geological era was it formed?
It is one of the world's oldest fold mountain systems formed in the Pre-Cambrian era, now heavily denuded into a Relict (Residual) Mountain.
Tap to view answer
From where to where does the Aravalli Range extend?
From Palanpur/Himmatnagar in Gujarat through Rajasthan and Haryana up to Delhi (Raisina Hills) over approximately 800 km.
Tap to view answer
Which is the highest peak of the Aravalli Range and where is it located?
Guru Shikhar (1,722 metres), located at Mount Abu in Sirohi district of Rajasthan.
Tap to view answer
What is the geomorphic nature of the Vindhyachal Range and what role does it play?
It is a block-fault scarp ridge that acts as the principal water divide between Northern India and Southern (Peninsular) India.
Tap to view answer
By what names are the eastward extensions of the Vindhyan Range known?
Bharner (Bhander) Range and Kaimur Range.
Tap to view answer
For which resources and building materials are the rocks of the Vindhyan System primarily famous?
Red sandstone, limestone, shale, and the diamond-bearing belts of Panna and Golconda.
Tap to view answer
What is the geomorphic nature of the Satpura Range?
It is a true Block Mountain (Horst) flanked by the Narmada Rift Valley to the north and the Tapti Rift Valley to the south.
Tap to view answer
What is the correct West-to-East sequence of the constituent hills of the Satpura Range?
Rajpipla Hills, Mahadeo Hills, and Maikal Range.
Tap to view answer
Which is the highest peak of the Satpura Range and where is it located?
Dhupgarh (1,350 metres), located near Pachmarhi in the Mahadeo Hills.
Tap to view answer
Which two rivers flowing in opposite directions originate from Amarkantak, the highest peak of the Maikal Range?
The Narmada River (flowing westward) and the Son River (flowing north-eastward).
Tap to view answer
What is the true geological nature of the Western Ghats (Sahyadri)?
They are not true fold mountains, but a steep western fault scarp of the Peninsular Plateau formed by down-faulting along the Arabian Sea.
Tap to view answer
Which line of latitude divides the Sahyadri Range into Northern Sahyadri and Southern Sahyadri?
The 16° North latitude line, which passes through the state of Goa.
Tap to view answer
Which is the highest mountain peak of the Northern Sahyadri?
Kalsubai (1,646 metres), located in the Nashik-Ahmednagar region of Maharashtra.
Tap to view answer
Which two major cities are connected by rail and road through the Thal Ghat (Kasara Ghat) pass?
Mumbai and Nashik (along the Mumbai-Kolkata route).
Tap to view answer
Between which two major urban centres does the Bhor Ghat pass establish connectivity?
Mumbai and Pune (along the Mumbai-Chennai route).
Tap to view answer
Which mountain knot forms the junction point of the Western Ghats and the Eastern Ghats?
Nilgiri Hills.
Tap to view answer
Which is the highest peak of the Nilgiri Hills?
Doddabetta (2,637 metres), which is the second highest peak in South India.
Tap to view answer
Which is the highest mountain peak of South India and the entire Western Ghats?
Anaimudi (2,695 metres), located in the Annamalai Hills in Idukki district of Kerala.
Tap to view answer
Between which two mountain ranges is the Palghat (Palakkad Gap) pass situated?
Between the Nilgiri Hills in the north and the Annamalai Hills in the south (connecting Coimbatore and Kochi).
Tap to view answer
Which two cities does the Shencottah Gap connect, and near which hills is it situated?
Thiruvananthapuram and Madurai; it lies near the southern end of the Cardamom Hills.
Tap to view answer
In which part of India are the Cardamom (Yelagiri/Elaichi) Hills located?
In the extreme south of Peninsular India, along the border of Kerala and Tamil Nadu.
Tap to view answer
In which year were the Western Ghats inscribed as a UNESCO World Natural Heritage Site?
In 2012 (recognized as one of the world's eight 'hottest hotspots' of biological diversity).
Tap to view answer
Why are the Eastern Ghats discontinuous and heavily fragmented compared to the Western Ghats?
Due to extensive valley erosion and dissection by large east-flowing peninsular rivers such as the Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, and Kaveri.
Tap to view answer
Which peak is recognized as the highest peak of the Eastern Ghats?
Jindhagada / Arma Konda (1,690 metres), located in the Araku Valley of Andhra Pradesh.
Tap to view answer
Which is the major historic peak of the Eastern Ghats located in Odisha?
Mahendragiri (1,501 metres), located in Gajapati district of Odisha.
Tap to view answer
In which state are the Nallamala, Palkonda, and Velikonda hills located?
In Andhra Pradesh (near the Krishna and Pennar river valleys).
Tap to view answer
In which state are the Javadi, Shevaroy, and Pachamalai hills located?
In Tamil Nadu (southern section of the Eastern Ghats).
Tap to view answer
In which hill range is the famous hill station 'Kodaikanal' situated?
In the Palani Hills (Tamil Nadu).
Tap to view answer
In which hills is the famous hill station 'Ooty' (Udhagamandalam) located?
In the Nilgiri Hills.
Tap to view answer
What type of natural vegetation is found on the western windward slopes of the Western Ghats?
Tropical Wet Evergreen and Semi-Evergreen Forests (due to heavy rainfall exceeding 250 cm).
Tap to view answer
Why does the eastern slope of the Western Ghats receive scanty rainfall?
Because it falls in the leeward Rain-Shadow Area of the Arabian Sea branch of the South-West Monsoon.
Tap to view answer
Which are the prominent hills of the Kathiawar Peninsula in Gujarat?
Girnar Hills, Mandav Hills, and Barda Hills.
Tap to view answer
In which state do the Ajanta, Balaghat, and Harishchandra mountain ranges lie?
Entirely in Maharashtra (within the Deccan Lava Plateau).
Tap to view answer
In which geological period were the Rajmahal Hills formed and what is their primary rock type?
Formed by volcanic eruptions during the Jurassic period; they are composed primarily of basaltic lava traps.
Tap to view answer
Which hill range radiates southward from the Annamalai mountain knot?
The Cardamom Hills.

One-Liner Questions

What is the fundamental geological difference in the origin of India's Western Coastal Plains and Eastern Coastal Plains?
The Western Coastal Plain is a narrow coast of submergence, whereas the Eastern Coastal Plain is a broad coast of emergence combined with alluvial deltaic deposition.
Tap to view answer
What is the correct North-to-South regional classification of the Western Coastal Plains?
Gujarat Coast (Kutch & Kathiawar), Konkan Coast (Maharashtra-Goa), Kannad/Karnataka Coast, and Malabar Coast (Kerala).
Tap to view answer
From where to where does the Konkan Coastal Plain extend geographically?
From Daman / Tapti estuary in the north to Goa in the south (approximately 500 km).
Tap to view answer
Between which two boundaries does the Kannad (Kanara) Coastal Plain stretch?
From Goa to Mangaluru (Mangalore) in Karnataka.
Tap to view answer
Between which boundaries does the Malabar Coastal Plain extend?
From Mangaluru to Kanyakumari (Cape Comorin), the southern tip of Peninsular India.
Tap to view answer
What are the backwaters or coastal lagoons found along the Malabar Coast locally called?
Kayals.
Tap to view answer
Which is the longest and largest Kayal / backwater lagoon lake in India?
Vembanad Lake (Kerala), which is approximately 96 km long.
Tap to view answer
In which Kayal is Kerala's famous snake-boat race 'Vallamkali' (Nehru Trophy) held?
Punnamada Kayal (a part of Vembanad Lake).
Tap to view answer
In which state is Ashtamudi Lake located and what type of landform is it?
An eight-branched Ramsar wetland lagoon lake located in Kollam district of Kerala.
Tap to view answer
Why do rivers of the Western Coastal Plains form estuaries instead of deltas?
Due to steep gradient, short course over hard rocky terrain, scarcity of silt load, and strong tidal wave action.
Tap to view answer
Which two major west-flowing rivers draining into the Arabian Sea form large estuaries?
The Narmada River and the Tapti River (in the Gulf of Khambhat).
Tap to view answer
What is the correct North-to-South regional classification of the Eastern Coastal Plains?
Utkal Coast (Odisha), Northern Circars Coast (Andhra Pradesh / Godavari-Krishna region), and Coromandel Coast (Tamil Nadu coast).
Tap to view answer
From where to where does the Coromandel Coast extend?
From False Divi Point near the Krishna Delta in Andhra Pradesh southwards to Kanyakumari.
Tap to view answer
Why is the Eastern Coastal Plain much broader and flatter than the Western Coastal Plain?
Due to continuous alluvial deposition and the formation of extensive deltas by large east-flowing peninsular rivers such as the Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, and Kaveri.
Tap to view answer
On which coast is Chilika Lake, India's largest brackish-water lagoon, located?
On the Utkal Coast of Odisha (south of the Mahanadi Delta).
Tap to view answer
What is the geographical location of Kolleru Lake and what type of lake is it?
A natural freshwater wetland lake situated between the deltas of the Godavari and Krishna rivers in Andhra Pradesh.
Tap to view answer
On the border of which two states is Pulicat Lake, a brackish-water lagoon, situated?
On the border of Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu.
Tap to view answer
Which barrier island, housing the Satish Dhawan Space Centre, separates Pulicat Lake from the sea?
Sriharikota Island (separating Pulicat Lake from the Bay of Bengal).
Tap to view answer
In which season and from which monsoon winds does the Coromandel Coast receive its maximum rainfall?
In the winter season (October–December), from the North-East Retreating Monsoon winds that pick up moisture over the Bay of Bengal.
Tap to view answer
Why is the Western Coast better suited for the development of natural harbours than the Eastern Coast?
Because it is a submerged coast with greater natural depth close to the shore, whereas the Eastern Coast is shallow and heavily silted.
Tap to view answer
Why are most ports along the Eastern Coast artificial or dependent on regular dredging?
Because rivers continuously deposit massive volumes of sediment there, making the continental shelf shallow.
Tap to view answer
What are the red sand dunes found along the coastal plains of Tamil Nadu (Tirunelveli and Thoothukudi) locally called?
Teris.

What is the average width of the Western Coastal Plains?
Approximately 50 to 65 km (very narrow).
Tap to view answer
What is the average width of the Eastern Coastal Plains?
Approximately 100 to 130 km (expanding up to 200 km in the Kaveri and Godavari deltas).
Tap to view answer
What type of geomorphic feature is the Rann of Kutch?
A shallow saline marshland formed by tidal inundation and the recession of the sea.
