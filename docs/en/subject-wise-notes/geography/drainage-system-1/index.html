View Answer

View Answer
Originates from Verinag Spring at the foot of the Pir Panjal in Jammu & Kashmir; flows through Wular Lake
View Answer

Chenab River
View Answer
Originates as two headstreams, Chandra and Bhaga, on opposite sides of the Bara-lacha La pass in Lahaul-Spiti, Himachal Pradesh (Chandrabhaga)
View Answer
Ancient name 'Asikni'; largest tributary of the Indus; called Chandrabhaga after confluence at Tandi; Dul Hasti, Baglihar & Salal hydroelectric projects
Ravi River
View Answer
Originates west of the Rohtang Pass in Kangra district of Himachal Pradesh; flows through Kullu and Chamba valleys
View Answer
Ancient name 'Parushni' or 'Iravati'; drains the area between Pir Panjal and Dhauladhar ranges; Ranjit Sagar (Thein) Dam in Punjab is built on it
Beas River
View Answer
Originates from Beas Kund near the Rohtang Pass at an elevation of 4,062 metres; flows through the Kullu Valley
View Answer
Ancient name 'Vipasha'; the only river of the Indus system that flows entirely within India; joins the Sutlej at Harike Pattan (source of the Indira Gandhi Canal)
Sutlej River
View Answer
Rakshastal (Rakas Tal) Lake near Mansarovar in Tibet at an altitude of 4,570 m above sea level
View Answer
Ancient name 'Shutudri'; an antecedent river; enters India through Shipki La pass; Bhakra Dam near Naina Devi forms the Gobind Sagar reservoir

Important Facts and Contributions
Drainage System 1
River / BasinOrigin / CharacteristicKey Facts
Ganga River System (Ganga Basin)
View Answer
Originates as 'Bhagirathi' from the Gangotri Glacier (Gaumukh) in Uttarkashi district of Uttarakhand; total length 2,525 km
View Answer
Named 'Ganga' after joining Alaknanda at Devprayag; enters the plains at Haridwar; largest river basin system in India (~26.2% of total geographical area)
Alaknanda River
View Answer
Originates from the confluence of Satopanth Glacier and Bhagirath Kharak Glacier above Badrinath in Uttarakhand
View Answer
Meets Dhauliganga, Nandakini, Pindar & Mandakini to form the Panch Prayag; the sacred Badrinath Dham is situated on its banks
Yamuna River
View Answer
Yamunotri Glacier (6,316 m) on the western slope of the Bandarpunch range in Uttarakhand
View Answer
Westernmost and longest tributary of the Ganga (1,376 km); joins Ganga at Prayagraj; Chambal, Sindh, Betwa, and Ken are its major right-bank tributaries
Brahmaputra River System
View Answer
Chemayungdung / Angsi Glacier east of Lake Mansarovar in Tibet; total length 2,900 km (916 km in India)
View Answer
Called 'Yarlung Tsangpo' in Tibet and 'Dihang' in Arunachal Pradesh; becomes 'Brahmaputra' in Assam after joining Dibang and Lohit; forms 'Majuli', the world's largest river island
Antecedent Rivers
View Answer
Rivers that existed prior to the uplift of the Himalayas and maintained their courses by downcutting their valleys at pace with the mountain uplift
View Answer
Indus, Sutlej, Brahmaputra, Kali (Sharda), Gandak, Kosi, Teesta; cut across the Himalayas to form deep I-shaped gorges (canyons)

What is the geological definition of an 'Antecedent Drainage System'?
Rivers that originated prior to the tectonic uplift of the landmass across which they flow and maintained their original course throughout the uplift

How did antecedent rivers succeed in maintaining their original courses despite the uplift of the Himalayas?
