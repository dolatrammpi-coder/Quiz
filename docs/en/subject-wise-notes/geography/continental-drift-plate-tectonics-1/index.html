Continental Drift and Plate Tectonics - Part 1 (Continental Drift Plate Tectonics 1)

Alfred Wegener's Continental Drift Theory, Division of Pangaea & Panthalassa, and Geological & Paleontological Evidence in Support of Drift

🔍
Quick Reference
Important Facts & Contributions
Continental Drift Plate Tectonics 1
Concept / EvidenceKey CharacteristicExamples
Pangaea
View Answer
A single unified supercontinent ('All Earth') comprising all continental landmasses during the Carboniferous Period
View Answer
Subsequently split into a northern block 'Laurasia' (Angaraland) and a southern block 'Gondwanaland'
Panthalassa
View Answer
The vast universal primordial ocean ('All Water') surrounding the supercontinent Pangaea on all sides
View Answer
The modern Pacific Ocean is considered the contracted remnant of the ancient Panthalassa Ocean
Tethys Geosyncline (Tethys Sea)
View Answer
A shallow, narrow east-west elongated sea formed between Laurasia and Gondwanaland upon the splitting of Pangaea
View Answer
Compression and folding of Tethys marine sediments subsequently gave rise to the Himalayas, Alps, and Zagros mountains
Polflucht Force (Polar Fleeing Force)
View Answer
Centrifugal force generated by Earth's rotation, which Wegener considered responsible for equatorward drift
View Answer
Northward / equatorward movement of continental blocks away from the poles
Tidal Force
View Answer
Marine tidal friction and gravitational pull exerted by the Sun and the Moon
View Answer
Westward drift of North and South America and the opening of the Atlantic Ocean in between
Floating of Sial over Sima
View Answer
Frictionless floating of lighter silica-aluminium continental crust (Sial) over heavier basaltic oceanic substratum (Sima)
View Answer
Wegener's core geological assumption, which was later rejected by geophysicists

Important Facts & Contributions
Continental Drift Plate Tectonics 1
Concept / EvidenceKey CharacteristicExamples
Jig-Saw Fit (Shoreline Symmetry)
View Answer
Remarkable puzzle-like fit of the opposing coastlines of the Atlantic Ocean (South America and West Africa)
View Answer
Proven with precision in 1965 by Sir Edward Bullard using computer modelling at the 500-fathom (~1,000 m) contour
Trans-Oceanic Rock Structure
View Answer
Continuous extension of rock ages, geological provinces, and ancient mountain belts across continents on both sides of the Atlantic
View Answer
2-billion-year-old rock belt of the Brazilian coast matching West Africa (Ghana); Appalachian and Caledonian mountains
Tillite (Glacial Deposits)
View Answer
Unsorted boulder- and clay-rich sedimentary rocks deposited by glaciers confirming ancient glaciation
View Answer
Correlation of India's Talchir Tillite with tillites of South Africa (Dwyka), Brazil (Itararé), Australia, and Antarctica
Placer Deposits
View Answer
Rich alluvial accumulations of precious minerals in coastal sands whose source mother rocks lie across the ocean
View Answer
Rich gold placer deposits along the Ghana coast whose gold-bearing quartz veins lie in the Brazilian Plateau
Glossopteris Flora Fossils
View Answer
Distinctive tongue-shaped leaf fern plant that grew in the cold temperate climate of the Permo-Carboniferous period
View Answer
Abundant fossils found across Gondwana rocks of India, South Africa, Australia, Antarctica, Madagascar, and South America
Mesosaurus Fossils
View Answer
Small bony reptile inhabiting shallow fresh and brackish water, incapable of swimming across the 5,000 km wide salty Atlantic
View Answer
Fossils restricted to only two formations worldwide: the 'Whitehill Shale' of South Africa and 'Irati Shale' of Brazil

One-Liner Questions

Which scholar propounded the Continental Drift Theory in 1912?
Alfred Wegener

Which country did Alfred Wegener originally belong to and what was his primary field of specialization?
German Meteorologist and Climatologist
Tap to view answer
In which historic book published in 1915 did Wegener present a detailed account of his famous theory?
'The Origin of Continents and Oceans' (Die Entstehung der Kontinente und Ozeane)
Tap to view answer
What was Wegener's primary scientific objective in proposing the Continental Drift Theory?
To explain past climatic changes and paleoclimatic anomalies observed across different continents
Tap to view answer
What was the main paleoclimatic puzzle before Wegener that inspired him to conceive continental drift?
Evidence of ancient glaciation in tropical peninsular India, Africa, and Brazil, alongside coal seams in cold regions
Tap to view answer
Which two alternative explanations were available to Wegener to resolve ancient climatic anomalies?
1. Either the climatic zones themselves shifted, or 2. The continental landmasses drifted from one place to another
Tap to view answer
Why did Wegener accept the option that climatic zones remained stationary while continents drifted?
Because such a massive global shift in the Earth's axial tilt and polar position was physically impossible
Tap to view answer
Prior to Wegener, which French scientist drew the first map showing the joining and splitting of continents in 1858?
Antonio Snider-Pellegrini
Tap to view answer
Who proposed the hypothesis of horizontal continental drift in 1908 to explain the origin of folded mountains in North America?
F.B. Taylor
Tap to view answer
On explaining which geological problem was Taylor's drift hypothesis primarily focused?
Explaining the origin of Tertiary fold mountains (Rockies, Andes, Himalayas, Alps)
Tap to view answer
According to Wegener, by what name was the single landmass formed by all continents joined together during the Carboniferous Period known?
Pangaea

What is the literal meaning of the Greek word 'Pangaea'?
All Earth (Entire Landmass)

What name did Wegener give to the vast global ocean surrounding the supercontinent Pangaea?
Panthalassa

What is the literal meaning of the Greek word 'Panthalassa'?
All Water

The modern Pacific Ocean is geologically considered the remnant of which ancient water body?
Panthalassa

According to Wegener, in which geological era did the breakup of Pangaea begin?
At the beginning of the Mesozoic Era (Triassic Period, approximately 200 million years ago)

Which two giant continental blocks came into existence from the initial splitting of Pangaea?
The northern block 'Laurasia' (Angaraland) and the southern block 'Gondwanaland'

North America, Greenland, and Eurasia (excluding the Indian subcontinent)

Which present-day continents and landmasses were included under Gondwanaland?
South America, Africa, Madagascar, Peninsular India, Australia, and Antarctica

After which ancient tribe and historical region of central India was 'Gondwanaland' named?
Named after the Gond tribe and their historical Gondwana region (by Eduard Suess)

Which narrow and shallow sea came into existence between Laurasia and Gondwanaland when they separated?
Tethys Sea (Tethys Geosyncline)

The present-day Mediterranean Sea is considered the contracted remnant of which ancient oceanic waterway?
Tethys Sea

According to Wegener, in which two main directions did the drift of continents take place?
1. Towards the Equator (Northward) and 2. Towards the West (Atlanticward)
Tap to view answer
Which two ancient landmasses moved closer together due to the equatorward (northward) drift of continents?
Gondwanaland and Laurasia (which compressed the sediments of the Tethys Sea)
Tap to view answer
Which ocean was formed as a result of the westward drift of North and South America?
Atlantic Ocean
Tap to view answer
Which primary force did Wegener hold responsible for the equatorward drift of continents?
Polflucht Force (Polar Fleeing Force / Centrifugal Force) and gravitational buoyancy
Tap to view answer
Due to which property of the Earth does the Polflucht Force originate?
Due to the Earth's rotation on its axis and its equatorial bulge
Tap to view answer
Which external astronomical force did Wegener hold responsible for the westward drift of continents?
Tidal Force of the Sun and the Moon
Tap to view answer
How does Tidal Friction push the Earth's continents towards the west according to Wegener?
While the Earth rotates from west to east, the tidal bulge drags the continents backward (towards the west)
Tap to view answer
According to Wegener, what caused the origin of the Rockies and Andes fold mountains?
Crumpling and folding of the western margins of the Americas due to resistance offered by the Pacific Sima during westward drift
Tap to view answer
According to Wegener, how were the Himalayas, Alps, and Atlas mountains formed?
By folding of the Tethys Sea sediments due to the northward drift of Africa and India towards Eurasia

What is the striking complementary similarity of opposing continental coastlines called?
Jig-Saw Fit (Shoreline Symmetry)

Between the coastlines of which two continents is the most striking and famous example of a Jig-Saw Fit observed?
The eastern bulge of South America (Brazil) and the western indentation of Africa (Gulf of Guinea)
Tap to view answer
With the western coast of which continent do the eastern coast of North America and Greenland fit accurately?
With the coast of Western Europe (British Isles and Scandinavia)

Which scientist presented the most accurate computer-assisted Jig-Saw Fit of continental margins in 1965?
Sir Edward Bullard
Tap to view answer
At what depth below sea level did Bullard match the continents instead of using the present-day shoreline?
At the 500-fathom contour (approximately 914 metres or 3,000 feet / ~1,000-metre isobath)
Tap to view answer
Why did Bullard choose the 500-fathom depth as the true boundary rather than the present coastline?
Because present coastlines change due to marine erosion and deposition, whereas 500 fathoms marks the true edge of the continental shelf
Tap to view answer
With which African coast does the 2-billion-year-old ancient rock belt of coastal Brazil match geologically?
With the coast of Ghana, Ivory Coast, and Nigeria in West Africa
Tap to view answer
With which mountain ranges of Europe does the Appalachian mountain system of North America show continuity in geological age and structure?
With the Caledonian mountain ranges of Ireland, Scotland (British Isles), and Norway

What are unsorted sedimentary rocks rich in boulders, pebbles, and clay deposited directly by glaciers called?

Which tillite formation found in Brazil (South America) correlates with the tillites of India and Africa?

What are alluvial concentrations of heavy precious minerals mixed with coastal sand and gravel called?

Placer deposits of Gold

What was the geological anomaly regarding the coastal gold placer deposits of Ghana that proved continental drift?
Gold is found in alluvial sands in Ghana, but there is no parent gold-bearing rock (Mother Lode) anywhere in Ghana

Where are the original gold-bearing quartz veins that supplied Ghana's placer gold actually located?
Across the ocean in the Brazilian Plateau of South America

Glossopteris Flora

Because this small shallow-water reptile could not have swum across the 5,000 km wide deep salty Atlantic Ocean

What was Wegener's most controversial geological assumption that was sharply criticized by physicists?
The assumption that Sial (continents) ploughs through Sima (ocean floor) without deformation
