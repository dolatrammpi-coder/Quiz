World Climatic Classification - Part 1 (World Climate 1)

Köppen's Climatic Classification, Equatorial, Monsoon, Savanna, and Desert Climates

🔍

One-Liner Questions

In which year did Wladimir Köppen present the first version of his famous World Climatic Classification?
In the year 1900 (with major revised versions in 1918 and 1936).
Tap to view answer
On which scientific methodology is Köppen's climatic classification based?
Empirical methodology (based on observed quantitative data).
Tap to view answer
Which two statistical climatic variables did Köppen use as the primary basis of his classification?
Mean monthly and annual values of Temperature and Precipitation.
Tap to view answer
On which botanist's 1874 vegetation physiological classification was Köppen's climatic scheme primarily modelled?
Alphonse de Candolle's vegetation groups.
Tap to view answer
Into how many major primary groups (capital letters) did Köppen divide world climates?
Into 5 major groups (A, B, C, D, E), with Highland climate (H) added later.
Tap to view answer
What does the capital letter 'A' represent in Köppen's classification?
Tropical Humid Climates (where the mean temperature of the coldest month is 18°C or higher).
Tap to view answer
What type of climate does the capital letter 'B' represent in Köppen's classification?
Dry Climates (where potential evaporation and transpiration exceed annual precipitation).
Tap to view answer
What climatic group does the capital letter 'C' symbolize in Köppen's system?
Warm Temperate (Mesothermal) Mid-Latitude Climates (coldest month average between -3°C and 18°C).
Tap to view answer
What does the capital letter 'D' indicate in Köppen's classification?
Cold Snow-Forest (Microthermal / Continental) Climates (coldest month below -3°C and warmest month above 10°C).
Tap to view answer
Which climatic group does the capital letter 'E' represent in Köppen's scheme?
Polar Climates (where the mean temperature of even the warmest month is below 10°C).
Tap to view answer
For which climate is the capital letter 'H' used in modified Köppen–Trewartha classifications?
Highland (Mountain) Climate governed by altitude.
Tap to view answer
Among Köppen's 5 main climate groups, which is the only group defined by precipitation/aridity criteria rather than temperature alone?
Group 'B' (Dry Climates).
Tap to view answer
What does the lowercase letter 'f' signify in Köppen's system?
No dry season — moist throughout the year (from German 'feucht').
Tap to view answer
What does the lowercase letter 'm' signify in Köppen's classification?
Monsoon climate (heavy seasonal rainfall compensating for a short dry season).
Tap to view answer
What does the lowercase letter 'w' mean in Köppen's scheme?
Winter dry season (with rainfall concentrated in summer).
Tap to view answer
What does the lowercase letter 's' indicate in Köppen's classification?
Summer dry season (with rainfall concentrated in winter).
Tap to view answer
In the subdivision of Dry Climates (B), what does the second capital letter 'W' stand for?
True Desert / Arid climate (from German 'Wüste').
Tap to view answer
In Dry Climates (B), what does the second capital letter 'S' stand for?
Semi-arid or Steppe grassland climate.
Tap to view answer
What does the third lowercase letter 'h' signify in dry climates?
Hot and dry (mean annual temperature 18°C or higher — from German 'heiss').
Tap to view answer
What does the third lowercase letter 'k' signify in dry climates?
Cold and dry (mean annual temperature below 18°C — from German 'kalt').
Tap to view answer
Under Polar Climates (E), what does the code 'ET' stand for?
Tundra Climate (mean temperature of the warmest month between 0°C and 10°C).
Tap to view answer
Under Polar Climates, what condition does the code 'EF' represent?
Perpetual Frost / Ice Cap Climate (mean temperature of all months below 0°C).
Tap to view answer
According to Köppen, which climate is represented by the code 'Af'?
Tropical Wet / Equatorial Rainforest Climate.
Tap to view answer
Which climate is symbolized by the code 'Am' in Köppen's system?
Tropical Monsoon Climate.
Tap to view answer
What climate does the code 'Aw' represent in Köppen's classification?
Tropical Wet and Dry / Savanna (Sudan-type) Climate.
Tap to view answer
What type of climate is indicated by the code 'BWh'?
Subtropical / Tropical Hot Desert Climate (such as the Sahara and Thar deserts).
Tap to view answer
Which climate does the code 'BWk' represent?
Mid-Latitude Cold Desert Climate (such as the Gobi and Ladakh deserts).
Tap to view answer
What type of climate does the code 'BSh' represent?
Tropical / Subtropical Low-Latitude Semi-Arid Steppe Climate.
Tap to view answer
According to Köppen, which famous climate is represented by the code 'Cs' (Csa/Csb)?
Mediterranean Climate (warm/hot dry summer and mild wet winter).
Tap to view answer
What does the code 'Cwg' (or 'Cwa') represent in Köppen's classification of India?
Subtropical Monsoon Climate with dry winter (characteristic of the Gangetic Plains).
Tap to view answer
According to Köppen, which climate is represented by the code 'Cfa'?
Humid Subtropical Climate (China-type climate — no dry season, hot summer).
Tap to view answer
What type of climate does the code 'Cfb' indicate?
Marine West Coast Climate (Western European type — mild year-round wet climate).
Tap to view answer
Which major forest belt's climate is represented by the codes 'Dfc' and 'Dfd'?
Subarctic Taiga or Boreal Coniferous Forest Climate.
Tap to view answer
What is the main scientific criticism of Köppen's climatic classification?
It is purely empirical and descriptive, ignoring genetic causes of climate such as air masses, fronts, jet streams, and pressure dynamics.
Tap to view answer
What is the primary reason for the enduring global popularity and pedagogical use of Köppen's classification?
Its simple shorthand letter-code system and direct visual correlation with natural vegetation belts.

One-Liner Questions

Between which latitudes does the Equatorial Climate (Af) primarily extend?
Between 5° and 10° North and South latitudes on both sides of the Equator.
Tap to view answer
What is the mean annual temperature in the Equatorial climatic region?
Approximately 27°C (uniformly high warmth throughout the year).
Tap to view answer
How large is the Annual Range of Temperature in the Equatorial region?
Extremely small (only about 2°C to 3°C).
Tap to view answer
Regarding which climatic region is it famously said that 'Night is the winter of the tropics'?
Equatorial Climate Region (because the diurnal temperature range of 6°–8°C exceeds the annual temperature range of 2°–3°C).
Tap to view answer
What type of daily rainfall is characteristic of the Equatorial belt?
Heavy afternoon Convectional Rainfall.
Tap to view answer
By what popular name is the daily torrential afternoon thundershower in the Equatorial belt known?
'4 O'Clock Rain'.
Tap to view answer
What is the average annual rainfall in the Equatorial climatic region?
More than 200 cm to 250 cm (with no dry month in the year).
Tap to view answer
By what local name are the dense equatorial evergreen rainforests of the Amazon Basin known?
Selvas.
Tap to view answer
By what ecological nickname are the Amazon Tropical Rainforests known due to their massive carbon cycling and oxygen output?
'Lungs of the Planet'.
Tap to view answer
What is the most prominent structural feature of trees in Equatorial Rainforests?
A dense, stratified Multi-layered Canopy (with emergent trees reaching 45–60 metres) blocking sunlight from reaching the forest floor.
Tap to view answer
What are the thick, woody climbing vines that twine up tall tree trunks in equatorial rainforests to reach sunlight called?
Lianas.
Tap to view answer
What are plants that grow non-parasitically perched on the branches of tall rainforest trees without soil contact called?
Epiphytes (Air Plants, such as orchids, bromeliads, and ferns).
Tap to view answer
Which are five major commercially valuable hardwood and latex-bearing trees of Equatorial Rainforests?
Mahogany, Ebony, Rosewood, Rubber (Hevea brasiliensis), and Cinchona.
Tap to view answer
From the bark of which equatorial tree is the anti-malarial drug 'Quinine' obtained?
From the bark of the Cinchona tree.
Tap to view answer
Despite immense timber volume, why is commercial lumbering difficult in Equatorial Rainforests?
Because trees do not occur in pure stands (high species heterogeneity), logs of heavy hardwoods sink in rivers, and the ground is swampy and inaccessible.
Tap to view answer
What type of infertile, heavily leached soil develops under the hot, wet conditions of the Equatorial climate?
Laterite (Oxisol / Latosol) soil (due to intense leaching of silica and soluble nutrients).
Tap to view answer
Which indigenous hunter-gatherer tribe inhabits the dense equatorial rainforests of the Congo Basin (Ituri Forest)?
Pygmies (Mbuti / Bambuti).
Tap to view answer
By what names are the indigenous forest tribes of the Amazon Basin known?
Amerindians / Amazonian Tribes (such as the Yanomami and Kayapo).
Tap to view answer
Which traditional nomadic tribes inhabit the equatorial rainforests of the Malay Peninsula?
Semang and Sakai (Orang Asli).
Tap to view answer
Between which latitudes is the Tropical Monsoon Climate (Am) generally found?
Between 10° and 30° (or 5° and 25°) latitudes in both hemispheres.
Tap to view answer
What is the most fundamental and defining feature of the Tropical Monsoon Climate?
Complete seasonal reversal (by ~180°) in the direction of prevailing winds.
Tap to view answer
What is the basic physical mechanism driving monsoon wind circulation?
Seasonal differential heating and cooling of vast continental landmasses (Asia) and adjacent oceans (Indian Ocean), coupled with ITCZ migration.
Tap to view answer
Into which three distinct seasons does the Tropical Monsoon Climate divide the year?
Hot Dry Summer, Hot Wet Rainy (Monsoon) Season, and Cool Dry Winter.
Tap to view answer
By which monsoon branch is the bulk of annual rainfall delivered in South and South-East Asian monsoon regions?
By the South-West Summer Monsoon (from June to September).
Tap to view answer
Which place records the highest average annual rainfall in the world, and in which Indian state is it located?
Mawsynram (Meghalaya, India — receiving approximately 1,187 cm of annual rainfall).
Tap to view answer
What is the main topographic reason for the world-record orographic rainfall at Mawsynram and Cherrapunji?
The funnel-shaped alignment of the Garo, Khasi, and Jaintia hills trapping moist Bay of Bengal monsoon winds.
Tap to view answer
What is the natural vegetation characteristic of Tropical Monsoon Climate regions?
Tropical Deciduous Forests (Monsoon Forests).
Tap to view answer
Why do trees in Tropical Monsoon Forests shed their leaves at the onset of the dry spring/summer season?
To conserve moisture by preventing water loss through leaf transpiration during the dry season.
Tap to view answer
Which are the most commercially valuable timber trees of Tropical Monsoon Deciduous Forests?
Teak, Sal, Shisham (Rosewood), Sandalwood, Mahua, and Bamboo.
Tap to view answer
Which country's monsoon forests are world-famous for producing the finest natural 'Teak' timber?
Myanmar (Burma).

Which is the principal staple foodgrain crop supporting dense populations in Tropical Monsoon regions?
Paddy / Wet Rice (the basis of intensive subsistence agriculture).
Tap to view answer
Why is the Indian agricultural economy traditionally described as a 'Gamble in the Monsoon'?
Because crop yields and water resources depend heavily on the erratic arrival, spatial distribution, and withdrawal of monsoon rains.
Tap to view answer
In the monsoon regime, which wind system brings winter rainfall (October–December) to the Coromandel Coast of Tamil Nadu?
The North-East Monsoon (Retreating Monsoon picking up moisture over the Bay of Bengal).
Tap to view answer
Which part of the Australian continent experiences a Tropical Monsoon Climate?
Northern Australia (Arnhem Land, Darwin, and Cape York Peninsula).
Tap to view answer
What is the primary climatic distinction between the Equatorial Climate (Af) and the Tropical Monsoon Climate (Am)?
Equatorial climate receives heavy rain throughout all 12 months, whereas Monsoon climate has a concentrated rainy season and a distinct dry season.

Between which latitudes is the Tropical Savanna Climate (Aw) generally distributed?
Between 10° and 20° latitudes on either side of the Equator (transitional between equatorial forests and hot deserts).
Tap to view answer
Because the Savanna climate is most typically and extensively developed in Sudan, by what other name is it known?
Sudan-Type Climate.
Tap to view answer
What is the main temperature and rainfall regime of the Savanna Climate?
Alternation of a hot, wet summer rainy season (when ITCZ shifts poleward) and a warm, distinctly dry winter season (under dry Trade Winds).
Tap to view answer
What is the average annual rainfall in the Tropical Savanna climatic region?
Approximately 75 cm to 150 cm (decreasing as one moves toward the desert margins).
Tap to view answer
What is the characteristic natural vegetation of the Savanna climatic region?
Tall, coarse tropical grasses interspersed with scattered, umbrella-shaped drought-resistant deciduous trees.

What is the tall, coarse perennial grass growing up to 3 to 4 metres high in the humid African Savanna called?

By what landscape term is the open grassland dotted with scattered clumps of trees in the Savanna known?

Which iconic Savanna trees are adapted to seasonal drought through thick water-storing trunks or flat umbrella crowns?

Why are hot tropical deserts located predominantly on the western margins of continents?
