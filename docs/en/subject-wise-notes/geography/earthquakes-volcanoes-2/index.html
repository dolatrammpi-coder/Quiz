Types of Volcanoes (Active, Dormant, Extinct), Intrusive & Extrusive Landforms, and Volcanoes of India (Barren & Narcondam)

Volcanic Type / LandformCharacteristicExamples / Regions
Active Volcano
View Answer
Volcanoes that currently or in historical times frequently erupt lava, gases, ash, and pyroclastic materials
View Answer
Kilauea & Mauna Loa (Hawaii), Mount Etna & Stromboli (Italy), Cotopaxi (Ecuador), Mount Merapi (Indonesia), Barren Island (India)
Dormant Volcano
View Answer
Volcanoes that are currently quiet but retain full potential to erupt suddenly and violently in the future
View Answer
Mount Vesuvius (Italy), Mount Fujiyama (Japan), Krakatoa (Indonesia), Narcondam Island (Andaman, India)
Extinct Volcano
View Answer
Volcanoes with no eruption in historical times, whose vent is plugged by solidified lava and have zero probability of future eruption
View Answer
Mount Popa (Myanmar), Kilimanjaro (Tanzania), Chimborazo (Ecuador), Aconcagua (Andes), Damavand & Koh-i-Sultan (Iran)
Shield Volcano
View Answer
Broad, gently sloping dome-shaped volcano built by slow, quiet effusions of highly fluid basaltic lava (largest volcanoes on Earth)
View Answer
Hawaiian volcanoes (Mauna Loa & Kilauea); total height from the ocean floor exceeds 9,000 metres
Composite / Stratovolcano
View Answer
Tall, steep-sided conical mountains built of alternating layers of viscous acidic lava and pyroclastic debris (ash and tephra)
View Answer
Mount Mayon (Philippines — most symmetrical cone), Mount Fuji (Japan), Mount Rainier & Mount St. Helens (USA)
Crater & Caldera
View Answer
Crater: funnel-shaped depression at the volcanic vent; Caldera: giant cauldron formed by violent explosion or collapse of an emptied magma chamber
View Answer
Crater Lake (Oregon, USA), Toba Caldera (Sumatra, Indonesia), Yellowstone Caldera (USA), Lonar Crater (Maharashtra)

Important Facts & Contributions
Earthquakes Volcanoes 2
Volcanic Type / LandformCharacteristicExamples / Regions
Batholith
View Answer
Massive, irregular, dome-shaped deep-seated plutonic igneous bodies formed by slow cooling of magma (spanning hundreds of km)
View Answer
Granitic cores of mountain ranges; Idaho Batholith (USA), Ranchi Plateau (Chota Nagpur Granite Batholith)
Laccolith & Lopolith
View Answer
Laccolith: dome/mushroom-shaped magma intrusion beneatharched sedimentary strata; Lopolith: saucer-shaped concave intrusion
View Answer
Henry Mountains (Utah, USA); Bushveld Igneous Complex in South Africa (Lopolith — rich in platinum and chromium)
Sill & Dike
View Answer
Sill: horizontal sheet-like intrusion parallel to sedimentary bedding planes; Dike: vertical wall-like intrusion cutting across strata
View Answer
Great Whin Sill (Britain); extensive dike swarms in the Damodar Coal Valley and Deccan Trap region of Western Maharashtra
Barren Island
View Answer
Located 138 km north-east of Port Blair in the Andaman Sea; the only active volcano in India and South Asia
View Answer
First recorded eruption in 1787; reactivated in 1991, 2005–06, and 2017–2022; part of the Andaman–Sunda subduction arc
Narcondam Island
View Answer
Isolated volcanic island located north-east of Barren Island in the Andaman Sea; currently classified as a dormant volcano
View Answer
Sole natural habitat of the rare, endemic 'Narcondam Hornbill'; secondary volcanic cone
Deccan Traps (Deccan Basalt Plateau)
View Answer
Stepped layers of highly fluid flood basalt lava erupted through fissures at the end of the Cretaceous Period (~65 million years ago)
View Answer
Covers ~500,000 sq km across Maharashtra, Gujarat, and Madhya Pradesh; product of the Réunion Hotspot; parent rock of Black Cotton Soil (Regur)
