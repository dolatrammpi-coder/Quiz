Natural Vegetation of India: Evergreen, Deciduous and Thorn Forests (Natural Vegetation 1)

Forest Types in India (Evergreen, Deciduous, Thorn), Mangrove Forests, Montane Forests (Sholas) and Geographical Distribution of Forests

🔍
Quick Reference
Important Facts and Contributions
Natural Vegetation 1
Vegetation TypeClimatic ConditionsKey Characteristics
Tropical Wet Evergreen Forests
View Answer
Annual rainfall >200 cm; mean annual temperature >22°C; high relative humidity (>70%) year-round; very brief dry season
View Answer
Western slope of the Western Ghats, hills of North-East India & Andaman-Nicobar; trees exceed 60 m in height; multi-layered stratified canopy; Rosewood, Mahogany, Ebony, Cinchona & Bamboo
Tropical Semi-Evergreen Forests
View Answer
Annual rainfall 200 to 250 cm; transitional zone between wet evergreen and moist deciduous forests
View Answer
Mixture of evergreen and moist deciduous tree species; less dense canopy than true evergreen forests; White Cedar, Hollock, Kail, Kusum & Mango
Tropical Moist Deciduous Forests
View Answer
Annual rainfall 100 to 200 cm; moderate temperature; dry spell of 6 to 8 weeks in early summer
View Answer
Monsoon forests of India; trees shed leaves in summer to conserve moisture; commercially most valuable timber forests of India; Teak, Sal, Shisham, Sandalwood, Mahua, Amla & Mulberry
Tropical Dry Deciduous Forests
View Answer
Annual rainfall 70 to 100 cm; prolonged dry season; high summer temperatures
View Answer
Most extensive forest type in India by area; leafless landscape during the dry season; open parkland structure; Tendu, Palash (Dhak), Amaltas, Bel, Khair, Axlewood & Babool
Tropical Thorn Forests and Scrubs
View Answer
Annual rainfall <70 cm (mostly <50 cm); high rate of evaporation; low humidity
View Answer
Rajasthan, Gujarat, MP & interior Deccan rain-shadow belt; leaves reduced to small sizes or modified into spines; deep taproots & succulent stems; Khejri (Shami), Babool (Acacia), Ber, Date Palm & Cactus (Opuntia)
Montane Forests (Himalayan Zonations)
View Answer
Sharp drop in temperature with increasing altitude (transitioning from subtropical to alpine tundra)
View Answer
1000–2000 m: Wet Temperate Broadleaf (Oak, Chestnut); 1500–3000 m: Coniferous Forests (Chir Pine, Deodar, Silver Fir, Spruce); 3000–4000 m: Alpine Vegetation (Juniper, Birch/Bhojpatra, Rhododendron/Buransh)
Southern Montane Forests (Shola Forests)
View Answer
Nilgiri, Annamalai & Palani hills (altitude >1500 m); temperate climate within tropical latitudes
View Answer
Relict wet temperate patches sheltered in mountain folds and valleys; stunted trees, dense branching & moss-lichen cover; Magnolia, Laurel, Cinchona & Rhododendron surrounded by rolling grasslands
Mangrove Forests (Tidal / Littoral Forests)
View Answer
Coastal marshes influenced by marine tides; mixing of saline and fresh water; oxygen-deficient muddy soil
View Answer
Halophytic adaptations; pneumatophores (respiratory roots) & stilt/prop roots; vivipary germination; Sundarbans (Sundari tree), Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna deltas & Andaman; natural bio-shield against tsunamis and cyclones

Important Facts and Contributions
Natural Vegetation 1
Vegetation TypeClimatic ConditionsKey Characteristics
Subtropical Chir Pine Forests
View Answer
Altitudes of 1000 to 1800 metres in the Himalayas; moderate rainfall and winter frost conditions
View Answer
Nearly pure stands of Chir Pine (Pinus roxburghii); primary source of resin (rosin) and turpentine oil; dry pine needles (Pirul) on hill slopes pose high forest-fire risk
Moist Temperate Coniferous Forests
View Answer
Western and Central Himalayas (1800 to 3000 metres); heavy winter snowfall
View Answer
Deodar (Cedrus deodara — finest durable timber), Silver Fir, Blue Pine (Kail) & Spruce; tall straight softwood trees used for railway sleepers and construction
Alpine Pastures (Bugyals and Margs)
View Answer
Above the Himalayan tree line (3300–3600 m) up to the permanent snow line; snow melts during summer
View Answer
Called 'Bugyals / Payars' in Uttarakhand and 'Margs' (Gulmarg, Sonamarg) in Kashmir; primary summer grazing grounds supporting seasonal transhumance of Gujjar,akarwal, Gaddi & Bhotia pastoralists
Littoral and Freshwater Swamp Vegetation
View Answer
Deltaic freshwater marshes and lagoon margins; high water saturation
View Answer
Casuarina (Jhau), Screw Pine (Kewda/Pandanus), Cane, Palms, Coconut & Reed grasses; stabilize coastal sand dunes and protect shorelines from cyclonic winds
