Continental Drift and Plate Tectonics - Part 2 (Continental Drift Plate Tectonics 2)

Plate Tectonics Theory, Types of Plate Boundaries (Convergent, Divergent, Transform), and Sea Floor Spreading

🔍
Quick Reference
Important Facts & Contributions
Continental Drift Plate Tectonics 2
Plate BoundaryMotion / ProcessMajor Landforms
Divergent Boundary (Oceanic)
View Answer
Separation of two plates in opposite directions; continuous upwelling and solidification of magma from the asthenosphere (Constructive Boundary)
View Answer
Mid-oceanic ridges (e.g., Mid-Atlantic Ridge), axial rift valleys, and creation of new basaltic oceanic crust
Divergent Boundary (Continental)
View Answer
Rifting, faulting, and horizontal extension within a continental landmass due to tensional stress
View Answer
Great East African Rift Valley, Red Sea (proto-ocean), and rift lakes (Tanganyika, Malawi)
Convergent Boundary (Oceanic–Continental)
View Answer
Subduction of a denser, heavier oceanic plate beneath a relatively buoyant continental plate (Destructive Boundary)
View Answer
Coastal fold mountain ranges (Andes and Rockies), deep oceanic trenches (Peru-Chile Trench), and volcanic continental arcs
Convergent Boundary (Oceanic–Oceanic)
View Answer
Collision of two oceanic plates; subduction of the older, colder, and denser oceanic plate into the mantle
View Answer
Volcanic island arcs (Japan, Philippines, Aleutians, Indonesia) and the world's deepest trenches (Mariana Trench)
Convergent Boundary (Continental–Continental)
View Answer
Head-on collision of two buoyant continental crusts; neither undergoes deep subduction, resulting in intense compression and folding
View Answer
Massive Alpine-Himalayan fold mountain system (Himalayas, Tibetan Plateau, Alps); powerful earthquakes but absence of volcanism
Transform / Conservative Boundary
View Answer
Two plates sliding horizontally past each other along a fault without creation or destruction of crust
View Answer
Transform faults; San Andreas Fault in California, shallow destructive earthquakes, and offset landforms

Continental Drift Plate Tectonics 2
Concept / MechanismMotion / ProcessMajor Landforms & Evidence
Sea Floor Spreading (Hess, 1960)
View Answer
Continuous lateral movement of oceanic crust away from ridge axes and renewal of the seabed by fresh lava from below
View Answer
Symmetrical paleomagnetic striping on both sides of mid-ocean ridges and increasing rock age away from the ridge crest
Wadati–Benioff Zone
View Answer
Inclined planar seismic zone dipping up to 700 km depth along a subducting slab in a subduction zone
View Answer
Zone of concentration of shallow-, intermediate-, and deep-focus destructive earthquakes
Mantle Plumes & Hotspots
View Answer
Stationary thermal magma plumes rising from the core-mantle boundary beneath moving lithospheric plates
View Answer
Linear volcanic chain of the Hawaiian Islands, Réunion Island (source of Deccan Traps), and Yellowstone Caldera
Wilson Cycle
View Answer
Approximately 500-million-year cyclical process of opening, expansion, contraction, and closure of ocean basins into mountains
View Answer
Rift Valley (East Africa) → Narrow Sea (Red Sea) → Open Ocean (Atlantic) → Contracting Ocean (Pacific) → Fold Mountains (Himalayas)
