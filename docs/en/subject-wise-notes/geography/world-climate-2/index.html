World Climatic Classification - Part 2 (World Climate 2)

Mediterranean, Steppe, Taiga–Tundra Climates, and Climate Change

🔍

One-Liner Questions

Between which latitudes and on which continental margins is the Mediterranean Climate found in both hemispheres?
Between 30° and 45° latitudes along the western margins of continents.
Tap to view answer
By which letter code is the Mediterranean Climate represented in Köppen's classification?
'Cs' (Csa — hot-summer Mediterranean; Csb — warm-summer Mediterranean).
Tap to view answer
What is the most unique and defining seasonal feature of the Mediterranean Climate?
Warm-to-hot dry summers and mild, rainy (wet) winters.
Tap to view answer
Why does the summer season remain completely dry in the Mediterranean Climate?
Because in summer the Subtropical High-Pressure Belt shifts poleward over this zone, bringing subsiding dry air and offshore Trade Winds.
Tap to view answer
Which wind system brings most of the winter rainfall to Mediterranean climate regions?
Onshore Prevailing Westerlies and travelling temperate cyclonic depressions.
Tap to view answer
What is the average annual rainfall recorded in Mediterranean climate regions?
Approximately 40 cm to 75 cm (concentrated mainly in the winter half-year).
Tap to view answer
Which is the premier representative region of the Mediterranean Climate in North America?
Central and Southern California (San Francisco and Los Angeles valleys).
Tap to view answer
In which narrow coastal strip of South America is the Mediterranean Climate found?
Central Chile (around Santiago and Valparaíso).
Tap to view answer
At which southern extremity of the African continent is the Mediterranean Climate found?
The south-western Cape region of South Africa (around Cape Town).
Tap to view answer
In which two parts of Australia is the Mediterranean Climate developed?
South-Western Australia (around Perth / Swanland) and parts of Southern Australia (around Adelaide).
Tap to view answer
What is the drought-adapted evergreen natural vegetation of the Mediterranean Climate scientifically called?
Sclerophyllous Vegetation (hard-leaved evergreen woodland and scrub).
Tap to view answer
What are the key structural adaptations of Sclerophyllous Mediterranean plants to survive summer drought?
Small, thick, leathery, waxy leaves (to minimize transpiration), thick corky bark, and deep root systems.
Tap to view answer
By what regional name is the Mediterranean evergreen scrub vegetation known in California?
Chaparral.
Tap to view answer
What is the dense Mediterranean evergreen shrubland along the European coast (southern France and Corsica) called?
Maquis (Macchia in Italy).
Tap to view answer
What is the open, low-growing aromatic scrub growing on dry limestone soils in the Mediterranean basin called?
Garrigue.
Tap to view answer
What is the species-rich Mediterranean shrubland vegetation of the Cape region of South Africa called?
Fynbos.
Tap to view answer
By what name is the eucalyptus-dominated Mediterranean scrub vegetation of southern Australia known?
Mallee scrub.
Tap to view answer
Which is the most iconic and economically characteristic evergreen tree of the Mediterranean basin?
Olive tree.
Tap to view answer
From the thick bark of which Mediterranean tree is commercial bottle cork harvested?
Cork Oak (Quercus suber — abundant in Portugal and Spain).
Tap to view answer
By what famous agricultural sobriquet are the Mediterranean climate regions known globally?
'Orchard Lands of the World'.
Tap to view answer
Which specific fruits are grown on a massive commercial scale in the Mediterranean Climate?
Citrus fruits (oranges, lemons, limes, grapefruits), grapes, olives, figs, peaches, and apricots.
Tap to view answer
Commercial grape cultivation ('Viticulture') is the hallmark of which climatic region?
Mediterranean Climate.
Tap to view answer
In which Mediterranean climate regions is the world's finest wine-making industry concentrated?
France (Champagne, Bordeaux), Italy, Spain, Portugal, California (Napa Valley), and Central Chile.
Tap to view answer
What is the distinctive residual red clay soil formed by the weathering of limestone in the Mediterranean region called?
Terra Rossa.
Tap to view answer
Why are Mediterranean coasts (such as the French and Italian Rivieras) world-famous centres of health resorts and tourism?
Due to year-round abundant sunshine, mild frost-free winters, and clear blue skies ('Climate of Enjoyment').
Tap to view answer
Which hot, dust-laden wind blows from the Sahara Desert across the Mediterranean Sea into southern Europe?
Sirocco.
Tap to view answer
Which cold, dry northerly wind funnels down the Rhône Valley of France to the Mediterranean coast?
Mistral.
Tap to view answer
Which bitterly cold katabatic wind blows down onto the Adriatic coast?
Bora.
Tap to view answer
What climatic factor played a key role in the early establishment of the outdoor motion-picture industry in Hollywood (California)?
Year-round bright sunshine, clear cloudless skies, and mild dry Mediterranean weather.
Tap to view answer
What makes Mediterranean scrublands (like California chaparral and Australian mallee) highly prone to summer Wildfires?
Prolonged hot, rainless summers, strong dry winds (like Santa Ana), and resinous/oily flammable shrubs.
Tap to view answer
What is the fibrous 'Esparto Grass' growing on dry steppes of south-eastern Spain and North Africa used for?
Making high-grade paper, cordage, baskets, and espadrille soles.
Tap to view answer
During which season are cereal crops like wheat and barley sown and grown in the Mediterranean region?
In the winter season (utilizing the winter cyclonic rains and harvested in late spring).
Tap to view answer
Which water-efficient modern irrigation techniques are widely used in Mediterranean horticulture (such as in Israel and California)?
Drip Irrigation and Micro-Sprinkler Irrigation.
Tap to view answer
What is the typical Annual Range of Temperature in coastal Mediterranean regions?
Moderate (approximately 10°C to 15°C due to maritime moderation).
Tap to view answer
Approximately what percentage of the Earth's total land surface area is covered by the Mediterranean Climate?
Less than 2% (small in area, yet disproportionately rich in horticulture and biodiversity).

Climate RegionGlobal DistributionKey Characteristics & Impact
Mediterranean Climate (Cs)
View Answer
Western margins of continents (30°–45° N/S) — Mediterranean basin, California, Central Chile, Cape Town, SW Australia
View Answer
Winter rainfall from onshore Westerlies, dry sunny summers, citrus horticulture & viticulture (grape cultivation), wine industry, and tourism
Temperate Steppe (BSk)
View Answer
Continental interiors (30°–50° N/S) — Eurasian Steppes, North American Prairies, Pampas, Velds, Australian Downs
View Answer
High continentality, fertile black Chernozem soil, extensive mechanized wheat farming ('Granaries of the World'), and commercial cattle/sheep ranching
Taiga / Boreal Forest (Df / Dw)
View Answer
High-latitude belt of Northern Hemisphere (50°–65° N) — Siberia (Russia), northern Canada, Alaska, Scandinavia
View Answer
World's highest annual temperature range, evergreen softwood coniferous forests (pine, fir, spruce), pulp & paper industry, and fur trapping
Tundra Climate (ET)
View Answer
Arctic coastal fringe (>65° N) — northern Alaska, Canadian Arctic Archipelago, Greenland coast, Eurasian Arctic coast
View Answer
Permanently frozen subsoil (permafrost), treeless vegetation of mosses and lichens, very brief summer thaw, and indigenous Inuit/Sami communities
Hot Desert Climate (BWh)
View Answer
Western tropical margins (15°–30° N/S) — Sahara, Thar, Atacama, Arabian, Kalahari, and Great Australian Desert
View Answer
Scanty rainfall (<25 cm), extreme diurnal temperature range, xerophytic thorny vegetation, oasis farming (date palms), and mineral extraction
Equatorial Wet (Af)
View Answer
Within 0°–10° N/S of the Equator — Amazon Basin, Congo Basin, Indonesian Archipelago, Malaysia
View Answer
Uniformly high temperature and daily convectional rain, multi-layered rainforests (Selvas), highest biodiversity, and leached lateritic soils
Marine West Coast (Cfb)
View Answer
Western margins of continents (45°–60° N/S) — Britain, NW Europe, British Columbia, southern Chile, New Zealand
View Answer
Year-round cyclonic rainfall from Westerlies, mild winters and cool summers, deciduous forests, intensive dairy farming, and high population density
Polar Ice Cap (EF)
View Answer
High polar interiors (75°–90° N/S) — Antarctic continent and interior Greenland ice sheet
View Answer
Permanent thick ice sheets, highest planetary surface albedo, complete absence of vegetation, and scientific research stations

Where is the Temperate Steppe Climate (BSk) geographically located?
Between 30° and 50° latitudes in the deep interiors of continents, far from maritime moisture.

What is the primary cause of the large annual temperature range (hot summers and freezing winters) in the Steppe Climate?

Between which latitudes does the Taiga (Subarctic / Boreal) Climate extend?

Why is the Taiga (Subarctic) Climate completely absent in the Southern Hemisphere?

One-Liner Questions

Why is the Natural Greenhouse Effect essential for life on Earth?
It maintains the Earth's average surface temperature at a habitable +15°C (without it, the Earth would be frozen at -18°C).
Tap to view answer
Which is the most abundant natural greenhouse gas in the atmosphere accounting for ~60% of the natural greenhouse effect?
Water Vapour (H₂O).
Tap to view answer
Which greenhouse gas is the single largest contributor (65%–70%) to anthropogenic (human-induced) Global Warming?
Carbon Dioxide (CO₂).
Tap to view answer
What was the atmospheric concentration of Carbon Dioxide (CO₂) prior to the Industrial Revolution (around 1750)?
Approximately 280 parts per million (ppm).
Tap to view answer
What threshold has the atmospheric concentration of Carbon Dioxide (CO₂) crossed in the present era?
Over 420 parts per million (ppm).
Tap to view answer
Where is the world-famous 'Keeling Curve' observatory—recording continuous daily atmospheric CO₂ measurements since 1958—located?
Mauna Loa Observatory (Hawaii, USA).
Tap to view answer
How many times higher is the 100-year Global Warming Potential (GWP) of Methane (CH₄) compared to CO₂?
Approximately 28 to 30 times higher.
Tap to view answer
Which are the two largest agricultural sources of Methane (CH₄) emissions?
Waterlogged paddy (rice) fields and enteric fermentation (digestion) in ruminant livestock (cattle).
Tap to view answer
What is the primary anthropogenic source of Nitrous Oxide (N₂O) emissions?
Excessive agricultural application of synthetic nitrogenous fertilizers.

Which synthetic industrial gas has the highest known Global Warming Potential (GWP ~23,500 times that of CO₂)?
Sulphur Hexafluoride (SF₆ — used as an electrical insulator in high-voltage switchgear).

In 1988; jointly established by the World Meteorological Organization (WMO) and the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP).

In Geneva (Switzerland).

Adopted in December 1997 (in Kyoto, Japan) and entered into force on 16 February 2005.

What was the primary legal objective of the Kyoto Protocol?
To set legally binding greenhouse gas emission reduction targets for developed (Annex I) countries.

Which three flexible market-based mechanisms were introduced under the Kyoto Protocol?
Clean Development Mechanism (CDM), Joint Implementation (JI), and International Emissions Trading (Carbon Trading).

At which Conference of the Parties and in which year was the historic Paris Climate Agreement adopted?
At COP-21 in Paris in December 2015 (entered into force on 4 November 2016).
Tap to view answer
What is the primary global temperature goal set by the 2015 Paris Agreement?
To hold global average temperature rise well below 2°C above pre-industrial levels and pursue efforts to limit it to 1.5°C.

What are the national climate action plans and emission targets voluntarily submitted by each country under the Paris Agreement called?

By which target year has India pledged to achieve 'Net Zero' carbon emissions?
By the year 2070 (announced under the 'Panchamrit' pledges at COP-26 in Glasgow, 2021).
Tap to view answer
What are the two primary physical drivers of global Sea-Level Rise caused by global warming?
Thermal expansion of warming ocean water and melting of land-based glaciers and ice sheets (Greenland & Antarctica).
Tap to view answer
By which chemical process does Ocean Acidification occur?
Absorption of excess atmospheric CO₂ by seawater, forming carbonic acid (H₂CO₃) and lowering ocean pH.
Tap to view answer
What destructive impact do ocean warming and ocean acidification have on tropical coral reefs?
Coral Bleaching (expulsion of symbiotic zooxanthellae algae, turning corals white) and impaired skeleton calcification.
Tap to view answer
How does the melting of Arctic sea ice affect surface Albedo and global warming?
It lowers surface albedo, exposing dark ocean water that absorbs more solar heat and accelerates further melting (Positive Ice-Albedo Feedback).
Tap to view answer
The thawing of Arctic Permafrost threatens to release massive quantities of which potent greenhouse gases?
Methane (CH₄) and Carbon Dioxide (from decomposing ancient organic matter).

The process of capturing and storing atmospheric carbon dioxide in long-term biological or geological carbon sinks.

Injecting reflective sulphate aerosols into the stratosphere (or brightening marine clouds) to reflect a small fraction of sunlight back into space.
