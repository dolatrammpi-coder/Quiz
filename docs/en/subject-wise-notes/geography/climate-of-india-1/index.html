Climate of India - Part 1 (Climate Of India 1)

Origin of Indian Monsoon (Thermal and Dynamic Concepts), Controlling Factors (Himalayas, Jet Streams), South-West and North-East Monsoon

🔍
Quick Reference
Important Facts and Contributions
Climate Of India 1
Component / ProcessKey CharacteristicMonsoonal Impact
Thermal Concept (Sir Edmond Halley, 1686)
View Answer
Differential heating between the intense summer heating of the vast landmass of the Indian Subcontinent and the relatively cooler waters of the Indian Ocean
View Answer
Development of intense low pressure over North-Western India and high pressure over the Indian Ocean; strong attraction of moisture-laden monsoon winds from sea to land
Dynamic Concept (Hermann Flohn, 1951)
View Answer
Northward apparent shift of the Sun causes the Inter-Tropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ) to shift over the Ganga Plain (20°–25° N) as the Monsoon Trough
View Answer
South-East Trade Winds of the Southern Hemisphere cross the Equator, deflect right due to the Coriolis force, and enter India as the South-West Monsoon
Tibetan Plateau (Elevation ~4,500 metres)
View Answer
Owing to its vast area and high altitude, it heats up intensely through insolation and acts as a massive convective Thermal Engine
View Answer
Causes strong vertical ascent of air over the plateau and forms a powerful Upper Tropospheric Anticyclone that drives monsoonal circulation
Subtropical Westerly Jet Stream (SWJ)
View Answer
Bifurcates into two branches (northern and southern) in winter due to the Himalayan barrier; the southern branch flows at the 200 mb level over the Ganga Plain
View Answer
With the onset of summer, the southern branch withdraws northward beyond the Himalayas over Tibet; without this shift, the burst of the South-West Monsoon in India cannot occur
Tropical Easterly Jet Stream (TEJ)
View Answer
High-velocity upper-air wind originating from the anticyclone over the Tibetan Plateau and flowing westward at the 150–100 mb level along ~15° N latitude over Peninsular India
View Answer
Intensifies upper-air subsidence over the Mascarene High and greatly strengthens the vigour and activity of the South-West Monsoon (Koteswaram's Concept)
Somali Jet Stream (Findlater Jet)
View Answer
Powerful low-level cross-equatorial monsoon wind current flowing north-eastward at 1–1.5 km altitude parallel to the Madagascar and East African coast
View Answer
Enhances cold-water upwelling in the Arabian Sea and accelerates moisture transport towards the Indian Subcontinent

Important Facts and Contributions
Climate Of India 1
Component / ProcessKey CharacteristicMonsoonal Impact
Physical Presence of the Himalayas
View Answer
Continuous arcuate mountain barrier averaging 6,000 metres in height; natural barrier against freezing katabatic winds from Central Asia and Siberia
View Answer
Protects India from freezing in winter and intercepts the South-West Monsoon winds to compel widespread orographic rainfall within the country
Mascarene High
View Answer
Powerful dynamic high-pressure cell situated in the southern Indian Ocean east of Madagascar between 20°–35° S latitudes
View Answer
Acts as the primary source region of the Indian Monsoon, propelling a strong cross-equatorial airflow towards the Indian low-pressure trough
South-West Monsoon: Arabian Sea Branch
View Answer
Moisture-laden wind striking the Western Ghats perpendicularly; splits into three sub-branches (Coastal, Central India & Rajasthan)
View Answer
Heavy orographic rainfall (250–400 cm) on the windward slope of the Western Ghats (Konkan-Malabar); scanty rainfall in the Deccan rain-shadow zone; blows parallel to the Aravallis
South-West Monsoon: Bay of Bengal Branch
View Answer
Monsoon branch deflected by Myanmar's Arakan Yoma mountains towards the Ganga Plain and North-East India
View Answer
Funnel-shaped Garo-Khasi hills (Mawsynram) record the highest rainfall in the world; progressive decrease in rainfall from east to west along the Ganga Plain
North-East Monsoon (Retreating Monsoon)
View Answer
Southward shift of the Sun causes the retreat of the ITCZ; dry continental winds blowing towards the Bay of Bengal pick up moisture over the sea
View Answer
Brings widespread cyclonic and orographic rainfall during October–December to the Coromandel Coast of Tamil Nadu and southern Andhra Pradesh (50–60% of Tamil Nadu's annual rainfall)
October Heat (Kwar ki Gham)
View Answer
Oppressive transition period during the retreat of the monsoon (October) marked by clear skies, high temperatures, and high humidity from moist ground
View Answer
Causes sultry, sticky, and oppressive daytime weather conditions while nights become relatively pleasant and cool
