Wind System and Cyclones - Part 2 (Wind System Cyclones 2)

Tropical & Temperate Cyclones, Frontal Systems, and Local Winds of the World

🔍

One-Liner Questions

What is the fundamental definition of a Tropical Cyclone?
A rapidly rotating, non-frontal intense low-pressure storm system originating over warm tropical oceans.
Tap to view answer
What is the direction of wind circulation in a Tropical Cyclone in both hemispheres?
Counter-clockwise (anticlockwise) in the Northern Hemisphere and clockwise in the Southern Hemisphere.
Tap to view answer
What is the minimum Sea Surface Temperature (SST) required for the genesis of a Tropical Cyclone?
At least 27°C (extending to a depth of 50–60 metres).
Tap to view answer
What is the primary source of energy that powers a Tropical Cyclone?
Latent Heat of Condensation released by rising moist marine air.
Tap to view answer
Why do Tropical Cyclones never form directly over the Equator (0° to 5° latitude)?
Because the Coriolis force is zero at the Equator, preventing winds from spiralling into a vortex.
Tap to view answer
What condition of Vertical Wind Shear is required for the development of a Tropical Cyclone?
Low / weak vertical wind shear (so that the vertical convective core is not sheared apart).
Tap to view answer
What is the calm, cloudless central core of a mature Tropical Cyclone called?
Eye of the Cyclone.
Tap to view answer
Why are the sky clear and winds calm inside the Eye of a Cyclone?
Due to slow downward subsidence (sinking) of dry air at the very centre.
Tap to view answer
What is the typical average diameter of the Eye of a Tropical Cyclone?
Approximately 20 to 50 kilometres.
Tap to view answer
Which is the most violent and destructive part of a Tropical Cyclone?
Eye Wall (the ring of towering clouds immediately surrounding the Eye).
Tap to view answer
What type of towering storm clouds form the dense ring of the Eye Wall?
Towering Cumulonimbus Clouds.
Tap to view answer
Where in a Tropical Cyclone are the highest wind velocities and heaviest torrential rains recorded?
Along the inner edge of the Eye Wall.
Tap to view answer
What are the curved bands of convective clouds and rain spiralling inward toward the Eye Wall called?
Spiral Rainbands.
Tap to view answer
What is the shape and spacing of Isobars in a Tropical Cyclone?
Closely packed, concentric, and nearly circular.
Tap to view answer
What is the meteorological definition of cyclone 'Landfall'?
The crossing of the centre (Eye) of a tropical cyclone from the sea onto the coastal land.
Tap to view answer
Why does a Tropical Cyclone rapidly weaken and dissipate after making landfall?
Because its supply of warm marine moisture (latent heat of condensation) is cut off and surface land friction increases.
Tap to view answer
Which is the deadliest hazard associated with a Tropical Cyclone that causes catastrophic coastal inundation?
Storm Surge (abnormal dome-like rise of sea water above normal tide).
Tap to view answer
What are the two main physical causes of a Storm Surge?
Fierce onshore winds piling water against the coast and the suction effect of extremely low central atmospheric pressure.
Tap to view answer
What is the general initial direction of movement of Tropical Cyclones?
From East to West (steered by the tropical Trade Winds, before recurving poleward).
Tap to view answer
What are Tropical Cyclones called in the North Atlantic Ocean, Caribbean Sea, and North-Eastern Pacific?
Hurricanes.
Tap to view answer
By what name are Tropical Cyclones known in the Western North Pacific and South China Sea?
Typhoons.
Tap to view answer
What are Tropical Cyclones called in the Indian Ocean (Bay of Bengal and Arabian Sea)?
Cyclones (Cyclonic Storms).
Tap to view answer
What is the regional name for Tropical Cyclones off the coast of North-Western Australia?
Willy-Willies.
Tap to view answer
By what local name are Tropical Cyclones known in the Philippines archipelago?
Baguio.
Tap to view answer
What are Tropical Cyclones called in Japan?
Taifu.
Tap to view answer
Which standard scale (Categories 1 to 5) is used to classify Hurricane intensity based on sustained wind speed?
Saffir–Simpson Hurricane Wind Scale.
Tap to view answer
What is the minimum sustained wind speed of a Category 5 Hurricane on the Saffir–Simpson Scale?
252 km/hour (157 mph or 137 knots) or higher.
Tap to view answer
On which scale is the intensity and structural damage of a Tornado measured?
Enhanced Fujita Scale (EF Scale — EF0 to EF5).
Tap to view answer
In which geographical region of the world is the highest frequency of Tornadoes recorded?
Tornado Alley (Great Plains / Mississippi–Missouri Basin in the central United States).
Tap to view answer
According to the India Meteorological Department (IMD), what is the sustained wind speed criterion for a 'Super Cyclonic Storm'?
222 km/hour or higher (120 knots or more).
Tap to view answer
In which year did the formal naming of tropical cyclones begin in the North Indian Ocean basin?
In the year 2004.
Tap to view answer
How many member countries are currently part of the WMO/ESCAP Panel on Tropical Cyclones for naming storms in the North Indian Ocean?
13 member countries (each contributing 13 names, totalling a list of 169 names).
Tap to view answer
Why do significantly more tropical cyclones form in the Bay of Bengal than in the Arabian Sea?
Due to higher sea surface temperatures, lower salinity/stratification from river influx, higher humidity, and remnants of Pacific typhoons entering the Bay.
Tap to view answer
Which eastern coastal state of India has historically been the most frequently hit by severe cyclones?
Odisha (notably hit by the devastating October 1999 Odisha Super Cyclone).
Tap to view answer
What are the 4-stage colour codes issued by the India Meteorological Department (IMD) for cyclone warnings?
Green (No warning), Yellow (Be updated / Watch), Orange (Be prepared / Alert), and Red (Take action / Warning).

One-Liner Questions

Between which latitudes do Temperate Cyclones originate in both hemispheres?
Between 35° and 65° latitudes in both hemispheres.
Tap to view answer
By what other technical names are Temperate Cyclones known?
Extratropical Cyclones, Mid-Latitude Cyclones, Wave Cyclones, or Depressions.
Tap to view answer
Who propounded the widely accepted 'Polar Front Theory' of the origin of Temperate Cyclones in 1918?
Vilhelm Bjerknes and Jacob Bjerknes (the Norwegian School of Meteorology).
Tap to view answer
What is the meteorological definition of a 'Front'?
A sloping three-dimensional transition zone separating two converging air masses of contrasting temperature and humidity.
Tap to view answer
What is meant by Frontogenesis?
The process of formation of a new front or intensification of an existing thermal front.
Tap to view answer
What does the process of Frontolysis signify?
The weakening, dissipation, and eventual disappearance of a front.
Tap to view answer
Into how many main types are Fronts classified?
4 types (Cold Front, Warm Front, Occluded Front, and Stationary Front).
Tap to view answer
What is a Cold Front?
A front along which an advancing cold, dense air mass wedges under and rapidly uplifts warmer air ahead of it.
Tap to view answer
What is the typical slope ratio of a Cold Front?
A steep slope (approximately 1:50 to 1:100).
Tap to view answer
What type of clouds and precipitation occur along an advancing Cold Front?
Towering Cumulonimbus clouds producing heavy, short-duration downpours accompanied by thunder, lightning, and squalls.
Tap to view answer
Under what condition is a Warm Front formed?
When an advancing warm, lighter air mass glides gently upward over a retreating wedge of cold, denser air.
Tap to view answer
What is the typical slope ratio of a Warm Front?
A gentle slope (approximately 1:100 to 1:400).
Tap to view answer
Which high-altitude clouds give the earliest visual indication of an approaching Warm Front?
Cirrus (mares' tails) followed by halo-forming Cirrostratus clouds.
Tap to view answer
What type of rainfall occurs during the passage of a Warm Front?
Widespread, steady, moderate-to-light continuous precipitation from Nimbostratus and Altostratus clouds.
Tap to view answer
When does an Occluded Front form?
When a faster-moving Cold Front overtakes a Warm Front and lifts the warm sector completely off the ground.
Tap to view answer
What is a Stationary Front?
A front along which neither the cold air mass nor the warm air mass is strong enough to displace the other horizontally.
Tap to view answer
According to the Bjerknes Polar Front Model, how many stages make up the complete life cycle of a Temperate Cyclone?
6 stages (from initial stationary front through nascent wave, mature open wave, and occlusion to dissipation).
Tap to view answer
What is the typical shape and horizontal diameter of a Temperate Cyclone?
Oval or V-shaped isobars (spanning 1,500 to 3,000 km across).
Tap to view answer
What is the general direction of movement of Temperate Cyclones?
From West to East (steered by the prevailing Westerlies and Polar Front Jet Stream).
Tap to view answer
Do Temperate Cyclones possess a calm, cloudless central 'Eye' like Tropical Cyclones?
No; a calm central Eye is completely absent in Temperate Cyclones.
Tap to view answer
What is the primary source of energy driving a Temperate Cyclone?
Baroclinic instability (available potential energy released by the temperature and density contrast between cold and warm air masses).
Tap to view answer
Why do Temperate Cyclones not dissipate immediately upon moving over land?
Because their energy derives from the thermal contrast between air masses rather than solely from warm ocean evaporation.
Tap to view answer
How does the spatial size of a Temperate Cyclone compare with that of a Tropical Cyclone?
Temperate cyclones are typically 3 to 4 times larger in diameter than tropical cyclones.
Tap to view answer
What type of weather system is a 'Western Disturbance' affecting India?
An extratropical (temperate) cyclonic depression originating over the Mediterranean Sea.
Tap to view answer
Which upper-tropospheric wind current transports Western Disturbances eastward into India?
Subtropical Westerly Jet Stream.
Tap to view answer
Which weather system brings winter rainfall ('Mawat') to north-western India (Punjab, Haryana, Western UP), proving a boon for the Rabi wheat crop?
Western Disturbances.
Tap to view answer
What is the primary cause of heavy winter snowfall in Kashmir, Ladakh, Himachal Pradesh, and Uttarakhand?
Western Disturbances.
Tap to view answer
How do wind speeds in Temperate Cyclones generally compare with those in Tropical Cyclones?
Moderate and less destructive (generally 30 to 60 km/hour, compared to 120–250+ km/hour in tropical cyclones).
Tap to view answer
What is the meteorological definition of an 'Air Mass'?
A vast body of air extending over thousands of kilometres having uniform horizontal temperature and humidity characteristics at any given level.
Tap to view answer
On what two primary criteria are Air Masses classified according to their source regions?
Nature of surface: Continental (c) vs. Maritime (m); and Latitudinal thermal zone: Tropical (T) vs. Polar (P) / Arctic (A).
Tap to view answer
What do the air mass symbols 'cP' and 'mT' stand for?
cP = Continental Polar (cold and dry); mT = Maritime Tropical (warm and moist).
Tap to view answer
In which part of the surface pressure pattern do Fronts always lie?
Along a low-pressure Trough (where isobars kink sharply away from the low).
Tap to view answer
What changes occur in local temperature and atmospheric pressure immediately after the passage of a Warm Front?
Temperature rises (as the warm sector arrives) and atmospheric pressure stops falling or levels off.
Tap to view answer
What changes occur in local weather immediately after the passage of a Cold Front?
A sharp drop in temperature, rapid rise in atmospheric pressure, wind shift, and clearing skies.
Tap to view answer
What is 'Bombogenesis' or a 'Bomb Cyclone'?
Rapid intensification of a mid-latitude cyclone in which central barometric pressure drops by at least 24 millibars within 24 hours.

One-Liner Questions

What is the fundamental definition of an Anticyclone?
A large-scale atmospheric circulation system with highest atmospheric pressure at its centre and pressure decreasing outward.
Tap to view answer
What is the direction of wind circulation in an Anticyclone in both hemispheres?
Clockwise in the Northern Hemisphere and counter-clockwise (anticlockwise) in the Southern Hemisphere.
Tap to view answer
In which vertical direction does air move at the centre of an Anticyclone?
Downward subsidence (sinking from aloft toward the surface).
Tap to view answer
Why does the weather in an Anticyclone always remain fair, clear, and rainless?
Because subsiding air warms adiabatically by compression, reducing relative humidity and preventing cloud condensation.
Tap to view answer
How are Isobars spaced in an Anticyclone?
Widely spaced apart (indicating a weak pressure gradient and light/calm winds).
Tap to view answer
What type of anticyclones develop over snow-covered Siberia and northern Canada during winter?
Cold-Core Thermal Anticyclones.
Tap to view answer
What type of anticyclones persist over the Subtropical High-Pressure Belts (Horse Latitudes)?
Warm-Core Dynamic Anticyclones.
Tap to view answer
What impact does a stationary 'Blocking High' (Blocking Anticyclone) have on regional weather?
It diverts oncoming rain-bearing cyclones, causing prolonged heatwaves, droughts, or persistent cold spells.
Tap to view answer
