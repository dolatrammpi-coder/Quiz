Insolation and Pressure Belts - Part 2 (Insolation Pressure Belts 2)

Atmospheric Pressure, Isobars, Major Pressure Belts of the World, Planetary Winds, and Seasonal Shifting of Pressure Belts

🔍
Quick Reference
Important Facts & Contributions
Insolation Pressure Belts 2
Wind / Pressure BeltDirection / LocationMajor Impact
Equatorial Low-Pressure Belt (Doldrums)
View Answer
Between 5° North and 5° South latitudes on both sides of the Equator (Thermally induced belt)
View Answer
Continuous convective ascent of air due to intense solar heating; absence of horizontal surface winds; calm air, high humidity, and daily afternoon cumulonimbus rainfall
Subtropical High-Pressure Belt (Horse Latitudes)
View Answer
Between 30° and 35° North and South latitudes in both hemispheres (Dynamically induced belt)
View Answer
Subsidence (sinking) of cool, dry upper air; calm anticyclonic conditions; formation of major hot deserts along the western margins of continents
Subpolar Low-Pressure Belt
View Answer
Between 60° and 65° North and South latitudes in both hemispheres (Dynamically induced belt)
View Answer
Dynamic divergence of air due to Earth's rotation and convergence of warm Westerlies with cold Polar Easterlies; zone of the Polar Front and temperate cyclones
Polar High-Pressure Belt
View Answer
Around 85° to 90° North and South at both Poles (Thermally induced belt)
View Answer
Perennial ice cover and intense radiative cooling cause cold, dense air to sink; persistent high pressure and frigid, dry polar conditions

Important Facts & Contributions
Insolation Pressure Belts 2
Wind / Pressure BeltDirection / LocationMajor Impact
Trade Winds
View Answer
From Subtropical High (30°–35°) to Equatorial Low (0°–5°); blow from NE in Northern Hemisphere and SE in Southern Hemisphere
View Answer
Bring heavy onshore rainfall to the eastern coasts of tropical continents; blow offshore and dry over western margins, creating tropical deserts
Westerlies
View Answer
From Subtropical High (30°–35°) to Subpolar Low (60°–65°); blow from SW in Northern Hemisphere and NW in Southern Hemisphere
View Answer
Bring year-round rainfall to western margins of mid-latitude continents; blow with gale force over the Southern Ocean as Roaring Forties (40°S), Furious Fifties (50°S), and Shrieking Sixties (60°S)
Polar Easterlies
View Answer
From Polar High (90°) to Subpolar Low (60°–65°); blow from NE in Northern Hemisphere and SE in Southern Hemisphere
View Answer
Bitterly cold, dry winds; collide with warmer Westerlies in the subpolar zone to form the Polar Front and mid-latitude cyclonic storms
Summer Shifting of Pressure Belts
View Answer
Northward shift of all pressure belts by 5° to 10° around 21 June (Summer Solstice in Northern Hemisphere)
View Answer
Triggers the onset of the South-West Monsoon over the Indian subcontinent; brings dry subtropical high pressure over Mediterranean regions in summer
Winter Shifting of Pressure Belts
View Answer
Southward shift of all pressure belts by 5° to 10° around 22 December (Winter Solstice in Northern Hemisphere)
View Answer
Brings Mediterranean regions (30°–45° N/S) under the influence of onshore Westerlies, causing winter cyclonic rainfall ideal for citrus horticulture
