Rocks - Part 2 (Rocks 2)

Sedimentary and Metamorphic Rocks, Formation Processes, and Transformation Cycle

🔍

One-Liner Questions

From which Latin word is the term 'Sedimentary' derived?
From 'Sedimentum', which means 'settling down'.
Tap to view answer
What is the sequential process of the formation of Sedimentary Rocks?
Weathering, erosion, transportation, deposition, and lithification.
Tap to view answer
What is the process of loose unconsolidated sediments turning into solid rock called?
Lithification.
Tap to view answer
Which are the two main sub-processes involved in Lithification?
Compaction and Cementation.
Tap to view answer
Which are the principal natural cementing agents that bind sedimentary grains together?
Silica, Calcite, and Iron Oxide.
Tap to view answer
What is the most prominent physical characteristic of Sedimentary Rocks?
Layered or Stratified (bedded) structure.
Tap to view answer
Approximately what percentage of the Earth's land surface area is covered by Sedimentary Rocks?
Approximately 75% of the land surface.
Tap to view answer
What is the contribution of Sedimentary Rocks to the total volume of the entire Earth's Crust?
Only 5% (the remaining 95% consists of igneous and metamorphic rocks).
Tap to view answer
In which type of rocks are Fossils preserved?
Exclusively in Sedimentary Rocks.
Tap to view answer
In which rocks are reserves of coal, mineral oil (petroleum), and natural gas accumulated?
Within the strata of Sedimentary Rocks.
Tap to view answer
Into which three categories are Sedimentary Rocks divided based on their mode of formation?
Mechanically formed (Clastic), Organically formed, and Chemically formed.
Tap to view answer
What are Mechanically Formed (Clastic) Sedimentary Rocks?
Rocks formed by the deposition and consolidation of fragmented clasts of pre-existing rocks.
Tap to view answer
What type of sedimentary rock is Sandstone?
A mechanically formed arenaceous rock composed of sand grains.
Tap to view answer
What is the main identifying feature of Conglomerate?
A sedimentary rock formed by the cementation of rounded gravel and pebbles.
Tap to view answer
What is the structural difference between Breccia and Conglomerate?
Breccia contains angular, sharp-edged rock fragments, whereas Conglomerate contains rounded pebbles.
Tap to view answer
From the deposition of which sediments is Shale formed?
From the compaction of very fine clay and silt particles.
Tap to view answer
What is formed by the wind-blown deposition of extremely fine yellowish dust and silt particles?
Loess (such as the Loess Plains of northern China).
Tap to view answer
What are unsorted and mixed sediments deposited directly by glaciers called?
Till or Tillite rock.
Tap to view answer
What is the primary basis of Organically Formed Sedimentary Rocks?
Accumulation and compaction of dead remains of plants and animals.
Tap to view answer
What is the primary chemical composition of Limestone?
Calcium Carbonate (CaCO₃).
Tap to view answer
What type of sedimentary rock is Chalk?
A soft limestone rock formed from the calcareous shells of microscopic marine organisms (Foraminifera).
Tap to view answer
What is the fundamental biological source of Coal formation?
Ancient dense vegetation remains buried under swampy basins.
Tap to view answer
What is the progressive developmental sequence of Coal formation?
Peat → Lignite → Bituminous → Anthracite.
Tap to view answer
Which type of coal is called 'Brown Coal'?
Lignite.
Tap to view answer
Which type of coal is most widely produced and used in India?
Bituminous Coal (of Gondwana age).
Tap to view answer
How are Chemically Formed Sedimentary Rocks formed?
By evaporation of water and chemical precipitation of dissolved minerals.
Tap to view answer
By which process is Rock Salt (Halite) formed?
By evaporation of saline water (evaporite deposit).
Tap to view answer
What is Gypsum chemically?
Hydrated Calcium Sulphate (CaSO₄·2H₂O).
Tap to view answer
What are the icicle-like limestone columns hanging downward from the ceilings of underground caves called?
Stalactites.
Tap to view answer
What are the limestone deposits growing upward from the floor of a cave called?
Stalagmites.
Tap to view answer
What are the wavy undulations formed on sedimentary beds by wind or water currents called?
Ripple Marks.
Tap to view answer
What are the polygonal cracks formed in wet mud when it dries and shrinks called?
Mud Cracks (Sun Cracks / Desiccation Cracks).
Tap to view answer
What does Cross Bedding (Current Bedding) in sedimentary strata indicate?
Changing direction of wind or water currents during sediment deposition.
Tap to view answer
More than 98% of India's coal reserves belong to which geological rock system?
Gondwana Rock System (Damodar, Son, and Mahanadi valleys).
Tap to view answer
Of which rock are the Red Fort (Delhi) and Fatehpur Sikri built?
Red Sandstone of the Vindhyan System.
Tap to view answer
Between which rock layers are natural reservoirs of mineral oil (petroleum) trapped?
In porous sandstone or limestone reservoir beds capped by impermeable shale strata.

One-Liner Questions

What is the literal meaning of the word 'Metamorphism'?
'Change of Form'.
Tap to view answer
How are Metamorphic Rocks formed?
Through the transformation of pre-existing rocks under high heat, pressure, and chemically active fluids.
Tap to view answer
Does the process of metamorphism take place in a solid state or a molten state?
Strictly in a solid state (without complete melting).
Tap to view answer
Which are the three main agents of metamorphism?
Heat (Temperature), Pressure (Stress), and Chemically Active Fluids.
Tap to view answer
What is the normal value of the Geothermal Gradient in the upper crust?
An increase of approximately 1°C for every 32 metres of depth.
Tap to view answer
What is the main cause of Thermal or Contact Metamorphism?
Intense heat generated by direct contact with intruding hot magma.
Tap to view answer
What is the baked metamorphic zone formed around a magma intrusion called?
Metamorphic Aureole (Contact Halo).
Tap to view answer
Which extremely hard, non-foliated rock is formed by the baking of shale during contact metamorphism?
Hornfels.
Tap to view answer
What is the main cause of Dynamic or Regional Metamorphism?
Tectonic compression, mountain-building forces (orogeny), and widespread heat and pressure.
Tap to view answer
What is the spatial extent of Regional Metamorphism?
Extensive belts covering thousands of square kilometres.
Tap to view answer
By what agent is Hydrothermal Metamorphism caused?
By superheated, mineral-rich aqueous solutions circulating through rocks.
Tap to view answer
What is metamorphism caused by the weight of thick overlying sedimentary strata in deep basins called?
Burial Metamorphism.
Tap to view answer
What is metamorphism caused by the ultra-high pressure and heat of a meteorite impact called?
Shock (Impact) Metamorphism.
Tap to view answer
What is the effect of Differential (Directed) Stress during metamorphism?
Flattening and parallel alignment of platy and elongated mineral grains in a preferred direction.
Tap to view answer
What is the geological definition of Foliation?
The planar or parallel arrangement of mineral grains or structural bands within a metamorphic rock.
Tap to view answer
What does Gneissose Structure (Gneissic Banding) exhibit?
Alternating light-coloured (felsic) and dark-coloured (mafic) mineral bands.
Tap to view answer
What type of metamorphic texture is Schistosity?
Visible parallel alignment of platy minerals like mica that allows the rock to split along wavy planes.
Tap to view answer
What is the characteristic property of Slaty Cleavage?
The ability of a fine-grained rock (slate) to split cleanly into very thin, flat parallel sheets.
Tap to view answer
Which are the most common examples of Non-foliated Metamorphic Rocks?
Marble and Quartzite.
Tap to view answer
Why is foliation absent in Marble and Quartzite?
Because they are composed of equidimensional (equigranular) minerals like calcite or quartz rather than platy minerals.
Tap to view answer
What does Metamorphic Grade indicate?
The relative intensity of temperature and pressure under which metamorphism occurred.
Tap to view answer
Which rock is an ideal example of Low-Grade Metamorphism?
Slate (formed at approximately 200°C to 320°C).
Tap to view answer
Which rocks are primary examples of High-Grade Metamorphism?
Gneiss and Granulite.
Tap to view answer
What is the geological significance of Index Minerals?
They pinpoint the specific temperature–pressure conditions and metamorphic grade of a rock.
Tap to view answer
Which are the key index minerals of High-Grade Metamorphism?
Sillimanite and Kyanite.
Tap to view answer
Which is the key index mineral of Low-Grade Metamorphism?
Chlorite.
Tap to view answer
What type of rock is a Migmatite?
A composite rock formed at the highest grade of metamorphism where partial melting (anatexis) begins, mixing igneous and metamorphic bands.
Tap to view answer
Why are fossils generally absent or destroyed in Metamorphic Rocks?
Because intense heat and tectonic compression deform or obliterate organic structures.
Tap to view answer
What is a Porphyroblast?
A large new crystal (such as Garnet or Staurolite) that grows within a finer-grained metamorphic matrix.
Tap to view answer
What happens during the process of Recrystallization?
Changes in crystal size and shape without altering the overall bulk chemical composition of the rock.
Tap to view answer
What is Metasomatism?
Metamorphic alteration in which hydrothermal fluids introduce new chemical elements and remove existing ones.
Tap to view answer
What is Retrograde Metamorphism?
The conversion of a high-grade metamorphic rock into a lower-grade assemblage as temperature and pressure decrease.
Tap to view answer
What is meant by Prograde Metamorphism?
Sequential metamorphic changes occurring as a rock is subjected to progressively increasing heat and pressure.
Tap to view answer
Of which rocks is the Peninsular Shield of India primarily composed?
Ancient Archean Gneiss and Schist rocks.
Tap to view answer
To which class of rocks do Bundelkhand Gneiss and Charnockite belong?
Ancient Precambrian Metamorphic / Granulitic Rocks.
Tap to view answer
What is the main identifying feature of Charnockite?
A high-grade orthopyroxene-bearing (hypersthene) granulite/granitic rock.
Tap to view answer
What is the typical porosity of Metamorphic Rocks?
Negligible or nearly zero (due to dense interlocking crystal packing).
Tap to view answer
