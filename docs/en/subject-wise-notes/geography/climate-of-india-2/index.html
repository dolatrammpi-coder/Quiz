Coromandel Coast of Tamil Nadu and adjoining coastal Andhra Pradesh; rainfall during October–December from the retreating North-East Monsoon
Aw (Tropical Savannah Climate)
View Answer
Peninsular plateau region south of the Tropic of Cancer; dry winter and summer monsoon rainfall of 75–150 cm
View Answer
Major plateau tracts of Maharashtra, Karnataka, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh & Odisha; supports moist and dry deciduous vegetation
BShw (Semi-Arid Steppe Climate)
View Answer
Annual rainfall 30 to 60 cm; high summer temperatures and dry winter; dominance of thorny scrub vegetation
View Answer
Western Haryana, Punjab, eastern Rajasthan, Gujarat, and the interior rain-shadow zone of the Western Ghats; drought-prone belt
BWhw (Hot Desert Climate)
View Answer
Annual rainfall below 20–25 cm; high diurnal and annual temperature range, scorching summers, and intense evaporation
View Answer
Western Rajasthan (Thar Desert — Barmer, Jaisalmer, Bikaner) & Kutch; severe desertification and acute water scarcity
Cwg (Monsoon with Dry Winter — Ganga Type)
View Answer
Northern Plains of India; intense summer heat with Loo winds, dry and cool winters; monsoon rainfall of 75–100 cm
View Answer
Great Ganga-Brahmaputra Plains, eastern Rajasthan, northern Madhya Pradesh; India's most densely populated and intensively cultivated region
Dfc (Cold Humid Winter with Short Summer)
View Answer
Extremely cold, long, and humid winters with a very brief, cool summer; precipitation throughout the year
View Answer
High mountainous regions of North-East India (Arunachal Pradesh, Sikkim); supports temperate and coniferous forests
E (Polar / Tundra Climate)
View Answer
Snow-bound for most months of the year; average temperature of the warmest month remains between 0°C and 10°C
View Answer
High Himalayan snow peaks of Jammu & Kashmir, Ladakh, Himachal Pradesh & Uttarakhand; alpine pastures and Bugyals

Important Facts and Contributions
Climate Of India 2
Climate / PhenomenonKey CharacteristicImpact on India
El Niño (ENSO)
View Answer
Abnormal warming of surface waters in the central and eastern tropical Pacific Ocean off the coast of Peru, replacing the cold Peruvian (Humboldt) Current
View Answer
Weakens the Walker Circulation; causes sluggish South-West Monsoon winds over India, sharp rainfall deficit, and heightened risk of severe drought
La Niña
View Answer
Abnormal cooling of surface waters in the eastern Pacific Ocean accompanied by strong accumulation of warm water in the western Pacific
View Answer
Hyper-activates the Walker Circulation; strengthens the South-West Monsoon over India, bringing abundant rainfall and frequent floods across several states
Positive IOD (+ve Indian Ocean Dipole)
View Answer
Western Indian Ocean (Arabian Sea) becomes warmer than normal while the eastern Indian Ocean near Indonesia remains relatively cooler
View Answer
Provides an additional boost to the Indian Monsoon; counteracts the adverse impact of El Niño and promotes good rainfall over India
Negative IOD (-ve Indian Ocean Dipole)
View Answer
Eastern Indian Ocean (near Indonesia) becomes warmer than normal while the western Indian Ocean remains cooler
View Answer
Weakens the Indian Monsoon; diverts convective moisture and rain-bearing clouds towards Indonesia, causing rainfall deficiency in India
Regions of Very Heavy Rainfall (>200 cm)
View Answer
Annual rainfall of 200 to 1,100+ cm; rapid orographic uplift of moisture-laden monsoon winds against mountain barriers
View Answer
Western slopes of the Western Ghats, North-East India (Meghalaya, Assam), Andaman-Nicobar; evergreen forests, landslides, and severe floods in the Brahmaputra valley
Low Rainfall & Drought-Prone Regions (<50 cm)
View Answer
Annual rainfall of 10 to 50 cm; located far from maritime monsoon tracks or inside leeward rain-shadow zones
View Answer
Western Rajasthan, Ladakh, Rayalaseema, Marathwada & Vidarbha; agrarian distress, depleting groundwater levels, and recurring droughts
