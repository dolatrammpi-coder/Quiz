Bhil

Rajasthan (Banswara, Dungarpur), Madhya Pradesh (Jhabua, Dhar), Gujarat, and Maharashtra
View Answer
India's largest tribal group (2011 Census); 'Bowmen' (Dravidian word 'Veel'); Bhagoria Haat festival; Ghoomar and Gair dances; Pithora wall painting
Gond
View Answer
Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh (Bastar), Maharashtra, Odisha, and Andhra Pradesh
View Answer
India's second-largest tribal group; Dravidian language family (Gondi); shifting cultivation (Bewar/Dipa); Madai fair and Karma dance; Ghotul youth dormitory system
Santhal
View Answer
Jharkhand (Santhal Pargana), West Bengal (Purulia), Odisha, and Bihar
View Answer
Proto-Australoid group; Santhali language (Ol Chiki script — 92nd Constitutional Amendment); Sohrai and Sarhul festivals; Manjhi–Pargana administrative system
Munda
View Answer
Jharkhand (Ranchi, Khunti, Singhbhum), Odisha, and West Bengal
View Answer
Austro-Asiatic Mundari language; Sarhul and Mage festivals; Sarna religion (nature worship — Singbonga); Sasan Diri (memorial stones); Birsa Munda's Ulgulan rebellion
Oraon (Kurukh)
View Answer
Jharkhand (Chotanagpur), Chhattisgarh, and Odisha
View Answer
Dravidian language family (Kurukh dialect); Dhumkuria youth dormitory (Jonkh-Edpa and Pele-Edpa); Sal tree worship at Sarhul; Jatra folk dance
Toda
View Answer
Nilgiri Hills of Tamil Nadu
View Answer
Buffalo-based pastoral economy (Dairy Temple); Fraternal Polyandry marriage system; semi-cylindrical bamboo huts (Mund); Puthukuli embroidered shawl (GI tag)
Gaddi
View Answer
Himachal Pradesh (Bharmour of Chamba, Kangra) and Jammu & Kashmir
View Answer
Semi-nomadic pastoralists of the Dhauladhar range; seasonal migration (Transhumance) with sheep and goats; ardent devotees of Lord Shiva; traditional Chola-Dora attire
Tharu
View Answer
Uttarakhand (Udham Singh Nagar) and Terai region of Uttar Pradesh
View Answer
Kirata descendants; historically observed Diwali as a day of mourning; joint families with matrilineal influence; Badla marriage custom

Major Tribes 1
TribeMajor RegionDistinctive Features
Jarawa
View Answer
South and Middle Andaman Islands (Jarawa Reserve Area)
View Answer
Ancient Negrito race; PVTG group; nomadic hunters and food gatherers; bow-arrow and bark-armour (Tor) making; external contact threat from Andaman Trunk Road (ATR)
Sentinelese
View Answer
North Sentinel Island (Andaman Islands)
View Answer
World's most isolated and uncontacted Negrito tribe; fierce resistance to external intrusion; 5-nautical-mile legal buffer zone
Onge
View Answer
Little Andaman Island (Dugong Creek and South Bay)
View Answer
Endangered Negrito PVTG; body painted with ochre and white clay; skilled canoe builders and hunters of sea turtles/dugong
Great Andamanese
View Answer
Strait Island (Andaman)
View Answer
Combined remnant of 10 ancient tribes; extremely small population (~50–60 individuals); Jeru dialect; most severely affected by colonial diseases
Shompen
View Answer
Great Nicobar Island (dense interior rainforests)
View Answer
Mongoloid racial group; PVTG status; raised tree-platform huts; wild boar hunting and Pandanus (screwpine) fruit bread; distinct from coastal Nicobarese
Nicobarese
View Answer
Nicobar Islands (Car Nicobar, Nancowry, etc.)
View Answer
Only non-PVTG and largest prosperous tribe of the archipelago; Mongoloid origin; horticulture, pig rearing, and coconut trade; predominantly Christian
