Geomorphic Processes - Part 1 (Geomorphic Processes 1)

Endogenic and Exogenic Forces, Diastrophism, Sudden Movements, and Physical, Chemical & Biological Weathering

🔍
Quick Reference
Important Facts & Contributions
Geomorphic Processes 1
Process / ForceMechanismMajor Outcomes
Diastrophic Forces (Epeirogenic / Continent-Building)
View Answer
Slow vertical movements acting radially upward or downward from the Earth's centre; continental uplift or subsidence
View Answer
Emergence or submergence of vast continental platforms, plateaus, and coastal tracts
Diastrophic Forces (Orogenic / Mountain-Building)
View Answer
Horizontal tangential forces acting face-to-face (compression) or in opposite directions (tension) parallel to the crust
View Answer
Compression produces folds and fold mountains (Himalayas, Alps); tension produces faults, rift valleys (Narmada), and block mountains
Sudden Endogenic Forces (Catastrophic Forces)
View Answer
Abrupt and rapid release of deep geothermal and tectonic energy causing instantaneous surface changes
View Answer
Destructive earthquakes (crustal shaking), volcanic eruptions (lava and ash deposition), tsunamis, and landslides
Exogenic Forces (Denudation)
View Answer
External processes driven by solar heat and gravity (weathering, erosion, mass wasting, and deposition)
View Answer
Degradation of elevated relief, aggradation of basins, and formation of peneplains
Physical Weathering (Mechanical Weathering)
View Answer
Physical disintegration of rocks without chemical change due to temperature fluctuations, frost, salt crystallization, and unloading
View Answer
Exfoliation (onion-peeling), block disintegration, granular disintegration, and frost wedging (scree and talus)
Chemical Weathering
View Answer
Molecular decomposition, dissolution, and alteration of minerals through contact with water, oxygen, carbon dioxide, and acids
View Answer
Oxidation (rusting), carbonation (karst caves), hydration, hydrolysis, and mineral leaching
Biological Weathering
View Answer
Expansion of plant roots, burrowing by rodents and organisms, and human mining and construction activities
View Answer
Widening of rock joints, soil mixing (pedoturbation), and rock dissolution by organic acids secreted by lichens and mosses
Quick Reference
Important Facts & Contributions
Geomorphic Processes 1
Process / ForceMechanismMajor Outcomes
Pressure Release / Unloading
View Answer
Reduction of overlying confining pressure due to erosional removal of superincumbent rock layers, causing upward expansion
View Answer
Development of horizontal sheet jointing and large Exfoliation Domes in granitic rocks
Frost Wedging (Frost Action)
View Answer
9% volume expansion when water trapped in rock crevices freezes into ice, exerting lateral pressure up to 2,000 kg/cm²
View Answer
Shattering of mountain peaks, formation of jagged crags, and accumulation of angular talus/block fields (Felsenmeer)
Carbonation
View Answer
CO₂ dissolves in rainwater to form weak carbonic acid, converting insoluble calcium carbonate into soluble calcium bicarbonate
View Answer
Formation of lapies, sinkholes, dolines, uvalas, and underground caverns in limestone regions
Oxidation
View Answer
Atmospheric oxygen reacting with ferrous iron compounds in rocks in the presence of moisture to form ferric oxide
View Answer
Rusting of iron-bearing rocks, weakening and crumbling of rock structure, and formation of red and yellow weathered soils
