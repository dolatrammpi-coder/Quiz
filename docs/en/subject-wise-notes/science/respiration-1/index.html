Aerobic and Anaerobic Respiration - From Glucose to ATP Energy (Respiration 1)

Concept of Respiration, Aerobic & Anaerobic Respiration, Glycolysis, Krebs Cycle, Fermentation, and ATP

🔍
Quick Reference
Key Facts and Contributions
Respiration 1
Respiration Type / ProcessEnergy YieldProducts / Specific Examples
Aerobic RespirationComplete oxidation of glucose yields 36 to 38 ATP (30 to 32 ATP / ~686 kcal energy by modern calculation)Carbon dioxide (6 CO₂), water (6 H₂O), and abundant ATP; all higher plants, animals, and most fungi
Glycolysis (EMP Pathway)4 ATP generated at substrate level, 2 ATP consumed; net gain: 2 ATP + 2 NADH (+ H⁺)Conversion of 1 glucose (6C) into 2 pyruvate (3C) in the cytoplasm without O₂; common to both aerobic and anaerobic respiration
Krebs Cycle (TCA Cycle / Citric Acid Cycle)Per glucose in mitochondrial matrix (2 turns): 2 GTP/ATP (SLP) + 6 NADH + 2 FADH₂4 CO₂ released; first product is citric acid (6C); amphibolic pathway (both catabolic and anabolic)
Electron Transport System & Oxidative PhosphorylationMaximum ATP (~26-28 ATP) synthesized from NADH and FADH₂ via ETS on mitochondrial cristaeFinal electron and proton acceptor O₂ forms metabolic water (H₂O); F₀-F₁ ATP synthase
Alcoholic FermentationLow net energy gain of only 2 ATP per glucose molecule in the absence of oxygen (~2% efficiency)Ethyl alcohol (2 C₂H₅OH) + 2 CO₂; Yeast (Saccharomyces); brewing industry, bread and bakery production
Lactic Acid FermentationNet gain of only 2 ATP per glucose molecule in the absence of oxygen; no CO₂ releasedLactic acid (2 C₃H₆O₃); Lactobacillus bacteria (curd formation) and human skeletal muscles during intense exercise

Cellular Energy Currency (ATP - Adenosine Triphosphate)Hydrolysis of the terminal high-energy phosphate bond releases about 30.5 kJ (7.3 kcal) of usable energy per moleADP + inorganic phosphate (Pi); muscle contraction, protein synthesis, nerve impulse transmission, and Na⁺/K⁺ pump
Obligate AnaerobesDie from toxic effects in the presence of oxygen; produce only 2 ATP via anaerobic pathwayClostridium botulinum (food poisoning), Clostridium tetani (tetanus bacterium), methanogens
Facultative AnaerobesPerform aerobic respiration (high energy) when O₂ is available, and survive via fermentation (2 ATP) when O₂ is absentYeast, Escherichia coli (E. coli); Pasteur effect (automatic slowing of fermentation when O₂ is supplied)
Cori Cycle (Lactic Acid Cycle)Conversion of lactic acid transported from muscles via blood to the liver back into glucose at a cost of 6 ATPRelief of muscle fatigue (cramps) and restoration of blood glucose levels via hepatic gluconeogenesis
Respiratory Quotient (RQ = CO₂ evolved / O₂ consumed)Varies in thermal and energy efficiency ratio depending on the respiratory substrateCarbohydrate RQ = 1.0; Fat (tripalmitin) RQ = 0.7; Protein RQ = 0.8-0.9; Anaerobic respiration RQ = ∞
