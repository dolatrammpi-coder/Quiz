Trends in Periodic Properties - Radius to Electronegativity (Periodic Table 2)

Periodic Properties, Atomic Radius, Ionization Energy, Electron Affinity, Electronegativity, and Metallic Trends

Key Facts & Contributions
Periodic Table 2
Periodic PropertyTrend Across Period / GroupReason / Comparative Facts
Atomic RadiusDecreases from left to right across a period; increases from top to bottom down a groupEffective nuclear charge (Z_eff) increases across a period; new energy shells (n) are added down a group; noble gases have the largest radius due to van der Waals radius
Ionization Enthalpy (IE / Ionization Energy)Generally increases from left to right across a period; decreases from top to bottom down a groupRemoving an electron becomes harder as nuclear attraction increases; Exceptions: Be (full 2s²) > B (2p¹) and N (half-filled 2p³) > O (2p⁴)
Electron Gain Enthalpy (Electron Affinity)Becomes more negative from left to right across a period; decreases from top to bottom down a groupChlorine (Cl) has higher electron affinity than fluorine (F) due to strong inter-electronic repulsion in fluorine's compact 2p orbital
ElectronegativityIncreases continuously from left to right across a period; decreases from top to bottom down a groupTendency to attract the shared pair of electrons in a covalent bond; on the Pauling scale, fluorine (F = 4.0) is the highest and cesium (0.7) is the lowest
Metallic Character (Electropositivity)Decreases from left to right across a period; increases from top to bottom down a groupTendency to lose electrons; alkali metals (Group 1) are most metallic; cesium (Cs) is the most reactive metal and fluorine (F) is the most reactive non-metal
Acidic/Basic Nature of OxidesAcross a period, shifts from basic through amphoteric to strongly acidic; basicity increases down a groupNa₂O (strongly basic) → Al₂O₃ (amphoteric) → P₂O₅ (acidic) → SO₃ → Cl₂O₇ (most acidic); non-metallic oxides are acidic and metallic oxides are basic

Key Facts & Contributions
Periodic Table 2
Periodic PropertyTrend Across Period / GroupReason / Comparative Facts
Effective Nuclear Charge & ShieldingAcross a period, Z_eff increases by 0.65 per element; remains nearly constant down a groupInner-shell electrons reduce nuclear attraction on outer electrons (Shielding effect: s > p > d > f)
Ionic Radius (Cation vs. Anion)Size of a cation is always smaller than its parent atom; size of an anion is always larger than its parent atomIn cations (Fe³⁺ < Fe²⁺ < Fe), attraction per electron increases; in anions (I⁻ > I), electron-electron repulsion causes expansion
Isoelectronic SpeciesAmong ions with the same number of electrons, radius decreases as atomic number (Z) increasesSize order: C⁴⁻ > N³⁻ > O²⁻ > F⁻ > Na⁺ > Mg²⁺ > Al³⁺ (Al³⁺ has the highest Z=13, causing maximum nuclear pull)
Lanthanide ContractionDue to very poor shielding by 4f orbitals, the size of 5d elements becomes nearly equal to that of 4d elementsZr (4d, atomic number 40) and Hf (5d, atomic number 72) have nearly equal atomic radii (~160 pm); close similarity in chemical properties
Diagonal RelationshipCertain light elements of Period 2 show similarity with elements located diagonally in Period 3Similar ionic potential (polarizing power = charge/radius); Examples: Li ≈ Mg, Be ≈ Al, B ≈ Si
Anomalous Behavior of Period 2 ElementsLi, Be, B, C, N, O, F exhibit properties different from the heavier elements of their respective groupsExtremely small size, high electronegativity, high polarizing power, and complete absence of vacant d-orbitals in the valence shell
