Plant Tissues - From Meristem to Xylem & Phloem (Tissues 1)

Plant Tissues, Meristematic Tissue, Simple Permanent Tissues (Parenchyma, Collenchyma, Sclerenchyma), and Complex Tissues (Xylem & Phloem)

🔍
Quick Reference
Key Facts & Contributions
Tissues 1
Plant TissueStructure/TypeKey Function/Location
Apical MeristemUndifferentiated, rapidly dividing via mitosis, thin cellulose wall, dense protoplasm, cells lacking vacuolesAt growing tips of roots and shoots (Root & Shoot Apex); increases primary length of the plant
Intercalary MeristemPrimary meristem sandwiched between permanent tissues; elongates internodes via rapid divisionNear nodes of grass, bamboo, and mint; rapid regeneration of parts after grazing by herbivores
Lateral MeristemSecondary meristem arranged parallel to the axis of stem and root (vascular cambium and cork cambium)Causes secondary growth in thickness (girth) of stems and roots in woody plants
ParenchymaLiving, thin cellulosic wall, distinct intercellular spaces, isodiametric cells with a large central vacuoleFood storage (starch, fats), secretion, and basic ground packing tissue (Cortex, Pith, Mesophyll)
CollenchymaLiving, elongated cells with heavy corner thickenings of cellulose, hemicellulose, and pectin; intercellular spaces absentHypodermis of dicot stems and petioles; provides flexibility and mechanical support without breaking
SclerenchymaDead cells, heavily lignified thick secondary wall, narrow lumen; occurs as fibres and sclereidsVascular bundles of stem, seed coats, jute/hemp fibres, walnut shell; provides extreme rigidity and hardness to plant parts
Quick Reference
Key Facts & Contributions
Tissues 1
Plant TissueStructure/TypeKey Function/Location
Xylem TracheidsElongated, tubular cells with tapering ends, dead, lignified pitted wallsMain water-conducting component of pteridophytes and gymnosperms; mechanical support
Xylem Vessels (Tracheae)Wide cylindrical tubes with perforated end walls (perforation plates), dead, lignified thickeningCharacteristic feature of angiosperms; rapid vertical conduction of water and minerals from roots to leaves
Xylem ParenchymaThe only living component of xylem tissue; thin cellulose wall and active protoplasmStorage of starch and fats, and radial conduction of water via ray parenchyma
Sieve TubesElongated tubular living cells with perforated sieve plates; enucleated at maturityBidirectional translocation of food (sucrose) synthesized in leaves to various parts of the plant
Companion CellsSpecialized living parenchyma with dense protoplasm and a prominent large nucleus; closely associated with sieve tubesConnected to sieve tubes via plasmodesmata to regulate and maintain osmotic pressure gradient
Bast Fibres (Phloem Fibres)The only dead component of phloem tissue; elongated fibres with heavily lignified thick wallsCommercial fibres of jute, flax, and hemp; provides mechanical strength and tensile power to phloem
