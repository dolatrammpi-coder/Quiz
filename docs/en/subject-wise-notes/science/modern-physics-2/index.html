Quantum Theory and Relativity - From Photon to E=mc² (Modern Physics 2)

Quantum Theory, Planck's Constant, Photon, Photoelectric Effect, Matter Waves, and Theory of Relativity

Quick Reference
Important Facts and Contributions
Modern Physics 2
Modern Physics ConceptPrinciple / EquationExperiment / Application
Planck's Quantum TheoryDiscrete emission/absorption of electromagnetic radiation in energy packets (quanta): E = n h ν (where h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s)Successful resolution of the Black Body Radiation spectrum and the Ultraviolet Catastrophe
Basic Concept of PhotonEnergy E = hν = hc/λ, momentum p = h/λ, rest mass m₀ = 0, electrically neutral, travels at the speed of lightParticle nature of radiation; physical basis of laser technology, X-ray radiation, and photochemical reactions
Photoelectric EffectEinstein's photoelectric equation: hν = Φ₀ + K_max = hν₀ + e V₀ (quantum manifestation of energy conservation)Photoelectric cells, solar photovoltaic panels, automatic light controllers, burglar alarms, digital camera sensors
de Broglie Matter WavesDual nature of matter particles; wavelength associated with a moving particle: λ = h / p = h / (mv) = 1.227 / √V nm (for electrons)Verified by Davisson-Germer experiment; electron microscope (ultra-high magnification ~10⁶ times), nanotechnology
Heisenberg Uncertainty PrincipleSimultaneous exact measurement of a particle's position and momentum is impossible: Δx · Δp ≥ h / 4π (and ΔE · Δt ≥ h / 4π)Proof of the impossibility of free electrons existing inside the atomic nucleus; probabilistic orbital model in quantum mechanics
Compton Scattering EffectIncrease in wavelength of an X-ray photon after colliding with a stationary electron: Δλ = λ' - λ = (h / m₀c)(1 - cos θ)Irrefutable experimental proof of the photon's linear momentum and pure particle nature; gamma-ray astronomy and radiation physics

Modern Physics ConceptPrinciple / EquationExperiment / Application
Work Function and Threshold FrequencyMinimum energy for electron emission from a metal surface: Φ₀ = h ν₀ = h c / λ₀ (lowest for Caesium ~2.14 eV, highest for Platinum ~5.65 eV)Fabrication of photosensitive cathodes; construction of optical detectors sensitive to ultraviolet and visible light

Special Theory of RelativityLaws of physics are identical in all inertial frames of reference; speed of light 'c' in vacuum is absolute and universal for all observersMichelson-Morley experiment (disproving the hypothetical 'aether' medium); accurate relativistic analysis of cosmic particle motion
Time DilationA clock in a moving frame of reference runs slower relative to a stationary observer: Δt = Δt₀ / √(1 - v²/c²)Arrival of cosmic-ray muons at the Earth's surface; time correction in atomic clocks of GPS satellites
Length Contraction and Relativistic MassL = L₀ √(1 - v²/c²) (contraction along direction of motion) and m = m₀ / √(1 - v²/c²) (increase in mass with speed)Acceleration of protons in particle accelerators (LHC / CERN); impossibility of any massive particle attaining the speed of light (c)
Mass-Energy Equivalence (E=mc²)Mass and energy are two forms of the same entity: E = m c²; total energy equation: E² = p²c² + m₀²c⁴Immense energy of nuclear fission and fusion; Pair Production and Pair Annihilation processes
