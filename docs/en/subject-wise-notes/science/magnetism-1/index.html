Magnetic Field & Electromagnets - From Magnets to Solenoids (Magnetism 1)

Properties of Magnets, Magnetic Field Lines, Current-Carrying Conductors, Solenoids, and Electromagnets

🔍
Quick Reference
Key Facts & Contributions
Magnetism 1
Magnetic SystemField / Direction CharacteristicsFunction / Applications
Bar MagnetOutside: North to South pole (N→S); Inside: South to North pole (S→N); forms closed continuous loopsMagnetic compass for navigation, study of magnetic dipole moment, and basic magnetism demonstrations
Straight Current-Carrying ConductorConcentric circular field lines in a plane perpendicular to the wire; direction given by Right-Hand Thumb Rule (B ∝ I/r)Demonstrating the magnetic effect of electric current; analyzing magnetic fields around overhead power lines
Circular Current-Carrying CoilMagnetic field lines near the center are nearly straight and parallel (uniform field); B = μ₀NI / (2R)Galvanometer coils, enhancing magnetic field strength, and determining polarity using the Clock Face Rule
Current-Carrying SolenoidUniform and strong magnetic field inside (parallel lines, B = μ₀nI); behaves like a bar magnetGenerating uniform magnetic fields, constructing electromagnets, relay switches, and automatic valves
Electromagnet (Soft Iron Core)High magnetic permeability and low coercivity; produces a very strong, temporary magnetic field while current flowsCranes lifting heavy scrap iron, electric bells, telegraphs, MRI machines, and Maglev trains
Permanent Magnet (Steel / Alnico)High retentivity and high coercivity; retains magnetism even after the magnetizing current is removedLoudspeakers, microphones, dynamos, DC motors, and permanent poles in voltmeters and ammeters

Biot-Savart LawdB = (μ₀/4π) · (I dl sinθ / r²); infinitesimal magnetic field produced by a current elementTheoretical calculation of magnetic fields produced by current-carrying conductors of complex geometry
Right-Hand Thumb Rule (Maxwell)If the right-hand thumb points in the direction of current, the curled fingers indicate the direction of the magnetic fieldQuick determination of magnetic field direction around straight and circular conductors
Clock Face RuleClockwise current = South Pole (S); Anticlockwise current = North Pole (N)Accurate identification of magnetic polarity at the faces of a current-carrying loop or solenoid
Ampère's Circuital Law∮ B·dl = μ₀ · I_enclosed (analogous to Gauss's Law in electrostatics)Simple derivation of magnetic fields for solenoids, toroids, and thick cylindrical conductors
Earth's GeomagnetismMagnetic field directed from geographic South to geographic North; angle of dip is 90° at poles and 0° at the equatorMaritime and aviation navigation, bird migration orientation, and magnetic declination measurement
