Quick Reference
Key Facts and Contributions
Chemical Bonding 2
Type of Bond / ForceFormation or MechanismRelated Properties / Examples
Metallic Bond (Electron Sea Model)Immersion of positively charged metal ions (kernels) in a sea of delocalized valence electronsExcellent electrical and thermal conductivity, metallic lustre, malleability
Band TheoryFormation of valence band and conduction band through overlapping of atomic orbitalsClassification of electrical behavior of metals (conductors), semiconductors, and insulators
Metallic Bond StrengthDependence on the number of free valence electrons per atom and smaller cation sizeHighest melting point of tungsten (3422°C), extreme softness of sodium and potassium
Malleability and DuctilitySliding of positively charged layers over one another in the electron sea without breaking under external forceProperty of gold (Au) and silver (Ag) to be hammered into thin sheets and drawn into fine wires
Sonorous PropertyUnrestricted transmission of energy by delocalized electrons in the lattice when the metal is struckBell metal, strings of musical instruments

Key Facts and Contributions
Chemical Bonding 2
Type of Bond / ForceFormation or MechanismRelated Properties / Examples
London Dispersion ForceShort-lived weak attraction between instantaneous and induced dipoles in non-polar molecules or atomsCondensation and liquefaction of noble gases (He, Ne, Ar) as well as I2 and CH4
Dipole-Dipole Attraction (Keesom Force)Electrostatic attraction between opposite poles of permanent polar moleculesHigher melting and boiling points of HCl, SO2, and NH3 compared to non-polar molecules
Dipole-Induced Dipole Force (Debye Force)Distortion of the electron cloud of an adjacent non-polar molecule by a permanent polar moleculePartial solubility of oxygen (O2) and noble gases in water
Intermolecular Hydrogen BondAttraction between H and a highly electronegative atom (F, O, N) of two separate moleculesAbnormally high boiling point of water (H2O) at 100°C, solubility of alcohol in water
Intramolecular Hydrogen BondRing formation between H and an adjacent highly electronegative atom within the same moleculeSteam volatility and relatively lower boiling point of ortho-nitrophenol
