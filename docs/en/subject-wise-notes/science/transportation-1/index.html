Plant Transport - Xylem, Phloem & Transpiration (Transportation 1)

Transport of water, minerals, and organic food in plants, xylem, phloem, ascent of sap, and transpiration

🔍

Transportation 1
Plant Transport StructureMechanism of TransportSubstances / Specific Function
Root Hairs & EpidermisOsmosis of water from soil and active absorption of mineral ions via ATP expenditureWater and inorganic mineral ions (NO₃⁻, PO₄³⁻, K⁺); enormous increase in absorptive surface area

Key Facts & Contributions
Transportation 1
Plant Transport StructureMechanism of TransportSubstances / Specific Function
Stomatal TranspirationRelease of 80–90% of total transpiration into the atmosphere as water vapour through leaf stomataGenerates transpiration pull; produces a cooling effect of 10–15°C on leaf surfaces
Cuticular & Lenticular TranspirationContinuous slow loss of water vapour through the cuticle (3–10%) and lenticels (~1%) of woody stemsMaintains minimal internal water balance and gaseous exchange even when stomata are closed
HydathodesUnregulated oozing of liquid water droplets at vein endings along leaf margins of herbaceous plants (grasses, tomato)Exudation of excess water via guttation under high root pressure and low transpiration conditions
Phloem Loading & UnloadingATP-driven active transport of sucrose from leaf mesophyll into sieve tubes via H⁺/sucrose symporterBuilds high osmotic pressure in sieve tubes (water entry) and active removal of sucrose at the sink
Girdling / Ringing ExperimentRemoval of a ring of bark and phloem from a woody stem while keeping the inner xylem intactHistorical proof that organic food is conducted by phloem; accumulation of food and swelling above the ring
Cohesion-Adhesion-Surface Tension ForcesHydrogen bonding between water molecules (cohesion), attraction to xylem walls (adhesion), and high tensile strengthMaintains an unbroken continuous water column even in giant redwood (Sequoia) trees over 130 m tall
