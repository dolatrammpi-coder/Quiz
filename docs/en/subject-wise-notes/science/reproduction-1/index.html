Asexual Reproduction - Fission, Budding, Fragmentation to Vegetative Propagation (Reproduction 1)

Asexual Reproduction, Binary Fission, Multiple Fission, Budding, Fragmentation, Regeneration, Vegetative Propagation, and Spore Formation

🔍
Quick Reference
Key Facts and Contributions
Reproduction 1
Asexual Reproduction MethodProcessExamples / Pros & Cons
Binary FissionA mature parent cell divides mitotically into two identical daughter cells (karyokinesis followed by cytokinesis)Amoeba (irregular plane), Paramecium (transverse plane), Leishmania (longitudinal plane); Pros: rapid reproduction; Cons: lack of genetic variation
Multiple FissionRepeated division of the nucleus under unfavourable conditions to form numerous daughter nuclei, released simultaneously upon return of favourable conditionsPlasmodium (malarial parasite), Amoeba (via encystment); Pros: rapid production of hundreds of offspring from a single parent
BuddingDevelopment of an outgrowth (bud) on the parent body, which derives nutrition and detaches as an independent organismYeast (unicellular fungus), Hydra (multicellular cnidarian); Pros: rapid population growth; Cons: high density leads to competition for resources
FragmentationBreaking of a mature filamentous organism into two or more pieces due to water currents or mechanical pressure, with each fragment growing into a new organismSpirogyra, Ulothrix (filamentous green algae); Pros: simple and natural dispersal; Cons: possible only in organisms with simple body organization
RegenerationReconstruction of an entire organism from a cut or damaged body part through specialized regenerative cells (stem cells / neoblasts)Planaria (flatworm), Hydra, Starfish (arm attached to central disc); Pros: protection against accidental organ loss and reproduction; Cons: limited in higher organisms
Spore FormationFormation and dispersal of spherical, microscopic spores enclosed in a thick protective wall inside a sporangiumRhizopus (bread mould), Mucor, Ferns, Mosses; Pros: long-term survival in harsh dry conditions and wide dispersal by wind

Key Facts and Contributions
Reproduction 1
Asexual Reproduction MethodProcessExamples / Pros & Cons
Natural Vegetative Propagation (Tuber / Rhizome / Bulb)Sprouting of new shoots and roots from nodes and buds present on modified underground plant stemsPotato (eyes on stem tuber), Ginger and Turmeric (rhizome), Onion and Garlic (bulb); Pros: multiplication of seedless plants and food storage
Natural Vegetative Propagation (By Leaves)Germination of adventitious buds located in the marginal notches of leaves when placed on moist soilBryophyllum, Begonia; Pros: automatic generation of a nursery of new plantlets as soon as a leaf falls; Cons: local overcrowding
Natural Vegetative Propagation (Runner / Offset)Emergence of roots downward and leaves upward from the nodes of horizontal aerial or sub-aerial stems creeping along the soil or water surfaceCynodon (doob grass) and Strawberry (runners), Water Hyacinth / Pistia (offsets); extremely rapid spread of water hyacinth in water bodies (Terror of Bengal)
Artificial Vegetative Propagation (Cutting / Layering)Planting a detached stem segment in soil (Cutting) or bending an intact branch into soil to induce rooting (Layering)Rose, Sugarcane, Grapes (cutting); Jasmine, Mogra, Lemon (layering / gootee); Pros: 100% faithful preservation of superior parental traits
GraftingJoining a shoot of a superior variety (Scion) onto a rooted base plant (Stock) by aligning their cambium layersMango, Apple, Guava, Orange; possible only in dicotyledonous plants (due to presence of cambium); Pros: tasty fruits on disease-resistant rootstock
Tissue Culture (Micropropagation)Culturing cells from the apical meristem of a plant in a hormone-rich nutrient medium to form a callus and grow new plantletsOrchid, Banana, Ornamental plants, Dahlia; Pros: completely disease-free (virus-free) plants and production of millions of plants in a very short time
