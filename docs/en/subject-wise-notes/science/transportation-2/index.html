Circulatory System - Blood Journey from Heart to Capillaries (Transportation 2)

Human circulatory system, structure and physiology of the heart, blood vessels, double circulation, blood pressure, and heartbeat

🔍
Quick Reference
Key Facts & Contributions
Transportation 2
Circulatory StructureBlood Flow / Circulation ProcessFunction / Health Relevance
Right Atrium & Right Ventricle (Right Heart)Deoxygenated blood from superior and inferior vena cava enters the right atrium, then flows through the tricuspid valve into the right ventriclePumps blood to the lungs for oxygenation via the pulmonary artery; right ventricular failure occurs in pulmonary hypertension
Left Atrium & Left Ventricle (Left Heart)Oxygenated blood from four pulmonary veins enters the left atrium, then flows through the bicuspid (mitral) valve into the highly muscular left ventricleSupplies blood at high pressure to all body tissues via the aorta; left ventricle has the thickest wall (~3 times thicker)
ArteriesPulsatile blood flow at high pressure and high velocity away from the heart; thick, elastic, three-layered walls (valves absent)Carry oxygenated blood (exception: pulmonary artery); fat/cholesterol deposition leads to atherosclerosis and myocardial infarction
VeinsContinuous blood flow at low pressure toward the heart; thin walls, wide lumen, and semilunar valves preventing backflowCarry deoxygenated blood (exception: pulmonary veins); defective valves in leg veins cause varicose veins
Blood CapillariesMicroscopic vessels formed by a single layer of simple squamous endothelium; slowest velocity of blood flowActual site of diffusion and exchange of O₂, CO₂, glucose, and excretory wastes between blood and tissue fluid
Cardiac Muscle & Pacemaker (SA Node)Self-excitable nodal tissue in the upper corner of the right atrium; spontaneously generates 70–75 electrical impulses per minuteNatural pacemaker; initiates the cardiac cycle; an artificial lithium-ion pacemaker is implanted if it malfunctions

Key Facts & Contributions
Transportation 2
Circulatory StructureBlood Flow / Circulation ProcessFunction / Health Relevance
Pulmonary CirculationRight ventricle → Pulmonary artery → Lungs (O₂ exchange) → Pulmonary veins → Left atriumShort-distance, low-pressure circuit; oxygenation of deoxygenated blood in the lungs and elimination of CO₂
Systemic CirculationLeft ventricle → Aorta → Arteries → Tissue capillaries → Veins → Vena cava → Right atriumHigh-pressure main circulation; distributes nutrients, hormones, and gases to all tissues and removes metabolic wastes
Hepatic Portal SystemBlood from stomach and intestine capillaries goes directly to the liver via the hepatic portal vein before reaching the heartStorage of absorbed glucose as glycogen and detoxification of toxic substances arriving from the intestine
Coronary CirculationBlood supply to the heart muscles themselves via right and left coronary arteries arising from the base of the aortaSupplies O₂ and nutrients to myocardium; blockage causes angina pectoris (chest pain) and myocardial infarction (heart attack)
Blood Pressure Measurement (Sphygmomanometer)Measurement of systolic pressure (~120 mmHg) and diastolic pressure (~80 mmHg) on the brachial arteryReadings above 140/90 mmHg indicate hypertension; major risk factor for stroke and kidney failure
Electrocardiogram (ECG)Graphical representation of electrical potentials generated during the cardiac cycle (P-wave, QRS complex, T-wave)P-wave: atrial depolarization; QRS: ventricular depolarization; T-wave: ventricular repolarization; diagnostic test for arrhythmias and infarction
