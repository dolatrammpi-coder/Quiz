Reflection of LightAngle of incidence = Angle of reflection (∠i = ∠r); incident ray, reflected ray, and normal lie in the same planeBouncing back of light into the same medium from a polished surface; basis of image formation by mirrors
Plane MirrorFocal length f = ∞ ; Power P = 0 ; Magnification m = +1Virtual, erect, same-sized, and laterally inverted image; everyday looking-glass mirror and periscope
Number of Images in Plane Mirrorsn = (360°/θ) - 1 (if 360/θ is even) or based on symmetryKaleidoscope, parallel mirrors facing each other in salons; infinite images between parallel mirrors (θ = 0°)
Concave MirrorConverging nature; focal length f < 0 (negative)Magnified, virtual, and erect image when placed between pole and focus; shaving mirror, dentist's mirror, searchlight, solar cooker
Convex MirrorDiverging nature; focal length f > 0 (positive)Always forms a virtual, erect, and diminished image with a very wide field of view; vehicle rear-view mirrors and security mirrors
Curvature and Focus of Spherical Mirrorf = R / 2 (focal length is half of the radius of curvature)Fundamental geometric relation universally applicable to all small-aperture concave and convex spherical mirrors

Mirror Formula1/f = 1/v + 1/u (with sign convention for u, v, f)Relationship between object distance (u), image distance (v), and focal length (f); determining image position
