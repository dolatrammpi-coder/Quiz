Human Excretory System - Kidneys, Nephron, and Urine Formation Process (Excretion 1)

Concept of Excretion, Human Excretory System, Kidneys, Nephron, and Dialysis

Quick Reference
Key Facts and Contributions
Excretion 1
Excretory Organ/StructurePrimary ProcessFunction/Key Facts
KidneysContinuous blood filtration, osmoregulation, and chemical homeostasisTwo bean-shaped organs; retroperitoneal position; produce about 180 liters of filtrate per day
NephronPrimary functional unit of urine formation (Malpighian body + renal tubules)About 1 to 1.2 million microscopic tubules in each kidney that separate urea and toxic wastes from blood
Glomerulus and Bowman's CapsuleHigh-pressure ultrafiltration due to pressure difference between afferent and efferent arteriolesForm the Malpighian body; filtration of protein-free plasma through slit pores of podocytes
Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT)100% reabsorption of nutrients (glucose, amino acids) and 70-80% reabsorption of water and NaClLined by simple cuboidal brush-border epithelium; site of maximum reabsorption maintaining electrolyte balance
Loop of HenleTransport of water in descending limb and active/passive transport of NaCl in ascending limbWorks with vasa recta via the counter-current mechanism to produce concentrated urine
Ureters and Urinary BladderUrine transport by peristalsis and temporary storage in a muscular sac (500-800 ml)Elastic wall lined with transitional epithelium that regulates the micturition reflex

Key Facts and Contributions
Excretion 1
Excretory Organ/StructurePrimary ProcessFunction/Key Facts
UltrafiltrationNet Filtration Pressure (NFP ≈ 10-15 mmHg) across glomerular capillariesGFR = 125 ml/min or 180 L/day; filtration of entire plasma fluid except blood cells and large proteins
Selective ReabsorptionReabsorption of 99% of useful components (water, glucose, minerals) from filtrate back into bloodOut of 180 liters of initial filtrate, only about 1.5 liters of concentrated fluid remains as final urine
Tubular SecretionDirect secretion of H⁺, K⁺ ions, ammonia, and drugs from capillaries into the tubular lumenPrecise regulation of body fluid pH (acid-base balance) and ionic homeostasis
Micturition ReflexContraction of the detrusor muscle of the bladder and relaxation of internal/external urethral sphinctersInvoluntary spinal reflex under voluntary cortical control in adults
Hemodialysis (Artificial Kidney)Diffusion-based removal of urea from blood using semipermeable cellophane tubulesLife-saving blood purification technique for renal failure (uremia) patients; uses heparin
Accessory Excretory Organs (Liver & Skin)Urea synthesis in liver (ornithine cycle) and excretion of salts/water via sweat in skinExcretion of metabolic wastes through bile pigments (bilirubin/biliverdin), drugs, and sweat
