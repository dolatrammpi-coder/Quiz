Human Digestive System, Alimentary Canal, Digestive Glands, Enzymes, and Nutrient Absorption

Quick Reference
Key Facts & Contributions
Nutrition 2
Digestive OrganDigestive ProcessEnzyme / Nutrient Absorption Role
Oral CavityMastication (mechanical digestion) of food by teeth and mixing with saliva to form a swallowable 'bolus'Salivary amylase (ptyalin, pH 6.8) digests 30% of starch into maltose; lysozyme destroys bacteria
EsophagusTransports the bolus from the pharynx through the gastroesophageal sphincter into the stomach via peristalsisNo digestive enzymes secreted; only mucus lubricates the food passage and protects against friction
StomachMuscular churning mixes food with gastric juice to convert it into an acidic semi-liquid paste called 'chyme'HCl (pH 1.5–2.0) activates pepsinogen to pepsin; breaks down proteins into peptones and proteoses; Castle's intrinsic factor
Liver & GallbladderContinuous synthesis of bile juice and its concentration in the gallbladder; neutralizes chyme acidity and makes it alkalineContains no enzymes; bile salts (sodium glycocholate/taurocholate) emulsify fats; aids absorption of fat-soluble vitamins
PancreasSecretes pancreatic juice (pH 7.8–8.3 / complete digestive juice) into the duodenum; digests all major nutrient componentsTrypsin, chymotrypsin, carboxypeptidase (proteins); pancreatic amylase (carbohydrates); steapsin/lipase (emulsified fats)
Small IntestineSuccus entericus (intestinal juice) completes final conversion of nutrients into simplest absorbable unitsMaltase, lactase, sucrase (monosaccharides); dipeptidase (amino acids); intestinal lipase (fatty acids & glycerol); nucleosidase

Key Facts & Contributions
Nutrition 2
Digestive OrganDigestive ProcessEnzyme / Nutrient Absorption Role
Villi & MicrovilliInner mucosal folds of the small intestine; expand the absorptive surface area by approximately 600 timesGlucose and amino acids diffuse into blood capillaries toward the hepatic portal vein; fats enter lacteals (lymph) as chylomicrons
Metabolic Role of the LiverProcesses nutrients absorbed from the intestine, stores excess glucose, and detoxifies toxic metabolic wastesGlycogenesis (glycogen storage), urea cycle (ammonia to urea via ornithine cycle), synthesis of plasma proteins and heparin
Large IntestineAbsorbs water, mineral salts, and certain drugs from undigested residual 'chyle' and propels waste forwardSymbiotic bacteria (E. coli) synthesize vitamin K and vitamin B-complex; mucus lubricates feces
Gastrointestinal Hormonal RegulationLocal endocrine control of timely secretion of digestive juices and gastrointestinal motilityGastrin (stimulates gastric juice), secretin (pancreatic bicarbonate secretion), CCK-PZ (gallbladder contraction & enzyme secretion), GIP (gastric inhibitor)
Rectum & AnusTemporary storage of undigested excretory waste (feces) and periodic defecationStercobilin pigment gives feces their characteristic brown color; regulated by internal involuntary and external voluntary anal sphincters
Major Digestive Disorders & Clinical ConditionsDisorders caused by deficiency of digestive enzymes, infections, or impaired liver functionJaundice (elevated blood bilirubin), peptic ulcer (H. pylori infection), lactose intolerance (absence of lactase enzyme)
