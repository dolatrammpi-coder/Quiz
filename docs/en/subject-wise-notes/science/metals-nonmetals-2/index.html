Metal Extraction StageProcessProduct Obtained / Key Facts
Froth Flotation ConcentrationSeparation of sulphide ores from water by creating froth using pine oilZinc blende (ZnS), copper pyrites (CuFeS₂), galena (PbS); depression of ZnS using depressant NaCN
LeachingDissolving the ore in a chemical solvent (NaOH / NaCN) to separate insoluble impuritiesBayer's process for bauxite (pure alumina from Al₂O₃·2H₂O); leaching of gold and silver in the MacArthur-Forrest process
CalcinationHeating carbonate and hydrated ores below their melting point in the absence or limited supply of airCalamine (ZnCO₃ → ZnO + CO₂); removal of moisture, water vapour, and volatile impurities; ore becomes porous
RoastingStrongly heating sulphide ores below their melting point in a controlled excess of air2ZnS + 3O₂ → 2ZnO + 2SO₂; removal of sulphur and arsenic impurities as volatile oxides
Smelting in Blast FurnaceReduction of haematite (Fe₂O₃) at high temperature with coke (reducing agent) and limestone (flux)Pig iron (~4% C); conversion of silica impurity into calcium silicate slag (CaSiO₃)
Self-ReductionSpontaneous reaction of partially roasted sulphide ore with the remaining sulphide to liberate metalCopper (Cu₂S + 2Cu₂O → 6Cu + SO₂); blister copper obtained in the Bessemer converter

Important Facts and Contributions
Metals Nonmetals 2
Metal Extraction StageProcessProduct Obtained / Key Facts
Hall-Heroult ElectrolysisElectrolysis of molten alumina (Al₂O₃) mixed with cryolite (Na₃AlF₆) and fluorspar using carbon electrodes99.5% pure aluminium at the cathode; cryolite lowers the melting point from 2050°C to 950°C and increases conductivity
Electrolytic RefiningPassing electric current through acidic copper sulphate using impure metal as anode and a thin strip of pure metal as cathode99.99% ultra-pure copper at the cathode; deposition of 'Anode Mud' containing precious gold and silver below the anode
Zone RefiningMelting a metal rod using a mobile circular heater; impurities are more soluble in the molten zoneSilicon (Si), germanium (Ge), gallium (Ga); 99.9999% purity for transistor and semiconductor industries
Mond's ProcessHeating impure nickel with carbon monoxide (CO) at 330-350 K to form a volatile carbonylNi + 4CO → Ni(CO)₄; decomposes upon reheating at 450-470 K to yield 100% pure nickel metal
Van Arkel ProcessHeating titanium or zirconium with iodine to form volatile tetraiodide and decomposing it on a heated filamentUltra-pure titanium (Ti) and zirconium (Zr) free from oxygen and nitrogen for space and nuclear reactors
AlloyingMelting a base metal with other metals or carbon in a fixed proportion to form a homogeneous mixtureStainless steel (corrosion-resistant), brass, bronze, solder (low melting point), nitinol (shape memory)
