History and Modern Form of the Periodic Table - Dobereiner to Moseley (Periodic Table 1)

Periodic Classification of Elements, Dobereiner, Newlands, Mendeleev, and Structure of the Modern Periodic Table

Quick Reference
Key Facts & Contributions
Periodic Table 1
Periodic Table / ScientistKey Principle or ContributionLimitations / Significance
Dobereiner's Law of Triads (1817–1829)In a group of three elements with similar properties, the atomic weight of the middle element is the arithmetic mean of the atomic weights of the first and third elementsFirst attempt to relate properties of elements to atomic weight; Limitation: triads could be formed for only a few elements (e.g., Li-Na-K, Ca-Sr-Ba, Cl-Br-I)
Newlands' Law of Octaves (1864–1865)When elements are arranged in increasing order of atomic weight, every 8th element has properties similar to the first element (analogous to seven musical notes)Initiated the concept of periodicity; Limitation: applicable only up to calcium (Ca, Z=20), no space for new elements, failed after discovery of noble gases
Lothar Meyer's Curve (1869)Plot between atomic volume and atomic weight of elements; strong alkali metals at the peaks and halogens on the ascending portions of the curveGraphical representation of regular periodicity of physical properties; reached conclusions similar to Mendeleev but received less recognition due to delayed publication
Mendeleev's Original Periodic Table (1869)Physical and chemical properties of elements are periodic functions of their atomic weights; systematic arrangement of 63 elements into 8 groups and 6 periodsSystematic foundation for chemical study, accurate prediction of new elements (Ga, Sc, Ge); Limitations: uncertain position of hydrogen, no place for isotopes, heavier elements placed before lighter ones
Henry Moseley's Modern Periodic Law (1913)Characteristic X-ray spectrum experiment (√ν = a(Z - b)); the fundamental property of elements is atomic number (Z / proton count), not atomic weightLogical resolution of most anomalies in Mendeleev's periodic table (isotopes, Co-Ni order); scientific basis of the modern periodic table
Long Form of Modern Periodic Table (Bohr Model)Classification based on electronic configuration; 18 vertical groups and 7 horizontal periods; division of elements into four main blocks (s, p, d, f)Complete systematic classification of all 118 currently known elements; accurate analysis of chemical reactions, valency, and periodic trends

Periodic Table 1
Periodic Table / ScientistKey Principle or ContributionLimitations / Significance
s-Block Elements (Groups 1 and 2)ns¹ (alkali metals) and ns² (alkaline earth metals) configuration in the outermost shell; highly electropositive and strong reducing agentsReactive metals (Na, K, Ca, Mg); soft nature, low melting points, impart characteristic colors in flame tests; vigorous hydrogen liberation with water
p-Block Elements (Groups 13 to 18)ns² np¹⁻⁶ configuration in the outermost shell; the only block in the periodic table containing metals, non-metals, and metalloidsOxygen (O₂), basis of life (C), fertilizer components (N, P), noble gases (Group 18), and most electronegative halogens (Group 17)
d-Block Elements (Groups 3 to 12 / Transition Elements)(n-1)d¹⁻¹⁰ ns¹⁻² configuration in the penultimate shell; act as a bridge (transition) between s and p blocksVariable valency, formation of colored ions, paramagnetic properties, complex compounds, and industrial catalysts (Fe, Pt, Ni, V₂O₅)
f-Block Elements (Inner Transition Elements)Electrons enter the third-to-outermost shell (n-2)f¹⁻¹⁴; two horizontal rows placed below the main table (lanthanides and actinides)Lanthanide contraction (gradual decrease in atomic size); all actinides are radioactive and include synthetic transuranic elements
Zero Group / Group 18 (Noble Gases)Completely filled outermost shell (ns² np⁶, 1s² in helium); zero valency, chemically inert monoatomic gasesHelium, neon, argon, krypton, xenon, radon; refrigeration, inert atmospheres, advertising signs, and cancer radiotherapy
Mendeleev's Eka-PredictionsLeft blank spaces for undiscovered elements and predicted their properties in advance: Eka-boron (Sc), Eka-aluminium (Ga), Eka-silicon (Ge)Upon discovery, the properties of actual elements (scandium, gallium, germanium) matched Mendeleev's predictions completely
