Sexual Reproduction - From Flower Pollination to Embryo Formation (Reproduction 2)

Sexual Reproduction, Flower Structure, Pollination, Double Fertilization, Embryo Formation, and Human Gamete Physiology

Quick Reference
Key Facts and Contributions
Reproduction 2
Sexual Reproduction Stage / StructureProcessOutcome / Biological Significance
MeiosisHalving of chromosome number in reproductive mother cells (2n) to produce haploid (n) gametesMaintenance of constant chromosome number across generations; new gene combinations and genetic variation via crossing over
Androecium & StamenProduction of pollen grains (microspores / male gametophyte) via meiosis in the microsporangia of the antherProduction of male gametes (sperm / pollen nuclei) and formation of a protective exine (sporopollenin) for pollination
Gynoecium & Carpel / PistilDevelopment of a 7-celled, 8-nucleate female gametophyte (embryo sac) inside the ovule within the ovary via megasporogenesisProtection of the egg cell (female gamete) and attraction of the pollen tube via chemical signals (chemotropism)
PollinationTransfer of mature pollen grains from the anther to the stigma of the same flower or another flowerPrerequisite for fertilization; genetic purity via self-pollination and higher vigour and hybrid vigour (heterosis) via cross-pollination
Pollen Tube GrowthGermination of the pollen grain by absorbing sugary secretions of the stigma and growth of the pollen tube through the style to the ovuleSafe delivery of two non-motile male gametes to the synergids of the embryo sac
Double FertilizationFirst male gamete (n) + egg cell (n) = zygote (2n); second male gamete (n) + 2 polar nuclei (2n) = primary endosperm nucleus (3n)Unique feature of angiosperms; formation of both a diploid embryo and a triploid nutritive tissue (endosperm)

Key Facts and Contributions
Reproduction 2
Sexual Reproduction Stage / StructureProcessOutcome / Biological Significance
Post-Fertilization Fruit & Seed FormationGradual transformation of the ovary wall into the pericarp (fruit wall) and the ovule into the seed after fertilizationProtected dormancy period for the embryo; long-distance dispersal and spread of seeds and fruits via various agents
Sperm (Human Male Gamete)Formation of a tailed, motile, haploid (23 chromosomes) cell via spermatogenesis in the seminiferous tubules of the testisPenetration of egg membranes by acrosome (hyaluronidase enzyme); carrier of paternal genetic DNA (22+X or 22+Y)
Ovum (Human Female Gamete)Large, non-motile, nutrient-rich haploid cell formed via oogenesis in the ovary (surrounded by corona radiata and zona pellucida)Provides maternal genetic contribution (22+X) along with complete cytoplasm and mitochondria for early cleavage of the developing embryo
Human FertilizationFusion of pronuclei of sperm and ovum (syngamy) in the ampulla region of the oviduct (fallopian tube)Formation of diploid zygote (46 chromosomes); restoration of diploidy and sex determination (XX or XY)
Cleavage & ImplantationRapid mitotic divisions of the zygote (morula → blastocyst) and embedding into the endometrial layer of the uterusInitiation of placenta formation; continuous exchange of nutrients, respiratory gases, and wastes between maternal blood and the embryo
Evolutionary Significance of Sexual ReproductionFusion of genomes from two distinct individuals, genetic recombination (crossing over), and random gamete assortmentGeneration of genetic variation in populations; foundation for natural selection, disease resistance, and emergence of new species
