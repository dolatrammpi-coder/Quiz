Modern Physics 1
Nuclear Concept / RadiationProcess or PropertyApplications / Safety Facts
Alpha (α) RadiationHelium nucleus (⁴₂He²⁺); mass 4 u, charge +2e; highest ionizing power and lowest penetrating powerAmericium-241 smoke detectors; completely blocked by skin or a thin sheet of paper; fatal if ingested internally
Beta (β) RadiationFast-moving electrons (β⁻) or positrons (β⁺); moderate ionizing and penetrating power; continuous energy spectrumIndustrial thickness gauges, cancer brachytherapy; safely absorbed by an aluminium sheet a few millimetres thick
Gamma (γ) RadiationHigh-energy massless and uncharged electromagnetic photons; speed of light; highest penetrating powerCobalt-60 therapy (Gamma Knife), food irradiation sterilization; requires thick lead (Pb) or heavy concrete walls
Nuclear FissionSplitting of a heavy nucleus (²³⁵U) by a slow neutron into medium-mass nuclei + 2-3 new neutrons + ~200 MeV energyControlled chain reaction in nuclear power plants; uncontrolled fission forms the basis of the atomic bomb (Manhattan Project)
Nuclear FusionCombining of light nuclei (deuterium + tritium) at ultra-high temperature (~10⁷ K) and pressure (²H + ³H → ⁴He + n + 17.6 MeV)Inexhaustible source of energy in the Sun and stars; hydrogen bomb; ITER and Tokamak projects for future green energy
Radiocarbon Dating (C-14)Measuring the decay of ¹⁴C activity in dead organic remains; half-life T½ = 5730 yearsAccurate determination of the ancient age of wood, fossils, and archaeological samples up to about 50,000 years
