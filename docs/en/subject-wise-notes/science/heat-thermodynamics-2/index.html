Laws of Thermodynamics - From Zeroth to Second Law (Heat Thermodynamics 2)

Laws of thermodynamics, internal energy, heat engines, refrigerators, and entropy

🔍
Quick Reference
Key Facts & Principles
Heat Thermodynamics 2
Thermodynamic Law / ProcessStatement / RelationKey Applications / Consequences
Zeroth LawIf two systems A and B are separately in thermal equilibrium with a third system C, then A and B are in thermal equilibrium with each otherBasis for the concept of temperature; scientific foundation for temperature measurement using a thermometer
First LawHeat supplied to a system equals the sum of the increase in internal energy and external work done (ΔQ = ΔU + ΔW)Expression of the law of conservation of energy; Perpetual Motion Machine of the First Kind (PMM-1) is impossible
Isothermal ProcessTemperature remains constant (ΔT = 0, ΔU = 0); obeys Boyle's law (ΔQ = ΔW = nRT ln(V2/V1))Phase changes (melting/boiling); all heat absorbed by an ideal gas is converted into external work
Adiabatic ProcessNo heat exchange with the surroundings (ΔQ = 0); PV^γ = constant and ΔW = -ΔUCooling of air when a bicycle tube bursts, propagation of sound in air (Laplace's correction), diesel compression
Isochoric ProcessVolume remains constant (ΔV = 0), so external work W = PΔV = 0; all heat ΔQ = ΔUHeating gas in a closed rigid container; determination of molar specific heat at constant volume (Cv)

Key Facts & Principles
Heat Thermodynamics 2
Thermodynamic Law / ProcessStatement / RelationKey Applications / Consequences
Second Law (Kelvin-Planck)It is impossible to construct a cyclic device that extracts heat from a single reservoir and converts it 100% into workEfficiency of a heat engine is always less than 100% (η < 1); prohibition of PMM-2
Second Law (Clausius Statement)Heat cannot flow spontaneously from a colder body to a hotter body without external mechanical workFundamental governing principle for the operation of refrigerators and heat pumps
Carnot Theorem & Ideal CycleNo engine operating between given temperatures (T1 and T2) can be more efficient than a Carnot engine (η = 1 - T2/T1)Sets the upper limit on the maximum possible theoretical efficiency of heat engines
Law of EntropyMeasure of disorder or randomness of a system (dS = dQ/T); for an isolated system, ΔS ≥ 0 alwaysDetermines spontaneity and irreversibility of natural processes (Arrow of Time)
Third LawAt absolute zero (0 K), the entropy of a perfectly crystalline substance becomes zero (Nernst Theorem)Calculation of absolute entropy of substances; impossibility of reaching 0 K in a finite number of steps
