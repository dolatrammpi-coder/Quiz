Newton's Third Law and Conservation of Momentum - From Action-Reaction to Rocket Propulsion (Laws Of Motion 2)

Newton's third law, conservation of momentum, recoil of a gun, rocket propulsion, and collisions

🔍
Quick Reference
Key Facts & Contributions
Laws Of Motion 2
Law / Conservation PrinciplePhysical ExplanationApplications / Examples
Newton's Third Law of Motion (Action-Reaction)To every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction (F_AB = -F_BA); forces always act in pairs on two different bodiesWalking on the ground, swimming, a boat moving backward when jumping onto the shore, flight of birds
Law of Conservation of Linear MomentumIf no external unbalanced force acts on an isolated system (F_ext = 0), the total momentum of the system remains constant (Σ p = constant)Recoil of a gun, propulsion of rockets and jet aircraft, explosion of a bomb, collision of billiard balls
Recoil of a GunWhen a bullet moves forward, the gun moves backward to balance momentum; V_gun = -(m/M) v_bulletLower recoil velocity of a heavy gun; firing with the gun pressed firmly against the shoulder; hydraulic shock absorbers in cannons
Rocket PropulsionVariable mass system; forward thrust on the rocket opposes the momentum of high-velocity exhaust gases (F = v_rel · dm/dt)Spacecraft and satellite launches (PSLV, GSLV); independent propulsion even in a vacuum
Perfectly Elastic CollisionBoth linear momentum and kinetic energy remain completely conserved during the collision; coefficient of restitution e = 1Collisions of atomic and subatomic particles (electrons, neutrons); Newton's Cradle
Perfectly Inelastic CollisionLinear momentum is conserved, but there is maximum loss of kinetic energy; bodies stick together after collision (e = 0)Wet clay sticking to a wall; a bullet embedding into a wooden block and moving together with it
