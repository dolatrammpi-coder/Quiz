Electric Current, Voltage and Resistance - Fundamentals of Electricity (Electricity 1)

Electric Charge, Current, Potential Difference, Ohm's Law, Resistance, and Resistivity

🔍
Quick Reference
Key Facts and Contributions
Electricity 1
Electrical QuantityFormula / UnitKey Factors or Applications
Electric ChargeQ = I × t = ne / Coulomb (C)Fundamental property of matter; arises from electron transfer; 1 C contains 6.25 × 10^18 electrons
Electric CurrentI = Q / t / Ampere (A or C/s)Rate of flow of charge; measured in series in a circuit using an ammeter; scalar quantity
Electric Potential DifferenceV = W / Q / Volt (V or J/C)Work done in moving a unit charge from one point to another; measured in parallel using a voltmeter
Electrical ResistanceR = V / I / Ohm (Ω or V/A)Opposition offered by a conductor to current flow; depends on length, cross-sectional area, material, and temperature
Electrical ConductanceG = 1 / R / Siemens (S or mho or Ω^-1)Measure of ease of current flow; reciprocal of resistance

Key Facts and Contributions
Electricity 1
Electrical QuantityFormula / UnitKey Factors or Applications
Resistivity (Specific Resistance)ρ = R·A / l / Ohm-metre (Ω·m)Depends only on the nature of the material and temperature (not dimensions); high in nichrome, lowest in silver
Electrical Conductivity (Specific Conductance)σ = 1 / ρ / Siemens per metre (S/m or Ω^-1·m^-1)Intrinsic ability of a material to conduct current; highest in silver and copper
Ohm's LawV = IR (at constant temperature and physical conditions)Linear relationship between V and I in metallic conductors; diodes, transistors, and semiconductors are exceptions
Temperature Coefficient of Resistance (α)Rt = R0(1 + αΔT) / per °C or K^-1Positive in pure metals (R increases with temperature), negative in semiconductors (R decreases with temperature)
SuperconductivityR = 0 (at T < Tc) / Zero resistanceCurrent transmission without energy loss below critical temperature; used in MRI machines and bullet trains (Maglev)
