Key Facts & Contributions
Measurement Units 1
Physical Quantity / UnitDefinition or FormulaMeasuring Instrument / Use
Length / Metre (m)Distance travelled by light in vacuum in 1/299,792,458 of a secondMetre scale, Vernier callipers (fine thickness), Screw gauge (diameter of thin wire)
Mass / Kilogram (kg)Defined by fixing the numerical value of the Planck constant h (6.62607015 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s) (2019 redefinition)Beam balance, Kibble balance, spring balance
Time / Second (s)9,192,631,770 periods of radiation corresponding to the transition between two hyperfine levels of the ground state of the Caesium-133 atomCaesium atomic clock, stopwatch, quartz clock
Electric Current / Ampere (A)Defined by fixing the numerical value of the elementary electric charge e (1.602176634 × 10⁻¹⁹ C)Ammeter (connected in series), Galvanometer (detecting weak currents)
Thermodynamic Temperature / Kelvin (K)Defined by fixing the numerical value of the Boltzmann constant k (1.380649 × 10⁻²³ J/K)Gas thermometer, platinum resistance thermometer, total radiation pyrometer (high temperatures)
Luminous Intensity / Candela (cd)Luminous intensity of a source emitting monochromatic radiation of frequency 540 × 10¹² Hz with a radiant intensity of 1/683 W/srPhotometer, Lux meter (measuring illuminance levels)
Amount of Substance / Mole (mol)Amount of substance containing exactly 6.02214076 × 10²³ elementary entities (Avogadro constant NA)Stoichiometry, analytical chemical calculations, and molecular composition
