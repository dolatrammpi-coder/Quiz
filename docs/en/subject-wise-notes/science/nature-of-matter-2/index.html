Mixtures, Elements, and Compounds - Identifying Pure Substances (Nature Of Matter 2)

Pure substances, elements and compounds, homogeneous and heterogeneous mixtures, colloids, and separation techniques

🔍
Quick Reference
Key Facts and Contributions
Nature Of Matter 2
Type of Matter / MixtureStructural CharacteristicSeparation / Examples
ElementsPure substances composed of only one type of atom; cannot be broken down into simpler substances by chemical methodsGold (Au), silver (Ag), copper (Cu), iron (Fe), oxygen (O₂), hydrogen (H₂); metals, non-metals, and metalloids
CompoundsFormed by chemical combination of two or more elements in a fixed mass ratio; original properties of constituent elements are completely lostSeparated by chemical or electrolytic methods; water (H₂O - 1:8 mass ratio), common salt (NaCl), methane (CH₄)
Homogeneous Mixture (True Solution)Uniform composition and distribution of components throughout the mixture; particle size < 1 nm; invisible to naked eyes and microscopesSeparated by physical methods (evaporation/distillation); salt-water solution, sugar-water syrup, air, brass (alloy)
Heterogeneous Mixture (Suspension)Non-uniform distribution of components; particle size > 100 nm; settles down under gravity and scatters lightSeparated by simple filtration; muddy water, silty river water, chalk-water mixture, paint
Colloidal Solutions (Colloids)Appear homogeneous but are actually heterogeneous; particle size 1 to 100 nm; exhibit Tyndall effect and Brownian motionSeparated by centrifugation; milk, blood, gum, butter, ink, fog, aerosol
AlloysSolid-in-solid homogeneous mixture of two or more metals, or a metal and a non-metal; variable ratio of constituentsFractional crystallization or melting; brass (Cu 70% + Zn 30%), bronze (Cu + Sn), stainless steel (Fe + Cr + Ni + C)

Key Facts and Contributions
Nature Of Matter 2
Type of Matter / MixtureStructural CharacteristicSeparation / Examples
Simple DistillationHeating a mixture of volatile liquids to vaporize and condensing the vapour to separate them (difference in boiling points > 25 K)Separation of acetone (boiling point 56°C) and water (100°C); obtaining distilled water from seawater
Fractional DistillationUses a fractionating column for miscible liquids with a boiling point difference of less than 25 KSeparation of petrol, diesel, and kerosene from crude petroleum; extraction of oxygen, nitrogen, and argon from liquefied air
Steam DistillationFor organic compounds that are immiscible with water, volatile in steam, and boil below their normal boiling pointExtraction of rose water and natural essential oils; purification of aniline and turpentine oil
ChromatographyBased on differences in adsorption/solubility rates of solutes across stationary and mobile phasesSeparation of different dyes in ink, detection of drugs from blood, separation of chlorophyll pigments
CrystallizationSlowly cooling a hot saturated solution to form pure solid crystals while leaving impurities dissolved in the solutionPurification of impure sea salt; obtaining pure crystals of impure copper sulphate (blue vitriol) and alum
Separating FunnelFormation of distinct layers by immiscible liquids based on differences in their densitiesSeparation of kerosene and water, petrol and water, and carbon tetrachloride and water mixtures
