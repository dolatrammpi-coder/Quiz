Work, Energy, and Power - From Joule to Kinetic and Potential Energy (Work Energy Power 1)

Concept of Work, Energy, Kinetic and Potential Energy, Work-Energy Theorem, and Power

🔍
Quick Reference
Key Facts and Contributions
Work Energy Power 1
Physical QuantityFormula / UnitRelation to Energy / Power
Work (W)W = F · s · cos θ ; SI unit: Joule (J) ; CGS unit: ergScalar quantity; depends on the angle between force and displacement
Kinetic Energy (K)K = ½ m v² = p² / (2m) ; SI unit: Joule (J)Directly proportional to mass and the square of velocity; directly related to change in momentum
Gravitational Potential Energy (U)U = m · g · h ; SI unit: Joule (J)Depends on the height of the object above a reference level; energy stored in a gravitational field
Work-Energy TheoremTotal work done = Change in kinetic energy (W = ΔK)Work done by a force directly changes the speed and kinetic energy of the object
Power (P)P = W / t = F · v ; SI unit: Watt (W) ; Practical unit: 1 HP = 746 WRate of doing work; equal to the dot product of force and velocity
Conservation of Mechanical EnergyE = K + U = Constant (in an ideal frictionless system)Interconversion between kinetic and potential energy; total mechanical energy remains conserved
