Newton's Laws of Motion - Inertia, Momentum, and Force (Laws Of Motion 1)

Force, balanced and unbalanced forces, inertia, momentum, and Newton's first and second laws

🔍
Quick Reference
Key Facts & Contributions
Laws Of Motion 1
Force / Motion ConceptLaw / EquationEveryday Life Example
Inertia of RestAt external force F_ext = 0, a body remains at restPassengers lurch backward when a bus starts suddenly; dust particles fall when a carpet is beaten with a stick
Inertia of MotionAt external force F_ext = 0, uniform motion along a straight linePassengers lean forward when a fast-moving bus stops suddenly; a long jumper runs before jumping
Inertia of DirectionDirection of motion cannot change spontaneously without an external forcePassengers lean outward when a vehicle takes a sharp turn; mud flies tangentially off a spinning wheel
Balanced ForcesΣ F = 0 (net force is zero, acceleration a = 0)Both teams pulling with equal force in tug-of-war; a heavy book resting stationary on a table
Unbalanced ForcesΣ F ≠ 0 (non-zero net force, acceleration a = F/m)Kicking a football to set it in motion; a bicycle coming to rest when brakes are applied
Newton's First Law of MotionF_net = 0 ⇒ dv/dt = 0 (Law of Inertia / Galileo's Law)A ball rolling on a smooth floor maintains motion until friction stops it

Linear Momentump = m × v (SI unit: kg·m/s; vector quantity)Stopping a heavy cricket ball is harder than stopping a tennis ball moving at the same velocity
