Chemical Properties and States - Science of Change (Properties Of Matter 2)

Chemical Properties, Physical and Chemical Changes, Phase Transitions, Latent Heat, Melting, Boiling, and Sublimation

Quick Reference
Important Facts and Contributions
Properties Of Matter 2
Change / StateNature of ChangeExamples / Basis of Identification
Physical ChangeReversible; no new chemical substance is formed; chemical composition and molecular identity remain completely unchangedMelting of ice, evaporation of water, dissolving sugar/salt in water, melting of wax, magnetization of iron, breaking of glass
Chemical ChangeGenerally irreversible; breaking and rearrangement of chemical bonds; formation of entirely new substances with new propertiesRusting of iron, curdling of milk, digestion of food, burning of wood/paper, ripening of fruits, fermentation
Both Changes (Physical & Chemical)Simultaneous coexistence of physical state change and chemical combustionBurning of a candle (melting and solidifying of wax = physical change; burning of wick and vaporized wax to yield CO₂ and water vapor = chemical change)
Melting / FusionEndothermic phase transition (Solid → Liquid); absorption of latent heat of fusion against lattice energy at a constant melting pointConversion of ice into water at 0°C (latent heat = 80 cal/g or 3.34 × 10⁵ J/kg); contraction in volume when ice melts
BoilingEndothermic bulk phenomenon; occurs at a fixed boiling point when internal vapor pressure equals external atmospheric pressureBoiling of water at 100°C at 1 atm; rapid bubbles rising from the entire volume of liquid; increase in boiling point with increasing external pressure
EvaporationSpontaneous endothermic surface phenomenon; escape of high-kinetic-energy molecules into vapor at any temperature below the boiling pointCooling of water in an earthen pot, cooling of the body through sweat, drying of wet clothes; cooling caused by drawing latent heat from surroundings

Important Facts and Contributions
Properties Of Matter 2
Change / StateNature of ChangeExamples / Basis of Identification
SublimationDirect conversion of a solid into gaseous vapor without passing through the liquid state; endothermic process (omission of intermediate liquid phase)Camphor, naphthalene balls, ammonium chloride (sal ammoniac), solid CO₂ (dry ice), and iodine turning directly into vapor upon heating
DepositionDirect conversion of a gas into solid crystals without turning into a liquid (reverse of sublimation); exothermic processFormation of frost on windows and leaves on winter nights; deposition of soot inside industrial chimneys
CondensationConversion of vapor/gas into liquid state upon cooling; exothermic (release of latent heat of vaporization, 540 cal/g, into the environment)Cloud formation, rain, fog, dew drops, appearance of water droplets on the outer wall of a glass containing ice
Freezing / SolidificationConversion of a liquid into solid state upon cooling; exothermic process (release of latent heat of fusion)Freezing of water into ice at 0°C; anomalous behavior of water (9% expansion in volume and decrease in density upon freezing)
Chemical CorrosionSpontaneous degradation of metal surfaces into undesirable compounds under the influence of environmental moisture, O₂, CO₂, and H₂SBrown rust on iron (Fe₂O₃·xH₂O), green basic copper carbonate on copper, black silver sulfide (Ag₂S) coating on silver ornaments
RanciditySlow oxidation of fat- and oil-containing food items by atmospheric oxygenSpoiling of taste and odor in butter, chips, and snacks; prevented using nitrogen gas flushing and antioxidants like BHA/BHT
