Gravitation 1
Gravitational ConceptLaw/FormulaGoverning Factors/Significance
Newton's Universal Law of GravitationF = G(m1·m2) / r²Directly proportional to product of masses and inversely proportional to square of distance; purely attractive in nature
Universal Gravitational Constant (G)G = F·r² / (m1·m2) = 6.674 × 10^-11 N·m²/kg²Completely independent of medium, temperature, pressure, or chemical composition; measured by Henry Cavendish (1798) using torsion balance
Central and Conservative Force NatureF_vec = -G(m1·m2/r²) r_hat ; ∮ F·dr = 0Acts along the line joining centers of mass of both bodies; total work done over a closed loop is zero
Inverse Square LawF ∝ 1 / r²Doubling the distance reduces force to 1/4, while halving the distance increases force 4 times
Planetary Orbital MotionF_gravity = m·v² / r (provides centripetal force)Regular and stable revolution of planets around the Sun and of the Moon and artificial satellites around Earth
Origin of Ocean TidesF_tide ∝ M / r³ (differential gravitational pull)Periodic rise and fall of Earth's ocean waters due to the gravitational pull of the Moon and the Sun

Key Facts and Contributions
Gravitation 1
Gravitational ConceptLaw/FormulaGoverning Factors/Significance
Gravitational Field Intensity (E)E = F / m = GM / r² (SI unit: N/kg or m/s²)Gravitational force acting per unit mass; decreases with distance according to the inverse square law
Gravitational Potential (V)V = -GM / r (SI unit: J/kg)Work done in bringing unit mass from infinity; always negative due to attractive nature (zero at infinity)
Gravitational Potential Energy (U)U = -GMm / r (near Earth's surface U = mgh)Mechanical energy stored in a system due to mutual gravitational attraction between two or more bodies
Kepler's Third Law (Law of Periods)T² ∝ r³ (T²/r³ = constant)Rapid increase in orbital period with increasing mean distance from the Sun; theoretical basis for the inverse square law
Atmospheric Gaseous Envelopev_rms < v_escape (thermal velocity of gas molecules < escape velocity)Earth's gravitational pull retains the life-sustaining atmosphere and prevents it from escaping into space
Comparison of Four Fundamental ForcesF_g : F_w : F_em : F_s = 1 : 10^25 : 10^36 : 10^38Gravitation is the weakest fundamental force, yet infinite range and cumulative mass make it the primary regulator of cosmic motion
