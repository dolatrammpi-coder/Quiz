Electromagnetic Induction - From Faraday to Generators and Motors (Magnetism 2)

Electromagnetic Induction, Faraday's Laws, Fleming's Rules, Electric Generators, and Electric Motors

🔍
Quick Reference
Key Facts & Contributions
Magnetism 2
Electromagnetic PhenomenonLaw / DirectionApplications in Generators / Motors
Electromagnetic Induction (EMI)Faraday's Law: e = -dΦ/dt (induced EMF proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux)Basis of electric generators (dynamos), transformers, induction cooktops, and guitar pickups
Direction of Induced CurrentLenz's Law: Induced current always opposes the cause that produces itManifestation of conservation of energy; conversion of mechanical energy to electrical energy in generators, magnetic braking
Fleming's Right-Hand RuleThumb (motion of conductor), Forefinger (magnetic field B), Middle finger (direction of induced current I)Precise determination of the direction of induced current in the armature of AC and DC generators
Fleming's Left-Hand RuleThumb (magnetic force F), Forefinger (field B), Middle finger (flowing current I) - 'FBI' configurationDetermining the torque and direction of rotation of the armature coil in an electric motor (DC motor)
Electric MotorTorque on a current-carrying coil in a magnetic field (τ = NIAB sinθ); electrical energy → mechanical energyOperation of electric fans, washing machines, mixers, water pumps, submersible pumps, and electric vehicles (EVs)
Electric Generator (Dynamo)Rotation of an armature in a magnetic field (EMI principle); mechanical energy → electrical energyProduction of alternating current (AC) or direct current (DC) in thermal, hydroelectric, and nuclear power plants

Key Facts & Contributions
Magnetism 2
Electromagnetic PhenomenonLaw / DirectionApplications in Generators / Motors
Eddy CurrentsCircular localized currents (Foucault currents) induced in bulk metal pieces placed in a changing magnetic fieldMagnetic brakes in metro trains, melting metals in induction furnaces, dead-beat galvanometers, speedometers
Mutual InductionInduced EMF in a secondary coil due to a change of current in the primary coil (e = -M di/dt)Transformers (step-up and step-down), ignition coils, and wireless mobile charging systems
Self-InductionOpposition to the change of current in the coil itself (electrical inertia; e = -L di/dt)Choke coils in tube lights, inductor coils, and current control in electrical circuits
Split-Ring CommutatorAutomatically reverses the direction of current in the armature coil after every half rotationMaintaining continuous unidirectional rotation in DC motors and converting AC to DC in DC generators
Slip RingsContinuous transmission of current from a rotating armature to an external circuit without reversing directionDelivering pure alternating current to the external load circuit in an AC generator (alternator)
