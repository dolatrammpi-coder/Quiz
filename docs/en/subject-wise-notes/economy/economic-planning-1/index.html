Economic Planning Part 1

Meaning of economic planning, post-Independence necessity, objectives of planned development, and comprehensive analysis of the allocation of scarce resources

🔍
Quick Reference
Key Facts & Contributions
Economic Planning Part 1
Planning AspectKey Objective / FeatureEconomic Significance
Economic GrowthView Answer: Ensuring sustained and rapid increase in national income and real per capita income (continuous expansion in Gross Domestic Product).View Answer: Expanding the productive capacity of the economy, improving consumption standards, and lifting a developing country out of the low-income trap.
Self-RelianceView Answer: Eliminating foreign dependence in foodgrains, heavy capital goods, and basic technology while prioritizing domestic production.View Answer: Safeguarding sovereignty from foreign geopolitical pressures, controlling the Current Account Deficit (CAD), and insulating the domestic economy from external shocks.
Social Justice & EquityView Answer: Reducing the concentration of income, wealth, and economic power; ensuring equal participation of deprived, poor, and marginalized sections in national development gains.View Answer: Implementation of constitutional Directive Principles (Articles 38 and 39), reduction in social inequality, and establishment of inclusive economic welfare.
ModernizationView Answer: Adopting modern science, advanced machinery, and progressive institutional reforms (such as land reforms) in place of traditional production techniques.View Answer: A qualitative leap in agricultural and industrial productivity, improvement in economic competitiveness, and modernization of social outlook.
Full EmploymentView Answer: Generating productive employment for the working-age population through labour-intensive techniques, rural industrialization, and public works.View Answer: Optimal utilization of the demographic dividend, reduction in disguised and open unemployment, and direct eradication of rural poverty.
Balanced Regional DevelopmentView Answer: Reducing regional disparities by establishing public sector enterprises, steel plants, and infrastructure in industrially backward states and regions.View Answer: Reducing internal migration pressure, promoting national economic integration, and preventing socio-political discontent arising from geographical disparities.
Optimal Allocation of Scarce ResourcesView Answer: Planned allocation of scarce physical and financial resources according to national priorities instead of chaotic market forces.View Answer: Preventing wastage, prudent management of opportunity cost, and concentrated capital investment in core heavy industries.
Mixed Economy FrameworkView Answer: Assigning 'commanding heights' (strategic control) to the public sector while giving private enterprise a complementary role within a regulated framework.View Answer: A practical balance between socialist welfare goals and private innovation while avoiding capitalist inefficiencies and exploitation.

One-Liner Questions

What is the standard authoritative definition of 'Economic Planning' in economics?
The deliberate, systematic, and time-bound allocation of a country's scarce physical, human, and financial resources by a central authority to achieve predetermined socio-economic goals.
Tap to view answer
How did renowned economist H. D. Dickinson define economic planning?
The making of major economic decisions—'what and how much is to be produced, and to whom it is to be allocated'—by the conscious decision of a determinate authority, on the basis of a comprehensive survey of the economic system as a whole.
Tap to view answer
According to the Indian Planning Commission, what was the fundamental objective of economic planning?
To utilize human and material resources for maximum social benefit, thereby improving living standards, increasing production, and expanding employment opportunities.
Tap to view answer
Which country first implemented economic planning as a national policy in the world, and when?
The Soviet Union (USSR) in the year 1928 (under the First Five-Year Plan 'Gosplan' initiated by Joseph Stalin).
Tap to view answer
What was the most prominent technical and institutional feature of the Soviet Union's planning model?
Complete state ownership of all means of production, total absence of market forces, and rigid binding targets (Imperative Planning).
Tap to view answer
What is meant by 'Imperative / Directive Planning'?
Planning in which the directives of the central planning authority are legally binding, and all economic activities are conducted through state orders rather than the private sector.
Tap to view answer
What is the main modus operandi of 'Indicative Planning'?
The state only sets broad economic targets and a conducive environment, and motivates the private sector to achieve those targets through financial and fiscal incentives (such as the French model).
Tap to view answer
Toward which type of planning did India shift following the 1991 economic reforms and the Eighth Five-Year Plan (1992-97)?
From Imperative Planning toward Indicative Planning.
Tap to view answer
What is the meaning of 'Centralized Planning' (Planning from Above)?
Formulation of plans, resource allocation, and determination of priorities solely at the apex level by the central government (Top-Down Approach).
Tap to view answer
What is the concept of 'Decentralized Planning' (Planning from Below)?
Formulating plans at the Gram Sabha, block, and district levels according to local needs and then integrating them at the state and national levels (Bottom-Up Approach).
Tap to view answer
Which institutional mechanism for decentralized planning was mandated by the 73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendments (1992)?
District Planning Committees (DPC, Article 243ZD) and Metropolitan Planning Committees.
Tap to view answer
What is the typical time horizon of 'Perspective Planning'?
A long-term period of 15 to 20 or 25 years, under which a roadmap of the country's long-term structural goals is prepared.
Tap to view answer
How is a 'Five-Year Plan' related to Perspective Planning?
A Five-Year Plan is a medium-term (5-year) practical implementation segment of a long-term perspective plan.
Tap to view answer
What is the role of 'Short-term / Annual Planning'?
Setting specific targets for the annual budget and a one-year period so that the goals of the Five-Year Plan can be achieved in phases.

Who propounded the concept of 'Rolling Plan'?

By which government and for what period was the Rolling Plan implemented in India?

Tap to view answer
What is the most unique feature of the functioning of a Rolling Plan?
The plan is evaluated every year, and based on the previous year's performance, a new 5-year plan is added forward in the next year (flexibility).
Tap to view answer
Under which list of the 7th Schedule of the Constitution is 'Economic and Social Planning' placed?

Heavy state investment in basic and heavy industries, the leading role of the public sector, and the need for comprehensive central planning.

Agriculture, cottage and village industries, a self-reliant rural economy, and complete decentralization of economic power.

What is the difference between Democratic Planning and Totalitarian Planning?
In Democratic Planning, plans are formulated and implemented through democratic institutions and public consensus, whereas in Totalitarian Planning, the state enforces plans by force.
Tap to view answer
What is meant by Structural Planning?
Bringing fundamental changes in the basic socio-economic structure, institutional relationships, and production system of the economy.
Tap to view answer
What is Functional / Corrective Planning?
Correcting only economic disparities and immediate market failures without altering the existing socio-economic structure.
Tap to view answer
In economics, how is the nature of the Indian economy at the time of Independence in 1947 described?
A colonial, stagnant, semi-feudal, and dependent economy.
Tap to view answer
What was India's average annual growth rate during the last 50 years of colonial rule (1900-1947)?
GDP growth rate was less than 1% (around 0.5% to 0.9%) and per capita income growth rate was nearly zero (0.1%).
Tap to view answer
What severe imbalance existed in the occupational structure of the Indian workforce at the time of Independence?
About 72% to 75% of the working population depended solely on agriculture, while only 9-10% of the labour force was engaged in manufacturing.
Tap to view answer
Which major industrial and agricultural raw material sectors of India were crippled by the Partition of the country in 1947?
The jute and cotton industries; because the most fertile jute-growing areas went to East Pakistan (Bangladesh) and prime cotton-growing areas went to West Pakistan.
Tap to view answer
What was the biggest social reason for adopting planned development in India immediately after Independence?
Widespread poverty, starvation, the grim memory of the 1943 Bengal Famine, and the massive challenge of refugee rehabilitation.
Tap to view answer
Why could the Private Sector not set up large infrastructure projects in India at the time of Independence?
Because private industrialists lacked huge capital, had limited risk-bearing capacity, and basic industries had a very long gestation period.
Tap to view answer
What does 'Long Gestation Period' mean in economics?
A long time lag of several years between investing heavy capital in a project and the commencement of actual production or profit from it.
Tap to view answer
What was the most serious structural weakness of the Indian manufacturing sector at the time of Independence?
The absence of a Capital Goods Industry (such as heavy machinery, iron and steel); the country depended on Britain for everything from needles to railway locomotives.
Tap to view answer
Why did the Nehruvian leadership reject the pure capitalist market model (Laissez-faire)?
Because an unregulated market economy is profit-driven and would completely ignore social welfare, equity, and the development of backward regions.
Tap to view answer
Why did India choose a 'Mixed Economy' instead of the pure Soviet communist model?
To harmonize social justice and state-directed rapid development while preserving democratic freedoms, fundamental rights, and private property.
Tap to view answer
According to renowned economist Ragnar Nurkse, in which vicious circle are underdeveloped countries trapped, making planning essential to break it?
The 'Vicious Circle of Poverty': Low Income → Low Savings → Low Investment → Low Productivity → Low Income again.
Tap to view answer
How does Paul Rosenstein-Rodan's 'Big Push Theory' justify post-Independence planning?
It postulates that to pull a stagnant economy out of inertia, a massive minimum investment (Big Push) across multiple basic sectors simultaneously is mandatory, which only the state can undertake.
Tap to view answer
What does W. Arthur Lewis's 'Unlimited Supply of Labour Model' suggest in the context of Indian planning?
Absorbing surplus disguised labour with zero marginal productivity from agriculture into the modern capitalist/industrial sector.
Tap to view answer
At what dismal levels did India's Life Expectancy and Literacy Rate stand at the time of Independence?
Life expectancy was only 32 years and the literacy rate was about 16% to 18%, making planned human capital development imperative.
Tap to view answer
In which direction did the Industrial Policy Resolution of 1948 (IPR 1948) steer the Indian development model?
It formally initiated the mixed economy and established an exclusive state monopoly in strategic industries.
Tap to view answer
By what title is the Industrial Policy Resolution of 1956 (IPR 1956), which accelerated planned industrialization, known?

In December 1954, what did the Indian Parliament declare as the ultimate goal of India's economic and social development?

What was meant by assigning 'Commanding Heights' to the public sector in post-Independence planned development?
Complete government control and ownership over all strategic, core, and heavy industries (steel, coal, power, heavy machinery) of the economy.

Why was the strategy of 'Import Substitution Industrialization' adopted in Indian planning?
To conserve scarce foreign exchange reserves and protect domestic manufacturing industries from cut-throat competition by foreign multinational corporations.

What was the state of Indian financial markets (banking and stock markets) at Independence that made government planning indispensable?
The capital market was extremely narrow, commercial banks provided only short-term trade credit to big merchants in cities, and there was a complete absence of long-term industrial finance.

Why was planning considered an essential instrument to bridge the vast economic divide among states in independent India?
Because private capital would flow only to prosperous and coastal regions; central planned intervention was mandatory to set up heavy industries in backward states (Bihar, Odisha, Madhya Pradesh).

By what name is the planned development model adopted by India after Independence known in economics?

Tap to view answer
What was the central theoretical argument of the Nehru-Mahalanobis model?
Investing in the production of 'machines to make machines' (Heavy Capital Goods) so that a permanent foundation for producing consumer goods in the future could be laid.
Tap to view answer
In the 1950s, overcoming which crisis on the food security front did Indian planners regard as the top priority?
Freedom from the humiliating and politically sensitive 'Ship-to-Mouth' dependence on US PL-480 food aid.

Which exploitative land tenure systems existed in agriculture at the time of Independence that had to be abolished through planning?
Intermediary land ownership systems such as Zamindari, Mahalwari, and Ryotwari, which had reduced farmers to landless bonded labourers.

Where were the philosophical roots of 'Self-Reliance' and 'Swadeshi' in Indian planning anchored?
In the Swadeshi Movement of the Indian freedom struggle and Mahatma Gandhi's philosophy of self-reliance.
Tap to view answer
What was the state of power and transport infrastructure in India at the time of Independence?
Power generation capacity was merely 1,362 MW and roads were unpaved and dilapidated, which only the state could build.
Tap to view answer
How did the 'De-industrialization' of Indian industries during the colonial period create the need for planning?
British policies had destroyed traditional Indian handicrafts and handlooms, making state protection essential to revive them.
Tap to view answer
What steps were taken in planning after Independence to build scientific and technical manpower?
Planned expansion of the Indian Institutes of Technology (IITs), the Atomic Energy Commission (AEC), and CSIR laboratories.
Tap to view answer
How does the concept of Market Failure justify post-Independence planning?
Private markets were completely incapable of adequately supplying social goods such as healthcare, education, and clean drinking water.

What was the status of the national savings rate at the time of Independence?
Only about 8% to 9% of GDP, raising which to at least 20% was a primary goal of planned development.

Which are the Four Core Long-term Objectives of Indian planning?
1. Growth, 2. Modernization, 3. Self-Reliance, and 4. Equity / Social Justice.

What is the real technical yardstick of 'Economic Growth' in the context of planning?
A sustained and sustainable growth rate in Gross Domestic Product (GDP) and real per capita income.

What was the precise meaning of 'Self-Reliance' in Indian planning?
Ending dependence on foreign aid and food imports, and domestically producing those goods that could otherwise be imported into the country.

Self-reliance does not mean cutting off completely from foreign trade, but acquiring the capability to pay for essential imports through one's own export earnings.

Why is 'Modernization' as an objective of planning not limited merely to adopting new technology?
Because it also includes progressive changes in social outlook (such as equal rights for women, ending superstitions, and spreading scientific temper).
Tap to view answer
Why were 'Equity and Social Justice' considered more important than the growth rate in planning?
Because if the benefits of growth remain confined to a handful of wealthy classes, the poverty, hunger, and social unrest of the general public would deepen further.

Of which Five-Year Plan did the direct slogan 'Garibi Hatao' (Poverty Eradication) become the central focus in Indian planning?
In the Fifth Five-Year Plan (1974-79) (under the leadership of Smt. Indira Gandhi).
Tap to view answer
What was the planners' early belief known as the 'Trickle-Down Theory'?
The notion that if a high GDP growth rate is achieved in the economy, its benefits will automatically trickle down to the lowest rungs of society.
Tap to view answer
Why did planners abandon the 'Trickle-Down Theory' in the 1970s?
Because experience proved that despite higher growth, poverty and inequality did not decline, necessitating directly targeted employment and nutrition programmes for the poor.
Tap to view answer
What is the meaning of the objective of 'Full Employment' in planning?
A social condition where all persons who are willing and able to work at the prevailing wage rate can obtain productive employment.
Tap to view answer
To which failure on the employment front did 'Capital-Intensive' industrialization during Indian planning give rise?
'Jobless Growth,' where output increased but sufficient new jobs could not be created in the organized sector.

What steps were taken to achieve 'Balanced Regional Development' as an objective of planning?
Massive steel plants like Bhilai, Rourkela, Durgapur, and Bokaro were deliberately established in backward and tribal-dominated regions.

What was the objective of the 'Freight Equalization Policy, 1952' and what was its adverse impact?

The amount of additional capital required to produce one additional unit of output (an indicator of the efficiency of capital investment).
