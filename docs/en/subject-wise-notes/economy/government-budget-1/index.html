Government Budget Part 1

Meaning of Government Budget, Constitutional Basis (Article 112), Key Objectives of Budget, Revenue Receipts (Tax & Non-Tax), and Detailed Analysis of Capital Receipts

Quick Reference
Key Facts & Contributions
Government Budget Part 1
Budget ItemKey FeatureFiscal Significance
Annual Financial Statement (Article 112)Official terminology used in the Constitution in place of the word 'Budget'; a comprehensive statement of estimated receipts and expenditure of the government for the upcoming financial year (1 April to 31 March).Establishes democratic accountability; prevents the executive from levying any tax or spending a single rupee without legislative approval of Parliament.
Objective of Reallocation of ResourcesDirecting capital flow toward social priorities (health, education, housing) rather than private profit through tax concessions, subsidies, and public investment.Corrects market failure, ensures adequate supply of Public Goods, and achieves optimal national allocation of resources.
Objective of Reducing Economic InequalitiesProgressive direct taxation (higher taxes on the rich) and targeted welfare expenditure for the poor (food security, cash transfers, scholarships).Implements Directive Principles of State Policy (Articles 38 and 39); checks concentration of income and wealth to promote inclusive growth.
Revenue ReceiptsGovernment receipts that neither create any financial liability for the government nor cause any reduction in government assets.Forms the primary and sustainable non-debt foundation for financing day-to-day administration, salaries, pensions, and social welfare schemes.
Tax RevenueCompulsory statutory contribution paid by citizens and enterprises to the government; comprises Direct Taxes (Income Tax, Corporate Tax) and Indirect Taxes (GST, Customs Duty).Represents the government's net sovereign income; the Tax-to-GDP ratio is a key barometer of economic strength, tax compliance, and fiscal self-reliance.
Non-Tax RevenueAdministrative fees for government services, fines, dividends from RBI and Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs), and interest received on loans.Expands budgetary resources without imposing additional tax burden on taxpayers; ensures rational financial returns on government investments.
Debt-Creating Capital ReceiptsMarket borrowings (Government Securities, T-Bills), borrowings from the National Small Savings Fund (NSSF), and external sovereign foreign loans.Bridges the fiscal deficit and builds long-term capital infrastructure (Capex); excessive borrowing increases future interest and debt burden.
Non-Debt Capital Receipts (NDCR)Recovery of old loans extended to State Governments and PSUs, and Disinvestment Proceeds.Raises capital financial resources without incurring any new sovereign debt; monetizes idle assets to build productive assets.

One-Liner Questions

What is the standard authoritative definition of a 'Government Budget' in economics?
A systematically planned and detailed annual financial document of the government's estimated receipts (revenue and capital) and estimated expenditures during a financial year.

What is the foremost macroeconomic objective of a Government Budget?
To ensure optimal allocation of scarce economic resources according to national priorities and to achieve rapid, inclusive economic growth.
Tap to view answer
Through which dual mechanisms does the government achieve 'Reallocation of Resources' via the budget?
1. Through tax and subsidy policies (higher taxes on harmful goods and tax concessions on essential goods), and 2. By directly producing public goods itself.
Tap to view answer
What are 'Public Goods' in an economy that are provisioned through the government budget?
Goods that are 'Non-excludable' and 'Non-rivalrous' in consumption, such as national defense, law and order, roads, and street lighting.
Tap to view answer
How does the budget make 'Reducing Inequalities in Income and Wealth' possible?
By imposing progressive taxation (higher income tax on the wealthy) and utilizing that revenue to finance food, housing, healthcare, and education schemes for the poor.
Tap to view answer
What is meant by the 'Economic Stabilization' objective of the budget?
Protecting the economy from extreme business cycles of inflation (rising prices) and recession (unemployment) to maintain sustainable growth with price stability.
Tap to view answer
What type of budgetary policy is adopted during a recession to stimulate economic activity?
A sharp increase in public capital expenditure (Capex) and reduction in taxes (Expansionary Budget).
Tap to view answer
What should be the budgetary stance during periods of high inflation?
Cutting non-essential government expenditure and tightening tax collection (Contractionary Budget).
Tap to view answer
For what purpose is 'Management of Public Enterprises' undertaken through the budget?
To ensure self-reliance in strategic sectors, subsidized supply of foundational inputs (railways, electricity), and social welfare.
Tap to view answer
How is 'Reducing Regional Disparities' achieved through the budget?
By offering special tax concessions (Tax Holidays), capital subsidies, and central infrastructure funds for setting up industries in backward, hilly, and northeastern regions.
Tap to view answer
How does the budget directly promote 'Employment Generation'?
By providing financial outlays and incentives for MGNREGA, national highway construction, Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana, and labor-intensive MSME units.
Tap to view answer
Why is 'Capital Formation' prioritized in the government budget?
Because capital assets (roads, ports, digital networks) expand the future productivity and GDP capacity of the economy.
Tap to view answer
What is the budgetary objective of 'Gender Budgeting'?
Ensuring specific and dedicated financial allocations in the budget for the welfare, safety, nutrition, health, and economic empowerment of women.
Tap to view answer
Into which two parts is the Gender Budget classified in the Union Budget?
Part A (100% women-specific schemes such as maternity benefits) and Part B (schemes with at least 30% allocation for women).
Tap to view answer
What is the concept of 'Green Budgeting'?
Evaluating all policy proposals and expenditure items of the budget through the lens of environmental protection, renewable energy, climate adaptation, and carbon emission reduction.
Tap to view answer
What is the place of 'Financial Inclusion' among budgetary objectives?
Strengthening Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT), Jan Dhan accounts, and digital payment infrastructure to bring formal banking to the last person in society.

What are the two most essential criteria defining 'Revenue Receipts'?
1. They do not create any financial liability for the government, and 2. They do not cause any reduction in any government asset.

Why are Revenue Receipts considered 'Non-redeemable' in the economy?
Because the government has no legal obligation to return these funds with principal or interest to any donor or taxpayer in the future.

What are the two major components of Revenue Receipts?

What is the legal nature of a 'Tax' in a government budget?
A compulsory financial contribution imposed by the government under law, in exchange for which no specific direct service (quid pro quo) can be legally claimed.

Under which Article of the Constitution is the government empowered to levy taxes only by authority of law?
Under Article 265 ('No tax shall be levied or collected except by authority of law').

What is the standard definition of a 'Direct Tax'?
A tax whose legal impact (initial burden) and actual monetary incidence (final burden) fall on the same person or company; it cannot be shifted to others.

Which are the two largest direct taxes in the Union Budget?
1. Personal Income Tax and 2. Corporation Tax.

On what is Corporation Tax levied?

What is the key characteristic of an 'Indirect Tax'?
A tax whose initial impact falls on the seller or manufacturer, but its actual incidence (burden) is shifted onto the consumer by adding it to the price of goods.

Which is the largest unified indirect tax in India, implemented from 1 July 2017?

Which central indirect tax is levied on goods imported into India from foreign countries?

What is the principle of a 'Progressive Taxation' system?
The tax rate increases as the taxpayer's income increases—higher percentage tax on high-income groups and zero or lower tax on low-income groups.
Tap to view answer
Why are indirect taxes considered 'Regressive' by nature?
Because both the rich and the poor pay the exact same tax amount on a commodity (such as soap or biscuits), which constitutes a much larger share of a poor person's total income.

What is the special feature of a 'Surcharge' levied under Article 271 of the Constitution?
It is an additional tax on tax whose entire proceeds remain with the Centre and are not shared with the States in the divisible pool.

What is a 'Cess'?
A tax-on-tax levied for a predetermined specific public purpose (such as health, education, or road infrastructure); its proceeds can only be spent on that designated purpose.

What does 'Tax Buoyancy' measure?
The ratio of the percentage growth in gross tax revenue to the percentage growth in nominal GDP [(%Δ Tax Revenue) / (%Δ GDP)].

If GDP grows by 10% and tax collection grows by 15%, what will be the Tax Buoyancy?
1.5 (15 / 10 = 1.5), which indicates a highly efficient and buoyant tax system.

What is the difference between 'Tax Evasion' and 'Tax Avoidance'?
Tax Evasion is an illegal and punishable offense of concealing income, whereas Tax Avoidance is legally reducing tax liability by exploiting loopholes in tax laws.

What is the current 'Tax-to-GDP Ratio' in India?
The Central Government's gross tax-to-GDP ratio is approximately 11.5% to 12% (and the combined Centre-States ratio is roughly 17–18%).

What relationship does the Laffer Curve show between tax rates and tax revenue?
An inverted U-shaped relationship; raising tax rates beyond an optimal threshold increases tax evasion and causes the government's total tax revenue to decline.

What is the standard definition of 'Non-Tax Revenue'?
Revenue income received by the government from all regular sources other than taxes (fees for services, interest, dividends, and profits).

Who is generally the largest and most important contributor to Central Non-Tax Revenue?
The annual surplus/dividend transfer made by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) to the Central Government.

Which receipts fall under the 'Dividends and Profits' head of Non-Tax Revenue?

Where do 'Interest Receipts' in Non-Tax Revenue come from?

Proceeds from 5G/4G spectrum auctions, Spectrum Usage Charges (SUC), and license fees from telecom companies.

What are the major examples of 'Administrative Revenue'?
Passport and visa fees, court stamp fees, application fees for various competitive examinations, and registration slip fees at government hospitals.

What is the money collected by the government for violating laws called?
Fines and Penalties, whose primary objective is not to earn revenue but to maintain legal discipline.

What is the confiscation of heirless property called?

In which account are 'External Grants-in-aid' received from foreign governments or international institutions recorded?
In Non-Tax Revenue Receipts, because they do not have to be repaid and do not create any sovereign liability.

What is the legal and economic definition of 'Capital Receipts'?
Government receipts that either create a financial liability (debt) for the government or reduce government assets.
Tap to view answer
Into which two main categories are Capital Receipts divided?
1. Debt-Creating Capital Receipts, and 2. Non-Debt Capital Receipts (NDCR).

What are 'Debt-Creating Capital Receipts' (Debt Receipts)?
Receipts that the government must repay with interest in the future and that add to the sovereign debt.

Which is the largest and primary component of the Central Government's debt-creating capital receipts?
Domestic Market Borrowings, raised through Dated Government Securities and Treasury Bills (T-Bills).

What are loans raised by the Government of India from international institutions (World Bank, ADB) and foreign governments called?

What is the proportional share of external/foreign debt in the total sovereign debt of the Government of India?
Very small—only about 3% to 5% of total public debt (95% of India's sovereign debt is denominated in domestic currency, the Rupee).

What type of receipt for the government is the Provident Fund (GPF/PPF) contribution deposited by government employees?
Debt-creating capital receipt (Public Account liability), because it must be returned with interest upon retirement.
Tap to view answer
What is the definition of 'Non-Debt Capital Receipts' (NDCR)?
Capital receipts that do not add any new debt or liability on the government, but result in a reduction of government assets.
Tap to view answer
Which are the two most prominent and official components of Non-Debt Capital Receipts (NDCR)?
1. Recovery of Loans given to State Governments and others, and 2. Disinvestment Proceeds from the sale of government shares in PSUs.
Tap to view answer
Why is the repayment of principal on old loans by State Governments to the Centre (Recovery of Loans) a capital receipt?
Because it reduces the Central Government's financial asset (loan claim).

What is the sale of government shares in Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs) called?

Why is Disinvestment classified as a 'Non-Debt Capital Receipt'?
Because it does not increase future debt for the government, though it reduces the government's equity/asset in the public enterprise.

What is meant by 'Strategic Disinvestment'?
Transferring over 50% shareholding of a government enterprise along with its management and control to a private buyer (such as the privatization of Air India).

What is the objective of the 'National Monetization Pipeline' (NMP) announced in August 2021?
To raise private capital by leasing out operational rights of government assets like roads, railways, and transmission lines without selling their permanent ownership.

In the government budget, 'Fiscal Deficit' is mathematically equal to which capital item?
Exactly equal to the government's Total Net Debt-Creating Capital Receipts (Total Net Borrowings).

What is the correct equation for the government's 'Total Receipts' in the budget?
Total Receipts = Revenue Receipts + Capital Receipts (Debt Receipts + Non-Debt Receipts).
Tap to view answer
What is the formula for calculating the government's 'Non-Debt Total Receipts'?
Non-Debt Total Receipts = Total Revenue Receipts + Non-Debt Capital Receipts (Recovery of Loans + Disinvestment).
Tap to view answer
If tax revenue increases in a financial year, what is its impact on the budget deficit?
Both the Revenue Deficit and Fiscal Deficit automatically decrease because the government's non-debt income rises.
Tap to view answer
What happens if the government relies recklessly on 'Debt-Creating Capital Receipts' to meet its expenses?
The country's Public Debt-to-GDP ratio rises, and a large share of future budgets gets consumed solely by interest payments.

Between Revenue Receipts and Capital Receipts, which are considered 'Regular and Recurring'?
Revenue Receipts, because taxes and fees flow into the government continuously throughout the year.

Which receipts are considered 'Irregular and Non-recurring' in nature?
Capital Receipts (such as disinvestment or special borrowings).

Why is a proportionally higher share of Revenue Receipts and Non-Debt Capital Receipts considered superior for the government?
Because it reduces the government's dependence on borrowing and keeps the country's sovereign creditworthiness strong.

What is the ultimate macroeconomic conclusion from the comprehensive study of Government Budget Part 1?
A government budget is not merely an accounting ledger of income and expenditure, but the sovereign document of a nation's socio-economic philosophy and development priorities; maximizing sustainable revenue receipts (tax and non-tax) alongside prudent management of debt-creating capital receipts is the cornerstone of building a self-reliant, equitable, and developed India.
