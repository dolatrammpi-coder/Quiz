Inflation Part 2

Relationship between inflation and monetary policy, Flexible Inflation Targeting (FIT), monetary policy transmission mechanism (EBLR/MCLR), role of the repo rate, and supply-side constraints

Quick Reference
Key Facts & Contributions
Inflation Part 2
Monetary Policy MeasurePrimary RoleImpact on Inflation
Policy Rate Hike (Repo Rate Increase)View Answer: Raising the interest rate on short-term refinancing provided by the RBI to commercial banks, thereby increasing borrowing costs across the entire financial system.View Answer: Contraction in credit demand, consumer spending, and business investment; effectively curbing demand-pull inflation by reducing Aggregate Demand.
Standing Deposit Facility (SDF)View Answer: A non-collateralized facility allowing the RBI to absorb surplus overnight liquidity from banks without pledging any government securities as collateral.View Answer: Neutralizing excess cash in the banking system to prevent inter-bank call rates from falling below the floor, thereby suppressing liquidity-driven price pressures.
Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) HikeView Answer: Mandating commercial banks to hold a higher share of their total Net Demand and Time Liabilities (NDTL) as cash reserves with the RBI at zero interest.View Answer: Direct contraction of the banking system's credit-creation capacity (Credit Multiplier), curbing inflation by reducing broad money supply (M3) in the economy.
Open Market Sales (OMOs)View Answer: Outright cash sale of Government Securities (G-Secs) by the RBI to commercial banks and financial institutions in the secondary market.View Answer: Permanently draining excess durable liquidity from the market, reducing money supply to rein in demand-pull inflation.
External Benchmark-based Lending Rate (EBLR)View Answer: Linking interest rates on retail and MSME loans directly to external market benchmarks such as the repo rate or Treasury Bill yields since October 2019.View Answer: Making Monetary Policy Transmission faster and more transparent, ensuring immediate adjustment in borrowers' EMIs whenever the repo rate changes.
Expectations AnchoringView Answer: Winning public credibility through Forward Guidance by the Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) and an explicit inflation target (4% +/- 2%).View Answer: Stabilizing future inflation by preventing a Wage-Price Spiral and curbing speculative hoarding tendencies.
Forex Operations / SwapsView Answer: Selling US dollars in the foreign exchange market or conducting liquidity sterilization to prevent excessive depreciation of the domestic currency (Rupee).View Answer: Preventing surges in the domestic prices of crude oil and imported raw materials, thereby controlling imported cost-push inflation.
Qualitative / Selective Credit ControlsView Answer: Raising margin requirements on sensitive speculative commodities (foodgrains, oilseeds, real estate) and applying Moral Suasion.View Answer: Directly curbing speculative, artificial price hikes by blocking bank credit used for hoarding essential commodities.

One-Liner Questions

What is the primary and overarching statutory objective of 'Monetary Policy' in the economy?
Maintaining 'Price Stability' while keeping in mind the objective of economic growth.

What explicit mandate for monetary policy was inserted into the Preamble of the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934 through the 2016 amendment?
'To regulate the issue of Bank notes and keeping of reserves with a view to securing monetary stability in India and generally to operate the currency and credit system of the country to its advantage, and to maintain price stability while keeping in mind the objective of growth.'

On the recommendation of which committee was the 'Flexible Inflation Targeting' (FIT) framework adopted in India?
On the recommendation of the Dr. Urjit Patel Committee (2014).
Tap to view answer
When was the 'Monetary Policy Framework Agreement' (MPFA) formally signed between the Government of India and the Reserve Bank of India?
On 20 February 2015 (followed by the formal amendment to the RBI Act, 1934 via the Finance Act in June 2016).
Tap to view answer
Which price index was adopted as the primary nominal anchor for monetary policy on the recommendation of the Urjit Patel Committee?
Consumer Price Index - Combined (CPI-Combined / CPI-C).
Tap to view answer
Why was the Consumer Price Index (CPI) preferred over the Wholesale Price Index (WPI) for monetary policy?
Because the WPI did not cover services (education, healthcare, transport) and failed to reflect the actual retail price level paid by ordinary consumers.

Under Section 45ZA of the RBI Act, 1934, what official inflation target is notified by the Central Government in consultation with the RBI?
4% Consumer Price Index (CPI) inflation, with a Tolerance Band of +/- 2% (i.e., a range of 2% to 6%).
Tap to view answer
What is the economic rationale behind the +/- 2% 'Tolerance Band' in the inflation targeting framework?
To provide necessary policy flexibility for absorbing unpredictable Supply Shocks related to agriculture and crude oil in a developing economy.
Tap to view answer
Which statutory body was constituted under Section 45ZB of the RBI Act, 1934 to determine policy interest rates?
The Monetary Policy Committee (MPC).
Tap to view answer
How many total members are there in the Monetary Policy Committee (MPC), and what is its composition?
A total of 6 members: 3 members from the RBI (Governor, Deputy Governor-in-charge, and 1 other officer) and 3 independent external experts appointed by the Central Government.
Tap to view answer
Who serves as the Ex-officio Chairperson of the Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)?
The Governor of the Reserve Bank of India (RBI).
Tap to view answer
What is the tenure of the external members appointed to the MPC by the Central Government, and are they eligible for reappointment?
They hold office for a term of 4 years and are not eligible for reappointment.
Tap to view answer
How are decisions taken in the Monetary Policy Committee, and what is the rule in case of a tie?
By a majority vote of the members present and voting (each member has 1 vote); in the event of a tie, the RBI Governor has a second or 'Casting Vote'.
Tap to view answer
According to the RBI Act, what is the minimum number of MPC meetings mandated in a year?
At least 4 meetings a year (though by established convention, it meets bi-monthly, i.e., 6 times a year).
Tap to view answer
What is the minimum quorum required for a meeting of the MPC?
At least 4 members.
Tap to view answer
What is the statutory definition of 'Failure to Meet Inflation Target' by the MPC under the RBI Act, 1934?
If the average CPI inflation remains above the upper tolerance limit of 6% or below the lower tolerance limit of 2% for Three Consecutive Quarters.
Tap to view answer
What formal document is the RBI legally required to submit to the Central Government if it fails to meet the inflation target?
A written special Report stating the reasons for failure, remedial actions proposed to be taken, and an estimate of the time-period within which the inflation target will be achieved.
Tap to view answer
In which year after the implementation of the FIT framework in 2016 did the RBI have to submit its first formal failure report to the Central Government?
In November 2022, as inflation remained persistently above 6% during the first three quarters of 2022.
Tap to view answer
What does a 'Hawkish Stance' mean in the context of monetary policy?
An aggressive policy stance aimed at controlling inflation, under which the central bank raises interest rates and tightens money supply.
Tap to view answer
What does a 'Dovish Stance' or 'Accommodative Stance' signify?
Keeping interest rates low and maintaining ample liquidity in the market by the central bank to stimulate economic growth.
Tap to view answer
What is implied by a 'Neutral Stance' in monetary policy?
The central bank retains flexibility to move interest rates in either direction (hike or cut), depending on incoming macroeconomic data.
Tap to view answer
What policy signal does 'Withdrawal of Accommodation' convey?
Gradually pulling back crisis-era ultra-low interest rates and excess liquidity toward normal levels so that inflation does not flare up.
Tap to view answer
What does 'Taylor's Rule' in economics prescribe regarding monetary policy?
A formula-based guideline indicating how much a central bank should adjust its policy interest rate when actual inflation deviates from target inflation and output deviates from its potential level.
Tap to view answer
Why is 'Central Bank Credibility' considered the strongest weapon in the fight against inflation?
Because when the public firmly believes the central bank is committed to keeping inflation around 4%, future inflation expectations automatically anchor.
Tap to view answer
What is 'Forward Guidance' in the conduct of monetary policy?
Clear communication by a central bank to financial markets regarding its planned future stance on interest rates and liquidity.
Tap to view answer
What is the standard macroeconomic definition of 'Monetary Policy Transmission'?
The overall process through which changes in the central bank's policy rate (repo rate) pass through the financial system (bank lending/deposit rates, bond markets) to impact the real economy (spending, investment, employment, and ultimately inflation).
Tap to view answer
What are the five major traditional Transmission Channels of monetary policy?
1. Interest Rate Channel, 2. Credit / Bank Lending Channel, 3. Asset Price Channel, 4. Exchange Rate Channel, and 5. Inflation Expectations Channel.
Tap to view answer
How does the 'Interest Rate Channel' operate in monetary transmission?
A repo rate hike raises banks' borrowing costs -> banks raise lending rates -> costlier borrowing reduces consumer durable spending and corporate investment -> lower aggregate demand tames inflation.
Tap to view answer
What is the 'Credit Channel' (Bank Lending Channel) in monetary transmission?
Increases in the repo rate and CRR reduce loanable excess reserves with banks, prompting them to tighten new loan disbursements and contracting overall credit flow in the economy.
Tap to view answer
What does the 'Asset Price Channel' of monetary transmission demonstrate?
Higher interest rates depress stock market and real estate prices, reducing investors' net wealth (Negative Wealth Effect) and prompting them to cut back on consumption expenditure.
Tap to view answer
How does the 'Exchange Rate Channel' affect inflation during monetary transmission?
Higher domestic interest rates attract foreign portfolio capital (FPI) -> the domestic currency (Rupee) appreciates -> crude oil and import bills become cheaper -> imported inflation declines.
Tap to view answer
How does the 'Expectations Channel' function in monetary transmission?
Seeing the central bank's firm resolve in raising rates, consumers and wage-earners expect prices not to rise in the future, automatically curbing wage-hike pressures and speculative hoarding.
Tap to view answer
What is meant by a 'Time Lag' in monetary policy transmission?
The time elapsed between a policy rate decision and its actual visible impact on inflation and GDP on the ground (typically a lag of 9 to 18 months).
Tap to view answer
What is the difference between 'Inside Lag' and 'Outside Lag' in monetary policy transmission?
Inside lag is the time taken to recognize an economic problem and change the policy rate, whereas outside lag is the longer time taken for that policy change to affect real output and prices.
Tap to view answer
What have been the major structural reasons for historically slow and asymmetric monetary transmission in India?
High burden of Non-Performing Assets (NPAs) in banks, long-term fixed interest rates on Fixed Deposits (FDs), and high government-administered interest rates on small savings schemes (PPF, Sukanya Samriddhi).
Tap to view answer
Which four interest-rate setting regimes were implemented sequentially by commercial banks in India?
1. Prime Lending Rate (PLR), 2. Benchmark Prime Lending Rate (BPLR - 2003), 3. Base Rate system (2010), and 4. MCLR (2016).
Tap to view answer
In which year was the 'Marginal Cost of Funds based Lending Rate' (MCLR) system introduced by the RBI?
From 1 April 2016.
Tap to view answer
Which four key components formed the basis of MCLR calculation?
1. Marginal Cost of Funds, 2. Negative Carry on Account of CRR, 3. Operating Costs, and 4. Tenor Premium.
Tap to view answer
Why did the MCLR system also fail to ensure full and rapid monetary policy transmission?
Because it relied on banks' internal deposit costs (Internal Benchmark); even when the RBI cut the repo rate, banks cited legacy high-cost fixed deposits to delay cutting their lending rates.
Tap to view answer
Which transformative lending rate regime did the RBI mandate from 1 October 2019 to eliminate transmission failure?
External Benchmark-based Lending Rate (EBLR).
Tap to view answer
Under the EBLR regime, what were banks required to link all their new floating-rate retail (home/auto) and MSME loans to?
A transparent 'External Market Benchmark' authorized by the RBI.
Tap to view answer
According to RBI guidelines, which approved external benchmarks can banks choose from under EBLR?
1. RBI Policy Repo Rate, 2. Government of India 91-Day Treasury Bill yield, 3. 182-Day Treasury Bill yield, or 4. Any other benchmark market interest rate published by FBIL.
Tap to view answer
Which external benchmark has been most widely adopted by Indian commercial banks under EBLR?
The RBI's Policy Repo Rate (commonly termed Repo-Linked Lending Rate or RLLR).
Tap to view answer
Within what mandatory interval must banks reset their EBLR-linked lending rates in line with the external benchmark?
At least once every three months (quarterly).
Tap to view answer
What has been the biggest qualitative improvement in Indian monetary policy transmission since the introduction of EBLR?
As soon as the MPC alters the repo rate, nearly 100% pass-through (transmission) to home loan and MSME lending rates occurs within a matter of weeks.
Tap to view answer
What is the distinction between traditional 'Quantitative' and 'Qualitative' instruments used by a central bank to control inflation?
Quantitative tools indirectly regulate the total volume of credit across the entire economy, whereas qualitative tools direct and control credit flow toward specific sectors and end-uses.
Tap to view answer
How is the 'Cash Reserve Ratio' (CRR) used in controlling inflation?
When the RBI raises the CRR, banks must park a larger cash share of their deposits with the RBI, immediately shrinking banks' loanable funds and contracting money supply.
Tap to view answer
Do commercial banks earn any interest on the CRR balances maintained with the RBI?
No; the RBI pays zero (0%) interest to banks on CRR balances.
Tap to view answer
What was the 'Incremental Cash Reserve Ratio' (I-CRR) imposed by the RBI in August 2023?
A temporary 10% additional CRR on the increase in deposits between 19 May and 28 July 2023 to absorb sudden surplus liquidity following the withdrawal of ₹2,000 banknotes.
Tap to view answer
How does the 'Statutory Liquidity Ratio' (SLR) assist in controlling inflation?
Banks are mandated to invest a fixed percentage of their total deposits in approved liquid assets (primarily government securities), preventing reckless and excessive private credit expansion.
Tap to view answer
What action does the RBI take under 'Open Market Operations' (OMOs) during high inflation?
The RBI sells Government Securities (G-Secs) to banks in the secondary market (OMO Sale), transferring bank cash to the RBI and absorbing excess market liquidity.
Tap to view answer
What is the 'Standing Deposit Facility' (SDF) launched by the RBI in April 2022?
A regulatory liquidity tool under which banks can park their surplus cash with the RBI and earn interest without requiring the RBI to provide government securities as collateral.
Tap to view answer
What technical limitation did the RBI face in Reverse Repo operations prior to the introduction of SDF?
Under Reverse Repo, the RBI had to pledge G-Secs as collateral to absorb cash; during massive liquidity surpluses, the RBI could run short of collateral—a constraint SDF completely eliminated.
Tap to view answer
What is the current structure of the Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF) interest rate corridor?
The 'Repo Rate' is the centre of the corridor; the lower bound (floor) is the 'SDF Rate' (Repo - 0.25%), and the upper bound (ceiling) is the 'MSF Rate' (Repo + 0.25%).
Tap to view answer
What is the 'Marginal Standing Facility' (MSF)?
An emergency window where, if inter-bank liquidity dries up completely, banks can borrow overnight from the RBI at a penal rate 25 basis points above the repo rate by dipping into their statutory SLR securities up to a specified limit (2% of NDTL).
Tap to view answer
How does the qualitative tool of 'Margin Requirements' work in controlling inflation?
The RBI increases the safety margin between the value of pledged collateral and the loan sanctioned (e.g., reducing a loan against ₹100 of gold from ₹80 to ₹60), curbing credit flow into speculative sectors.
Tap to view answer
What is meant by 'Credit Rationing'?
Fixing maximum financial ceilings (Credit Quotas) on bank lending to various business sectors or industries so that excessive credit does not flow into non-priority areas.
Tap to view answer
What is 'Moral Suasion' in monetary control?
Persuasion by the RBI through meetings, circulars, and appeals to commercial bank heads to comply with monetary tightening guidelines without imposing direct legal penalties.
Tap to view answer
What is the biggest 'inherent limitation' of monetary policy when inflation is triggered purely by supply shocks?
Raising the repo rate can dampen demand, but it cannot fix drought-induced crop failures, fertilizer shortages from the Russia-Ukraine war, or global oil supply disruptions.
Tap to view answer
How does 'Fiscal Dominance' constrain the autonomy of monetary policy?
When the government's fiscal deficit is excessively large, heavy government market borrowing pushes up interest rates and puts intense pressure on the central bank to keep public borrowing cheap.
Tap to view answer
What is the standard technical definition of the 'Repo Rate' (Repurchase Option Rate)?
The interest rate at which the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) provides short-term loans to commercial banks against government securities as collateral to meet their temporary liquidity shortfalls.
Tap to view answer
Why is the repo rate referred to as the 'Key Policy Rate' or 'Signaling Rate' of the economy?
Because all interest rates across the financial system (call money, T-bills, commercial paper, bank deposit and lending rates) align with the level and direction of the repo rate.
Tap to view answer
How does the MPC adjust the repo rate when inflation breaches its upper tolerance band (6%)?
It implements a Rate Hike (tight or contractionary monetary policy).
Tap to view answer
What is the mathematical meaning of a 50 basis points (50 bps) hike in the repo rate?
A direct increase of 0.50% in the policy interest rate (1 basis point = 0.01%).
Tap to view answer
What is the immediate impact of a repo rate hike on banks' marginal cost of funds?
Borrowing from the central bank and the open money market becomes costlier for banks, raising their overall cost of mobilizing capital.
Tap to view answer
What incentive do commercial banks offer to their depositors when the repo rate rises?
Banks raise interest rates on Fixed Deposits (FDs) and savings accounts to attract more liquidity from the public.
Tap to view answer
How does a rise in Fixed Deposit (FD) rates help control inflation?
Consumers are incentivized to save money in banks for future returns rather than spending immediately, thereby reducing current demand for goods in the market.
Tap to view answer
What is the direct impact of a repo rate hike on the 'EMIs' of ordinary citizens' home and car loans?
Higher loan interest rates increase monthly EMIs or extend loan tenures, reducing households' Disposable Income available for spending on other daily consumption goods.
Tap to view answer
Why does demand for 'Consumer Durables' (automobiles, real estate, electronics) fall first when the repo rate rises?
Because over 80% of purchases of these big-ticket items rely on bank loans and EMIs; consumers defer purchases as soon as interest rates climb.
Tap to view answer
From the corporate sector's perspective, what is the consequence of a repo rate increase?
Working capital and capital expenditure (Capex) loans for setting up new factories become costlier, prompting companies to temporarily slow down new investments and expansion plans.
Tap to view answer
How does a decline in Aggregate Demand cool down demand-pull inflation?
When buyer footfall drops in shops and showrooms, producers and traders stop raising prices and begin offering discounts to clear inventory.
Tap to view answer
What is the impact of a repo rate hike on the country's 'Money Supply' (M3 / Broad Money)?
Slower creation of new loans by banks shrinks the Money Multiplier and reduces the growth rate of total money supply in the economy.
Tap to view answer
How does a repo rate hike influence 'Foreign Capital Inflows'?
It widens the Interest Rate Differential between India and developed economies, attracting foreign investors to bring dollars into the Indian debt market, strengthening the Rupee and curbing imported inflation.
Tap to view answer
What serious risk does excessive and continuous hiking of the repo rate pose to economic growth?
Economic growth can slow down sharply, industrial output may contract, and an Economic Slowdown or recession may set in.
Tap to view answer
What is the concept of a 'Soft Landing' in economics?
A central bank raising interest rates skillfully enough to successfully bring inflation under control without pushing the economy into a severe recession.
Tap to view answer
What is termed a 'Hard Landing' in economics?
When a central bank hikes interest rates so aggressively to crush inflation that the economy plunges into a deep recession and widespread unemployment.

What swift action did the RBI take on the repo rate between May 2022 and February 2023 to tame inflation?
It aggressively hiked the repo rate by 250 basis points (2.50%) in just 9 months, taking it from 4.00% to 6.50%.

What stance does the MPC adopt on the repo rate when inflation is firmly under control and economic growth is slowing?
It undertakes a Rate Cut (Dovish Stance) to make credit cheaper, boost investment and consumption, and accelerate GDP growth.

To what historic low did the RBI cut the repo rate during the COVID-19 pandemic (2020) to prevent an economic collapse?

What is the direct effect of the repo rate on 'Bond Yields'?

Why does the RBI avoid immediately hiking the repo rate during temporary spikes in food prices?

What are 'Second-Round Effects' in the context of inflation?
When temporary price shocks in food or fuel become entrenched and feed into transport, hospitality, manufacturing costs, and workers' wage demands, turning into broad-based inflation.
