Financial Market Part 2

Introduction to financial markets, structure of the money market, key short-term financial instruments (Call Money, T-Bills, CP, CD, Repo, Commercial Bills), and macroeconomic significance of high liquidity

🔍
Quick Reference
Key Facts & Contributions
Financial Market Part 2
Financial Instrument/MarketKey FeatureUse or Role
Call & Notice MoneyView Answer Unsecured inter-bank short-term lending market; 1 day (Call) to 14 days (Notice); interest rate determined entirely by market demand and supply.View Answer Maintaining daily CRR and SLR mandates by commercial banks and rapid adjustment of inter-bank cash imbalances.
Treasury Bills (T-Bills)View Answer Zero-coupon sovereign debt instruments issued by the RBI on behalf of the Government of India (91, 182, and 364 days); minimum denomination ₹25,000; issued at a discount.View Answer Bridging temporary cash mismatches of the government without printing currency, serving as SLR securities for banks, and acting as the economy's risk-free short-term benchmark.
Commercial Paper (CP)View Answer Short-term unsecured promissory notes issued by companies with high credit ratings (minimum A-2) (7 days to 1 year; minimum ₹5 lakh).View Answer Providing corporate houses with cheaper, flexible, and direct market financing for working capital compared to traditional bank loans.
Certificate of Deposit (CD)View Answer Negotiable term instruments issued by scheduled banks (7 days to 1 year) and AIFIs (1 to 3 years); minimum ₹1 lakh.View Answer Raising lump-sum wholesale liquidity by banks from large institutional investors during credit expansion; easily tradable in the secondary market.
Commercial / Trade BillsView Answer Bills of exchange drawn on the buyer upon credit sale of actual commercial goods; discounted by banks.View Answer Providing immediate cash to suppliers through bill discounting and the TReDS platform, imparting uninterrupted momentum to the trade cycle.
Market Repo & Reverse RepoView Answer Repurchase agreements backed by government securities as collateral; specified haircuts and pre-determined repurchase prices.View Answer Providing secure short-term borrowing and returns on surplus cash for financial institutions; collateralized liquidity management free from counterparty risk.
Tri-Party Repo (TREPS)View Answer Centrally cleared collateral market intermediated by CCIL; anonymous order matching and multilateral net settlement.View Answer A platform for mutual funds, insurance companies, and NBFCs to safely lend surplus cash overnight without bank intermediation.
Cash Management Bills (CMBs)View Answer Non-standard short-term sovereign instruments with maturity under 91 days to meet unforeseen cash needs of the Government of India.View Answer Bridging sudden gaps in the government's cash flow; a highly flexible and rapid liquidity management tool.

One-Liner Questions

What is the primary and most important function of the 'Financial Market' in an economy?
Mobilizing financial resources from surplus units (savers/households) and channeling them toward deficit units (entrepreneurs/governments) for productive investment.

What does 'Price Discovery' mean in the context of financial assets?
The determination of the fair market price of financial assets (shares, bonds, bills) by transparent, competitive market forces of demand and supply.

How does the financial market provide 'Liquidity' to investors?
By offering a ready platform to convert securities into cash at any time at minimal cost and without any major loss of value.

By bringing buyers and sellers onto a centralized technological platform, thereby minimizing search costs, brokerage costs, and contract enforcement costs.

What are the three universal characteristics of money market instruments?
1. Short-term maturity (up to 1 year), 2. Extremely high liquidity, and 3. Low default risk.

Why are money market instruments called 'Near Money' in economics?
Because they can be converted into actual cash immediately in minimal time without any major capital loss.

What type of financial needs does the money market primarily fulfill?
Short-term working capital and daily liquidity needs of businesses, banks, and governments.

Because transactions take place in massive financial sizes running into crores of rupees, and mainly large institutional players participate in it.

Why does the money market become the first channel for Monetary Policy Transmission?
Because as soon as the RBI changes the policy repo rate, the very first impact falls on inter-bank call rates and T-Bill yields in the money market.

Who are generally the 'Net Borrowers' in the money market?
Commercial banks (facing liquidity shortages), the Central Government, primary dealers, and large corporate houses.

Which institutions are generally the 'Net Lenders' in the money market?
Banks with surplus cash, mutual funds (especially liquid funds), and insurance companies (LIC, etc.).

How does the money market play a central role in government financing?
By enabling the government to bridge cash mismatches between daily expenditures and the arrival of quarterly tax revenues through T-Bills and CMBs without printing currency.

What is the role of 'Primary Dealers' (PDs) in the money market?
Specialist institutions authorized by the RBI that mandatorily bid in primary issuances of government securities and T-Bills and provide liquidity to the market.

What is the exact time limit of 'Call Money' in the money market?

What is referred to as 'Notice Money' in the money market?
Inter-bank borrowing for a pre-determined period ranging from 2 days to 14 days.

What is the tenure of 'Term Money' in the money market?
An inter-bank loan for a period exceeding 15 days and up to a maximum of 1 year.

What is the most unique credit feature of the call money market?
It is a completely 'Uncollateralized / Clean' market; no securities or assets are pledged as collateral in it.
Tap to view answer
Which institutions are permitted to borrow and lend in the call/notice money market?
Only scheduled commercial banks (including Small Finance Banks and RRBs), cooperative banks, and authorized Primary Dealers (PDs).
Tap to view answer
Why are Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs) and mutual funds barred from borrowing directly in the call money market?
To protect the inter-bank financial system from non-banking risks and contagion risk.
Tap to view answer
For the adjustment of which statutory requirement do commercial banks primarily borrow from the call money market?
To bridge temporary shortfalls in the daily Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) mandated by the RBI.
Tap to view answer
Which corridor does the RBI operate to limit the volatility of the Call Rate?
The LAF Interest Rate Corridor, whose lower bound is the SDF rate, upper bound is the MSF rate, and the repo rate sits at the center.

By whom and on whose behalf are Treasury Bills (T-Bills) issued?

Can state governments in India issue Treasury Bills?
No; the sovereign right to issue Treasury Bills rests solely with the Central Government of India.

Which three tenures of T-Bills are currently issued regularly in India?
91-day, 182-day, and 364-day T-Bills.

What type of yield instruments are Treasury Bills?
Zero-Coupon Securities; they carry no coupon interest, rather they are issued at a discount and redeemed at face value.

Which RBI scheme provides retail investors direct access to government securities and T-Bills?
RBI Retail Direct Scheme (November 2021).

What are Cash Management Bills (CMBs)?
Non-standard short-term T-Bills with a maturity period of less than 91 days, issued to meet unforeseen immediate cash needs of the government.

Why are Treasury Bills highly attractive to commercial banks?
Because they are 100% risk-free, can be sold for cash immediately, and fulfill the banks' Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR) requirement.

What is the standard definition of Commercial Paper (CP)?
An unsecured promissory note issued by top-rated corporate companies, financial institutions, and PDs to raise short-term working capital.

In the year 1990 (based on the recommendations of the Vaghul Committee).

A minimum credit rating of 'A-2' or its equivalent prescribed by SEBI.

Only a Scheduled Commercial Bank.

What is the main economic advantage of issuing CP for corporate companies compared to bank loans?
Companies with high creditworthiness obtain working capital from the open market at cheaper interest rates than banks' lending rates (MCLR/EBLR).

What is a Certificate of Deposit (CD)?
A negotiable term instrument issued by scheduled commercial banks and All India Financial Institutions (AIFIs) against a lump-sum amount deposited for a fixed period.

In which year was the Certificate of Deposit (CD) introduced in India?
In the year 1989.

What are the minimum and maximum maturity periods of a CD issued by commercial banks?
Minimum 7 days and maximum 1 year.

What is the most fundamental difference between an ordinary Bank Fixed Deposit (FD) and a Certificate of Deposit (CD)?
An FD is non-transferable and is broken at the bank before maturity, whereas a CD is a negotiable instrument that can be sold to another investor in the secondary market before maturity.

No; under RBI regulatory rules, banks cannot grant loans against their own CDs nor can they buy them back prematurely.

Under what circumstances do banks primarily resort to Certificates of Deposit (CDs)?
When credit demand in the economy is very strong while the growth rate of retail bank deposits is slow, banks issue CDs to attract wholesale institutional liquidity.

From a Bill of Exchange drawn by a seller on a buyer based on the actual credit purchase and sale of commercial goods in business transactions.

What benefit does a supplier derive from Bill Discounting?
They receive immediate cash from the bank (after deducting a nominal discount) without having to wait for the buyer's 60- or 90-day credit period.

What is the meaning of a Repo (Repurchase Agreement) contract?
An agreement in which one party sells approved government securities to raise cash and enters into a legal contract to buy them back at a pre-determined price on a future date.
Tap to view answer
What is a Reverse Repo contract?
The opposite perspective of Repo; in which one party lends cash to receive securities and later sells them back to recover its principal and interest.
Tap to view answer
What is the 'Repo Rate' in a repo contract?
The difference between the original sale price and the repurchase price of the securities, which is the interest rate paid by the borrower.
Tap to view answer
Why is a 'Haircut' (margin) applied in repo deals?
To protect the lender from potential fluctuations in the market value of the collateral securities (for example, lending ₹95 against a ₹100 security).
Tap to view answer
What is Tri-Party Repo (TREPS)?
A centrally cleared collateralized market where a neutral third party (CCIL) provides complete guarantees for collateral management, margining, and settlement.

Which older instrument in the Indian money market did TREPS replace in November 2018?
CBLO (Collateralized Borrowing and Lending Obligation).
Tap to view answer
What role does the Clearing Corporation of India (CCIL) play in money market Repo and TREPS deals?
It acts as a 'Central Counterparty' (CCP) to perform multilateral net settlement and eliminates default risk for both parties.

Where do Money Market Mutual Funds (MMMFs / Liquid Funds) invest their funds?
Exclusively in highly liquid and safe money market instruments (T-Bills, CP, CD, TREPS) with a residual maturity of up to 91 days.

What is the feature of the RBI's 'Standing Deposit Facility' (SDF)?
A facility launched in April 2022 under which banks can deposit their excess cash directly with the RBI without any collateral government securities and earn interest.

What is the use of the Marginal Standing Facility (MSF) in the money market?
During severe emergency cash crunches, banks can pledge their statutory SLR securities up to a limit to borrow overnight funds from the RBI at a penal interest rate higher than the repo rate.

What are Inter-Bank Participation Certificates (IBPCs)?
Short-term inter-bank instruments through which banks transfer a portion of their loan portfolio (either on a risk-sharing or non-risk-sharing basis) to other banks.

What is a Banker's Acceptance?
A commercial bill whose payment at maturity is officially guaranteed by a reputed bank affixing its stamp.

What is the authoritative definition of 'Liquidity' in the context of a financial asset in economics?
The ability to convert an asset into cash immediately without any major loss of value, in the shortest possible time, and with minimal transaction costs.

Why is the presence of 'High Liquidity' considered an essential prerequisite in the money market?
So that financial institutions and corporates can meet their unforeseen cash shortfalls immediately without delay and without the risk of insolvency.

How does the high liquidity of the money market facilitate the daily operations of commercial banks?
Banks do not need to keep a large share of their deposits as zero-interest idle cash; they deploy it in the money market to earn interest and withdraw it immediately when needed.
Tap to view answer
What is the direct benefit of high liquidity in the money market for the corporate sector?
The working capital cycle for companies' daily operations, raw material purchases, and employee salary payments is never disrupted.
Tap to view answer
What is the role of money market liquidity in the government's fiscal management?
Temporary cash gaps arising between the quarterly cycle of tax revenues and daily government expenditure can be immediately financed by issuing T-Bills.

If liquidity suddenly dries up in the money market (Liquidity Freeze), what is the immediate impact on the economy?
Short-term interest rates spike unexpectedly, banks stop lending to one another, companies' Commercial Papers (CPs) fail to roll over, and a credit crunch ensues.

What was the main reason for the liquidity freeze in the US money market during the 2008 global financial crisis?
Extreme counterparty distrust in Asset-Backed Commercial Paper (ABCP) and the inter-bank market following the bankruptcy of Lehman Brothers.

How did the IL&FS crisis in India in September 2018 shake the liquidity of the domestic money market?
After IL&FS defaulted on repaying its Commercial Papers (CPs), mutual funds stopped rolling over CPs of NBFCs, triggering a severe liquidity crisis across the entire non-banking sector.

What does the 'Liquidity Coverage Ratio' (LCR) mandate for banks under the Basel III regulatory framework?
Banks are required to hold 100% High-Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA such as government bonds, T-Bills) to withstand a 30-day severe financial stress scenario.
Tap to view answer
What is the objective of the 'Net Stable Funding Ratio' (NSFR) under Basel III?
To prevent banks from financing their long-term assets (over 1 year) solely with short-term, highly volatile money market borrowings (preventing maturity mismatch).
Tap to view answer
What is the macroeconomic risk of having 'Excess Liquidity' in the economy?
Short-term interest rates fall excessively low, speculation increases, and uncontrolled expansion in money supply creates the risk of fueling inflation.
Tap to view answer
When there is excessive surplus liquidity in the economy, what action does the RBI take in the money market?
The RBI absorbs cash from banks through Reverse Repo or SDF, or conducts Variable Rate Reverse Repo (VRRR) auctions.
Tap to view answer
What adverse impact does a 'Liquidity Deficit' in the economy have on economic activities?
Credit flow halts, borrowing becomes excessively expensive, and the pace of economic growth slows down.
Tap to view answer
Which instruments does the RBI use to provide relief to the money market during a severe liquidity deficit?
Repo auctions, Targeted Long-Term Repo Operations (TLTRO), Open Market Operation (OMO) Purchases, and opening the MSF window.

What is the difference between a 'Bilateral Market' and a 'Multilaterally Cleared Market' in the money market?
In a bilateral market, two parties bear risk directly, whereas in a multilaterally cleared market (such as TREPS/CCIL), individual counterparty risk is completely eliminated due to a central counterparty.
Tap to view answer
What positive impact does high efficiency of the money market have on the country's stock and bond markets (capital market)?
It provides securities brokers, underwriters, and institutional investors with quick and cheap short-term credit for their daily margins and trade settlements.

What does the short end of the 'Yield Curve' in the money market reflect?
The tightness or looseness of the RBI's current monetary policy and the immediate liquidity conditions in the market.
Tap to view answer
What is the role of the 'Negotiated Dealing System' (NDS-OM) in the digitization of the Indian money market?
The RBI's anonymous, screen-based electronic order-matching platform for secondary market trading of government securities and T-Bills.

How does money market liquidity avert the risk of a 'Bank Run' in the financial system?
If depositors suddenly start withdrawing heavy cash from a bank, that bank can immediately raise cash by selling its liquid money market assets (T-Bills, CDs) or entering into repo transactions.

What is the final and enduring macroeconomic conclusion from the complete study of Financial Market Part 2?
The money market is the sensitive nervous system of any financial system that facilitates short-term liquidity, risk-free pricing, and monetary policy transmission; without it, neither can the banking system function smoothly nor can the long-term edifice of the capital market remain stable.
