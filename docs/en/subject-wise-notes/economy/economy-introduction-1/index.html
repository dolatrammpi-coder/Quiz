Feature of the EconomyKey Form / StructureEconomic Role
Developing & Emerging EconomyView Answer: Lower-middle income bracket, rapid economic growth (6.5–7.5%), and the world's 5th largest Nominal GDP and 3rd largest PPP GDP.View Answer: Serving as a major engine of global economic growth, building a large domestic consumption market, and attracting international capital and investment.
Dualistic EconomyView Answer: Co-existence of a highly modern and globalized services/IT sector alongside traditional subsistence agriculture and a vast informal sector.View Answer: Creating disparities in economic productivity, where the informal sector provides livelihoods to 85–90% of the workforce but lags behind in capital formation.
Unique Structural TransformationView Answer: Direct transition to the dominance of the tertiary/services sector without moving from agriculture to traditional manufacturing.View Answer: Achieved rapid services-led growth, though a shortage of industrial jobs persisted for crores of semi-skilled workers shifting out of agriculture.
Mixed EconomyView Answer: Democratic synthesis of capitalist market forces (price mechanism) and socialist state planning (public welfare).View Answer: Granting private enterprises freedom for profit and innovation while the state guarantees strategic infrastructure and social welfare.
Public Sector: Commanding HeightsView Answer: Leadership of Central Public Sector Enterprises (CPSEs) in strategic and basic industries (steel, railways, energy, heavy machinery).View Answer: Laying the foundation for capital formation in the early decades, balanced regional development, curbing monopolies, and employment generation.
Public Sector: Modern ReformsView Answer: Post-1991 Navratna/Maharatna autonomy, disinvestment, and the policy of bare minimum government presence in only 4 strategic sectors (2021).View Answer: Improving commercial efficiency of public enterprises, reducing the fiscal burden on taxpayers, and state withdrawal from non-strategic sectors.
Private Sector: Engine of Economic GrowthView Answer: End of the License-Quota Raj after liberalization; rapid innovation, investment, and job creation by MSMEs and large corporates.View Answer: Healthy price and quality competition in the market, development of modern digital financial technology, and over 80% contribution to the country's total exports.
Public-Private Partnership (PPP Framework)View Answer: Co-creation of highways, ports, airports, and energy infrastructure through BOT, HAM, and EPC models.View Answer: Leveraging private capital, technical efficiency, and timely project execution for national development without straining the fiscal deficit.

One-Liner Questions

According to the World Bank's income classification, in which category does the Indian economy currently fall?
Lower-Middle Income Economy, where per capita Gross National Income (GNI) falls within the prescribed threshold.

Based on Nominal Gross Domestic Product (Nominal GDP), which largest economy in the world is India currently?
The 5th largest economy in the world (after the USA, China, Germany, and Japan).
Tap to view answer
What is the global rank of the Indian economy on the basis of Purchasing Power Parity (PPP)?
The 3rd largest economy in the world, behind only China and the United States of America.
Tap to view answer
Why is the Indian economy called an 'Emerging Market Economy'?
Because it represents rapid GDP growth, an expanding middle-class population, modernization of financial markets, and growing integration into global trade.
Tap to view answer
What is the statistical paradox of 'Agrarian Nature' in the Indian economy?
Agriculture's contribution to GDP has declined to just 15% to 18%, yet nearly 44% to 46% of the country's working population still depends on agriculture for livelihood.
Tap to view answer
What is the status of India's 'Per Capita Income' as a developing economy?
Despite the massive size of overall GDP, per capita income remains considerably low compared to developed countries due to a huge population of over 1.4 billion.
Tap to view answer
What does 'Dualistic Economy' mean in economics, and how does it apply to India?
The simultaneous existence of a highly modern, capital-intensive, and technological sector (such as IT and pharma) alongside traditional, subsistence-level agriculture and a vast informal sector.
Tap to view answer
What is the extent of the 'Informal Sector' (unorganized sector) in the Indian economy?
About 85% to 90% of the country's total workforce is employed in the unorganized sector, where job security, fixed wages, and social security cover are absent.
Tap to view answer
What is meant by 'Demographic Dividend' in the Indian economy?
Having the working-age population (15 to 64 years) exceed 65% of the total population and a minimal Dependency Ratio, which offers an opportunity for rapid economic growth.
Tap to view answer
Until when is India's demographic dividend projected to remain favourable?
Until around 2055–2060, making India the largest global hub for supplying a young workforce.
Tap to view answer
Why is the Structural Transformation of India's economic development considered 'unusual or unique'?
Because unlike the traditional development model (Agriculture → Manufacturing → Services), India leaped directly from agriculture to the Services Sector, and the manufacturing sector could not expand as expected.
Tap to view answer
What is the current approximate contribution of different sectors to GDP?
Services sector ~54–55%, Industry/Manufacturing sector ~28–30%, and Agriculture sector ~15–17%.
Tap to view answer
In which sector is 'Disguised Unemployment' primarily found in the Indian economy?
In the agricultural sector, where more workers are engaged than required and removing a few workers causes no adverse impact on total output (marginal productivity is zero).
Tap to view answer
What has been the general level of the Gross Savings and Investment Rate in the Indian economy?
Around 30% to 32% of GDP, which provides a healthy foundation for a developing economy.
Tap to view answer
What does the 'Gini Coefficient', which measures income inequality in the Indian economy, indicate?
Rising economic inequality despite growth, where the top 10% of the population holds over 60% to 70% of the country's total national wealth.
Tap to view answer
What is India's status in the 'Human Development Index' (HDI) as a developing economy?
Medium Human Development Category; where steady improvements are occurring in health, education, and life expectancy, though significant challenges still remain.
Tap to view answer
What is the significance of domestic consumption (Private Final Consumption Expenditure - PFCE) in India's growth rate?
It accounts for about 58% to 60% of India's GDP, providing stability to the economy through internal demand even during global recessions.
Tap to view answer
What is the biggest structural vulnerability of India's External Sector?
Dependence on foreign imports for over 85% of crude oil requirements, causing global oil shocks to directly impact the Current Account Deficit and inflation.

What is the standard definition of a 'Mixed Economy' in economics?
An economic system in which both the public sector (state) and the private sector (individual entrepreneurs) co-exist and operate together, and the market price mechanism and government planning jointly determine resource allocation.
Tap to view answer
Of which two opposing economic ideologies is a mixed economy a practical synthesis?
Capitalism (free-market system) and Socialism (state-controlled production system).
Tap to view answer
With which dual objectives are economic activities conducted in a mixed economy?
With the 'Profit Motive' by the private sector and 'Social Welfare' by the public sector.
Tap to view answer
The ideas of which great 20th-century British economist strengthened the theoretical foundation of the mixed economy?
John Maynard Keynes (J. M. Keynes), who advocated fiscal intervention by the state in place of unregulated capitalism after the Great Depression of 1930.
Tap to view answer
Why did independent India choose a mixed economy instead of a purely capitalist or purely communist model?
Because capitalism would neglect social justice and equity, while communism would abolish democratic freedoms and private property; hence, this synthesis was chosen to achieve economic justice alongside democracy.
Tap to view answer
By what name is the mixed-economy philosophy of independent India's first Prime Minister Pandit Jawaharlal Nehru known?
'Democratic Socialism'.
Tap to view answer
In which industrial policy of independent India was the mixed-economy framework formally accepted for the first time?
In the Industrial Policy Resolution, 1948 (IPR 1948).
Tap to view answer
In which historic 1955 Congress session was the resolution for a 'Socialistic Pattern of Society' passed?
At the Avadi Session (Madras).
Tap to view answer
What role did the Industrial Policy Resolution 1956 (IPR 1956) assign to the public sector in the mixed economy?
The role of 'Commanding Heights', where the development of all heavy, basic, and strategic industries was kept under the exclusive monopoly of the state.
Tap to view answer
What is the role of the 'Price Mechanism' in a mixed economy?
Prices of consumer goods and non-strategic products are determined by market demand and supply, whereas for essential commodities (medicines, fertilizers, public foodgrains), the government enforces price controls and subsidies.
Tap to view answer
What is the place of 'Economic Planning' in a mixed economy?
The state formulates plans based on national socio-economic priorities and guides the private sector toward those goals through public investment and regulatory policies.
Tap to view answer
Which word was added to the Preamble of the Indian Constitution by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment (1976) to clarify the socialist tilt of the mixed economy?
The word 'Socialist'.
Tap to view answer
Which Articles of the Constitution provide the legal foundation for the social-justice goals of the mixed economy?
The Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP): Article 38 (welfare state) and Articles 39(b) and 39(c) (equitable distribution of resources and prevention of concentration of wealth).
Tap to view answer
What major shift in balance did the 1991 economic reforms (LPG) bring about in the nature of India's mixed economy?
A transition from a public-sector-dominated mixed economy to a market-oriented mixed economy, where the private sector was made the primary engine of economic growth.
Tap to view answer
What is the status of 'Consumer Sovereignty' in a mixed economy?
Consumers have full freedom to choose goods produced by the private sector, though the state can impose a complete ban on harmful goods (narcotics) in the public interest.
Tap to view answer
How are 'Labour Rights' protected in a mixed economy?
By preventing the exploitation of workers by private capitalists through minimum wage laws, social security codes, and industrial dispute laws.
Tap to view answer
What is the most serious potential inherent weakness or criticism of a mixed economy?
Excessive red tape, licensing inefficiency, and the growth of losses and corruption in the public sector (if the state's regulatory framework is not transparent).
Tap to view answer
Why was establishing the public sector in heavy basic industries indispensable in India at the time of Independence?
Because private industrialists lacked huge capital, had limited capacity to bear technical risks, and these projects had a very long Gestation Period.
Tap to view answer
Which department of the Central Government exercises administrative control over Central Public Sector Enterprises (CPSEs)?
The Department of Public Enterprises (DPE) under the Ministry of Heavy Industries and Public Enterprises.
Tap to view answer
Into which three categories are Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs) in India divided based on their organizational form?
1. Departmental Undertakings (such as Indian Railways, Department of Posts), 2. Statutory Corporations (such as LIC, RBI), and 3. Government Companies (such as ONGC, SAIL, BHEL).
Tap to view answer
According to Section 2(45) of the Companies Act, 2013, what is the legal definition of a 'Government Company'?
A company in which at least 51% of the paid-up share capital is held by the Central Government, any State Government, or jointly by both.
Tap to view answer
Which scheme was launched in 1997 to grant managerial and financial autonomy to Public Sector Undertakings?
The Navratna and Miniratna Scheme.
Tap to view answer
Into which three prestigious categories are Central Public Sector Enterprises (CPSEs) currently classified?
1. Maharatna, 2. Navratna, and 3. Miniratna (Category I and II).
Tap to view answer
In which year was the granting of 'Maharatna' status to a CPSE initiated?
In December 2009, so that large Indian public companies could be developed into Global Giants.
Tap to view answer
What are the major financial eligibility conditions a CPSE must fulfil to obtain 'Maharatna' status?
It must already have Navratna status, be listed on the stock exchange, and over the preceding 3 years have an average annual net profit exceeding ₹5,000 crore, average annual net worth exceeding ₹15,000 crore, and average annual turnover exceeding ₹25,000 crore.
Tap to view answer
How much autonomy does the Board of Directors of a Maharatna company have to invest in a single project without government approval?
15% of the company's net worth or a maximum of ₹5,000 crore (whichever is less).
Tap to view answer
Which entities are included among India's major Maharatna companies?
ONGC, IOCL, NTPC, SAIL, BHEL, GAIL, BPCL, HPCL, Coal India (CIL), Power Grid (PGCIL), PFC, REC, and Oil India Limited (OIL).
Tap to view answer
Up to what amount do Navratna companies have the financial authority to invest in a single project without prior government approval?
Up to a maximum of ₹1,000 crore or 15% of the company's net worth (whichever is less).
Tap to view answer
How did Public Sector Undertakings advance 'Balanced Regional Development' in India?
By establishing huge factories and townships in backward, tribal, and remote areas such as Bhilai, Rourkela, Durgapur, and Bokaro.
Tap to view answer
In which year was the 'Memorandum of Understanding' (MoU) system introduced in the capital expenditure of Public Sector Undertakings?
In 1986 (on the recommendation of the Arjun Sengupta Committee), to formalize a contract of performance and autonomy between the ministry and the PSU.
Tap to view answer
What serious inefficiencies and problems had emerged in the Indian public sector prior to 1991?
Excessive political and bureaucratic interference, overstaffing, loss-making pricing policies, and lack of accountability.
Tap to view answer
Which quasi-judicial body was established in 1987 to recommend the revival or closure of sick and loss-making public enterprises?
The Board for Industrial and Financial Reconstruction (BIFR).
Tap to view answer
What is meant by 'Disinvestment' in the public sector?
Raising financial resources by the government selling a part of its shareholding/equity in public sector enterprises to private investors or the general public.
Tap to view answer
In which year was the disinvestment process formally initiated in India?
In the financial year 1991–92 (based on the recommendations of the Dr. C. Rangarajan Committee).
Tap to view answer
Which dedicated department under the Ministry of Finance handles disinvestment and asset management?
DIPAM (Department of Investment and Public Asset Management).
Tap to view answer
What is the fundamental difference between normal disinvestment and 'Strategic Disinvestment'?
In normal disinvestment, the government sells only minority shares and retains control, whereas in strategic disinvestment, the government transfers management control along with more than 50% stake to a private buyer.
Tap to view answer
Which is the largest historic example of strategic disinvestment in India, completed in 2022?
The transfer of 100% stake in Air India to the Tata Group.
Tap to view answer
Under the 'New Public Sector Enterprise (PSE) Policy' announced in the Union Budget 2021–22, how many sectors have been classified as 'Strategic Sectors'?
Only 4 broad sectors: 1. Atomic Energy, Space, and Defence; 2. Transport and Telecommunications; 3. Power, Petroleum, Coal, and other Minerals; and 4. Banking, Insurance, and Financial Services.
Tap to view answer
According to the New PSE Policy (2021), what is the government's policy in 'Non-Strategic Sectors'?
All public sector enterprises in non-strategic sectors will either be privatized or completely closed down.
Tap to view answer
What is the 'National Monetization Pipeline' (NMP) announced in August 2021?
A plan to raise ₹6 lakh crore over 4 years by leasing operational rights of brownfield public assets—such as roads, railways, power transmission, and gas pipelines—to the private sector.
Tap to view answer
Is permanent ownership of government assets being sold under the National Monetization Pipeline (NMP)?
No; ownership remains with the government, and only the operational and revenue rights of the asset are given to the private sector for a fixed tenure.
Tap to view answer
What is the mandatory rate of 'Corporate Social Responsibility' (CSR) for Public Sector Undertakings to fulfil their social obligations?
Under Section 135 of the Companies Act, 2013, spending at least 2% of the average net profits of the preceding 3 years is mandatory.
Tap to view answer
What is the foremost and driving force of the 'Private Sector' in a mixed economy?
Individual initiative, innovation, risk-taking capacity, and the 'Profit Motive'.
Tap to view answer
Which regulatory regime exercised strict control over the Indian private sector prior to 1991?
The 'License-Permit-Quota Raj', where government permission was mandatory for capacity expansion, product selection, and imports.
Tap to view answer
How did the New Industrial Policy of 1991 liberate the private sector?
Compulsory industrial licensing was abolished for most industries, the number of industries reserved for the public sector was reduced, and doors were opened for Foreign Direct Investment (FDI).
Tap to view answer
Currently in India, for all industries except which ones has licensing been completely abolished?
Except for only 4–5 sensitive industries (such as defence aerospace, industrial explosives, hazardous chemicals, and tobacco products).

Which new law was enacted by repealing the old MRTP Act, 1969, which curbed monopolies of big industrial houses in the private sector?
The Competition Act, 2002.

Which institution established under the Competition Act, 2002 is tasked with maintaining healthy competition in the market and preventing unfair trade practices?
The Competition Commission of India (CCI).
Tap to view answer
What is the significance of 'Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises' (MSMEs) within the private sector in a mixed economy?
It accounts for ~30% of manufacturing output, ~45% of total exports, and is the second-largest employer after agriculture, employing over 11 crore people.
Tap to view answer
What is 'Public-Private Partnership' (PPP) used to involve the private sector in infrastructure projects?
A long-term contract between a government agency and private companies for building public services or infrastructure.
Tap to view answer
What does the 'BOT' model mean in PPP projects?
Build-Operate-Transfer; where a private company constructs the project, operates it by collecting tolls for a fixed period, and later hands it over to the government.
Tap to view answer
What is the 'Hybrid Annuity Model' (HAM) introduced in 2016 in road and highway construction?
The government provides 40% of the project cost during construction and the private developer invests the remaining 60%, which the government later repays in annual instalments (Annuity), with toll risk borne by the government.
Tap to view answer
What is meant by an 'EPC' contract in PPP projects?
Engineering, Procurement and Construction; where 100% of the funds are provided by the government and the private company executes the construction work strictly on contract.
Tap to view answer
Which high-level committee was constituted in 2015 to revisit and revitalize the PPP model in India?
The Dr. Vijay Kelkar Committee (Committee on Revisiting and Revitalising the PPP Model).
Tap to view answer
Who are the major sector Regulators constituted to regulate the private sector in a mixed economy?
SEBI for capital markets, TRAI for telecommunications, IRDAI for insurance, and CERC for electricity.
Tap to view answer
What is the contribution of the 'Information Technology' (IT-BPM) sector, which revolutionized the country's services exports through the private sector?
Annual foreign exchange earnings exceeding $200 billion and direct employment to over 50 lakh high-skilled professionals.
Tap to view answer
What is the status of private entrepreneurship in India's startup ecosystem?
India is the world's third-largest startup ecosystem, with over 100 'Unicorns' ($1B+ valuation) emerging from private-sector innovation.

What is meant by 'Crony Capitalism' in the private sector?

National Single Window System (NSWS), digitization of processes, reduction in corporate tax rates (22%, and 15% for new manufacturing), and simplification of labour codes.

What should be the ideal relationship between the public and private sectors in a mixed economy?
Not adversaries or competitors, but indispensable complements; where the public sector provides infrastructure and social security, and the private sector drives innovation, investment, and efficiency.

What is the share of the 'Tertiary Sector' in Gross Value Added (GVA) in the Indian economy?
A massive share of about 54% to 55%.

A mismatch between the skills required for jobs available in the market and the actual skills possessed by workers.

When a recession or contraction in demand across the overall economy reduces industrial output and leads to layoffs.

A severe condition in which the economy simultaneously experiences sluggish economic growth (stagnation), high unemployment, and high inflation.

The difference between the government's total expenditure and total revenue receipts excluding borrowings (the government's total annual borrowing requirement).

To limit the fiscal deficit to 3% of GDP, eliminate the revenue deficit, and ensure intergenerational equity.
