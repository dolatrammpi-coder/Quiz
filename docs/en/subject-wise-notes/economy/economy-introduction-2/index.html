Key Facts & Contributions
Introduction to Indian Economy Part 2
Economic Indicator / ConceptDefinition or Key FeatureEconomic Significance
Gross Domestic Product (GDP)Total market value of all final goods and services produced within the domestic geographical territory of a country during a financial year.The primary macroeconomic measure of an economy's overall economic size, growth rate, global ranking, and international comparisons.
Gross Value Added (GVA)Net value added obtained by subtracting Intermediate Consumption from the total value of output (a supply-side measure).Enables accurate sectoral analysis of the relative performance and productivity of different sectors of the economy (agriculture, industry, services).
Gross National Product (GNP)Total final value produced by the normal residents of a country; obtained by adding Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA) to GDP.Indicates citizens' real national purchasing power, income from foreign assets, and the strength of external economic transactions.
Net National Product at Factor Cost (NNP at FC)Value obtained by subtracting Net Indirect Taxes (NIT) from NNP at Market Prices; officially designated as 'National Income'.The true measure of total factor returns (rent, wages, interest, profit) actually earned by the factors of production (land, labour, capital, enterprise).
Per Capita Income (PCI)Average income obtained by dividing a country's real national income in a financial year by its total population (PCI = National Income / Population).A key indicator of citizens' average standard of living, purchasing power, and comparative economic welfare across states and countries.
Real GDP vs. Nominal GDPReal GDP is measured at constant base-year prices (2011-12), whereas Nominal GDP is measured at current market prices of the ongoing financial year.Measures real growth in the physical output of goods and services in the economy by eliminating the artificial distortion of inflation.
GDP DeflatorRatio of Nominal GDP to Real GDP [(Nominal GDP / Real GDP) × 100]; a comprehensive price index of all goods and services produced in the economy.The broadest indicator of economy-wide inflation; wider in scope than the Consumer Price Index (CPI) and Wholesale Price Index (WPI).
Personal Disposable Income (PDI)Net spendable income remaining with individuals after deducting direct personal taxes (income tax) and non-tax administrative payments from Personal Income.Determines the actual consumption expenditure capacity and financial savings of the household sector; the primary driver of aggregate private demand.

One-Liner Questions

Into which three major traditional sectors are economic activities of an economy classified based on the nature of production?
1. Primary Sector, 2. Secondary Sector, and 3. Tertiary Sector.

Which major activities are included under the 'Primary Sector' of the economy?
All activities directly based on the extraction or exploitation of natural resources, such as agriculture, forestry, fishing (pisciculture), animal husbandry, and mining and quarrying.
Tap to view answer
By what other name is the 'Secondary Sector' known, and what does it encompass?
The Industrial Sector; it includes manufacturing, construction, and electricity, gas, and water supply, which transform primary products into finished or semi-finished goods.
Tap to view answer
What is the defining characteristic of the 'Tertiary Sector'?
Also known as the Services Sector, it does not produce tangible goods but provides intangible services such as trade, transport, communications, banking, insurance, education, and healthcare.
Tap to view answer
What constitutes the 'Quaternary Sector' in a knowledge-based economy?
Information- and knowledge-based services, such as software development, research and development (R&D), financial consulting, data analytics, and information technology.
Tap to view answer
Who is included in the 'Quinary Sector' (Gold Collar Professions) of an economy?
Top-level policymakers, decision-makers, and strategists, such as senior government officials, jurists, scientific advisors, and corporate board directors.
Tap to view answer
What is the fundamental difference between 'Economic Growth' and 'Economic Development'?
Economic growth is a quantitative concept (increase in GDP and per capita income), whereas economic development is a qualitative concept (improvements in standard of living, health, education, equity, and institutional quality).
Tap to view answer
Is economic development possible without economic growth?
No; growth is a necessary precondition for development, though high growth alone does not automatically guarantee development if the benefits of growth are not equitably distributed.

In which year and by which visionary leader was the first estimate of National Income and Per Capita Income in India made?
In 1867-68, by the 'Grand Old Man of India', Dadabhai Naoroji.

In which famous book did Dadabhai Naoroji estimate India's annual per capita income at ₹20?
In 'Poverty and Un-British Rule in India', where he also propounded the 'Drain of Wealth' theory.

Who presented the First Scientific Estimate of National Income in India, and in which year?

Which innovative dual methodology did Dr. V. K. R. V. Rao use to estimate National Income?
A combined application of the Product Method for the agriculture sector and the Income Method for the industrial and services sectors.

Who chaired the 'National Income Committee' constituted by the Government of India in August 1949 after Independence?
Professor Prasanta Chandra Mahalanobis (P. C. Mahalanobis).
Tap to view answer
Which two eminent economists served as advisory members on the 1949 National Income Committee?
Dr. D. R. Gadgil and Dr. V. K. R. V. Rao (while Simon Kuznets served as an international advisor).
Tap to view answer
Which institution was established in May 1951 for the regular statistical compilation and publication of National Income in India?
The Central Statistics Office (CSO).
Tap to view answer
Which apex statistical body did the Government of India create in May 2019 by merging the CSO and NSSO?
The National Statistical Office (NSO), under the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI).

What is a 'Closed Economy' in economics?
An economy that has no foreign trade (imports or exports) or financial capital flows with the rest of the world (X = 0, M = 0).
Tap to view answer
What is the definition of an 'Open Economy'?
An economy that actively engages in free transactions of goods, services, financial assets, and capital with the rest of the world.
Tap to view answer
Which two components comprise the basic Two-Sector Model of the Circular Flow of Income?
The Household Sector and Business Firms.
Tap to view answer
What is the difference between 'Real Flow' and 'Money Flow' in the Circular Flow of Income?
Real Flow refers to the physical movement of factors of production and goods/services, whereas Money Flow refers to the monetary flow of factor payments (wages, rent) and consumption expenditure.
Tap to view answer
What are the main examples of 'Leakages' (Withdrawals) in the Circular Flow of Income?
Savings, taxes paid to the government, and import expenditure, all of which withdraw income from the circular flow.
Tap to view answer
What are 'Injections' in the Circular Flow of Income?
Investment, government expenditure, and export receipts, which add new income into the circular flow.
Tap to view answer
What is the difference between a 'Stock Variable' and a 'Flow Variable' in macroeconomics?
A stock variable is measured at a specific point of time (e.g., total wealth, foreign exchange reserves), whereas a flow variable is measured over a period of time (e.g., GDP, annual income).
Tap to view answer
Is National Income a stock variable or a flow variable?
It is a Flow Variable, as it is calculated over a specific period of one financial year (1 April to 31 March).
Tap to view answer
What is the standard and authoritative definition of Gross Domestic Product (GDP)?
The total monetary market value of all final goods and services produced within the domestic geographical territory of a country during a financial year.
Tap to view answer
What does the concept of 'Domestic Territory' include in the definition of GDP?
The country's political boundaries, Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ) up to 200 nautical miles, Indian embassies, consulates, and military bases abroad, and global operations of Indian ships and aircraft.
Tap to view answer
If an economic activity takes place inside the US Embassy located in India, whose GDP will it be included in?
In the GDP of the United States, because foreign embassies are treated as part of the domestic territory of their respective countries, not India.
Tap to view answer
Why are only 'Final Goods' included in the calculation of GDP?
To avoid the serious statistical error of 'Double Counting'.
Tap to view answer
What are 'Intermediate Goods', and why are they not added separately to GDP?
Goods used as raw materials in producing other goods or for resale; they are excluded because their value is already embedded in the value of the final good.
Tap to view answer
If a farmer sells wheat worth ₹20 to a mill owner, the mill owner sells flour worth ₹30 to a baker, and the baker sells bread worth ₹50 to a consumer, how much value is added to GDP?
Only ₹50 (the value of the final bread), or equivalently the sum of value added at all three stages (20 + 10 + 20 = ₹50).
Tap to view answer
What is Nominal GDP (GDP at Current Prices)?
GDP calculated by valuing the physical goods and services produced in the current financial year at the prevailing market prices of that same year.
Tap to view answer
What is the definition of Real GDP (GDP at Constant Prices)?
GDP calculated by valuing the actual physical output of a financial year at the fixed prices of a chosen Base Year.
Tap to view answer
Why is Real GDP considered superior to Nominal GDP for measuring an economy's actual physical progress and productive capacity?
Because growth in Real GDP reflects only an increase in physical output, free from the illusion of artificial price rises caused by inflation.
Tap to view answer
What is the current official 'Base Year' for calculating National Income and GDP in India?
Financial Year 2011-12 (implemented in January 2015, replacing the earlier 2004-05 base year).
Tap to view answer
What precautions are taken when selecting a 'Base Year' for National Income accounting?
It must be a normal year free from severe droughts, wars, pandemics, global financial crises, or abnormal price volatility.
Tap to view answer
What is the GDP Deflator, and what is its formula?
GDP Deflator = (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) × 100.
Tap to view answer
If India's Nominal GDP in a financial year is ₹300 lakh crore and Real GDP is ₹150 lakh crore, what will the GDP Deflator be?
200 [(300 / 150) × 100], indicating that the general price level has risen by 100% compared to the base year.
Tap to view answer
How is the GDP Deflator broader in coverage than the Consumer Price Index (CPI)?
Because it covers all goods and services produced in the economy (consumer, capital, and government), and its weights are not fixed but adjust automatically with changes in output composition.
Tap to view answer
What are the three traditional and theoretically equivalent methods of calculating GDP?
1. Product / Value Added Method, 2. Income Method, and 3. Expenditure Method.
Tap to view answer
What is the standard macroeconomic identity for calculating GDP under the Expenditure Method?
GDP = C + I + G + (X - M), where C = Private Consumption, I = Gross Investment, G = Government Expenditure, and (X - M) = Net Exports.
Tap to view answer
What does 'C', i.e., Private Final Consumption Expenditure (PFCE), represent in the Expenditure Method?
Total final consumption expenditure incurred by individuals, households, and non-profit institutions on food, clothing, housing, vehicles, and services.
Tap to view answer
What is the typical share of Private Final Consumption Expenditure (PFCE) in India's GDP?
Approximately 58% to 60% of total GDP (making it the largest demand component of the Indian economy).
Tap to view answer
What are the main sub-components of 'I', i.e., Gross Capital Formation (GCF), in the Expenditure Method?
1. Gross Fixed Capital Formation (GFCF — machinery, buildings, infrastructure), 2. Change in Inventories/Stocks (CII), and 3. Acquisition of Valuables (gold, jewellery).
Tap to view answer
Why is Gross Fixed Capital Formation (GFCF) considered most critical for an economy's future growth?
Because it measures real investment in new factories, highways, railways, bridges, and plants, which builds the economy's future productive capacity.
Tap to view answer
What is included in 'G', i.e., Government Final Consumption Expenditure (GFCE), under the Expenditure Method?
Current expenditure incurred by the government on employee salaries, administrative expenses, defence services, and public services provided free of charge to citizens.
Tap to view answer
Why does '(X - M)', i.e., Net Exports, generally remain negative in India's case under the Expenditure Method?
Because India's merchandise imports (M, especially crude oil, gold, and electronics) significantly exceed its total exports (X).
Tap to view answer
Which four factor payments are summed to calculate National Income under the Income Method?
1. Compensation of Employees (wages and salaries), 2. Rent, 3. Interest, and 4. Profits + Mixed Income of the Self-Employed.
Tap to view answer
What is the concept of 'Mixed Income of Self-Employed' in the Income Method?
Income of self-employed persons such as small shopkeepers, farmers, lawyers, and doctors, where it is statistically impossible to separate interest on their own capital, wages for their own labour, and profit.
Tap to view answer
How is Value Added calculated for each producing unit under the Product or Value Added Method?
Gross Value Added (GVA) = Value of Output - Intermediate Consumption.
Tap to view answer
What is the formula for calculating 'Value of Output'?
Value of Output = Total Annual Sales + Change in Stock (Closing Stock - Opening Stock) + Production for Self-Consumption.
Tap to view answer
Are unpaid household tasks such as cooking, childcare, and cleaning performed by homemakers included in GDP?
No; because these non-monetary services do not involve any formal market financial transaction, making accurate market valuation difficult.
Tap to view answer
Is the imputed benefit derived by a homeowner living in their own house included in GDP?
Yes; it is included as 'Imputed Rent', estimated on the basis of prevailing market rents for similar properties in the neighbourhood.
Tap to view answer
Is the amount received from the resale of used cars or old houses in the secondary market added to GDP?
No; their production was already counted in earlier years. Only the current brokerage or commission earned by intermediaries on that transaction is included in current GDP.
Tap to view answer
Is money received from buying and selling shares, bonds, or debentures included in GDP?
No; it is merely a paper transfer of financial claims and assets that generates no new real good or service (only brokerage fees are included).
Tap to view answer
Why are government scholarships, old-age pensions, unemployment allowances, and disaster relief grants excluded from National Income?
Because they are 'Transfer Payments' (unearned income) for which no direct productive activity or factor service is rendered in the current year.
Tap to view answer
Why is income earned from illegal activities (such as smuggling, black marketing, and gambling) excluded from GDP?
Because they are unlawful, lack official accounting records, and form part of the informal shadow economy (Black Economy).
Tap to view answer
What is the single biggest limitation of GDP that prevents it from being a complete indicator of human welfare?
It does not reflect income distribution or inequality; if GDP grows rapidly while all wealth accrues to the top 1%, general public welfare does not improve.
Tap to view answer
How does GDP misrepresent Environmental Degradation?
It does not deduct the loss caused by natural resource depletion and pollution from output; instead, it counts expenditure on pollution clean-up as an addition to GDP.
Tap to view answer
What is the concept of 'Green GDP'?
Net GDP obtained by subtracting the monetary value of environmental damage, pollution, and natural resource depletion caused by economic activities from conventional GDP.
Tap to view answer
What is the standard definition of Gross Value Added (GVA)?
The new real economic value added by a producer, industry, or sector in its production process over and above the cost of raw materials and services (intermediate inputs).
Tap to view answer
What is the fundamental conceptual difference between GDP and GVA in viewing the economy?
GDP reflects the 'Demand / Expenditure Side' of the economy, whereas GVA reflects the 'Supply / Sectoral Production Side'.
Tap to view answer
Which international United Nations framework for National Income accounting was adopted by the Central Statistics Office (CSO) in January 2015?
System of National Accounts 2008 (SNA 2008).
Tap to view answer
Prior to the historic 2015 revision, which indicator was used to measure Headline Economic Growth in India?
GDP at Factor Cost ($GDP_{FC}$).
Tap to view answer
After the 2015 revision, what is recognized as India's official Headline Economic Growth Rate?
GDP at Market Prices ($GDP_{MP}$).
Tap to view answer
After the 2015 revision, which standard is used to measure the performance of individual economic sectors such as agriculture, industry, and services?
Gross Value Added at Basic Prices (GVA at Basic Prices).
Tap to view answer
What is the mathematical relationship between GDP at Market Prices ($GDP_{MP}$) and GVA at Basic Prices ($GVA_{Basic}$)?
GDP (at Market Prices) = GVA (at Basic Prices) + Net Product Taxes, where Net Product Taxes = Product Taxes - Product Subsidies.
Tap to view answer
What is the technical difference between 'Product Taxes' and 'Production Taxes'?
Product taxes are levied directly per unit of output or sale (e.g., GST, customs duty, excise duty), whereas production taxes are independent of output volume and levied for carrying on business (e.g., land revenue, stamp duty, factory licence fees).
Tap to view answer
What is the difference between 'Product Subsidies' and 'Production Subsidies'?
Product subsidies are paid directly per unit of product (e.g., food, fertilizer, LPG, and petroleum subsidies), whereas production subsidies are given to enterprises as a whole (e.g., interest and power subsidies to small industries, khadi units, or farmers).
Tap to view answer
What is the relationship between GVA at Factor Cost ($GVA_{FC}$) and GVA at Basic Prices ($GVA_{Basic}$)?
GVA at Basic Prices = GVA at Factor Cost + Production Taxes - Production Subsidies.
Tap to view answer
If net product taxes collected by the government surge in a financial year, what impact will be visible on GDP versus GVA?
The GDP growth rate will appear higher than the GVA growth rate, because taxes inflate GDP without an equivalent increase in real production (GVA).
Tap to view answer
Why do policymakers find GVA more useful than GDP for Sectoral Analysis?
Because GVA is free from the distortion of product taxes and subsidies, clearly revealing the true rise or fall in productive capacity across agriculture, manufacturing, or services.
Tap to view answer
Which comprehensive database was introduced in the 2015 revision to incorporate corporate sector financial data into the National Accounts?
The MCA-21 database (Ministry of Corporate Affairs online database), covering annual financial statements of over 5 lakh active companies.
Tap to view answer
Which older methodology did the use of the MCA-21 database replace in National Income estimation?
Heavy reliance on RBI's small industrial surveys and the Annual Survey of Industries (ASI).
Tap to view answer
What is the definition of Gross National Product (GNP)?
The total market value of all final goods and services produced by the normal residents (citizens) of a country during a financial year, whether produced within the domestic territory or abroad.
Tap to view answer
What is the equation linking GDP and GNP?
GNP = GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA).
Tap to view answer
How is 'Net Factor Income from Abroad' (NFIA) calculated?
NFIA = Factor income earned by our normal residents from the rest of the world - Factor income earned by non-residents within our domestic territory and remitted abroad.
Tap to view answer
What are the three main components of NFIA?
1. Net compensation of employees from abroad, 2. Net income from property and entrepreneurship (dividends, interest, rent), and 3. Net retained earnings of Indian companies operating abroad.
Tap to view answer
Why is India's GNP generally lower than its GDP?
Because India's NFIA remains persistently negative; profits, dividends, and interest repatriated from India by foreign investors exceed the factor income earned abroad by Indian residents.
Tap to view answer
Why are remittances sent home by migrants to their families in India not included in NFIA?
Because remittances are 'Transfer Payments' (Current Transfers), not factor income; hence, they are added to National Disposable Income (NDI) rather than GNP.
Tap to view answer
What distinguishes 'Gross' from 'Net' National Income aggregates?
Depreciation (Consumption of Fixed Capital - CFC); subtracting depreciation from Gross yields Net (Net = Gross - Depreciation).
Tap to view answer
What is meant by 'Depreciation' (CFC) in economics?
The loss in value of fixed assets (machinery, equipment, buildings) during the production process due to normal wear and tear, breakage, and obsolescence.
Tap to view answer
How is Net Domestic Product (NDP) calculated?
NDP = GDP - Depreciation.
Tap to view answer
What is the formula for Net National Product at Market Prices ($NNP_{MP}$)?
$NNP_{MP}$ = GNP (at Market Prices) - Depreciation.
Tap to view answer
What official term is given to Net National Product at Factor Cost ($NNP_{FC}$) in economics?
'National Income' (NI).
Tap to view answer
What adjustment converts between Market Price ($MP$) and Factor Cost ($FC$)?
Net Indirect Taxes (NIT); $Market Price = Factor Cost + NIT$ (where NIT = Indirect Taxes - Subsidies).
Tap to view answer
How is NNP at Factor Cost ($NNP_{FC}$ / National Income) derived from NNP at Market Prices?
$NNP_{FC}$ = $NNP_{MP}$ - Indirect Taxes + Government Subsidies.
Tap to view answer
What is meant by 'Factor Cost'?
The total factor remuneration actually paid at the factory-gate level to the four factors of production (land, labour, capital, enterprise).
Tap to view answer
What is 'Market Price'?
The final price at which a good or service is actually purchased by the consumer in the market (including indirect taxes and net of subsidies).
Tap to view answer
If the government imposes heavy indirect taxes on a commodity, how does that affect its Factor Cost and Market Price?
Its Market Price becomes significantly higher than its Factor Cost ($MP > FC$).
Tap to view answer
If the government provides a subsidy on a commodity (such as foodgrains or fertilizers) that exceeds the tax on it, what situation arises?
Its Market Price falls below its actual Factor Cost ($MP < FC$).
Tap to view answer
What is 'National Disposable Income' (NDI)?
The total net income available to a country for consumption and saving from both domestic production and net current transfers (gifts/remittances) from abroad ($NDI = NNP_{MP} + \text{Net Current Transfers from the Rest of the World}$).
Tap to view answer
What is the concept of 'Private Income'?
Total income accruing to the private sector (individuals and private corporations) from all sources (factor income + current transfers) both domestically and from abroad.
Tap to view answer
What is meant by 'Personal Income' (PI)?
Total current income actually received by individuals and households from all sources before paying direct personal taxes.
Tap to view answer
Which items are subtracted from National Income when deriving Personal Income?
1. Corporate Tax, 2. Undistributed Corporate Profits (retained earnings), and 3. Social Security Contributions.
Tap to view answer
Which major item is added to National Income when calculating Personal Income?
Net transfer payments made by the government and business firms to individuals (such as pensions and relief grants).
Tap to view answer
What is the definition of 'Personal Disposable Income' (PDI)?
Actual net income available to individuals to spend or save at their discretion ($PDI = \text{Personal Income} - \text{Direct Personal Taxes / Income Tax} - \text{Miscellaneous Government Fees and Fines}$).
Tap to view answer
In which two primary ways is Personal Disposable Income (PDI) utilized?
Consumption (C) and Personal Savings (S); i.e., $PDI = C + S$.
Tap to view answer
What is the formula for calculating 'Per Capita Income' (PCI)?
Per Capita Income = Total National Income ($NNP_{FC}$) / Total Mid-Year Population of the country.
Tap to view answer
How is Real Per Capita Income (Real PCI) calculated?
By dividing Real National Income at constant base-year (2011-12) prices by the total population.
Tap to view answer
If a country's Real National Income is growing at 6% per annum and its population is growing at 2% per annum, what will the net growth in Per Capita Income be?
Approximately 4% net growth (6% - 2% = 4%).
Tap to view answer
What does the wide disparity in Per Capita Income across different states in India indicate?
Severe regional economic imbalance, where states like Goa and Sikkim have per capita incomes several times higher than Bihar and Uttar Pradesh.
Tap to view answer
What is the state-level aggregate that measures a state's total economic output?
Gross State Domestic Product (GSDP).
Tap to view answer
What is the net income of a state called?
Net State Domestic Product (NSDP); dividing NSDP by the state's total population yields the state's Per Capita Income.
Tap to view answer
Which methods does the World Bank use to compare Per Capita Income across different countries?
The World Bank Atlas Method and the Purchasing Power Parity (PPP) method.
Tap to view answer
What is the principle of 'Purchasing Power Parity' (PPP)?
Determining the exchange rate between two currencies based on the ratio of purchasing power required to buy an identical basket of goods in both countries.
Tap to view answer
Why is India's Per Capita Income on a PPP basis significantly higher than its Per Capita Income at market exchange rates?
Because non-tradable domestic services, food, transport, and labour are considerably cheaper in India than in developed countries.
Tap to view answer
Approximately how much is India's current Nominal Per Capita Income estimated to be?
Roughly ₹2,00,000 to ₹2,15,000 per annum (approximately $2,500 to $2,700 USD).

According to World Bank standards, how high must India's Per Capita Income be to qualify as a 'Developed Nation'?
A minimum Gross National Income per capita exceeding $14,000 USD (the high-income country threshold).

What is the 'Middle-Income Trap' that India must avoid in the future?
An economic situation where a developing country progresses from low-income to middle-income status, but fails to transition to high-income developed status due to low productivity and lack of innovation.

In National Income accounting, what is 'Capital Consumption Allowance' another name for?
Depreciation.

What happens in an economy when Gross Investment exceeds Depreciation?

'Capital Consumption' (capital erosion) takes place, and the economy's total productive capacity begins to shrink.
