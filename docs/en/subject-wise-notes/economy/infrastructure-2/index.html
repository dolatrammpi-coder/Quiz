Infrastructure Part 2

Complete analysis of the National Highways network, its role in economic growth, logistics cost efficiency (NLP 2022, FASTag, MMLP), and Bharatmala Pariyojana

Quick Reference
Key Facts & Contributions
Infrastructure Part 2
Infrastructure Project / SectorKey FeatureEconomic Significance
National Highways NetworkView Answer: Accounts for only about 2.2% (1,46,000+ km) of the country's total road network, yet carries 40% of total road freight and passenger traffic.View Answer: Artery of inter-state trade; integration of national markets, inter-regional connectivity, and direct contribution to economic growth.
Bharatmala PariyojanaView Answer: Umbrella programme based on a corridor-centric approach; development of economic corridors, feeder routes, and expressways across 34,800 km (Phase-1).View Answer: Improving average freight movement speed nationwide, connecting 550 districts with at least 4-lane highways, and reducing national logistics costs.
Greenfield ExpresswaysView Answer: Access-controlled 8-lane routes developed on entirely new alignments, such as the Delhi-Mumbai Expressway (1,386 km).View Answer: 50% reduction in travel time between metropolises, massive savings in vehicle operating and fuel costs, and emergence of new industrial clusters along routes.
Multi-Modal Logistics Parks (MMLPs)View Answer: Integrated hubs developed at 35 strategic locations on a Hub-and-Spoke Model; direct multi-modal convergence of rail, road, and inland waterways.View Answer: Freight consolidation, warehousing and cold-chain accessibility, and a key driver for bringing logistics costs down to 8% of GDP.
Electronic Tolling (FASTag & GNSS Tolling)View Answer: RFID-based automated toll collection and upcoming satellite-based (GNSS) barrier-free dynamic tolling system.View Answer: Eliminating waiting time at toll plazas, reducing fuel wastage and pollution, and ensuring transparent revenue collection and national logistics efficiency.
Hybrid Annuity Model (HAM)View Answer: PPP financing model; 40% of capital cost paid by the government during construction and 60% paid to the developer as annuities.View Answer: De-risking private developers to attract private capital, reviving stalled road projects, and ensuring timely construction.
Toll-Operate-Transfer & InvITs (TOT & InvITs)View Answer: Monetization of operational toll highways on long-term leases (National Monetisation Pipeline) and raising retail/institutional capital.View Answer: Unlocking blocked government capital to mobilize non-debt financial resources for building new greenfield highways.
Setu Bharatam & Char Dham Mahamarg ProjectView Answer: Making National Highways free of railway level crossings (ROB/RUB construction) and providing 900 km of all-weather connectivity to Himalayan pilgrimage sites.View Answer: Unprecedented improvement in road safety, reduction in accident fatalities, military mobility in border and strategic areas, and expansion of religious tourism.

What is the 'EPC Model' (Engineering, Procurement and Construction) in National Highway development?
A publicly funded model where the government bears 100% of construction costs and the private contractor only executes the construction work.
Tap to view answer
What are the two main variants of the 'BOT Model' (Build-Operate-Transfer) in highway PPPs?
1. BOT-Toll (the developer recovers costs by collecting tolls), and 2. BOT-Annuity (the government pays a fixed annual annuity to the developer).
Tap to view answer
What is the 'Hybrid Annuity Model' (HAM) introduced in 2016 to revive stalled road projects?
The government provides 40% of capital during construction while the developer arranges 60%; during operations, the government pays assured annuities to the developer over 15 years.
Tap to view answer
Who bears the financial risk of toll collection under the Hybrid Annuity Model (HAM)?
The entire right to collect tolls and the revenue risk rest with the government (NHAI), not the private developer.
Tap to view answer
What is the 'Toll-Operate-Transfer' (TOT Model) for highway monetisation?
Handing over already operational, toll-generating public highways to private investors for 15 to 30 years for toll collection and maintenance in exchange for an upfront lump-sum payment.
Tap to view answer
Which financial trust was established by NHAI to raise capital from retail and institutional investors?
NHAI InvIT (Infrastructure Investment Trust).
Tap to view answer
What is the primary revenue source of the Central Road and Infrastructure Fund (CRIF)?
The 'Road and Infrastructure Cess' levied on petrol and diesel.
Tap to view answer
Besides roads, in which other sectors is the CRIF fund utilized?
For developing railway infrastructure, waterways, urban public transport, and social infrastructure.

What is the difference between 'Brownfield' and 'Greenfield' projects in National Highway development?
Brownfield projects widen existing narrow roads (from 2-lane to 4/6-lane), whereas Greenfield projects build entirely new roads on fresh alignments away from dense habitations and old routes.

How does capital investment in National Highways boost 'Gross Value Added' (GVA) in the economy?
By generating massive demand for cement, steel, bitumen, and heavy machinery, and accelerating growth in the manufacturing and construction sectors.

Approximately 2.5 to 3.2 times; meaning every ₹1 spent on road construction generates over ₹3 in national output growth over subsequent years.

By linking remote rural production centres to major consumption hubs and ports, bringing price uniformity and supply stability across the country.

By enabling perishable crops (fruits, vegetables, milk) to reach distant large mandis within hours without spoilage, reducing post-harvest losses.

Warehouses, logistics parks, food processing units, dhabas, petrol pumps, and automobile workshops spring up on both sides of highways, creating a vast local economy.

How do National Highways help bridge 'Regional Disparities' in India?
By connecting backward regions such as Bundelkhand, the North-East, and Vidarbha to industrial corridors, bringing private investment, manufacturing, and new jobs.

'Direct employment' for civil engineers, equipment operators, and unskilled workers during construction; and 'Indirect employment' in tolling, maintenance, hospitality, and freight transport during operations.

How do National Highways propel India's Tourism Sector?
By providing comfortable and rapid road access to religious pilgrimage sites (Char Dham, Buddhist Circuit), historical monuments, and natural destinations.

What is the standard authoritative definition of 'Logistics' in economics?
The holistic, integrated management of planned physical transportation, warehousing, material handling, packaging, and information flow of raw materials and finished goods from point of origin to point of consumption.
