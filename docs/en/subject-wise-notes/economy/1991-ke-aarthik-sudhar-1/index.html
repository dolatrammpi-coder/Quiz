Economic Reforms of 1991 Part 1

In-depth analysis of the background of the 1991 economic crisis, Balance of Payments crisis, foreign exchange reserves, and fiscal imbalance

🔍
Quick Reference
Key Facts & Contributions
Economic Reforms of 1991 Part 1
Crisis / FactorKey Situation or ImpactLink to Reforms
Unprecedented Depletion of Foreign Exchange ReservesIn June 1991, reserves fell to just $1.1 billion (barely 2 to 3 weeks of import cover).Two-stage devaluation of the rupee (~18-19%), pledging of 67 tonnes of gold, and introduction of the LERMS dual exchange rate system.
Balance of Payments (BoP) & Current Account DeficitIn 1990-91, the Current Account Deficit (CAD) reached a precarious level of 3.1% of GDP (~$9.7 billion).Abolition of quantitative import restrictions, introduction of the Exim Scrips system, and gradual full convertibility of the rupee on the current account.
High & Uncontrolled Fiscal DeficitIn 1990-91, the Centre's Gross Fiscal Deficit was recorded at 7.8% of GDP and the Revenue Deficit at 3.5%.Cuts in fertilizer and sugar subsidies, budgetary discipline, disinvestment in public sector undertakings, and later laying the foundation for the FRBM Act.
Automatic Monetization of Deficit & InflationPrinting of currency via Ad-hoc Treasury Bills expanded money supply and pushed inflation to 13-14%.Formal termination of the automatic monetization system in 1997 (introduction of WMA) and independent monetary policy management.
External Debt & Unsustainable Debt-Service BurdenTotal external debt climbed to $83.8 billion (~30% of GDP) and the debt-service ratio reached a critical level of 35.3%.Avoidance of short-term high-cost borrowings, restructuring of NRI schemes, and promotion of non-debt capital inflows (FDI/FII).
Sovereign Credit Rating Downgrade & Capital FlightMoody's and S&P downgraded India's rating to speculative (Junk) grade; massive withdrawal of NRI deposits.Restoration of credibility among foreign investors, relaxation of restrictive FERA provisions, and mobilization of capital via India Development Bonds.
Licence-Permit-Quota Raj & PSU InefficiencyLack of domestic and external competition, high Incremental Capital-Output Ratio (ICOR), and public sector losses.New Industrial Policy (NIP) of 24 July 1991: delicensing most industries and shrinking the public sector reservation list.
External Shock of the Gulf War (1990-91)Sharp surge in international crude oil prices doubled the import bill and halted remittances from the Gulf.Energy sector diversification, gradual rationalization of petroleum price controls, and emergency borrowing (CCFF) from the IMF.

One-Liner Questions

What was the most prominent immediate external trigger of the 1991 Indian economic crisis?
The Gulf War of August 1990, which caused a sharp spike in international crude oil prices and abruptly halted foreign exchange remittances from Gulf countries.

Which fundamental tendency of the 1980s economic policies laid the foundation for the 1991 crisis?
Expansionary Fiscal Policy, under which the government indulged in unchecked public and consumption expenditure without an adequate revenue base.
Tap to view answer
What was the primary trade strategy adopted by India from Independence until the 1980s?
Import Substitution Industrialization (ISI), aimed at protecting domestic industries to minimize dependence on foreign imports.
Tap to view answer
How did the 'Licence-Permit-Quota Raj' damage the efficiency of the Indian industrial sector?
By eliminating competition and fostering monopolies, which led to poor product quality, excessive production costs, and stunted technological progress.
Tap to view answer
Why was India's average GDP growth rate in the 1980s (around 5.6%) considered unsustainable?
Because this growth was heavily debt-driven—fueled by excessive internal and external borrowing—rather than productivity gains or sound fiscal savings.
Tap to view answer
What was the primary symptom of the poor financial health of Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs) in the 1980s?
Extremely low or negative Return on Capital and heavy dependence on budgetary support to cover their losses.
Tap to view answer
How did India's domestic political situation between 1989 and 1991 deepen the crisis?
Political instability under coalition governments (the V. P. Singh and Chandra Shekhar governments), which prevented timely, tough fiscal and structural decisions.
Tap to view answer
To what level did India's inflation rate surge in 1990-91?
Around 13.8% to 14% (double digits), primarily driven by excessive deficit monetization and supply disruptions in essential commodities.
Tap to view answer
What was the impact of international credit rating agencies (Moody's and S&P) downgrading India's sovereign rating in 1991?
India's creditworthiness fell into the 'Junk / Speculative Grade' (Below Investment Grade), completely shutting off access to fresh commercial borrowing in international markets.
Tap to view answer
How did Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) react following the credit rating downgrade and fears of sovereign default?
Large-scale withdrawal of NRI deposits (Capital Flight), pushing the foreign exchange crisis to a breaking point.
Tap to view answer
What alternative did the Government of India adopt in the late 1980s when concessional foreign aid declined?
Heavy reliance on short-term, high-interest External Commercial Borrowings (ECBs) and non-resident foreign currency deposits.
Tap to view answer
Why was the 1991 crisis viewed not merely as a liquidity crisis, but as a threat of a solvency crisis?
With foreign exchange reserves nearly exhausted, India faced a real risk of formal sovereign default on its international debt obligations.
Tap to view answer
What was India's total public debt-to-GDP ratio during 1990-91?
Over 55% of GDP, indicating a severe debt sustainability crisis on both internal and external fronts.
Tap to view answer
What was the biggest structural weakness of Indian exports prior to 1991?
Low competitiveness due to an overvalued rupee and technological backwardness, alongside excessive dependence on primary and non-diversified products.
Tap to view answer
How did the disintegration of the Soviet Union (USSR) in 1990-91 act as an external economic shock for India?
The collapse of the Rupee-Rouble Trade arrangement abruptly deprived India of a large, protected export market.
Tap to view answer
What unbalanced impact did partial import liberalization in the 1980s have on the economy?
Import intensity rose rapidly, while the absence of strong export promotion measures caused the trade deficit to spiral out of control.
Tap to view answer
Who were the Prime Minister and Finance Minister who steered the reforms at the height of the 1991 crisis?
Prime Minister P. V. Narasimha Rao and Finance Minister Dr. Manmohan Singh.
Tap to view answer
Which landmark step was proposed in the Chandra Shekhar government's Interim Budget (1991) to mobilize fiscal resources?
The first formal proposal for non-strategic disinvestment of up to 20% equity in Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs).
Tap to view answer
Which agriculture-related items placed immense pressure on the government exchequer during the 1980s?
Fertilizer subsidies, unviable concessions on irrigation and electricity, and the onset of farm loan waivers.
Tap to view answer
What was the primary failure of India's banking system prior to 1991?
Pre-emption of bank resources to finance government deficits through high CRR/SLR, coupled with strict administrative controls on interest rates.
Tap to view answer
Which committee was constituted in August 1991 to study the financial sector crisis and outline reforms?
The M. Narasimham Committee (Narasimham Committee-I).
Tap to view answer
Which developmental ideology of India was permanently transformed by the 1991 economic crisis?
The Nehruvian state-directed closed socialist model (Fabian Socialism) was transformed into a market-friendly open economy.
Tap to view answer
What did the state of India's Incremental Capital-Output Ratio (ICOR) indicate in the 1980s?
An excessively high ICOR, meaning disproportionately large amounts of capital were being wasted to generate each additional unit of output.
Tap to view answer
What form of non-cooperation did foreign commercial banks exhibit toward Indian banks in early 1991?
They refused to confirm or guarantee Indian Letters of Credit (LCs), disrupting even routine import trade.
Tap to view answer
Why is the 1991 crisis regarded as a 'Watershed Moment' in Indian economic history?
Because it ended four decades of controls and protectionism and ushered in the era of Liberalization, Privatization, and Globalization (LPG).
Tap to view answer
Which law was considered the biggest barrier to Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) prior to 1991?
The Foreign Exchange Regulation Act, 1973 (FERA 1973), which generally capped foreign equity ownership at 40%.
Tap to view answer
What were the two main macroeconomic deficits that characterized the nature of the 1991 crisis?
The 'Fiscal Deficit' on the domestic front and the 'Current Account Deficit' on the external front (Twin Deficits).
Tap to view answer
Which law enacted in 1969 severely restricted capacity expansion and diversification of large business houses in India?
The Monopolies and Restrictive Trade Practices Act, 1969 (MRTP Act), which was largely dismantled during the 1991 reforms.
Tap to view answer
Which committee constituted in 1985 gave the first comprehensive recommendations for rationalizing India's monetary system and interest rate structure?
Prof. Sukhamoy Chakravarty Committee (1985).
Tap to view answer
What term did economist Prof. Raj Krishna coin for India's low average growth rate (3.5%) between the 1950s and 1980s?
The 'Hindu Rate of Growth', which India attempted to break out of in the 1980s through excessive borrowing.
Tap to view answer
How did the Small Scale Industries (SSI) Reservation policy affect the competitiveness of Indian manufacturing?
By preventing Economies of Scale, keeping Indian products expensive and uncompetitive compared to global standards.
Tap to view answer
Which political event in May 1991 dealt a severe blow to the confidence of foreign investors and NRIs?
The assassination of former Prime Minister Rajiv Gandhi, which created extreme political instability and uncertainty in India.
Tap to view answer
Which two components of non-developmental expenditure grew the fastest in the late 1980s?
Defence expenditure and government spending on internal and border security.
Tap to view answer
What is the standard definition of 'Balance of Payments' (BoP) in economics?
A systematic accounting record of all economic transactions executed between the residents of a country and the rest of the world during a financial year.
Tap to view answer
What are the two major accounts of the Balance of Payments?
The Current Account and the Capital Account.
Tap to view answer
When does a Current Account Deficit (CAD) occur?
When the total value of imports of goods, services, and unilateral transfers exceeds their total export value.
Tap to view answer
What percentage of GDP did India's Current Account Deficit (CAD) reach in 1990-91?
3.1% of GDP (approx. US $9.7 billion), which was completely unsustainable given the negligible foreign exchange reserves at the time.
Tap to view answer
What was India's average Current Account Deficit during the Sixth Five-Year Plan (1980-85)?
Only about 1.3% of GDP, which more than doubled during the Seventh Plan.
Tap to view answer
What is the fundamental difference between the Balance of Trade and the Current Account?
Balance of Trade measures only the import and export of visible goods (merchandise), whereas the Current Account also includes invisibles (services, remittances, income).
Tap to view answer
Approximately how much was India's Trade Deficit recorded at in 1990-91?
Around ₹9,449 crore (approx. US $5.9 billion).
Tap to view answer
What was the primary reason for the uncontrolled expansion of the trade deficit during 1985-90?
An extraordinary rise in import expenditure relative to exports, particularly in POL (Petroleum, Oil, and Lubricants) and capital goods imports.
Tap to view answer
How was India financing its massive Current Account Deficit in the final years of the 1980s?
Through short-term External Commercial Borrowings (ECBs) and high-interest NRI deposits.
Tap to view answer
Why was dependence on debt-creating capital flows considered dangerous?
Because they carry fixed repayment timelines, and investors pull out their capital immediately when market confidence wavers.
Tap to view answer
Which major event in the Capital Account crippled the Balance of Payments during the 1991 crisis?
Massive outflow of NRI deposits (Capital Flight) and refusal by international banks to roll over short-term credit lines.
Tap to view answer
Approximately how much capital was withdrawn from Foreign Currency Non-Resident (FCNR) accounts in 1991?
Net withdrawals exceeding US $1 billion, which completely dried up the country's foreign exchange liquidity.
Tap to view answer
What type of exchange rate regime was in force in India prior to 1991?
A basket-pegged managed exchange rate, under which the rupee's value was administratively fixed by the central bank.
Tap to view answer
What adverse impact did an administratively fixed, overvalued rupee have on Indian trade?
Indian exports became expensive and uncompetitive in global markets, while imports became artificially cheap and were incentivized.
Tap to view answer
What immediate defensive step did the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) take in 1990-91 to save the Balance of Payments?
Import Compression, under which a steep cash margin of up to 200% was made mandatory on imports.
Tap to view answer
What negative impact did strict Import Compression measures have on the Indian economy?
Shortages of raw materials and intermediate goods pushed the growth rate of industrial production (IIP) into negative territory, triggering a severe economic slowdown.
Tap to view answer
What double blow did India's Invisibles Account suffer due to the Gulf War?
Remittances from Indian workers in Kuwait and Iraq stopped, and massive foreign exchange had to be spent on airlifting stranded citizens.
Tap to view answer
From which special IMF facility did the Government of India first borrow emergency funds to tackle the BoP crisis?
The Compensatory and Contingency Financing Facility (CCFF) and a First Credit Tranche Stand-By Arrangement in January 1991.
Tap to view answer
What key structural demands did the IMF place in exchange for providing an emergency financial package to India?
Real devaluation of the rupee, sharp reduction in the fiscal deficit, lowering of import tariffs, and deregulation of trade.
Tap to view answer
What does the 'Twin Deficit Hypothesis' explain in the context of 1991?
An unchecked rise in the fiscal deficit directly widens the Current Account Deficit (CAD), as government deficits boost domestic aggregate demand and pull in additional imports.
Tap to view answer
According to national income accounting, what is the relationship between the Saving-Investment Gap and the Current Account Deficit?
CAD = (I - S) + (G - T); i.e., when private investment exceeds domestic savings and government expenditure exceeds revenue, a Current Account Deficit inevitably arises.
Tap to view answer
What was India's Export-to-Import Coverage Ratio in 1990-91?
Only about 60% to 66%, meaning India's export earnings could not even cover two-thirds of its import bill.
Tap to view answer
What was meant by 'Maturity Mismatch' in the 1991 crisis?
India's payable liabilities were extremely short-term, whereas its foreign exchange assets were negligible and not immediately liquidatable.
Tap to view answer
Which bonds did the government issue to attract NRIs amid the severe foreign exchange crisis?
India Development Bonds (IDBs), which mobilized substantial foreign currency in US dollars and British pounds.
Tap to view answer
Which new system was introduced in July 1991 in the trade sector to reduce the rigidity of import licensing?
The Exim Scrips system, which directly linked imports to export performance.
Tap to view answer
How did the Exim Scrips scheme operate?
Exporters were issued freely tradable import licences worth 30% of their export value, which could be bought and sold in the market at a premium.
Tap to view answer
What unintended consequence resulted from the overly stringent provisions of the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act (FERA) 1973?
Diversion of foreign exchange away from official banking channels and the unchecked expansion of a parallel informal Hawala market.
Tap to view answer
How was the 1991 BoP crisis more challenging than the 1966 crisis?
The 1966 crisis was centered primarily on agricultural drought and food aid, whereas the 1991 crisis involved large-scale international commercial debt, sovereign credibility, and foreign exchange liquidity.
Tap to view answer
Which financial export subsidy was immediately abolished in July 1991 to reduce the fiscal burden?
The Cash Compensatory Support (CCS) scheme.
Tap to view answer
Up to what level were peak customs tariff rates set in India prior to 1991?
Over 300% (with average tariff rates above 100%), which rendered Indian industries highly inefficient and globally isolated.
Tap to view answer
What was the main drawback of controlling imports through Quantitative Restrictions (QRs)?
Creation of monopolistic conditions for domestic producers and complete erosion of innovation and cost competitiveness.
Tap to view answer
What was the status of non-debt capital flows (FDI and FII) in India's Capital Account prior to 1991?
Virtually negligible (less than $100 million per year), as policies toward foreign investment were overly rigid and apprehensive.
Tap to view answer
What was the spread (Hawala premium) observed between the official exchange rate and the informal Hawala rate at the peak of the 1991 crisis?
Around 20% to 25%, causing remittances to flow through the Hawala market instead of official channels.
Tap to view answer
Which long-term trade policy did India adopt after the 1991 reforms to strengthen its Balance of Payments?
An outward-looking Export-Led Growth Strategy, emphasizing integration with global supply chains.

Which four components are included in a country's Foreign Exchange (Forex) Reserves?
1. Foreign Currency Assets (FCA), 2. Gold, 3. Special Drawing Rights (SDR), and 4. Reserve Tranche Position (RTP) in the IMF.

Barely 2 to 3 weeks (approx. 13 to 15 days) of essential import cover.

At least 3 months (12 weeks) of import expenditure.

What percentage decline was recorded in India's foreign exchange reserves between March 1990 and June 1991?
A drop of more than 65% (from $3.37 billion in March 1990 down to $1.1 billion).

Being declared in Sovereign Default on international debt installments, which could have led to India's global trade boycott.

The country's official Gold Reserves.

The State Bank of India (SBI) sold/swapped 20 metric tonnes of gold to the Union Bank of Switzerland (UBS), raising around $200 million.

What was the second major phase of gold pledging executed by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) in July 1991?
Pledging 46.91 metric tonnes of gold with the Bank of England and the Bank of Japan to obtain an emergency loan of approx. $405 million.
Tap to view answer
How much total gold was airlifted to foreign banks by India during May and July 1991?
A total of approx. 67 metric tonnes of gold (20 tonnes by SBI and 47 tonnes by RBI).

How was the airlifting of gold to London and Zurich perceived in Indian public opinion and politics?
As a deep blow to national pride and a sovereign embarrassment, making a radical overhaul of economic policies indisputable.

Did the Government of India subsequently redeem the 67 tonnes of pledged gold from foreign banks?
Yes, as the foreign exchange position improved by November-December 1991, India repaid the entire loan with interest and reclaimed all its gold.

In how many stages was the Rupee Devaluation carried out in July 1991?
In two planned stages: first on 1 July 1991 (~9%) and second on 3 July 1991 (~11%), resulting in a cumulative devaluation of approx. 18.5% to 19%.
Tap to view answer
Why was the devaluation split into two stages (1 and 3 July) instead of a single shock?
To gauge the immediate reaction of the foreign exchange market, curb speculation, and avoid panic in the market.
Tap to view answer
What is the technical difference between 'Devaluation' and 'Depreciation' of a currency in economics?
Devaluation occurs by government/central bank decree under a fixed exchange rate regime, whereas depreciation occurs automatically via market demand and supply under a floating exchange rate regime.
Tap to view answer
What was the immediate economic objective of the sharp devaluation of the rupee in July 1991?
To immediately stem capital flight, make Indian exports globally competitive, and bring NRI remittances back to official channels.
Tap to view answer
Which interim exchange rate system did India introduce in the March 1992 Budget following the devaluation?
The Liberalised Exchange Rate Management System (LERMS), also known as the Dual Exchange Rate System.
Tap to view answer
What conversion ratio was prescribed for foreign exchange earnings under the LERMS system?
A 40:60 ratio; meaning 40% of foreign exchange had to be surrendered at the official rate to the government for essential imports, while 60% could be converted at the market rate.
Tap to view answer
When did India adopt a Unified Market-Determined Exchange Rate System?
In the March 1993 Budget, when the dual rate under LERMS was abolished and the exchange rate was left entirely to market demand and supply.
Tap to view answer
In which year did India accept full convertibility of the rupee on the Current Account under Article VIII of the IMF?
In August 1994.

What does the 'Marshall-Lerner Condition' state in the context of foreign exchange?
Currency devaluation will improve the trade balance only if the sum of the price elasticities of demand for exports and imports is greater than 1 (|e_x + e_m| > 1).

How did the 'J-Curve Effect' manifest on India's trade balance immediately after the 1991 devaluation?
In the initial months, the trade deficit widened due to costlier import contracts, but over time, export expansion led to a durable improvement in the trade balance.

To prevent political outrage and panic in Parliament, the media, and the public, as well as unchecked speculation in financial markets.

Self-insurance (a 'War Chest') against external shocks and safeguarding the country's sovereign autonomy during global financial crises.

What is the standard technical definition of Gross Fiscal Deficit?
The excess of the government's total expenditure (revenue + capital expenditure) over its non-debt receipts (revenue receipts + recovery of loans + other capital receipts).
