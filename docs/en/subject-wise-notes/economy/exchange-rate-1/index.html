Exchange Rate Part 1

A Comprehensive Analysis of the Meaning of Exchange Rate, Nominal Exchange Rate (NER/NEER), Real Exchange Rate (RER/REER), Comparative Differences, and External Competitiveness

🔍
Quick Reference
Key Facts & Contributions
Exchange Rate Part 1
Type of Exchange RateKey FeatureEconomic Impact
Nominal Exchange Rate (NER)The direct monetary exchange ratio between two currencies (e.g., ₹83 per $1); it involves no adjustment for domestic price levels or inflation between countries.Determines daily foreign exchange market transactions, valuation of financial assets, external debt servicing costs, and immediate capital flows.
Real Exchange Rate (RER)Nominal Exchange Rate adjusted by the ratio of domestic and foreign price levels (inflation) [RER = NER * (P_domestic / P_foreign)]; a measure of the relative price of goods.The primary determinant of the true trade competitiveness of a country's products in international markets and its current account balance.
Nominal Effective Exchange Rate (NEER)A trade-weighted multilateral nominal index of the domestic currency relative to a basket of currencies of major trading partner countries (e.g., RBI's 40-currency basket).Moves beyond single-currency (dollar) dependence to provide a broad reflection of the domestic currency's overall external strength and exchange stability.
Real Effective Exchange Rate (REER)A trade-weighted multilateral index adjusted for domestic inflation differentials relative to trading partner countries (Base Year 2015-16 = 100).If REER > 100, it indicates currency overvaluation and declining export competitiveness; if REER < 100, it signals an edge in export competitiveness.
Fixed / Pegged Exchange RateOfficial determination of a currency's value by the central bank at a fixed level against a major foreign currency (like the US dollar) or gold.Provides trade stability against exchange risk, but forces the central bank to expend massive forex reserves to defend the peg and sacrifices monetary autonomy.
Flexible / Floating Exchange RateCurrency value is determined entirely and independently by market forces of demand and supply in the foreign exchange market (zero central bank intervention).Absorbs external economic shocks (such as oil shocks) automatically and preserves independent monetary policy, but carries risks of speculation and high volatility.
Managed Float / Dirty FloatExchange rate is determined by market demand and supply, but the central bank (RBI) periodically buys or sells dollars in the forex market to curb excessive volatility.Balances market-based efficiency with macroeconomic financial stability; successfully adopted by India since 1993.
Purchasing Power Parity (PPP) RateThe relative ratio of currencies required to purchase an identical basket of goods and services in two countries (based on the Law of One Price).The most accurate yardstick for international comparisons of real economic size and citizens' standard of living (India is the 3rd-largest economy by PPP GDP).

One-Liner Questions

What is the standard authoritative definition of 'Exchange Rate' in economics?
The rate at which one country's national currency is converted or exchanged for another country's currency (i.e., the price of a foreign currency expressed in terms of the domestic currency).

What type of market is the Foreign Exchange Market (Forex Market)?
A global, decentralized, 24x7 Over-the-Counter (OTC) financial market where different national currencies of the world are bought and sold.
Tap to view answer
What is meant by a 'Direct Quote' in foreign exchange quotation?
Expressing the amount of domestic currency required to purchase one fixed unit of foreign currency (e.g., 1 USD = ₹83.50).
Tap to view answer
What is an 'Indirect Quote'?
Expressing the amount of foreign currency obtained in exchange for one unit of domestic currency (e.g., ₹1 = 0.012 USD).
Tap to view answer
What is the difference between 'Base Currency' and 'Quote Currency' in the forex market?
The first currency in a pair is the Base Currency (whose value is taken as 1 unit), and the second is the Quote Currency, which states the price of the base currency (in USD/INR, USD is the base currency and INR is the quote currency).
Tap to view answer
What do 'Bid Price' and 'Ask / Offer Price' represent in the forex market?
The Bid Price is the rate at which a bank/dealer is willing to buy foreign currency, and the Ask Price is the rate at which the dealer is willing to sell foreign currency.
Tap to view answer
What is the 'Bid-Ask Spread' in the foreign exchange market?
The difference between the Ask Price and the Bid Price (Ask Price - Bid Price), which represents the forex dealer's profit margin and the market's liquidity cost.
Tap to view answer
What is meant by 'Spot Exchange Rate'?
The exchange rate at which immediate physical delivery and settlement of currencies take place, typically within two business days (T+2).
Tap to view answer
What is a 'Forward Exchange Rate'?
An exchange rate agreed upon today at which currencies will actually be exchanged on a predetermined future date (such as 1, 3, or 6 months later).
Tap to view answer
For what primary purpose are Forward Contracts used in international trade?
For 'Hedging' (risk protection) against adverse future fluctuations in the exchange rate.
Tap to view answer
What is the concept of a 'Cross Exchange Rate'?
The exchange rate between any two currencies derived indirectly through a third major common currency (usually the US dollar).
Tap to view answer
If 1 USD = ₹83 and 1 USD = 150 Japanese Yen (JPY), what will be the cross rate between the rupee and the yen?
1 INR = 1.807 JPY (150 / 83 = 1.807 yen per rupee).

What is 'Currency Depreciation'?
An automatic fall in the market value of the domestic currency relative to a foreign currency under a floating exchange rate system due to market forces of demand and supply.
Tap to view answer
How is 'Currency Devaluation' technically different from Currency Depreciation?
Devaluation is a deliberate, official reduction in a currency's value by administrative order of the government or central bank under a fixed or pegged exchange rate regime.
Tap to view answer
What does 'Currency Appreciation' mean?
An automatic strengthening of the domestic currency under a floating exchange rate system due to increased supply of foreign currency or higher demand for the domestic currency.
Tap to view answer
What is 'Currency Revaluation'?
An official upward revision in the value of the domestic currency against a foreign currency by a policy decision of the government or central bank under a fixed exchange rate system.
Tap to view answer
When the Indian rupee falls from ₹80 to ₹84 per US dollar, what is this called?
Depreciation of the rupee (a fall in the value of the rupee relative to the dollar).

When the rupee strengthens from ₹84 to ₹80 per US dollar, what is it termed in economics?
Appreciation of the rupee.

What is the immediate impact of domestic currency depreciation on a country's exports?
Exports become cheaper and more price-competitive for foreign buyers, stimulating export demand.
Tap to view answer
What is the impact of domestic currency depreciation on a country's imports?
Imports become costlier for domestic consumers and industries, discouraging import demand.

What is 'Imported Inflation', and how is it linked to the exchange rate?
When depreciation of the domestic currency raises the cost of imported raw materials (especially crude oil), increasing domestic production costs and general price levels.
Tap to view answer
What negative impact does domestic currency depreciation have on Indian companies that have raised External Commercial Borrowings (ECB)?
The rupee cost of servicing principal and interest on their foreign loans (Debt Servicing Cost) rises sharply if the loans are unhedged.

What are the positive and negative impacts of domestic currency appreciation on the domestic economy?
Positive: imports become cheaper and inflation eases; Negative: exports become costlier in foreign markets and lose competitiveness.
Tap to view answer
What is 'Speculation' in the foreign exchange market?
Attempting to earn short-term profits solely by buying and selling currencies based on anticipated future movements in exchange rates.
Tap to view answer
What is 'Arbitrage'?
Exploiting exchange rate differentials across two different forex markets by simultaneously buying a currency cheaply in one market and selling it at a higher rate in another to earn a risk-free profit.
Tap to view answer
What is a 'Currency Swap' in the foreign exchange market?
A simultaneous agreement between two parties to exchange one currency for another and reverse the exchange at a predetermined rate on a future date.
Tap to view answer
What is the strategic importance of a Bilateral Swap Arrangement between central banks?
It enables countries to obtain emergency liquidity in each other's local currency during a forex liquidity crunch without relying on the dollar market (e.g., the India–Japan swap agreement).
Tap to view answer
What is a 'Crawling Peg' exchange rate system?
A system in which the exchange rate is kept fixed but is adjusted periodically in small, pre-announced increments based on inflation differentials.
Tap to view answer
How does the exchange rate operate under a 'Currency Board System'?
Through a strict legal commitment whereby the domestic currency is 100% backed by foreign exchange reserves and is fully convertible at a fixed rate.
Tap to view answer
What is meant by the economic process of 'Dollarization'?
When a country facing severe hyperinflation or a currency crisis completely abandons its national currency and adopts the US dollar as its official legal tender (e.g., Ecuador).
Tap to view answer
What is the biggest advantage and biggest disadvantage of a Fixed Exchange Rate system?
Advantage: eliminates exchange rate risk in international trade and investment; Disadvantage: the central bank must sacrifice an independent monetary policy.
Tap to view answer
What is the greatest macroeconomic advantage of a Flexible (Floating) Exchange Rate system?
Its ability to absorb external economic shocks (such as oil price spikes or global recessions) through automatic exchange rate adjustment.
Tap to view answer
Why is Managed Float referred to as 'Dirty Float' in financial economics?
Because although the rate is formally described as market-determined, the central bank intervenes behind the scenes by buying or selling dollars to steer the rate in a desired direction.
Tap to view answer
What is 'Transaction Exposure' in the foreign exchange market?
The risk of potential financial loss arising from adverse exchange rate movements between the time a contract is signed and the actual cash settlement occurs.
Tap to view answer
How does 'Economic Exposure' arise for companies from exchange rate movements?
Through the profound impact of long-term exchange rate fluctuations on a company's future cash-flow capacity, market share, and international competitiveness.
Tap to view answer
What is the exact definition of 'Nominal Exchange Rate' (NER)?
The direct monetary exchange ratio between two countries' currencies without any adjustment for domestic price levels (price indices or inflation).
Tap to view answer
What does the Bilateral Nominal Exchange Rate measure?
The direct exchange ratio between the currencies of only two specific countries (e.g., INR vs. USD, or INR vs. Euro).
Tap to view answer
If the market rate is 1 US dollar = ₹83.00, what is the Nominal Exchange Rate in this quote?
83 rupees per dollar.
Tap to view answer
Which factors generate the Demand for Forex in determining the Nominal Exchange Rate (NER)?
Imports of foreign goods and services, foreign travel and education by Indian citizens, repayment of external debt, and investment in foreign assets.
Tap to view answer
From which major sources does the Supply of Forex originate in a country?
Exports of goods and services, Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) and Foreign Portfolio Investment (FPI), External Commercial Borrowings (ECB), and remittances from Non-Resident Indians (NRIs).
Tap to view answer
What is the slope of the Demand Curve for Foreign Exchange, and why?
Downward sloping (negative slope); because the cheaper the foreign currency becomes, the cheaper foreign goods are, leading to higher demand for forex to finance imports.
Tap to view answer
What is the slope of the Supply Curve for Foreign Exchange?
Upward sloping (positive slope); because the costlier foreign currency becomes (weaker rupee), the cheaper Indian exports become for foreigners, encouraging them to buy more and bring in more forex.
Tap to view answer
Where is the Equilibrium Exchange Rate established?
At the point where total market demand for foreign exchange equals total market supply of foreign exchange (Demand = Supply).
Tap to view answer
If Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPIs) suddenly pull billions of dollars out of the Indian stock market, how will it affect the NER?
Demand for dollars will surge while dollar supply drops, causing a sharp depreciation in the rupee's nominal exchange rate.
Tap to view answer
When the US Federal Reserve raises its policy interest rates aggressively, what happens to the NER of the Indian rupee?
Capital flows out of India toward US assets, strengthening the dollar and weakening the Indian rupee.
Tap to view answer
What is the definition of 'Nominal Effective Exchange Rate' (NEER)?
A trade-weighted geometric average index of a country's currency relative to a basket of currencies of its major trading partner countries.
Tap to view answer
Why is it necessary to calculate NEER when bilateral NER is already available?
Because a country trades with many nations; the rupee might weaken against the dollar while strengthening against the euro or yen, so NEER captures the currency's overall external strength.
Tap to view answer
How many trading partner currencies are currently included in the RBI's currency basket for calculating the NEER index?
A basket of 40 major trading partner currencies (upgraded from the earlier 36-currency basket).
Tap to view answer
On what basis are weights assigned to different foreign currencies in the RBI's 40-currency NEER basket?
Based on each partner country's proportional share in India's total external trade (imports plus exports of goods and services).
Tap to view answer
Which currencies traditionally hold the largest weights in the RBI's NEER basket?
The US Dollar (USD), Chinese Yuan (CNY), Euro (EUR), UAE Dirham (AED), and British Pound (GBP).
Tap to view answer
What is the current official Base Year for the RBI's 40-currency NEER and REER indices?
Financial Year 2015-16 = 100 (revised by the RBI in December 2020).
Tap to view answer
If India's NEER index rises from 100 to 105, what does it signify?
The Indian rupee has appreciated by 5% in nominal terms relative to the overall basket of 40 major trading partner currencies.
Tap to view answer
If India's NEER index falls from 100 to 95, what does that indicate?
A trade-weighted nominal depreciation of the Indian rupee against trading partner currencies.
Tap to view answer
Why does the Nominal Exchange Rate (NER) fail to accurately reflect a country's true international trade competitiveness?
Because it completely ignores inflation (price level) differentials between the two countries.
Tap to view answer
What does Exchange Rate Pass-Through (ERPT) measure?
The percentage change in domestic import prices and consumer inflation resulting from a 1% change in the nominal exchange rate.
Tap to view answer
Why is Exchange Rate Pass-Through incomplete in India?
Because importers and manufacturers do not immediately pass on the entire burden of currency depreciation to customers, absorbing part of it in their profit margins.
Tap to view answer
If international crude oil prices remain unchanged while the rupee falls 5% against the dollar, how will Indian refineries' import bill be affected?
The rupee-denominated crude oil import bill will immediately rise by 5%, pushing up petrol and diesel prices.
Tap to view answer
Who propounded the 'Overshooting Model' in the foreign exchange market?
Rudiger Dornbusch (1976), demonstrating that following a monetary shock, the exchange rate overshoots its long-run equilibrium in the short run.
Tap to view answer
What does Covered Interest Parity (CIP) state?
The interest rate differential between two countries must equal the forward exchange rate premium or discount, ensuring identical returns on risk-free foreign investments.
Tap to view answer
What is the principle of Uncovered Interest Parity (UIP)?
The currency of a country with a higher interest rate is expected to depreciate in the future by an amount equal to the interest rate differential.
Tap to view answer
What is the fundamental macroeconomic definition of 'Real Exchange Rate' (RER)?
The nominal exchange rate adjusted by the relative ratio of domestic and foreign price levels (inflation); it measures the relative price of domestic goods in terms of foreign goods.
Tap to view answer
What is the standard mathematical formula for calculating the Real Exchange Rate (RER)?
RER = NER * (P_domestic / P_foreign) or e * (P / P*), where e is the direct nominal exchange rate, P is the domestic price level, and P* is the foreign price level.
Tap to view answer
What does the Real Exchange Rate (RER) actually measure from an economic standpoint?
It measures the real ratio of purchasing power at which one country's goods and services can be physically traded for those of another country.
Tap to view answer
If the nominal exchange rate between India and the US is $1 = ₹80, a shirt costs ₹800 in India, and an identical shirt costs $10 in the US, what will the RER be?
RER = 1, since 80 * (10 / 800) = 1 (the real cost of the shirt is identical in both countries).
Tap to view answer
If the nominal exchange rate remains fixed at $1 = ₹80, while inflation is 10% in India and 2% in the US, how will the Real Exchange Rate (RER) be affected?
The rupee will undergo Real Appreciation; Indian goods will become costlier relative to US goods, eroding India's export competitiveness.
Tap to view answer
What adverse impact does a rise in the Real Exchange Rate (Real Appreciation) have on a country's trade balance?
Domestic products become costlier for foreign buyers while foreign products become cheaper for domestic buyers, reducing exports, increasing imports, and widening the trade deficit.
Tap to view answer
What positive impact does a fall in the Real Exchange Rate (Real Depreciation) have on a country's trade?
The international competitiveness of domestic goods improves, boosting exports and improving the current account balance.
Tap to view answer
What is the definition of 'Real Effective Exchange Rate' (REER)?
The weighted index of the Nominal Effective Exchange Rate (NEER) adjusted for domestic and foreign inflation differentials (Consumer Price Indices) relative to major trading partner countries.
Tap to view answer
What is the formal conceptual formula for calculating REER in terms of NEER?
REER = NEER * (Domestic Price Index / Weighted Foreign Price Index).
Tap to view answer
Which price index does the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) use to measure price levels in its 40-currency REER index?
The Consumer Price Index (CPI).
Tap to view answer
Prior to 2014, which index did the RBI use to measure domestic inflation in its REER index before replacing it?
The Wholesale Price Index (WPI), which was replaced with the CPI on the recommendation of the Raghuram Rajan Committee to align with international standards.
Tap to view answer
If India's REER index rises from its base year value of 100 to 110, how is this situation technically described?
Approximately 10% 'Real Overvaluation' of the Indian rupee, indicating that Indian exports have become less competitive internationally.
Tap to view answer
If India's REER index drops from 100 to 92, what does this indicate?
An 8% 'Real Undervaluation' of the rupee, giving Indian exporters a competitive price advantage globally.
Tap to view answer
Why does a country's central bank closely monitor the REER index?
To check whether the national currency has become artificially overvalued and is hurting the country's exports and manufacturing sector.
Tap to view answer
If the Nominal Exchange Rate (NER) is falling, yet domestic inflation is much higher than foreign inflation, how can the REER behave?
REER can still rise (Real Appreciation) despite a falling NER, because high domestic inflation completely offsets the benefit of nominal depreciation.

Who propounded the 'Purchasing Power Parity (PPP) Theory' in modern economics?
Swedish economist Gustav Cassel, in 1918.
Tap to view answer
What does the 'Law of One Price' (LOOP) under Absolute PPP state?
In the absence of transport costs and trade barriers, an identical good must have the same price across all world markets when expressed in a single currency.
Tap to view answer
What does the Relative Purchasing Power Parity (Relative PPP) theory predict regarding the relationship between exchange rates and inflation?
The percentage change in the nominal exchange rate between two currencies should equal the inflation rate differential between the two countries (%Δe = Inflation_domestic - Inflation_foreign).
Tap to view answer
If annual inflation is 6% in India and 2% in the US, what should happen to the rupee according to the Relative PPP theory?
The rupee should undergo a nominal depreciation of roughly 4% (6% - 2%) per year against the US dollar.
Tap to view answer
What is the 'Big Mac Index' launched by the international magazine 'The Economist' in 1986?
An informal and popular PPP indicator that compares local prices of McDonald's Big Mac burgers across countries to gauge whether a currency is overvalued or undervalued.
Tap to view answer
What are the three main practical barriers preventing the Purchasing Power Parity (PPP) theory from holding completely in real life?
1. Transport costs and customs tariffs, 2. Non-tradable goods and services (such as haircuts and house rent), and 3. Massive speculative capital flows that influence exchange rates more than trade.
Tap to view answer
What does the 'Balassa-Samuelson Effect' explain?
In rapidly growing developing countries with high productivity gains, rising wages in the tradable sector push up prices of non-tradable services, causing their Real Exchange Rate (REER) to appreciate naturally over time.
Tap to view answer
What does the concept of 'Dutch Disease' illustrate in the context of exchange rates?
A sharp real appreciation of the national currency triggered by a sudden major discovery of natural resources or huge capital inflows, which devastates non-resource manufacturing and agricultural exports.
Tap to view answer
What is the difference between Terms of Trade (ToT) and the Real Exchange Rate (RER)?
ToT is simply the ratio of export prices to import prices (Px/Pm), whereas RER compares a country's overall aggregate price level against the foreign price level.
Tap to view answer
What is the most fundamental difference between the Nominal Exchange Rate (NER) and the Real Exchange Rate (RER)?
NER is merely a monetary ratio of currencies, whereas RER is inflation-adjusted and reflects the real purchasing power and trade competitiveness of two countries' goods and services.
Tap to view answer
Does a 10% fall in the Nominal Exchange Rate automatically mean a 10% gain in a country's export competitiveness?
No; if domestic inflation simultaneously exceeds foreign inflation by 10%, the Real Exchange Rate (RER) remains unchanged, yielding zero competitiveness gain.

What is the 'J-Curve Effect', which depicts the short-run and long-run behaviour of the trade deficit after devaluation or depreciation?
Immediately after currency devaluation, the trade deficit worsens in the short run (the downward dip of the J) because existing import contracts become costlier; in the medium run, as volumes adjust, exports rise and the trade deficit improves sustainably (the upward curve of the J).

What does the 'Marshall-Lerner Condition' state?
Depreciation of the domestic currency will improve the trade balance only if the sum of the price elasticities of demand for exports and imports exceeds 1 (|e_x + e_m| > 1).

If the Marshall-Lerner Condition is not satisfied in a country (|e_x + e_m| < 1), what will be the impact of currency devaluation on the trade deficit?
Instead of narrowing, the trade deficit will widen further (Perverse Effect).

What is the 'Impossible Trinity' (Mundell-Fleming Trilemma) in international finance?
No country can simultaneously achieve all three goals: 1. A fixed exchange rate, 2. An independent monetary policy, and 3. Free capital mobility; it must compulsorily give up one.

Which two pillars of the 'Impossible Trinity' (Trilemma) has India primarily chosen since 1991?
