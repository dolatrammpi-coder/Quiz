Key Facts & Contributions
Banking System Part 2
Banking ConceptKey FeatureEconomic Role
Demand Deposits / CASAView Answer: Repayable immediately on customer demand without prior notice; withdrawable via cheque, debit card, net banking, and UPI.View Answer: Major component of narrow money (M1) in the economy; primary medium of exchange and the cheapest working capital for banks.
Time Deposits / Term DepositsView Answer: Deposits for a fixed maturity period (7 days to 10 years); interest deduction/penalty on premature withdrawal; higher interest rate.View Answer: Main pillar for mobilizing household savings; provides stable resources to banks for extending long-term loans (infrastructure and industry).
Derivative / Active DepositsView Answer: An account opened in the borrower's name when a bank sanctions a loan, into which the loan amount is credited ('loans create deposits').View Answer: Multiple credit creation by the banking system; expansion of money supply (M3) and aggregate economic demand.
Cash Credit (CC)View Answer: Working capital limit sanctioned against hypothecation of raw materials and stock; interest payable only on the daily utilized amount.View Answer: Meets the daily liquidity needs of manufacturing and trading units; ensures uninterrupted momentum in the industrial cycle.
Overdraft (OD)View Answer: Facility to withdraw beyond the actual available balance in the account up to a pre-approved limit; against financial assets (FD, shares) or personal creditworthiness.View Answer: Provides immediate protection to traders and micro-enterprises against short-term cash flow mismatches.
Term LoansView Answer: Lump-sum loans sanctioned for a fixed tenure, repaid in EMIs or predetermined installments; categorized as short-, medium-, and long-term.View Answer: Drives Gross Fixed Capital Formation (GFCF), creation of fixed assets (plant, machinery, housing), and long-term expansion of production capacity.
Bills Discounting (Hundi / Bill Discounting)View Answer: Cash payment by a bank before maturity for Bills of Exchange issued against the supply of commercial goods, after deducting a commission.View Answer: Provides immediate working capital to suppliers; accelerates the payment cycle in the supply chain and promotes trade confidence.
External Benchmark Lending Rate (EBLR)View Answer: Linking interest rates on retail and MSME loans directly to the Repo Rate or Treasury Bill yields since October 2019 under RBI directives.View Answer: Unprecedented transparency in monetary policy transmission; immediate pass-through of policy Repo Rate cuts to end customers.

One-Liner Questions

What is the legal nature of a 'Bank Deposit' under the Banking Regulation Act, 1949?
A statutory relationship of 'Creditor and Debtor' between the depositor and the bank; where the bank is the borrower (debtor) and the depositor is an unsecured/insured creditor of the bank.

When a citizen deposits cash into a bank, what is its accounting impact on the bank's balance sheet?
The bank's cash assets (Assets) increase, accompanied by an equal increase in the bank's liability (Liabilities) toward the depositor.
Tap to view answer
What is meant by 'Primary / Passive Deposits'?
Actual money deposited directly by the public into a bank out of their real savings in the form of cash, cheques, or drafts.
Tap to view answer
What are 'Derivative / Active Deposits'?
The credit amount deposited into a bank account opened in the borrower's name when banks sanction a loan, instead of handing over cash.
Tap to view answer
What famous statement did renowned British economist J. M. Keynes make regarding primary and derivative deposits?
'Loans Create Deposits,' meaning that loans sanctioned by banks themselves give birth to new deposits in the banking system.
Tap to view answer
Into which two main categories are bank deposits divided based on their withdrawal nature and maturity?
1. Demand Deposits and 2. Time Deposits.
Tap to view answer
What are Net Demand and Time Liabilities (NDTL)?
The balance obtained by subtracting inter-bank assets deposited with other banks from a bank's total demand and time deposit liabilities toward the public.
Tap to view answer
For implementing which two major regulatory ratios does the Reserve Bank of India calculate NDTL?
For determining the Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) and the Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR).
Tap to view answer
What is included in banks' 'Other Demand and Time Liabilities' (ODTL)?
Unpaid dividends, drafts payable, Demand Drafts (DD), bills deposited, and employees' provident fund balances held with the bank.
Tap to view answer
In the context of banks, what are 'Core Deposits'?
Savings and current accounts as well as small FDs opened by local retail customers, which are less sensitive to interest rate fluctuations and highly stable.
Tap to view answer
What are 'Non-Core Deposits' or 'Volatile / Wholesale Deposits'?
Short-term bulk deposits parked by corporates and large institutions lured by high interest rates, which immediately flee if slightly better returns are available elsewhere in the market.

What type of banking instrument is a Certificate of Deposit (CD)?
A negotiable and transferable money market promissory note issued by Scheduled Commercial Banks at a discount for a maturity of 7 days to 1 year.
Tap to view answer
What is the minimum amount prescribed for issuing a Certificate of Deposit (CD) in India?
Minimum ₹5 lakh and in multiples thereof.

In which currency is the 'Foreign Currency Non-Resident Account' [FCNR(B)] operated for Non-Resident Indians (NRIs)?

Tap to view answer
What is the main difference between a Non-Resident External (NRE) Account and a Non-Resident Ordinary (NRO) Account?
An NRE account is for holding foreign currency earned from overseas sources in Indian Rupees (fully tax-free and repatriable), whereas an NRO account is for holding income earned in India (rent, dividends), which is taxable.

Automatic conversion of amounts exceeding a predetermined threshold in a savings account into a Fixed Deposit (FD), and automatic transfer back to the savings account without penalty when a cheque is issued.

When no customer-induced financial transaction has been carried out in a current or savings account for 2 consecutive years (24 months).

Savings or current accounts that have not been operated for 10 years, or term deposits that have not been claimed even 10 years after their maturity date.
Tap to view answer

Into the Depositor Education and Awareness Fund (DEAF), which is used for the financial protection and awareness of customers.

The UDGAM Portal (Unclaimed Deposits-Gateway to Access inforMation).
Tap to view answer
What is included in banks' 'Cost of Funds' (CoF)?
The average interest rate paid to depositors as well as the operational expenses incurred in mobilizing and servicing deposits.

In a high interest rate environment, why do banks emphasize mobilizing CASA deposits instead of term deposits?
Because the interest cost on CASA deposits is extremely low, allowing the bank to maintain a high Net Interest Margin (NIM).

Why are bank deposits economically considered a 'safe haven for savings' in developing countries?
Because they offer high liquidity, zero default risk (due to DICGC guarantee), and assured monetary returns.

For traders, industrial companies, partnership firms, and commercial entities that need to conduct a large number of transactions daily.

Zero interest; banks are not permitted to pay interest on Current Account deposits.

There is no restriction; unlimited deposits and withdrawals can be made in a day.

The Overdraft (OD) facility, enabling payments up to a fixed limit even when the account has zero balance.

What is the primary socio-economic objective of a 'Savings Bank Account'?
To encourage the habit of short-term saving among individuals, households, and society at large.

In which year did the Reserve Bank of India completely deregulate interest rates on Savings Bank Accounts?
In October 2011, after which banks became free to set their own savings interest rates according to market competition.

On what basis do banks currently calculate interest on Savings Accounts?
On a Daily Product Basis (Daily End-of-Day Balance), which was made mandatory from April 1, 2010.

On the minimum balance available between the 10th and the last day of the month, causing heavy interest losses to account holders.

What regulatory limits can apply to withdrawals in a Savings Bank Account?
Banks can limit the number of free withdrawals per month via ATM and cheque, and prohibit commercial transactions.

What is the formula for calculating the CASA Ratio?
(Current Account Deposits + Savings Account Deposits) / Total Bank Deposits × 100.

What is the strategic importance of a high CASA Ratio (such as 40% to 50%) for a commercial bank?
It keeps the bank's cost of deposits minimal, enabling the bank to earn a high Net Interest Margin (NIM) even while lending at lower interest rates.

What is the main feature of 'Basic Savings Bank Deposit Accounts' (BSBDA / Jan Dhan accounts) opened for financial inclusion?
Zero minimum balance requirement, no mandatory minimum deposit, and a free RuPay debit card.
Tap to view answer
Which primary function of money does the facility of transferring demand deposits via cheque fulfill?
The function of 'Medium of Exchange,' eliminating the risk and cost of carrying physical currency notes.
Tap to view answer
How has the expansion of digital banking and UPI affected the velocity of circulation of demand deposits?
It has significantly increased the velocity of circulation of money, as funds are transferred from one account to another within seconds.

Why do banks not keep 100% of their demand deposits in cash with themselves?
Because according to the Law of Large Numbers, all depositors do not come to withdraw their entire deposited amount on the same day.

What is Fractional Reserve Banking?
A banking system in which banks retain only a statutory fraction (CRR and SLR) of their total demand and time deposits in liquid form and lend out the remaining amount.

What additional benefit do banks offer to senior citizens on Fixed Deposits compared to general customers?

How do bank deposits accelerate the country's 'Gross Fixed Capital Formation' (GFCF)?
By pooling scattered idle household savings of millions of small families and transforming them into long-term loans for large infrastructure, manufacturing, and power plants.

What is the 'Financial Intermediation' function of bank deposits in the economy?
Ensuring the safe and efficient flow of financial resources from surplus units (savers) to deficit units (investors and entrepreneurs).

Minimizing transaction costs and information asymmetry, which an individual lender cannot achieve on their own.

Bank deposits form the primary base for credit expansion; the larger the deposits, the greater the total credit creation in the economy by the banking system.

In banking terminology, what is the subtle technical difference between a 'Loan' and an 'Advance'?

How does a 'Cash Credit' (CC) account operate?
Businesses are granted a Drawing Power limit based on the hypothecation of their current stock and debtors, and interest is charged on a day-to-day basis only on the utilized amount.

When a bank pays cash to a seller for a goods supply bill (Usance Bill) before its maturity date after deducting its commission/interest, and recovers the full amount directly from the buyer on the due date.

What is an 'Unsecured Loan' and what are its major examples?
A loan granted without any collateral asset solely on the borrower's personal creditworthiness and income capacity; e.g., credit card dues, Personal Loans.

Why do banks charge significantly higher interest rates on unsecured loans compared to secured loans?

What does 'Pledge' mean in the context of loan security?
When physical possession of the pledged movable property is handed over to the lending bank (such as keeping gold ornaments in the bank's vault in a gold loan).

What is 'Hypothecation' in the context of loan security?
When the bank holds a legal charge over the asset, but physical possession and use of the asset remain with the borrower (such as using a car in a car loan or business stock).

The transfer of a specific interest in immovable property (such as land, building, house) made to secure the repayment of a loan.

In an Equitable Mortgage, only the original title deeds of the property are deposited with the bank, whereas in a Registered Mortgage, the deed is duly registered at the sub-registrar's office after paying stamp duty.

What is the function of the 'Loan to Value (LTV) Ratio' in bank loans?
