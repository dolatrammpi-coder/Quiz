One-Liner Questions

What is the standard definition of a 'Balance of Payments Crisis' in economics?
A situation where a country becomes unable to pay for its international imports and mandatory external debt obligations because its foreign exchange reserves are nearly exhausted and it cannot obtain fresh external loans.

By what other names is a Balance of Payments Crisis known in financial economics?
Currency Crisis or External Liquidity Crisis.

Which economist propounded the concept of 'Sudden Stop of Capital Inflows'?
Guillermo Calvo, which serves as a major driver of a BoP crisis.
Tap to view answer
What does Paul Krugman's 'First-Generation Crisis Model' identify as the root cause of a BoP crisis?
Inconsistency between expansionary fiscal/monetary policies and a fixed exchange rate regime, where the government finances deficits by printing money and exhausts foreign exchange reserves.
Tap to view answer
What is Maurice Obstfeld's 'Second-Generation Crisis Model' centered on?
Speculative attacks and self-fulfilling expectations, where market fears themselves trigger a currency collapse.
Tap to view answer
What does the 'Third-Generation Crisis Model', developed after the Asian Financial Crisis (1997), link a BoP crisis to?
Banking sector fragility, unhedged foreign currency corporate debt, and balance-sheet mismatches.
Tap to view answer
What is the fundamental difference between a 'Liquidity Crisis' and a 'Solvency Crisis'?
A liquidity crisis is an immediate short-term unavailability of cash/foreign exchange, whereas a solvency crisis is a state where a country's total external liabilities permanently exceed its total external assets.
Tap to view answer
When does a 'Speculative Attack' occur on a country's currency?
When speculators become convinced that the central bank cannot sustain a fixed exchange rate, prompting them to sell domestic currency and generate massive demand for foreign currency.
Tap to view answer
What is the conventional monetary step taken by a central bank to defend its currency during a BoP crisis?
Sharply raising interest rates to stem capital flight and make domestic assets more attractive.
Tap to view answer
When foreign exchange reserves are completely depleted, what compulsion must a central bank ultimately accept?
A sharp devaluation of the domestic currency or abandoning a fixed exchange rate in favor of a floating rate.
Tap to view answer
Which international institution acts as the 'Lender of Last Resort' to avert a BoP crisis?
The International Monetary Fund (IMF).
Tap to view answer
What is meant by 'IMF Conditionality'?
Imposing conditions on the recipient country—such as reducing fiscal deficits, currency devaluation, trade liberalization, and subsidy cuts—in exchange for emergency financial assistance.
Tap to view answer
What is the immediate objective of 'Macroeconomic Stabilization'?
Controlling inflation in the short run, curbing the budget deficit, and safeguarding foreign exchange reserves.
Tap to view answer
How does 'Structural Adjustment' differ from stabilization?
It is a medium-to-long-term economic reform aimed at transforming the economy's supply-side capacity, productivity, trade policies, and institutional framework.
Tap to view answer
What is the relationship between a 'Debt Crisis' and a BoP crisis?
A BoP crisis often morphs into an external debt crisis when a country commits sovereign default on principal and interest installments of its external loans.
Tap to view answer
What is the long-term impact of a 'Sovereign Default' on a country's economy?
The country is locked out of global capital markets for years, trade credit dries up, and future borrowing costs become prohibitively expensive.
Tap to view answer
What caused the Latin American Debt Crisis of the 1980s (e.g., Mexico, Brazil)?
Abrupt steep interest rate hikes by the U.S. Federal Reserve (Volcker Shock) and falling global prices of primary commodities.
Tap to view answer
Which major BoP vulnerability was exposed by the 1997 East Asian Financial Crisis?
Over-reliance on short-term external commercial borrowings and the fragility of pegged exchange rate regimes.

What does the 'Greenspan-Guidotti Rule' recommend regarding foreign exchange reserves?
A country's foreign exchange reserves should at least equal 100% of its total short-term external debt falling due over the next 12 months.
Tap to view answer
What situation arises if a country's short-term external debt exceeds its total foreign exchange reserves?
The country falls into severe liquidity and rollover risk, which can trigger a sudden BoP crisis.

What is 'Capital Flight'?
The rapid shifting of liquid capital by domestic and foreign investors into safe foreign assets out of panic over an economic or political crisis in the country.
Tap to view answer
What impact does capital flight have on the domestic banking system?
Bank deposits shrink rapidly (Bank Run), liquidity evaporates, and credit disbursement grinds to a complete halt.

What is known as the 'Contagion Effect' in international financial crises?
The spread of a currency or financial crisis from one country across borders to neighboring or emerging market economies with similar economic characteristics.

Why is an excessive overvaluation of the Real Effective Exchange Rate (REER) considered a leading indicator of a BoP crisis?
Because an overvalued REER destroys the competitiveness of domestic exports and makes imports artificially cheap, widening the trade deficit.

How does Trade Credit (Supplier's Credit) behave during a BoP crisis?
Foreign banks and suppliers refuse to extend short-term credit to the crisis-hit country, forcing upfront cash payments and pushing the liquidity crisis to an extreme.
Tap to view answer
What roles do the 'Paris Club' and the 'London Club' play in an international debt crisis?
The Paris Club is a forum for restructuring bilateral official (government) debt, whereas the London Club handles the restructuring of private debt owed to commercial banks.

What was the fundamental and deep structural cause of the 1991 Indian economic crisis?
Uncontrolled and unsustainable fiscal deficits throughout the 1980s, which boosted domestic consumption and pushed the Current Account Deficit (CAD) to perilous levels.

Through which harmful instrument did the Government of India primarily finance its massive budget deficits in the 1980s?
Automatic monetization of the deficit (money printing) through 91-day Ad-hoc Treasury Bills.

What devastating impact did excessive deficit monetization have on India's price stability in 1990-91?
Money supply (M3) surged, and Wholesale Price Index (WPI)-based inflation reached 13.8% to 14% (double digits).

What percentage of GDP was India's Gross Fiscal Deficit during 1990-91?
The Central Government's fiscal deficit was 7.8% of GDP (and the combined deficit including states was around 9.4% to 10%).
Tap to view answer
To what level of GDP had India's Revenue Deficit reached in 1990-91?
About 3.3% to 3.5% of GDP, indicating that the government was borrowing heavily even to meet routine expenses like salaries, pensions, and subsidies.
Tap to view answer
Why do economists call India's average GDP growth rate in the 1980s (about 5.6%) 'Borrowed Growth'?
Because this growth was achieved not through productivity improvements, but on the strength of heavy internal and external commercial borrowings and fiscal profligacy.

When concessional foreign aid declined in the late 1980s, which risky alternative did the Government of India choose?
Heavy reliance on short-term External Commercial Borrowings (ECBs) at hard market terms and high-interest Non-Resident Indian (NRI) deposits.

How did Iraq's invasion of Kuwait in August 1990 (the Gulf War) deal the first immediate shock to India's BoP?
International crude oil prices suddenly jumped from $15-18 per barrel to over $35-40 per barrel, doubling India's oil import bill overnight.

How did the Gulf Crisis cripple India's invisibles account?
Private remittances from Indian expatriates in Kuwait and Iraq stopped completely, and airlifting over 1.5 lakh stranded Indians incurred massive foreign exchange expenditure.

Why did the political and economic disintegration of the Soviet Union (USSR) in 1990-91 prove to be an external trade shock for India?
The decades-old 'Rupee-Rouble bilateral trade arrangement' collapsed, abruptly depriving Indian exporters of a vast, protected, non-dollar export market.

How did political instability in India between 1989 and 1991 deepen the crisis?
The rapid succession of three Prime Ministers (Rajiv Gandhi, V. P. Singh, Chandra Shekhar) and the collapse of coalition governments prevented timely presentation of a tough budget and corrective decisions.

What was the immediate impact of former Prime Minister Rajiv Gandhi's assassination at an election rally in May 1991 on international financial markets?
Panic peaked among foreign investors and NRIs, accelerating capital outflows at an unprecedented pace.

What action did international credit rating agencies (Moody's and S&P) take regarding India's sovereign creditworthiness in 1990-91?
They downgraded India's sovereign rating from Investment Grade to Speculative / Sub-investment Grade (Junk Grade).

What was the direct impact of the downgrade to junk status on Indian commercial banks and enterprises?
Short-term credit from international money markets dried up, and global banks refused to confirm Letters of Credit (LCs) issued by Indian banks.

How much capital flowed out of Foreign Currency Non-Resident (FCNR) accounts in 1990-91?

To what alarming level had India's External Debt Service Ratio climbed in 1991?
About 35.3%; meaning more than one-third of the country's total current foreign exchange earnings was consumed solely by paying interest and principal on past external loans.

What was India's total External Debt by the end of March 1991?
About $83.8 billion (more than 30% of GDP), making India the third-largest debtor among developing countries.

What was the state of short-term debt in India's total external debt in 1990-91?
Short-term debt accounted for 10.2% of total debt, yet it exceeded 140% of India's total foreign exchange reserves at that time.

What was 'Import Compression' enforced by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) to conserve foreign exchange during the 1991 crisis?
Mandating a cash margin requirement of up to 200% on non-essential imports and imposing strict administrative curbs on imports of capital goods.

What negative impact did these harsh emergency import compression measures have on the domestic economy?
Severe shortages of raw materials and spare parts pushed the growth rate of the Index of Industrial Production (IIP) down to -0.6% (negative) in 1991-92.

Which three items accounted for the largest share of India's non-plan revenue expenditure in the 1980s?

What was the actual level of India's foreign exchange reserves in June 1991 during the BoP crisis?
Foreign exchange reserves fell to just $1.1 billion (~₹2,500 crore), placing the country on the verge of sovereign default.

When and by how much was the Indian rupee devalued in two stages in July 1991?

Why was the rupee devalued in two stages (1 and 3 July) instead of a single abrupt stroke?
To gauge the immediate reaction of the foreign exchange market, foil speculation, and prevent market panic.

What was the primary objective of the rupee devaluation in July 1991?

What is the standard macroeconomic definition of 'Current Account Deficit' (CAD)?
When a country's total foreign exchange expenditure on imports of goods, services, primary factor income, and unilateral transfers exceeds its total exports and foreign receipts.
Tap to view answer
What was India's average annual Current Account Deficit during the Sixth Five-Year Plan (1980-85)?
Only about 1.3% of GDP, which rose to an average of 2.2% in the Seventh Plan (1985-90) and 3.1% in 1990-91.

How does Sidney Alexander's 'Absorption Approach' define the Current Account Deficit?
CAD = Total National Output (Y) - Aggregate Domestic Absorption (A = C + I + G); that is, when a country consumes and invests more than its total output.
Tap to view answer
What is the relationship of CAD in the Saving-Investment Gap equation of national income accounting?
CAD = (I - S) + (G - T); where (I - S) is the private sector saving-investment gap and (G - T) is the government's fiscal deficit.
Tap to view answer
What does the 'Twin Deficit Hypothesis' demonstrate in the context of the 1991 crisis?
The government's unbridled fiscal deficit (G - T) boosted aggregate domestic demand, directly making the Current Account Deficit (CAD) explosive.
Tap to view answer
How much did the merchandise trade deficit contribute to widening India's Current Account Deficit in 1990-91?
About ₹9,449 crore (a trade deficit of roughly $5.9 billion), accounting for over 60% of the total CAD.
Tap to view answer
What are the two main methods of financing the Current Account Deficit (CAD)?
1. Through net foreign capital inflows in the capital account (FDI, FPI, external borrowings), or 2. By drawing down the central bank's foreign exchange reserves.
Tap to view answer
What is the difference between Debt-Creating Flows and Non-Debt Flows?
Debt flows (ECBs, external loans) carry a legal obligation to repay principal and interest, whereas non-debt flows (FDI, FPI) carry no fixed debt-service obligation.
Tap to view answer
On which type of capital flows did India rely heavily in the 1980s to finance its CAD?
Debt-creating capital flows (short-term commercial bank loans and high-interest NRI deposits), which were the first to flee during the crisis.
Tap to view answer
What sustainable safe limit did the Dr. C. Rangarajan Committee (1993) set for India's Current Account Deficit (CAD)?
2.0% to 2.5% of Gross Domestic Product (GDP) (maximum 3%).

What crisis unfolds in financial markets if a country's Current Account Deficit (CAD) rises above 3% of GDP?
Heavy depreciation pressure builds on the currency, foreign portfolio investors begin pulling out capital, and foreign exchange reserves come under strain.

To what historic peak did India's Current Account Deficit (CAD) climb in 2012-13?

According to the 'Marshall-Lerner Condition', when can a depreciation in the exchange rate improve the CAD?
