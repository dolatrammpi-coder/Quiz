Taxation Part 1

Concept of Taxation, Direct & Indirect Taxes, and Income Tax

🔍

One-Liner Questions

1. What is the mandatory legal definition of a Tax?
A tax is a compulsory legal payment imposed by the government on citizens and business entities without any guarantee of a direct return benefit (Quid Pro Quo).
Tap to view answer
2. What is the fundamental difference between a Tax and a Fee?
A tax offers no direct benefit in return, whereas a fee is a service-specific payment (Quid Pro Quo) paid in exchange for a particular government service.
Tap to view answer
3. What is the basic distinction between a Tax and a Penalty?
A tax is an instrument for raising public revenue, whereas a penalty is imposed as a punishment for violating laws or regulations.
Tap to view answer
4. Which are the four main Canons of Taxation propounded by Adam Smith?
According to Adam Smith, the four canons are: Canon of Equity, Canon of Certainty, Canon of Convenience, and Canon of Economy.
Tap to view answer
5. What does the 'Canon of Equity' in taxation explain?
It states that taxpayers should contribute in proportion to their ability to pay, thereby ensuring socio-economic justice.
Tap to view answer
6. What does the 'Canon of Certainty' in taxation ensure?
The taxpayer should have clear knowledge of the time, amount, manner, and place of tax payment so that there is no administrative arbitrariness.
Tap to view answer
7. What does the 'Canon of Convenience' in taxation indicate?
Taxes should be collected at a time and in a manner most convenient for the taxpayer (such as at the time income is received).
Tap to view answer
8. What does the 'Canon of Economy' in taxation demand?
The administrative cost of tax collection should be minimal so that the maximum share of collected revenue can be utilized for public welfare.
Tap to view answer
9. What does the 'Canon of Productivity' in taxation propose?
The tax system should generate adequate revenue for the government without harming production and investment in the economy.
Tap to view answer
10. What is the 'Canon of Elasticity' in taxation?
The tax system should be flexible enough to allow adjusting tax rates to increase or decrease revenue when needed (such as during emergencies or recessions).
Tap to view answer
11. What does the 'Canon of Diversity' in taxation recommend?
The tax system should not rely on just one or two taxes, but should comprise a balanced mix of different types of taxes.
Tap to view answer
12. Why is the 'Canon of Simplicity' necessary in taxation?
Tax laws should be simple and clear enough for ordinary taxpayers to understand without requiring complex legal assistance.
Tap to view answer
13. What is meant by the 'Impact of Tax'?
The initial physical impact of a tax falls on the person or entity upon whom the tax is legally imposed and who deposits it with the government first.
Tap to view answer
14. What does the 'Incidence of Tax' mean?
The incidence of tax rests on the final person who bears the ultimate economic burden of the tax and cannot shift it onto anyone else.
Tap to view answer
15. What is 'Shifting of Tax'?
When the person bearing the primary impact of a tax passes its financial burden onto another person by adding it to the price of a good or service.
Tap to view answer
16. What is the difference between Forward Shifting and Backward Shifting?
In forward shifting, the tax burden is passed on to the buyer/consumer, whereas in backward shifting, the burden is pushed back onto raw material suppliers or workers by reducing prices or wages.
Tap to view answer
17. What is meant by 'Tax Base'?
The total legal or economic scope of assets, income, or transactions on which tax rates are applied.
Tap to view answer
18. What does 'Tax Rate' mean?
The statutory percentage or monetary ratio levied per unit value of the tax base.
Tap to view answer
19. What is the key feature of a Progressive Tax?
As the taxpayer's income level rises, the tax rate also increases, placing a higher tax burden on higher-income groups.
Tap to view answer
20. What is the economic impact of a Regressive Tax system?
The effective tax rate decreases as income rises, placing a disproportionately heavier economic burden on lower-income groups (the poor).
Tap to view answer
21. What is a Proportional Tax system?
A system in which a uniform flat tax rate is applied across all income levels.
Tap to view answer
22. What does a Degressive Tax system mean?
In this system, the tax rate rises with income up to a certain threshold, after which it becomes flat (proportional).
Tap to view answer
23. Which two variables does the Laffer Curve represent?
The Laffer Curve illustrates the relationship between the tax rate and the total tax revenue collected by the government.
Tap to view answer
24. According to the Laffer Curve, what is the impact of excessively high tax rates on revenue?
Beyond an optimal rate, raising tax rates further discourages economic activity and increases tax evasion, causing total tax revenue to decline.
Tap to view answer
25. What is the definition of Tax Buoyancy?
Tax buoyancy is the ratio of the automatic and policy-driven (including rate/base changes) growth in total tax revenue to the growth in Gross Domestic Product (GDP).
Tap to view answer
26. What is the main difference between Tax Elasticity and Tax Buoyancy?
Tax elasticity measures only the automatic growth in tax revenue resulting from GDP growth without any changes in tax policy or tax rates.
Tap to view answer
27. What does the Tax-to-GDP Ratio indicate?
It represents government tax revenue as a percentage of the country's total GDP, serving as an indicator of fiscal capacity and formalization of the economy.
Tap to view answer
28. What is a Pigouvian Tax?
It is a corrective tax levied on harmful activities to discourage negative externalities such as pollution or public health damage.
Tap to view answer

One-Liner Questions

29. On what is the Tobin Tax levied?
A tax levied on short-term, speculative international foreign exchange transactions to curb currency market volatility.
Tap to view answer
30. What is the fundamental basis of a Carbon Tax?
A tax levied on the volume of greenhouse gases (especially CO2) emitted from the consumption of fossil fuels.
Tap to view answer
31. When is a Windfall Tax imposed?
When an industry or company earns sudden, unusually high profits (windfall profits) due to unforeseen external market conditions.
Tap to view answer
32. What is Tax Evasion?
Non-payment or underpayment of taxes through illegal means such as concealing income or falsifying records; it is a punishable offence.
Tap to view answer
33. What is Tax Avoidance?
Reducing one's tax liability by exploiting loopholes in tax laws while remaining within legal boundaries.
Tap to view answer
34. What is the objective of Tax Planning?
Minimizing tax liability through legitimate means by availing statutory exemptions, deductions, and incentives provided by the government.
Tap to view answer
35. What is the concept of Tax Expenditure?
Tax expenditure refers to the revenue forgone by the government on account of tax exemptions, deductions, and reliefs granted to taxpayers.
Tap to view answer
36. What is a Surcharge?
A surcharge is an additional 'tax on tax' levied on the basic tax payable when income exceeds a specified threshold.
Tap to view answer
37. What is the distinguishing feature of a Cess?
A cess is levied for a specific public purpose (such as primary education, health, or clean environment) and its proceeds can only be spent on that designated purpose.
Tap to view answer
38. What does Article 265 of the Indian Constitution mandate for taxation?
According to Article 265, no tax shall be levied or collected except by authority of law.
Tap to view answer
39. What is the difference in tax revenue distribution between Article 270 and Article 271?
Taxes collected under Article 270 are shared between the Union and the States, whereas surcharges and cesses levied under Article 271 remain exclusively with the Central Government.
Tap to view answer
40. What is meant by the 'Divisible Pool of Taxes'?
The portion of gross central tax revenue that is shared between the Centre and the States based on the recommendations of the Finance Commission.
Tap to view answer
41. What is the basic definition of a Direct Tax?
A tax in which both the initial impact and the final incidence fall on the same individual or entity, and the burden cannot be shifted to others.
Tap to view answer
42. What is the major socio-economic characteristic of Direct Taxes?
They are primarily progressive in nature and help reduce income and wealth inequality in society.
Tap to view answer
43. What is the role of the Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT) in India?
Working under the Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance, CBDT oversees the administration, policy formulation, and collection of all direct taxes.
Tap to view answer
44. Under which Act was the CBDT established?
It was established under the Central Boards of Revenue Act, 1963.
Tap to view answer
45. On what is Corporate Tax levied?
Corporate tax is levied on the net profit or income earned by companies (both domestic and foreign) during a financial year.
Tap to view answer
46. What is the corporate tax rate for domestic companies under Section 115BAA?
Under the new concessional regime without exemptions, the rate is 22% (25.17% inclusive of surcharge and cess).

47. What is the corporate tax rate for new manufacturing companies under Section 115BAB?
Eligible new domestic manufacturing companies are provided a concessional base tax rate of 15%.

48. What was the primary reason for introducing Minimum Alternate Tax (MAT)?
To bring 'Zero-Tax Companies'—which earned huge profits yet paid no tax by claiming various statutory exemptions—under the tax net.
Tap to view answer
49. Under which section of the Income Tax Act is MAT levied?
MAT is levied on the 'Book Profit' of companies under Section 115JB of the Income Tax Act, 1961.
Tap to view answer
50. When does Capital Gains Tax become payable?
When a capital gain arises from the sale or transfer of a capital asset (such as land, building, shares, or bonds).
Tap to view answer
51. What is Short-Term Capital Gains Tax (STCG)?
Tax levied on profits earned from selling capital assets held for less than a specified period (e.g., 12 months for listed shares or 24 months for immovable property).
Tap to view answer
52. What is Long-Term Capital Gains Tax (LTCG)?
Tax applicable on gains earned from the sale of capital assets held for longer than the prescribed holding period.
Tap to view answer
53. When and on what was Securities Transaction Tax (STT) introduced?
STT was introduced in 2004 on transactions in equities and derivatives conducted on recognized stock exchanges.
Tap to view answer
54. On what is Commodities Transaction Tax (CTT) levied?
A tax levied on non-agricultural futures and options transactions traded on recognized commodity exchanges.
Tap to view answer
55. What is Equalisation Levy or 'Google Tax'?
It is a direct tax levied on online advertising and B2B digital services provided in India by non-resident digital companies.
Tap to view answer
56. When was Dividend Distribution Tax (DDT) abolished?
DDT on companies was abolished through the Union Budget 2020-21, making dividends taxable directly in the hands of recipient investors.

It was a tax levied on collective additional perks (such as club memberships, travel, and entertainment) provided by employers to employees; it was abolished in 2009.

61. Why was Banking Cash Transaction Tax (BCTT) introduced?
It was introduced in 2005 to track large cash withdrawals and curb the flow of black money (abolished in 2009).

62. What was the core idea behind the Direct Taxes Code (DTC)?
A proposed legislative draft aimed at simplifying, consolidating, and modernizing India's direct tax laws.

63. What is the primary goal of the Faceless Assessment Scheme?
To eliminate direct human interface between taxpayers and income tax officials, thereby enhancing transparency, preventing harassment, and improving efficiency.

64. What was the main objective of the Direct Tax 'Vivad se Vishwas' Scheme?
To collect locked-up tax revenue by enabling speedy settlement of pending direct tax disputes and court litigations.

To confiscate undisclosed properties purchased in the names of others and effectively curb the parking of black money.
Tap to view answer
66. What is the primary definition of an Indirect Tax?
A tax whose initial impact falls on the producer or seller, while its final incidence is shifted onto the ultimate consumer.
Tap to view answer
67. What is the general nature of Indirect Taxes?
Indirect taxes are generally regressive in nature because both rich and poor consumers pay the same tax amount on a given commodity.
Tap to view answer
68. What is the biggest revenue advantage of Indirect Taxes?
They have an extremely broad tax base, as every member of society contributes to them through consumption.
Tap to view answer
69. Which central body is responsible for administering Indirect Taxes in India?
The Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBIC), which functions under the Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance.
Tap to view answer
70. What is meant by the 'Cascading Effect of Tax'?
When a tax is levied on top of an already paid tax (tax on tax) at successive stages of production and distribution.
Tap to view answer
71. What is the benefit of the Input Tax Credit (ITC) system?
Eliminating the cascading effect at every stage of production and sale by allowing credit for taxes paid on inputs.
Tap to view answer
72. What type of indirect tax is Customs Duty?
It is a tax levied on the import and export of goods across the territorial borders of India.
Tap to view answer
73. On what value is Basic Customs Duty (BCD) levied?
Under the Customs Act, 1962, it is levied on the Assessable Value (CIF Value) of imported goods.
Tap to view answer
74. What is the primary objective of imposing Countervailing Duty (CVD)?
To neutralize the effect of subsidies granted by foreign governments to their exporters and provide a level playing field to domestic manufacturers.
Tap to view answer
75. When is Anti-Dumping Duty imposed?
When a foreign country exports goods to India at a price lower than their normal domestic market value (dumping), this duty is levied to prevent unfair trade practices.
Tap to view answer
76. What is the function of Safeguard Duty?
To protect domestic industries from serious injury caused by a sudden and sharp surge in imports of a particular commodity.
Tap to view answer
77. On which goods does Central Excise Duty currently apply?
Following the rollout of GST, it is largely confined to petroleum crude, petrol, diesel, ATF, natural gas, and tobacco products.
Tap to view answer
78. What provision does Article 268 of the Constitution make regarding Stamp Duties?
Under Article 268, Union List stamp duties are levied by the Centre, but collected and appropriated by the respective States.
Tap to view answer
79. What is the characteristic feature of an Ad Valorem Duty?
This tax is levied as a fixed percentage of the monetary value of the good rather than its physical quantity.
Tap to view answer
80. On what basis is a Specific Duty collected?
It is levied based on the physical unit, weight, size, or count of a commodity regardless of its monetary value.

They were early reforms in central excise duty that introduced Input Tax Credit (ITC) mechanisms to mitigate the cascading effect.

Indirect TaxIncidence of tax is shifted onto the final consumer; included in the price of goods or services (e.g., GST, Customs Duty).Broad tax base and high revenue collection; regressive in nature with inflationary potential.
Progressive TaxTax rate increases more than proportionately with an increase in the taxpayer's income level.Ensures equitable redistribution of wealth and income; excessively high rates may disincentivize work and investment.
Regressive TaxEffective tax rate steadily decreases as the taxpayer's income increases.Places a relatively heavier economic burden on lower-income groups; risks widening income inequality.

Pigouvian TaxLevied to deter environmental damage or negative externalities.Reduces pollution and social harm; generates targeted corrective revenue.
