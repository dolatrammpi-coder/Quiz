Economic Growth and Development Part 2

Comprehensive analysis of dimensions of human development (HDI, IHDI, GII), social indicators (health, education, nutrition), Multidimensional Poverty (MPI), and the policy utility of social indicators

Quick Reference
Key Facts & Contributions
Economic Growth and Development Part 2
Development IndicatorKey FeatureImpact on Human Development
Human Development Index (HDI)View Answer: Geometric mean of health (life expectancy), education (mean and expected years of schooling), and standard of living (GNI per capita in PPP $).View Answer: The most authoritative benchmark beyond economic growth for measuring actual quality of life, expansion of capabilities, and international comparisons.
Inequality-adjusted Human Development Index (IHDI)View Answer: A proportional deduction from the HDI value based on prevailing inequality within a society across all three dimensions of health, education, and income.View Answer: Reveals the net percentage loss in a country's actual human development achievements caused by internal inequality.
Gender Inequality Index (GII)View Answer: Composite measurement of disparities between women and men across three dimensions: reproductive health, empowerment, and labor market participation.View Answer: Identifying human development losses resulting from gender discrimination and guiding women's empowerment policies.
Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI)View Answer: Based on the Alkire-Foster methodology; evaluates overlapping deprivations across 10 indicators (12 in National MPI) spanning health, education, and standard of living.View Answer: Moving beyond income-based poverty lines to expose the real physical deprivations that households face simultaneously in daily life.
Infant & Under-5 Mortality Rates (IMR & U5MR)View Answer: Annual number of deaths per 1,000 live births occurring before age 1 (IMR) and before age 5 (U5MR), respectively.View Answer: A primary mirror of the effectiveness of public health systems, access to primary healthcare, safe motherhood, clean drinking water, and sanitation.
Maternal Mortality Ratio (MMR)View Answer: Total number of maternal deaths due to pregnancy- or childbirth-related complications per 100,000 live births.View Answer: A direct indicator of reproductive health infrastructure, institutional deliveries, availability of skilled birth attendants, and quality of emergency obstetric care.
Nutrition Indicators (Stunting, Wasting, Anaemia)View Answer: Low height-for-age (Stunting - chronic malnutrition), low weight-for-height (Wasting - acute malnutrition), and hemoglobin deficiency (Anaemia).View Answer: Determining children's cognitive and physical development, future learning capacity, and the foundation of a country's future labor productivity.
Gross Enrolment Ratio & Years of Schooling (GER, MYS, EYS)View Answer: Total enrolment across primary, secondary, and higher education, along with mean years of schooling for adults aged 25+ and expected years of schooling for school-entry-age children.View Answer: The primary driver of human capital formation, technological adaptability, social mobility, and income-earning capacity for a knowledge-based economy.

One-Liner Questions

What is the standard authoritative definition of 'Human Development' in economics?
A holistic process of expanding people's choices and freedoms, and elevating their levels of well-being, capabilities, and opportunities.

Who first pioneered the concept of Human Development, and in which year?
Pakistani economist Dr. Mahbub ul Haq in 1990 (in collaboration with Nobel laureate Prof. Amartya Sen).

In which year was the first 'Human Development Report' (HDR) released by the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP)?
In the year 1990.
Tap to view answer
How does the Human Development approach fundamentally differ from the traditional income-based development approach?
The traditional approach treated GDP and income as the ultimate end, whereas the Human Development approach views income merely as a means and the expansion of human welfare and capabilities as the true end.
Tap to view answer
On which three major dimensions is the Human Development Index (HDI) based?
1. A long and healthy life (Health), 2. Access to knowledge (Education), and 3. A decent standard of living (Living Standard / Income).
Tap to view answer
Which specific indicator is used in the HDI to measure 'A long and healthy life' (Health dimension)?
Life Expectancy at Birth.
Tap to view answer
Which two indicators are used together in the HDI to measure 'Knowledge' (Education dimension)?
1. Mean Years of Schooling for adults aged 25 years and older, and 2. Expected Years of Schooling for children of school-entering age.
Tap to view answer
Which monetary indicator is used in the HDI to measure 'A decent standard of living' (Standard of Living dimension)?
Gross National Income (GNI) per capita at Purchasing Power Parity (PPP $).
Tap to view answer
Which major mathematical revision was introduced in the HDI calculation methodology in the 2010 Human Development Report?
Adopting the Geometric Mean of the three dimension indices in place of the Arithmetic Mean.
Tap to view answer
What was the main statistical and policy rationale for adopting the Geometric Mean instead of the Arithmetic Mean in HDI calculation?
To prevent perfect substitutability across dimensions; if a country lags severely in health or education, it cannot achieve a high HDI solely by showing high income.
Tap to view answer
Between what numerical bounds does the HDI score lie?
Between 0 and 1 (0 indicates the lowest human development and 1 indicates the highest human development).
Tap to view answer
Into which four official categories does the UNDP classify countries based on their HDI score?
1. Very High Human Development (0.800 or above), 2. High Human Development (0.700 to 0.799), 3. Medium Human Development (0.550 to 0.699), and 4. Low Human Development (below 0.550).
Tap to view answer
Under which official human development category does India currently fall?
Medium Human Development category (with an HDI value around 0.644).
Tap to view answer
What does the 'Inequality-adjusted Human Development Index' (IHDI), introduced by the UNDP in 2010, represent?
The actual level of human development in a society after accounting for inequality across all three dimensions of health, education, and income.
Tap to view answer
If a country has perfect equality in the distribution of health, education, and income, what will be the relationship between its HDI and IHDI?
Both HDI and IHDI values will be exactly equal.
Tap to view answer
What does the percentage difference between HDI and IHDI reveal?
The net percentage loss in a country's potential human development due to inequality (Loss due to Inequality).
Tap to view answer
Across which three dimensions does the 'Gender Inequality Index' (GII) measure gender gaps?
1. Reproductive Health (MMR and adolescent birth rate), 2. Empowerment (seats in parliament and secondary education), and 3. Labor Market (labor force participation rate).
Tap to view answer
What does the numerical value of GII indicate, and what should its ideal value be?
It ranges from 0 to 1, where 0 indicates complete gender equality and 1 indicates complete inequality against women (a lower value is better).
Tap to view answer
What does the 'Gender Development Index' (GDI) measure?
The ratio of separately calculated HDI values for females and males (Female HDI / Male HDI).
Tap to view answer
What is the 'Planetary Pressures-adjusted Human Development Index' (PHDI), introduced by the UNDP in 2020?
An index derived by adjusting the traditional HDI for a country's per capita carbon dioxide emissions and per capita material footprint.
Tap to view answer
What is the primary policy objective of the PHDI?
To highlight how a country ensures human development for its citizens without destroying the environment and Earth's ecosystems.
Tap to view answer
What is the difference between 'Human Capital' and 'Human Development' in the human development framework?
Human capital views humans as a means/input to raise production and GDP, whereas human development views human well-being, dignity, and self-realization as the ultimate end in themselves.
Tap to view answer
How does human development enhance the pace and sustainability of a country's economic growth?
Healthy, educated, and skilled citizens multiply labor productivity, accelerating technological innovation and making growth more durable.
Tap to view answer
How does human development convert the 'Demographic Dividend' into real economic gains?
By providing the youth population with high-quality education, modern skills, and healthcare; otherwise, an unskilled youth bulge can turn into a 'demographic disaster'.
Tap to view answer
How does human development strengthen social and political stability?
By providing equal opportunities, reducing poverty, and increasing social mobility, thereby minimizing the likelihood of crime, civil strife, and extremism.
Tap to view answer
What role does human development play in breaking the 'Inter-Generational Poverty Cycle'?
By providing quality education and nutrition to children of poor parents, enabling them to escape their parents' poverty trap and enter higher income brackets.
Tap to view answer
How does human development contribute to women's empowerment and the stabilization of the Total Fertility Rate (TFR)?
Improvements in girls' education and women's health raise the age at marriage, reduce child mortality, and increase awareness of family planning.
Tap to view answer
How is Amartya Sen's 'Entitlement Approach' to famines linked to human development?
Famines occur not from an absolute physical shortage of food, but from the collapse of people's economic and social entitlements to purchase or acquire food.
Tap to view answer
What is meant by 'Social Infrastructure' in holistic social development?
Schools, colleges, universities, primary health centers, hospitals, drinking water supply, sanitation facilities, and housing infrastructure.
Tap to view answer
What does the World Bank's 'Human Capital Index' (HCI) measure?
The percentage of maximum potential productivity that a child born today can expect to attain by age 18, given survival, full schooling, and health risks.
Tap to view answer
What does 'Human Capability' mean in the context of economic development?
A person's real freedom to achieve the various functionings and states of being that they have reason to value.
Tap to view answer
According to Amartya Sen, what is the difference between 'Functionings' and 'Capability'?
Functionings are the actual states a person achieves (such as being well-nourished or educated), whereas capability is the set of all feasible functionings from which a person has the freedom to choose.

Why is 'Life Expectancy at Birth' a central indicator of social health?
Because it reflects the overall quality of healthcare services, nutritional standards, sanitation, safe drinking water, and general living conditions in a society.

What is the standard definition of 'Infant Mortality Rate' (IMR)?
The total number of infants dying before completing 1 year of age per 1,000 live births in a given year.

Tap to view answer
What is 'Neonatal Mortality Rate' (NMR)?
The number of deaths occurring within the first 28 days (4 weeks) of life per 1,000 live births.
Tap to view answer
What does the 'Under-5 Mortality Rate' (U5MR) measure?
The probability of a child dying between birth and exactly 5 years of age, expressed per 1,000 live births.
Tap to view answer
On what exact basis is the 'Maternal Mortality Ratio' (MMR) calculated?
The number of maternal deaths due to pregnancy, childbirth, or within 42 days of termination of pregnancy per 100,000 live births.

Reducing MMR to less than 70 per 100,000 live births (India has made notable progress in this direction by reaching 97 at the national level).

How does 'Institutional Delivery' help reduce maternal and infant mortality rates?
By ensuring childbirth in hospitals under the supervision of trained doctors and nurses to prevent infections and obstetric complications (~89% institutional deliveries in NFHS-5).

What is the demographic definition of 'Total Fertility Rate' (TFR)?
The average number of children a woman would give birth to during her entire reproductive span (15 to 49 years).
Tap to view answer
What TFR value is considered the 'Replacement Level Fertility' of a population?
2.1 (an average of 2.1 children born per woman).

What does 'Stunting' indicate among child nutrition indicators?
