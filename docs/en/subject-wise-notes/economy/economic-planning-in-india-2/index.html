Economic Planning in India Part 2

Role of Five-Year Plans, key objectives, shifting priorities between agriculture and industry, and an in-depth macroeconomic analysis of sectoral linkages

Economic Planning in India Part 2
Plan / PolicyKey ObjectivesSectoral / Economic Impact
First Five-Year Plan (1951–56)View Answer: Agricultural development, mega multipurpose irrigation networks (Bhakra Nangal, DVC, Hirakud), and resolving the post-war and Partition-induced food crisis.View Answer: A historic 36% jump in foodgrain production, restoration of price stability, and an encouraging growth rate of 3.6% against the target of 2.1%.
Second Five-Year Plan (1956–61)View Answer: Rapid public-sector development of basic and heavy capital goods industries under the Nehru-Mahalanobis strategy.View Answer: Establishment of steel plants at Bhilai, Durgapur, and Rourkela; built the basic industrial framework, though relative neglect of agriculture triggered inflation and a food crisis.
Third Five-Year Plan (1961–66)View Answer: Building a self-sustaining and self-reliant economy through balanced growth in both agriculture and heavy industry.View Answer: Severely derailed by the 1962 and 1965 wars and the severe drought of 1965–66; heavy dependence on PL-480 foodgrain imports and the 1966 rupee devaluation.
Plan Holiday & Fourth Plan (1966–74)View Answer: Intensive spread of Green Revolution technology, growth with stability, and complete elimination of foreign dependence for food security.View Answer: Revolutionary surge in wheat and rice production; India became self-sufficient in foodgrains; nationalization of 14 major banks in 1969 accelerated rural credit flow.
Vakil-Brahmananda Wage-Goods Model (1956)View Answer: Advocated rapid investment in consumer/wage goods (foodgrains, cloth) and agriculture as an alternative to the Mahalanobis heavy-industry model.View Answer: Ignored by contemporary planners, though later events proved that neglecting agriculture and wage goods was the root cause of inflation and unemployment.
Fifth & Sixth Five-Year Plans (1974–85)View Answer: Poverty eradication ('Garibi Hatao'), Minimum Needs Programme (MNP), and rural employment and asset creation (IRDP, NREP).View Answer: Decline in poverty ratio, expansion of rural credit architecture (establishment of NABARD in 1982), and breaking through the 'Hindu Rate of Growth' (3.5%) barrier.
Seventh Five-Year Plan (1985–90)View Answer: Triple objective of 'Food, Work, and Productivity'; improving agricultural productivity, technological modernization, and promoting the private sector.View Answer: Recorded a rapid growth rate of 6.0%; for the first time, the proportionate share of private capital investment exceeded that of the public sector.
Eighth to Twelfth Plans (1992–2017)View Answer: Indicative planning, human resource development, rapid infrastructure creation, and 'Faster, Sustainable and More Inclusive Growth'.View Answer: High average growth rate of 7–8%, digital and telecom revolution, and unprecedented services boom, though the income gap between agricultural and non-agricultural sectors widened.

One-Liner Questions

What was the foremost macroeconomic role of the Five-Year Plans (FYPs) in independent India?
Breaking colonial stagnation to build a modern, self-reliant, and integrated national economy through state-directed investment.
Tap to view answer
What was India's average annual economic growth rate between 1900 and 1947 at the time of Independence?
Less than 1% per annum (around 0.7% to 0.9%), while per capita income growth was practically near zero (0.1%).
Tap to view answer
How did the Five-Year Plans accelerate 'Gross Fixed Capital Formation' in India?
By raising the domestic savings rate from 8–9% of GDP (1950) to 25–30% (by the 1990s), enabling intensive capital investment in basic infrastructure and factories.
Tap to view answer
What historic responsibility was assigned to Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs) under planned development?
To occupy the 'commanding heights' of the economy, undertake large capital risks, and supply foundational inputs (steel, coal, power) for private-sector growth.
Tap to view answer
How did the Five-Year Plans lay the foundation of scientific and technological self-reliance in the country?
Through the planned establishment of IITs, IIMs, AIIMS, the Atomic Energy Commission (AEC), ISRO, and CSIR laboratories.
Tap to view answer
Which Development Financial Institutions (DFIs) were established during the planning era to provide concessional and accessible long-term finance to industrial projects?
IFCI (1948), ICICI (1955), IDBI (1964), and UTI (1964).
Tap to view answer
How was the nationalization of 14 major commercial banks in July 1969 linked to plan priorities?
By freeing bank credit from concentration in big industrial houses and directing it toward plan priorities—agriculture, small industries, exports, and rural areas.
Tap to view answer
What was the central role of the 'Import Substitution Industrialization' (ISI) strategy in economic planning?
Reducing dependence on imported foreign goods, protecting domestic manufacturing through tariffs and quotas, and conserving scarce foreign exchange.
Tap to view answer
What was the nature of Indian Five-Year Plans prior to 1991?
Imperative and Physical Planning, where the state administratively fixed production targets, licences, and prices.
Tap to view answer
What fundamental shift occurred in the methodology of Indian planning from the Eighth Five-Year Plan (1992–97) onward?
A shift from imperative planning to Indicative Planning, where the state became a facilitator and guide rather than a controller.
Tap to view answer
How did the Five-Year Plans influence the transfer of financial resources within India's federal structure?
Through the allocation of plan grants and loans to states by the Planning Commission under the Gadgil-Mukherjee Formula.
Tap to view answer
What did 'Plan Expenditure' mean during the era of Five-Year Plans?
Government expenditure incurred on new developmental projects, asset creation, and social programmes under the ongoing Five-Year Plan.
Tap to view answer
What was termed 'Non-Plan Expenditure' during the era of Five-Year Plans?
Expenditure on day-to-day administrative functioning of the government, maintenance of assets created in earlier plans, salaries, pensions, defence, and interest payments on debt.
Tap to view answer
When and on the recommendation of which committee was the artificial distinction between Plan and Non-Plan expenditure abolished in the budget?
From the Union Budget 2017–18, based on the recommendations of the Dr. C. Rangarajan Committee.
Tap to view answer
What was the constitutional status of the Planning Commission of India?
It was a non-constitutional and extra-statutory body, constituted on 15 March 1950 by an executive resolution of the Union Cabinet.
Tap to view answer
What was the role of the National Development Council (NDC) in the planning process?
Constituted on 6 August 1952, the NDC was the apex body to deliberate on draft plans and grant them final sovereign approval.
Tap to view answer
Who served as the ex-officio Chairman of the Planning Commission and the National Development Council (NDC)?
The Prime Minister of India.

Which new body was established on 1 January 2015 in place of the Planning Commission?
NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India).
Tap to view answer
What is the guiding philosophy of NITI Aayog compared to the top-down approach of the Planning Commission?
Bottom-up policy guidance based on Cooperative Federalism and Competitive Federalism.
Tap to view answer
Which new planning framework was adopted by NITI Aayog after ending the Soviet-style 5-year plans?
A 15-year 'Vision Document', a 7-year 'Strategy', and a 3-year 'Action Agenda'.
Tap to view answer
What was the place of Deficit Financing in mobilizing financial resources for the Five-Year Plans?
Covering expenditure by printing currency against ad-hoc Treasury Bills to bridge the shortfall left by taxes and domestic borrowing.
Tap to view answer
What adverse impact did excessive deficit financing have on the Indian economy during the 1960s and 1970s?
Excessive expansion in money supply (M3) and severe double-digit inflation.
Tap to view answer
What was the impact of Foreign Aid in financing the Five-Year Plans?
Heavy dependence on foreign aid (Aid India Consortium) in the early plans increased the external debt burden and put pressure on the Balance of Payments.
Tap to view answer
Which savings institutions did the Five-Year Plans expand to promote domestic financial intermediation?
Post Office Savings Banks, Life Insurance Corporation of India (LIC - 1956), and Unit Trust of India (UTI - 1964).
Tap to view answer
How did planned development transform transport infrastructure in India?
Extensive electrification and modernization of Indian Railways, expansion of the National Highway network, and capacity enhancement of major ports.
Tap to view answer
What was the biggest strategic achievement of the Five-Year Plans in the energy sector?
Increasing power generation capacity from a mere 1,362 MW in 1950 to over 3,00,000 MW by the Twelfth Plan.
Tap to view answer
What effect of public investment on private investment was observed (Crowding-in vs Crowding-out)?
Public investment in infrastructure attracted private investment (crowding-in), although excessive government borrowing occasionally squeezed private credit (crowding-out).
Tap to view answer
What form did 'Physical Controls' take during the era of Five-Year Plans?
Price controls, import licensing, foreign exchange rationing, and restrictions on the movement of commodities under the Essential Commodities Act (1955).
Tap to view answer
What was the main challenge of coordination between central ministries and state governments in implementing the Five-Year Plans?
Erosion of states' financial autonomy and imposition of central agendas over state priorities through Centrally Sponsored Schemes (CSS).
Tap to view answer
Which specialized division within the Planning Commission worked for the evaluation of Five-Year Plans?
The Programme Evaluation Organisation (PEO).
Tap to view answer
What was the significance of the Mid-Term Appraisal of plans?
Reviewing actual achievements after two and a half to three years of a plan and revising priorities and financial allocations for the remaining period.
Tap to view answer
How did the Five-Year Plans strengthen the compilation of national income and statistics in India?
By establishing the Central Statistical Organisation (CSO) and the National Sample Survey Organisation (NSSO) to build an accurate macroeconomic database.
Tap to view answer
What self-reliance did India achieve in space research under planned development?
Developing the INSAT and IRS satellite systems through ISRO, achieving global standing in communication and remote sensing.
Tap to view answer
What place was given to Homi Jehangir Bhabha's three-stage nuclear power programme in planning?
Construction of research reactors and the Tarapur and Rawatbhata nuclear power plants for thorium-based long-term energy security.
Tap to view answer
What was the greatest historic contribution of the Five-Year Plans to India's national economic integration?
Binding a country fragmented into hundreds of princely states and British provinces into a single integrated national market, unified monetary system, and shared economic goals.
Tap to view answer
What have been the four major overarching long-term objectives of the Five-Year Plans?
1. Economic Growth, 2. Modernization, 3. Self-Reliance, and 4. Equity and Social Justice.
Tap to view answer
What is the real measure of 'Economic Growth' in the context of planning?
Continuous and sustainable expansion in Gross Domestic Product (GDP) and real per capita income.
Tap to view answer
What name did economist Prof. Raj Krishna give to India's low average growth rate of 3.5% between 1950 and 1980?
The 'Hindu Rate of Growth'.
Tap to view answer
In which Five-Year Plan was the barrier of the 'Hindu Rate of Growth' first broken in Indian planning?
In the Sixth Five-Year Plan (1980–85), when an average growth rate of 5.66% was recorded.
Tap to view answer
What growth trajectory did the Indian economy touch during the 8th to 11th Five-Year Plans?
A rapid average growth rate of 6.8% to 8.0%, placing India among the fastest-growing major economies in the world.
Tap to view answer
What is meant by 'Modernization' as an objective of planning?
Adopting modern science and advanced technology in production, along with promoting women's equality and a progressive outlook in the social structure.
Tap to view answer
Which major reforms were advanced in planning under institutional modernization?
Abolition of intermediary middlemen, tenancy reforms, end of Zamindari, and institutional expansion of Panchayati Raj.
Tap to view answer
What was the strategic meaning of 'Self-Reliance' in Indian planning?
Ending dependence on foreign food aid and external loans, and domestically producing all essential goods that could be imported.
Tap to view answer
What is the fundamental difference between Self-Reliance and Autarky (complete isolation)?
Self-reliance does not eliminate foreign trade; rather, it builds the capacity to finance essential imports through one's own export earnings.
Tap to view answer
Why was 'Self-Reliance' made the supreme national goal in the Third and Fourth Five-Year Plans?
Because the diplomatic threat by the US to halt PL-480 food supplies during the 1965 war and drought endangered national sovereignty.
Tap to view answer
What was the objective of 'Equity & Social Justice' in planning?
In accordance with Articles 38 and 39 of the Constitution, reducing the concentration of income, wealth, and economic power and providing a basic standard of living to weaker sections.
Tap to view answer
What did the 'Trickle-Down Theory' of the early Five-Year Plans assume?
That if a high GDP growth rate is achieved, its economic benefits will automatically trickle down to the poorest person in society.
Tap to view answer
Why did the Planning Commission reject the 'Trickle-Down Theory' in the 1970s?
Because the benefits of growth remained confined to affluent sections while poverty persisted unchanged, necessitating direct targeted poverty alleviation programmes.
Tap to view answer
What was the central and most popular slogan of the Fifth Five-Year Plan (1974–79)?
'Garibi Hatao' (Eradicate Poverty).
Tap to view answer
What was the 'Minimum Needs Programme' (MNP - 1974) launched in the Fifth Plan?
A government guarantee of basic civic amenities such as rural primary education, rural health, drinking water, nutrition, rural electrification, and housing.
Tap to view answer
What was the primary objective of the 'Twenty Point Programme' (TPP) announced in 1975?
A direct assault on rural poverty, abolition of bonded labour, house sites for the landless, and minimum wages for agricultural labourers.
Tap to view answer
What was the core mechanism of the Integrated Rural Development Programme (IRDP), which was scaled up nationwide in the Sixth Plan?
Providing income-generating assets (milch cattle, sewing machines) to Below Poverty Line families through bank credit and government subsidy.
Tap to view answer
What was the status of 'Full Employment' as an objective of planning?
Due to the heavy-industry model, capital-intensive techniques were adopted, which increased output but failed to generate sufficient jobs (jobless growth).
Tap to view answer
Which landmark scheme was given statutory form in 2005 to guarantee employment in rural areas?
The Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (MGNREGA).
Tap to view answer
What steps were taken in planning for 'Balanced Regional Development'?
Establishing public steel plants (Bhilai, Rourkela, Bokaro) in backward and tribal regions and providing concessional industrial credit.
Tap to view answer
What impact did the 1952 'Freight Equalization Policy' have on the development of eastern states (Bihar, Bengal, Odisha)?
They lost their local comparative advantage in minerals, causing industrialization in these states to lag behind and deepening regional imbalances.
Tap to view answer
Which states were granted 'Special Category Status' under the Gadgil Formula (1969)?
Assam, Nagaland, and Jammu & Kashmir (later extended to other North-Eastern and Himalayan states).
Tap to view answer
What financial preference in central assistance was given to Special Category Status states?
90% central grant and only 10% loan (compared to 30% grant and 70% loan for general category states).
Tap to view answer
What was the most distinctive planning methodology of the Tenth Five-Year Plan (2002–2007)?
Introduction of 11 specific 'Monitorable Targets' on poverty, literacy, infant mortality rate, and forest cover alongside GDP growth.
Tap to view answer
What was the central theme of the 11th Five-Year Plan (2007–12)?
'Towards Faster and More Inclusive Growth'.
Tap to view answer
Which indicators were prioritized to measure 'Inclusive Growth' in the 11th Plan?
Agricultural growth rate (4%), 10% reduction in poverty, 5.8 crore new non-farm jobs, and reduction in gender inequality.
Tap to view answer
What was the official motto of the 12th Five-Year Plan (2012–17)?
'Faster, Sustainable and More Inclusive Growth'.
Tap to view answer
Which dimension was made a central pillar of planning by adding the word 'Sustainability' in the 12th Plan?
Environmental conservation, prudent use of natural resources, and climate change adaptation.
Tap to view answer
Which law was enacted in 1969 under planning objectives to curb the concentration of economic power?
The Monopolies and Restrictive Trade Practices Act (MRTP Act, 1969).
Tap to view answer
Which system was built to maintain Price Stability in planning?
The Public Distribution System (PDS) and buffer stock management through the Food Corporation of India (FCI).
Tap to view answer
On which primary social sectors was expenditure increased under planning to improve the Human Development Index (HDI)?
Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan (SSA), National Rural Health Mission (NRHM), and Integrated Child Development Services (ICDS).
Tap to view answer
Which sector received the highest share of total plan expenditure in the First Five-Year Plan (1951–56)?
Agriculture, community development, irrigation, and flood control (over 31% of total expenditure).
Tap to view answer
What was the rationale behind giving top priority to agriculture in the First Plan?
Due to Partition, 82% of the population remained in India while a large share of fertile irrigated land went to Pakistan; halting starvation and inflation was a matter of survival.
Tap to view answer
What increase was recorded in foodgrain production during the First Five-Year Plan?
Foodgrain production rose from 52 million tonnes to 66 million tonnes (an extraordinary increase of about 27% to 36%).
Tap to view answer
Which major irrigation projects were initiated alongside agriculture in the First Plan?
Bhakra Nangal (Punjab-Himachal), Damodar Valley (Bihar-Bengal), and Hirakud (Odisha).
Tap to view answer
What dramatic shift in priorities occurred in the Second Five-Year Plan (1956–61)?
Priority was shifted away from agriculture to place supreme emphasis on heavy, basic capital goods industries and minerals.
Tap to view answer
From 4% in the First Plan, to what share did the plan allocation for the industrial sector rise in the Second Plan?
To about 20% to 24% of total plan expenditure.
Tap to view answer
What was the Nehru-Mahalanobis economic rationale behind prioritizing heavy industries in the Second Plan?
Domestic production of heavy machinery, iron and steel, and power is essential for long-term self-reliance; even agricultural implements can only be manufactured when the country has a steel industry.
Tap to view answer
Which three major public-sector steel plants were established during the Second Plan?
Bhilai (with Soviet/Russian assistance), Rourkela (with German assistance), and Durgapur (with British assistance).
Tap to view answer
What disastrous consequence did the relative neglect of agriculture in the Second Plan produce in the mid-1960s?
The country suffered severe foodgrain famine, inflation spiralled out of control, and India became entirely dependent on US PL-480 foodgrain aid.
Tap to view answer
How was an attempt made to balance priorities between agriculture and industry in the Third Five-Year Plan (1961–66)?
Agriculture was again given top priority to achieve foodgrain self-sufficiency, while simultaneously making a dual effort to continue heavy industrial development.
Tap to view answer
Which major steel plant was established in 1964 during the Third Plan with Soviet assistance?
Bokaro Steel Plant (Jharkhand).
Tap to view answer
Following the failure of the Third Plan, what historic strategic shift occurred in agricultural policy during the Plan Holiday (1966–69)?
Technological reforms (Green Revolution — HYV seeds, fertilizers, tube wells) were prioritized over institutional reforms (land reforms).
Tap to view answer
Which two bodies were constituted in 1965 during the Plan Holiday to institutionalize agricultural price policy?
The Agricultural Prices Commission (APC, now CACP) and the Food Corporation of India (FCI).
Tap to view answer
What form did the priority of the agricultural sector take in the Fourth Five-Year Plan (1969–74)?
Achieving complete self-sufficiency in foodgrains through the intensive spread of the Green Revolution and building a buffer stock.
Tap to view answer
Which industries were included alongside heavy industries in the industrial priorities of the Fifth and Sixth Plans?
Small, cottage, and village industries (SSIs) and the energy sector (oil exploration at Bombay High and thermal power).
Tap to view answer
Which apex institution dedicated to rural and agricultural financing was established in 1982 during the Sixth Five-Year Plan?
National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development (NABARD — on the recommendation of the Sivaraman Committee).
Tap to view answer
In which slogan was the synthesis of agricultural and industrial priorities reflected in the Seventh Five-Year Plan (1985–90)?
'Food, Work, and Productivity'; raising productivity in agriculture and technological modernization in industrial manufacturing.
Tap to view answer
Whose share in capital investment exceeded that of the public sector for the first time in the Seventh Plan?
The private sector's share (Private sector: ~52%, Public sector: ~48%).
Tap to view answer
Toward which new sector did priorities tilt from the 8th to the 12th Plan after the 1991 reforms?
The services sector (software, telecom, banking) and core infrastructure (roads, ports, airports, metro rail).
Tap to view answer
Which mission was launched in the 11th Plan (2007–12) to boost foodgrain production?
The National Food Security Mission (NFSM - 2007), to expand production of rice, wheat, and pulses.
Tap to view answer
Which scheme was launched in the 11th Plan to give states flexibility in agricultural planning?
Rashtriya Krishi Vikas Yojana (RKVY - 2007).

What historic shift occurred in the share of agriculture and industry in Gross Domestic Product (GDP) over the 12 Five-Year Plans?
Agriculture's share in GDP fell from ~55% in 1950 to ~15–16%, while industry's share rose from ~15% to stabilize at ~28–30%.
Tap to view answer
What is the evidence of 'Structural Asymmetry' in the Indian economy that highlights the limitation of planning?
Agriculture's share in GDP fell to 15–16%, yet nearly 45–46% of the country's working population still depends on agriculture for livelihood.
Tap to view answer
What position did the Services Sector come to occupy in the structure of the Indian economy?
The largest contributor to GDP at over 54%, although it absorbs only about 30–32% of total employment.
Tap to view answer
What failure of the Indian manufacturing sector could not be overcome during the planning era?
'Manufacturing Stagnation'; manufacturing's share in GDP remained stuck in a narrow band of 15–17% for decades.
Tap to view answer
What kind of 'Interdependence' exists between agriculture and industry in planning?
Agriculture supplies raw materials, foodgrains (wage goods), and a vast market to industry, while industry supplies modern inputs (tractors, fertilizers, pesticides, tube wells) to agriculture.
Tap to view answer
What are the 'Forward Linkages' provided by the agricultural sector to industries?
Supplying raw materials to agro-based industries such as cotton for textiles, sugarcane for sugar, oilseeds for edible oil, and jute for sacking.
Tap to view answer
What are the 'Backward Linkages' provided by the industrial sector to agriculture?
Supply of chemical fertilizers (urea, DAP), pesticides, electric pump sets, diesel engines, tractors, threshers, and drip irrigation equipment.
Tap to view answer
What are called 'Wage Goods' in economics?
Those essential basic consumption goods (especially foodgrains such as wheat, rice, and pulses) that are indispensable for workers to survive and work.
Tap to view answer
What crucial role does the supply of foodgrains (wage goods) play in the process of industrial development?
If the supply of foodgrains is adequate and affordable, the wage cost of industrial workers and inflation remain low, boosting industrial profits and investment.
Tap to view answer
Which famous model did Prof. C. N. Vakil and Dr. P. R. Brahmananda present in 1956 as an alternative to the Mahalanobis model?
The 'Wage-Goods Model of Development'.
Tap to view answer
What was the main central argument of the Vakil-Brahmananda Wage-Goods Model?
The primary constraint to development in underdeveloped countries is not a shortage of heavy machines, but a shortage of wage goods (foodgrains and cloth); hence, investment should go into agriculture and consumer goods.
Tap to view answer
According to the Vakil-Brahmananda model, what would have been the impact on employment of investing in agriculture and wage goods?
Being labour-intensive, this model could have provided immediate productive employment to crores of unemployed and disguisedly unemployed rural workers.
Tap to view answer
Why did contemporary planners reject the Vakil-Brahmananda Wage-Goods Model in 1956?
Because Nehru and Mahalanobis were deeply influenced by the Soviet heavy industrialization model and wanted to rapidly turn India into a military and industrial power.
Tap to view answer
Which prediction of the Vakil-Brahmananda model was proven true by the crisis of the mid-1960s?
That neglecting agriculture would cause food shortages to stall industrial growth, trigger inflation, and leave the country dependent on foreign food aid.
Tap to view answer
What was Soviet economist Yevgeni Preobrazhensky's debate on 'Primitive Socialist Accumulation'?
Extracting surplus from agriculture for building heavy industries by taxing the agricultural sector or suppressing agricultural product prices.
Tap to view answer
Did India forcibly tax agriculture to extract surplus for industry like the Soviet Union in its planning?
No; India was a democratic country where farmers held massive electoral clout; therefore, no direct income tax was levied on agriculture, and instead heavy subsidies were given on fertilizers, power, and water.
Tap to view answer
What is meant by 'Terms of Trade (ToT) between Agriculture and Industry'?
The ratio of the price index of agricultural products to the price index of manufactured industrial products (Pa / Pi × 100).
Tap to view answer
What happens when the Terms of Trade turn unfavourable (against agriculture)?
Farmers receive lower prices for their produce while having to buy tractors, fertilizers, and consumer goods at higher prices (Price Scissors).
Tap to view answer
What did economist Dr. Ashok Mitra argue in his book 'Terms of Trade and Class Relations' (1977)?
After the Green Revolution, due to the political influence of the rich farmer lobby, the terms of trade tilted in favour of agriculture, reducing industrial profits and slowing down industrialization.
Tap to view answer
What does British economist Michael Lipton's 'Urban Bias Thesis' state?
Planners and politicians in developing countries, under pressure from urban elites, divert policies and resources toward cities and industries, keeping the rural majority poor.
Tap to view answer
How does W. Arthur Lewis's 'Dual Economy Model' view the relationship between agriculture and industry?
Surplus labour in the traditional agricultural sector has zero marginal productivity; industrialization becomes possible by transferring this labour to the modern industrial sector at a constant subsistence wage.
Tap to view answer
Why could the Lewis model not succeed fully in India?
Because Indian industries adopted capital-intensive rather than labour-intensive techniques, leaving them unable to absorb crores of surplus workers moving from agriculture.
Tap to view answer
How is the agricultural sector the largest 'Demand Driver' for India's domestic manufactured goods?
The country's 65% rural population forms a massive domestic consumption market for FMCG, two-wheelers, textiles, cement, and electronics.
Tap to view answer
When the monsoon fails in India and agricultural output falls, what is its direct impact on industrial manufacturing?
Rural purchasing power collapses, reducing demand for factory products and causing a slowdown in the Index of Industrial Production (IIP).
Tap to view answer
What is the significance of the 'Food Processing Industry' (FPI) as a bridge between agriculture and industry?
It adds value to agricultural produce, prevents post-harvest wastage, raises farmers' incomes, and generates massive non-farm employment in rural areas.
Tap to view answer
What is the main structural objective of the Mega Food Parks scheme?
To directly connect farmers, processors, and retailers by establishing a seamless cold chain and modern processing infrastructure from farm to retail market.
Tap to view answer
What impact does a decline in Public Capital Formation in agriculture have on industry?
Irrigation, canal networks, and rural infrastructure weaken, lowering agricultural productivity and making raw materials supplied to industries more expensive.
Tap to view answer
Which mechanism recommended by CACP operates to stabilize prices of agricultural produce during planning?
Minimum Support Price (MSP), announced for 22 mandated crops, along with Fair and Remunerative Price (FRP) for sugarcane.
Tap to view answer
What key recommendation did the Ashok Dalwai Committee (2017–18) make to strengthen agricultural and non-agricultural linkages?
To double farmers' income, shift agriculture from being solely production-centric and link it with agribusiness, processing, and rural non-farm enterprises.
Tap to view answer
What is the role of 'Farmer Producer Organizations' (FPOs) in rural industrialization?
Organizing small farmers to provide collective processing, branding, and direct price-bargaining capacity with corporate industry.
Tap to view answer
What is the relationship between 'Disguised Unemployment' in agriculture and industrial development?
Deploying surplus labour engaged in agriculture—whose marginal productivity in farming is zero—into industries so that net total national output can increase.
Tap to view answer
Into which three schedules were industries divided in the Industrial Policy Resolution of 1956 (IPR 1956)?
Schedule A (17 exclusively state-owned industries), Schedule B (12 mixed industries), and Schedule C (remaining industries for the private sector).
Tap to view answer
Which system was spawned by the Industries (Development and Regulation) Act, 1951 (IDRA 1951)?
The 'License-Permit-Quota Raj', which imposed strict bureaucratic control over the establishment and capacity expansion of private industries.
Tap to view answer
Which committee constituted in 1969 to investigate the License Raj exposed the misuse of licences by big industrial houses?
The Dutt Committee (Industrial Licensing Policy Inquiry Committee).
Tap to view answer
What was the main finding of the Hazari Committee (1967) report?
Big industrial houses like Birla had cornered advance licences far in excess of capacity to block competitors and create monopolies.
Tap to view answer
Which policy was implemented during planning to protect the Small Scale Industries (SSI) sector?
Reservation of Products, under which hundreds of items were reserved exclusively for manufacture by small-scale units.
Tap to view answer
What was the negative economic impact of the policy of mandatory product reservation for small industries?
Indian industries could not achieve Economies of Scale and lagged behind China and East Asian countries in global export markets.
Tap to view answer
Which committee recommended ending the policy of reservation for small industries in 1997?
The Abid Hussain Committee on Small Scale Enterprises.

In which year was the Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development (MSMED) Act enacted?
In 2006, classifying manufacturing and service enterprises into micro, small, and medium categories.
Tap to view answer
Which two criteria were included in the new composite definition of MSMEs under the Atmanirbhar Bharat package in 2020?
A combined criterion of investment in plant and machinery and annual turnover.

Investment up to ₹1 crore and turnover up to ₹5 crore.
Tap to view answer

Investment up to ₹10 crore and turnover between ₹5 crore and ₹50 crore.

According to the new MSME definition, what is the threshold for a 'Medium Enterprise'?
Investment up to ₹50 crore and turnover between ₹50 crore and ₹250 crore.
Tap to view answer
What demand-side impact did the Green Revolution have on Indian industrialization?
Rising incomes of affluent farmers in Punjab, Haryana, and western Uttar Pradesh triggered a massive surge in demand for tractors, motorcycles, diesel engines, and consumer goods.
