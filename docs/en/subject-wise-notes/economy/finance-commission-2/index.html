Finance Commission Part 2

Comprehensive analysis of the report submission process, Article 281, the Divisible Pool of taxes, the 80th Constitutional Amendment, and Vertical Devolution

Quick Reference
Key Facts & Contributions
Finance Commission Part 2
Recommendation / Distribution MechanismKey ProvisionsImpact on Fiscal Federalism
Submission of Report to the PresidentView Answer Under Article 280(1), the Finance Commission submits its final signed report containing its recommendations directly to the President of India.View Answer Establishes impartial constitutional arbitration in fiscal federalism; an unbiased institutional assessment independent of the Union executive.
Article 281 (Tabling in Parliament)View Answer The President causes the Commission's recommendations, along with an explanatory memorandum on the action taken by the government (Action Taken Report - ATR), to be laid before both Houses of Parliament.View Answer Strengthens fiscal accountability and transparency; makes it mandatory for the government to state logical reasons to Parliament if any recommendation is rejected.
80th Constitutional Amendment Act (2000)View Answer Amended Article 270 on the recommendation of the 10th Finance Commission; brought all Union taxes (barring specific exceptions) into a single 'Divisible Pool'.View Answer Eliminated central bias in tax selection; made Centre–State tax sharing broader, more transparent, and predictable.
Divisible Pool of TaxesView Answer Net tax proceeds remaining after deducting cesses, surcharges, cost of collection, and taxes collected in Union Territories from the Union's Gross Tax Revenue.View Answer Forms the real base for tax devolution to States; unchecked expansion of cesses and surcharges shrinks the divisible pool and is a major friction point in federalism.
Vertical DevolutionView Answer A fixed percentage of the divisible tax pool transferred collectively to all States (e.g., 42% under the 14th FC and 41% under the 15th FC).View Answer Bridges Vertical Fiscal Imbalance (VFI); provides untied constitutional financial resources to the States.
14th Finance Commission's Leap to 42%View Answer Raised the vertical share directly from 32% to 42%; granted States automatic fiscal entitlement in place of discretionary Planning Commission grants.View Answer Historic expansion in States' budgetary autonomy; restructuring of Centrally Sponsored Schemes (CSS) and greater fiscal responsibility for States.
15th Finance Commission's 41% AdjustmentView Answer Adjusted the divisible pool share from 42% to 41%; the 1% reduction was reallocated to the Centre for the newly created Union Territories of Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh.View Answer Realistic adjustment for the Centre's security and developmental liabilities in the new Union Territories while safeguarding the States' share.
Defence & Internal Security Fund (MFDIS)View Answer Recommendation of a non-lapsable dedicated fund for defence modernization by the 15th Finance Commission under its Terms of Reference (ToR).View Answer A new policy experiment balancing supreme national security priorities with the developmental fiscal demands of the States.

Which specific Article of the Indian Constitution governs the laying of Finance Commission recommendations before Parliament?
Article 281 of the Constitution.

What does the core constitutional text of Article 281 mandate?
'The President shall cause every recommendation made by the Finance Commission under the provisions of this Constitution together with an explanatory memorandum as to the action taken thereon to be laid before each House of Parliament.'

What is the 'explanatory memorandum' laid alongside the report under Article 281 commonly called?

What is the primary purpose of the Explanatory Memorandum (ATR)?
To formally inform Parliament which recommendations of the Commission the government has accepted, partially accepted, or rejected, along with the reasons therefor.

On which occasion is the Finance Commission report normally tabled in Parliament?
Alongside the presentation of the Union Budget, so that budgetary allocations for tax devolution and grants for the upcoming financial year are clear.

Can Parliament (Lok Sabha or Rajya Sabha) amend or alter the recommendations of the Finance Commission on its own?
No; Parliament cannot directly modify or amend the Finance Commission's report; it can only debate the report and the government's ATR.

What is the legal status of Finance Commission recommendations from a constitutional standpoint?
They are purely advisory in nature and not legally binding on the Union Government.

Despite being advisory, which recommendations does the Union Government normally accept in toto?
Vertical and horizontal tax devolution and statutory Revenue Deficit Grants under Article 275.

Which categories of recommendations has the Union Government exercised discretion to reject or defer?
Sector-specific grants and State-specific grants.

Does a State government have the right to approach the Supreme Court directly against the implementation of a Finance Commission report?
Direct challenge is limited because recommendations are advisory, though an original suit under Article 131 can be filed if the Union acts arbitrarily or mala fide.

After tabling in Parliament, does any parliamentary standing committee examine the report in detail?
Yes; the Department-related Standing Committee on Finance and the Public Accounts Committee (PAC) can examine its fiscal implications.

Is the Finance Commission report laid before both Houses of Parliament (Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha) simultaneously?
Yes; under Article 281, it is laid before each House of Parliament.

Under Articles 270 and 275 of the Constitution.

Is there any fixed constitutional time limit for tabling the Finance Commission report in Parliament?
The Constitution specifies no fixed number of days or months, but by established convention it must be tabled before the new award period begins.

In which House of Parliament are State interests most vocally represented during debates on the Finance Commission report?
In the Rajya Sabha (Council of States), as it is the House representing the States.

How does Article 281 strengthen financial accountability?
By compelling the executive to explain to Parliament the scientific formula on which national resources are being distributed among the States.
Tap to view answer
Can the President refer a recommendation back to the Finance Commission for reconsideration?
Generally no; because upon submitting its report, the Commission's tenure ends and the body stands dissolved.

What does 'Tax Devolution' mean in fiscal federalism?
The unconditional transfer of a fixed constitutional percentage of the net proceeds of Union taxes by the Central Government to State governments.
Tap to view answer
Why is Tax Devolution referred to as 'Untied Funds' for the States?
Because the Centre cannot impose any preconditions or spending directives on these funds; States have complete freedom to budget them according to local priorities.
Tap to view answer
What is the fundamental difference between Tax Devolution and Centrally Sponsored Scheme (CSS) grants?
Tax devolution is an untied constitutional entitlement of States, whereas CSS grants are bound by strict central conditions, guidelines, and matching State shares.
Tap to view answer
What is the authoritative definition of the 'Divisible Pool of Central Taxes'?
The net tax proceeds available for sharing with the States after deducting cesses, surcharges, cost of collection, and taxes of Union Territories from the Centre's Gross Tax Revenue.
Tap to view answer
Under which Article of the Constitution is the basic framework for distributing the divisible pool between the Centre and States determined?
Article 270 of the Constitution.
Tap to view answer
Prior to the 80th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2000, what was the system for sharing central taxes in India?
Only personal income tax (Article 270) and Union excise duties (Article 272) were shared with the States, while corporation tax and customs duties remained wholly with the Centre.
Tap to view answer
Before the 80th Constitutional Amendment (2000), what was the States' share in income tax and Union excise duties?
About 85% of income tax and about 40% to 45% of Union excise duties were transferred to the States.
Tap to view answer
What was the most serious distortion in the pre-80th Amendment tax-sharing system?
The Union Government lacked incentive to raise income tax and excise rates (since most of it went to States) and instead raised corporation tax and customs duties, distorting the tax structure.
Tap to view answer
Based on which Finance Commission's historic recommendation was the 80th Constitutional Amendment (2000) enacted?
On the 'Alternative Scheme of Devolution' recommended by the 10th Finance Commission (Chairman: K. C. Pant).
Tap to view answer
What transformative arrangement did the 80th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2000 create by amending Article 270?
It pooled all Union List taxes and duties (corporation tax, customs duty, income tax, excise duty, service tax, etc.) into a single Divisible Pool.
Tap to view answer
Which major central taxes are currently included in the Union Divisible Pool?
1. Corporation Tax, 2. Personal Income Tax, 3. Central GST (CGST), 4. Centre's share of Integrated GST (IGST), 5. Customs Duty, and 6. Union Excise Duty on non-GST goods (such as petroleum).
Tap to view answer
Which items are legally excluded from the Union Divisible Pool?
1. Cesses and surcharges levied under Article 271, 2. Administrative cost of tax collection, and 3. Taxes collected from Union Territories.
Tap to view answer
Who holds the constitutional authority to certify the cost of collection deducted from 'Net Proceeds' of taxes?
The Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) of India (under Article 279).
Tap to view answer
What is the distinguishing feature of a 'Surcharge' levied under Article 271 of the Constitution?
An additional levy imposed by Parliament on taxes for specific Union purposes, 100% of which goes to the Consolidated Fund of India with no share for the States.
Tap to view answer
What is a 'Cess' and how does it differ from a general tax?
A 'tax on tax' levied for a pre-specified purpose (such as education, health, roads & infrastructure, or agriculture); its proceeds can only be used for that designated purpose and remain outside the divisible pool.
Tap to view answer
Which are the major cesses and surcharges currently levied by the Union Government?
Road and Infrastructure Cess (RIC), Agriculture Infrastructure and Development Cess (AIDC), Health and Education Cess (4%), and surcharge on high-income earners.
Tap to view answer
What is the biggest objection raised by States regarding cesses and surcharges in the context of the divisible pool?
'Shrinking of the Divisible Pool'; the share of cesses and surcharges in the Centre's Gross Tax Revenue has risen from ~9% in 2011–12 to 18%–20% currently, reducing the effective tax share reaching the States.
Tap to view answer
If the Union Government's Gross Tax Revenue is ₹100, of which ₹20 is cesses/surcharges and ₹2 is collection cost, what is the size of the divisible pool?
₹78 [100 - (20 + 2) = ₹78]; and States receive their 41% share out of this ₹78 (i.e., ₹31.98), not out of the full ₹100.
Tap to view answer
What policy advice did the 15th Finance Commission offer to the Union Government regarding the excessive use of cesses and surcharges?
To rationalize and limit the use of cesses and surcharges, sunset them once their purpose is served, or amend the Constitution to bring them into the divisible pool.
Tap to view answer
What is the 'Effective Devolution Rate' in fiscal federalism?
The actual percentage ratio of total tax devolved to States relative to the Centre's 'Gross Tax Revenue' (which drops to roughly 30%–33% compared to the statutory 41%).
Tap to view answer
What amendment was made to Article 270 following the introduction of GST?
The Central Goods and Services Tax (CGST) and the Centre's share of Integrated Goods and Services Tax (IGST) were included in the divisible pool.
Tap to view answer
Why are taxes falling under Article 268 not included in the divisible pool?
Because although levied by the Union, they are directly collected and appropriated by the States (such as stamp duties).
Tap to view answer
What is the arrangement for taxes collected under Article 269?
They are levied and collected by the Centre on inter-State trade but assigned directly to the States, hence they do not enter the divisible pool.
Tap to view answer
Is the Union Government's non-tax revenue (such as dividends or spectrum auctions) included in the divisible pool?
No; the divisible pool is confined solely to tax revenue, while non-tax revenue stays entirely with the Centre.
Tap to view answer
What is meant by 'Vertical Devolution / Vertical Distribution' in fiscal federalism?
The overall division ratio of financial resources from the central divisible tax pool between the Union Government and all State governments collectively.
Tap to view answer
What is the primary structural cause of Vertical Fiscal Imbalance (VFI)?
The Constitution assigned elastic and broad-based taxes (corporation tax, income tax, customs, GST) to the Centre, while assigning heavy public-welfare expenditure responsibilities (education, health, agriculture, policing, law and order) to the States.
Tap to view answer
What is the proportional balance between the Centre and States in total tax collection and total developmental expenditure in India?
The Centre collects roughly 60%–65% of total tax revenue, whereas over 60% of combined government expenditure is incurred by the States.
Tap to view answer
What is the Finance Commission's primary constitutional role in bridging Vertical Fiscal Imbalance?
To transfer an equitable share of the Centre's revenue surplus to the States so as to bridge their fiscal gap and balance national priorities.
Tap to view answer
What percentage of net income tax did the First Finance Commission (1952–57) recommend for devolution to the States?
55% share.
Tap to view answer
How was the States' share in income tax progressively raised from the Second to the Sixth Finance Commission?
60% in the 2nd FC, 66.66% in the 3rd FC, 75% in the 4th FC, and raised to 80% in the 6th FC (1974–79).
Tap to view answer
What peak did the States' share in income tax reach during the Seventh and Eighth Finance Commissions (1979–89)?
85% of the net proceeds of income tax (an all-time historical high for the States).
Tap to view answer
What share did the 10th Finance Commission (1995–2000) propose for States from a single pool of all central taxes prior to the 80th Amendment?
29% share (26% from all taxes + 3% from additional excise duties and others).
Tap to view answer
What vertical devolution percentage of the total divisible pool did the 11th Finance Commission (2000–2005) recommend under the amended Article 270?
28% share (plus an additional 1.5% for States not levying sales tax, totalling 29.5%).
Tap to view answer
To what level did the 12th Finance Commission (2005–2010, Chairman: Dr. C. Rangarajan) raise vertical devolution?
30.5% of the total divisible tax pool.
Tap to view answer
What vertical share did the 13th Finance Commission (2010–2015, Chairman: Dr. Vijay Kelkar) fix for the States?
32.0% of the total divisible tax pool.
Tap to view answer
What historic recommendation did the 14th Finance Commission (2015–2020, Chairman: Dr. Y. V. Reddy) make in vertical tax devolution?
An epoch-making jump of 10 percentage points, raising the States' share from 32% to 42%.
Tap to view answer
What was the main economic rationale behind the 14th Finance Commission's sharp 10% hike (from 32% to 42%) in vertical share?
To grant States 'untied fiscal space' so they could spend according to their own priorities without being dependent on discretionary Central schemes (CSS).
Tap to view answer
How did the then Union Government respond to the 14th Finance Commission's 42% tax devolution recommendation?
The government accepted this historic recommendation in full, though it offset the fiscal impact by reducing the central funding share in Centrally Sponsored Schemes (CSS).
Tap to view answer
What vertical share did the 15th Finance Commission (2020–2026, Chairman: N. K. Singh) fix for the States?
41% of the total divisible tax pool.

Why did the 15th Finance Commission reduce the vertical share from the 14th Commission's 42% to 41% (a 1% cut)?
To adjust for the Centre meeting the financial needs of the two newly created Union Territories (Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh) following the Jammu & Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019.
Tap to view answer
Did the 1% reduction by the 15th Finance Commission result in any real loss in the fiscal entitlement of regular States?
No; under the 14th FC, Jammu & Kashmir was among the 29 States and received roughly 0.85%–1%; the 15th FC simply returned that 1% to the Centre so it could fund those Union Territories directly.
Tap to view answer
Which mandatory financial commitments of the Union Government does the Finance Commission factor in when deciding vertical devolution?
National defence expenditure, internal security, heavy interest payments on legacy public debt, sovereign administration, and national infrastructure creation.
Tap to view answer
Which priorities of State governments does the Commission evaluate when deciding vertical devolution?
Education, health, agriculture, irrigation, law and order, social welfare pensions, and States' rising fiscal deficits.
Tap to view answer
Which new and controversial defence-related item was included in the Terms of Reference (ToR) of the 15th Finance Commission?
Whether a separate non-lapsable fund should be set up to finance defence and internal security, and whether resources should be ring-fenced for it beforehand.
Tap to view answer
What serious constitutional objection did non-BJP-ruled and southern States raise against this defence financing ToR?
That defence is exclusively a Union List subject and 100% the Centre's responsibility; carving out funds from the States' divisible pool in the name of defence would violate the spirit of the Constitution.
Tap to view answer
What final recommendation did the 15th Finance Commission make regarding the 'Modernisation Fund for Defence and Internal Security' (MFDIS)?
It recommended creating a ₹2.38 lakh crore fund, to be financed largely from the Consolidated Fund of India (non-divisible sources, defence land monetization, and disinvestment) without touching the States' 41% share.

How does 'Tax Buoyancy' in the divisible pool affect vertical devolution?
When central tax revenue grows rapidly alongside GDP growth (high tax buoyancy), the actual cash amount devolved to States rises automatically.
Tap to view answer
If central tax collections fall sharply in a financial year due to an economic slowdown or pandemic, how does it affect States' tax devolution?
States' tax devolution also falls proportionately, as it is a percentage (41%) of net collections rather than a fixed sum.

What special mechanism did the Union Government adopt during the COVID-19 pandemic (2020–21) to shield States from the sharp drop in tax devolution?
States' borrowing limit (FRBM limit) was raised from 3% to 5% of GSDP, and a 50-year interest-free capital loan scheme was launched.

Why was the GST Compensation Cess not part of the divisible pool?
Because it was created under a specific parliamentary law (GST Compensation Act, 2017) solely to provide 100% compensation to States for any shortfall below their 14% protected revenue growth for 5 years.

What demand did States raise before the Finance Commission and the Centre after the GST compensation period ended in June 2022?
Either extend the compensation period by another 5 years or raise vertical tax devolution from 41% to 50%.

Has any Finance Commission ever recommended raising the States' share in the divisible tax pool to 50%?
No; no Commission has recommended 50% so far (the highest has been 42% by the 14th FC and 41% by the 15th FC), so that the Centre retains adequate resources for national responsibilities.

What are the key demands of major States before the 16th Finance Commission regarding vertical devolution?
Creating a constitutional mechanism to include cesses and surcharges in the divisible pool, raising vertical devolution to at least 45%–50%, and restoring sectoral grants.
Tap to view answer
How does vertical tax devolution strengthen the fiscal capacity of States?
It gives States an assured, predictable, and non-intrusive revenue stream into their Consolidated Funds, enabling independent budgetary policymaking aligned with local priorities.
Tap to view answer
What is the core difference between Vertical Devolution and Horizontal Distribution?
Vertical distribution is the first aggregate split of taxes between the Centre and all States (41%), whereas horizontal distribution is the second stage of allocating that 41% share among the 28 individual States.

In the context of the Union Budget, what is the relationship between 'Gross Tax Revenue' (GTR) and 'Net Tax Revenue to the Centre' (NTR)?
Centre's Net Tax Revenue = Gross Tax Revenue - Tax Devolution to States - National Disaster Response Fund (NDRF) allocation.

Why do economists consider 'Tax Devolution over Grants' superior in fiscal federalism?
Because tax devolution involves no political bias or bureaucratic discretion, whereas central grants carry the risk of political arbitrariness and central conditionalities.

How does the vertical ratio set by the Finance Commission affect India's macroeconomic stability?
If States get too little, they fall into a debt trap; if the Centre retains too little, national security, external debt servicing, and the overall fiscal deficit can spiral; thus, the ratio balances both.

How are monthly instalments of tax devolution released between the Centre and the States?
The Union Ministry of Finance releases States' tax shares on the 10th of every month based on 14 equal monthly instalments.

Can tax devolution recommended by the Finance Commission be withheld on grounds of a State's financial mismanagement?
No; it is a constitutional entitlement of the States and the Centre cannot withhold or forfeit it on any administrative pretext.

Do direct taxes and indirect taxes in the Union's total tax revenue have the same impact on the divisible pool?
After the 80th Amendment, both form part of the divisible pool; hence, whether direct or indirect taxes rise, States benefit equally from their net collections.
