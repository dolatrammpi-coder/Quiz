Key Facts and Contributions
International Economic Organizations Part 2
Organization / InstitutionObjective or RoleSignificance for India
Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank (AIIB)Multilateral development bank established in 2016 to finance sustainable infrastructure, cross-border connectivity, and green projects in Asia and beyond.India is the second-largest shareholder (7.6% voting share) and historically the bank's largest cumulative borrower ($10B+).
New Development Bank (NDB / BRICS Bank)Established by BRICS nations (Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa) to finance infrastructure and sustainable development in emerging economies.Equal shareholding and equal voting power (20% per founding member); major financier of India's renewable energy, urban water supply, and metro projects.
Asian Development Bank (ADB)Manila-based regional bank established in 1966; focused on poverty eradication, social development, and economic infrastructure building in Asia-Pacific.India is the fourth-largest shareholder after Japan and the USA; key lender for Indian national highways, Smart Cities Mission, industrial corridors, and skill development.
International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (IBRD / World Bank)Long-term sovereign development loans at market rates, policy reform credit, and institutional technical assistance to middle-income developing nations.India's decades-long premier development partner; largest multilateral financier of roads, railways (Eastern DFC), Namami Gange Mission, and primary education reforms.
International Development Association (IDA / World Bank)Providing zero/concessional interest 'Soft Loans' with 30–40 year tenors and outright development grants to the world's poorest nations.India has historically been the largest beneficiary of IDA (in 2014, India 'graduated' to become eligible solely for IBRD loans).
International Finance Corporation (IFC / World Bank)Providing direct commercial loans, equity participation, structured finance, and asset management services to private enterprises in developing countries.Boosting foreign capital inflows into India's private sector for green energy (solar/wind), affordable housing, start-ups, and through 'Masala Bonds'.
AIIB Project Preparation Special Fund (Special Fund Window)Grants to low- and middle-income member countries to prepare technical feasibility studies, environmental impact assessments, and Detailed Project Reports (DPRs) for infrastructure projects.Financial assistance in preparing bankable proposals for complex infrastructure projects in India's North-Eastern and lagging states.
AIIB Crisis Recovery Facility (CRF)Emergency liquidity support to member nations' public health systems and budget balances during the COVID-19 pandemic and global supply chain crises.Rapid emergency loans of $1.25 billion+ to India for pandemic control, vaccine procurement, health infrastructure strengthening, and welfare packages for the poor.

What is the final and overarching macroeconomic takeaway from the complete study of International Economic Organizations Part 2?
The Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank (AIIB) stands as the most successful institutional symbol of 21st-century multilateralism; as its second-largest shareholder and largest beneficiary, India is safeguarding its sovereign policy autonomy while mobilizing affordable international capital for the National Infrastructure Pipeline (NIP), metro rail, rural roads, and green energy transition to strengthen its resolve for 'Viksit Bharat @2047'.
