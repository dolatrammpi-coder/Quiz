Complete analysis of the New Industrial Policy 1991, abolition of industrial licensing, MRTP amendments, and impact on private enterprises

Quick Reference
Key Facts & Contributions
Economic Reforms of 1991 Part 2
Reform / PolicyKey ProvisionsEconomic Impact
Widespread Abolition of Industrial Licensing (Delicensing)Compulsory industrial licensing abolished for all sectors except 18 industries linked to security, strategic, and environmental concerns.End of bureaucratic red tape, faster enterprise setup, and autonomy to determine capacity based on market demand.
Public Sector De-reservationThe 17 industries reserved under Schedule-A of the 1956 Industrial Policy Resolution were reduced to just 8 (currently only 2 remain reserved).Entry of private capital into core infrastructure, mining, and heavy industries; end of public sector monopoly and rise of competition.
Structural Rationalization of MRTP Act, 1969₹100 crore asset limit for large companies abolished; mandatory prior approval for capacity expansion, diversification, and mergers scrapped.Freedom for Indian corporations to achieve Economies of Scale; paved the way for the Competition Act, 2002.
Automatic Approval for Foreign Direct Investment (FDI)Automatic Route approval via RBI for up to 51% foreign equity participation in 34 high-priority industrial sectors.Rapid inflow of foreign capital, advanced global manufacturing technologies, and modern corporate management practices into Indian industry.
Liberalization of Foreign Technology AgreementsPrior government approval abolished for lump-sum technical payments up to $1 million and royalties of 5% domestic / 8% export in high-priority industries.Technological modernization of domestic manufacturing, direct access to patented processes, and shift from import substitution to export competitiveness.
Disinvestment in Non-Strategic PSUsPolicy initiative to partially sell equity of state-owned enterprises to mutual funds, financial institutions, employees, and the general public.Reduction of fiscal pressure on the government budget, financial discipline in PSUs, and development of market-based corporate accountability.
Small Scale Industries (SSI/MSME) Policy ReformsLarge and foreign enterprises permitted up to 24% equity ownership in SSIs; phased contraction of the Reservation List.Expansion of ancillarization between small and large enterprises; easier availability of capital and modern technology.
Flexibility in Industrial Locational PolicyMandatory prior central locational approval abolished for setting up non-polluting industries in cities with a population under 1 million.Rapid industrial expansion in Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities, reduction in regional disparities, and lower logistics costs for industries.

One-Liner Questions

What was the most fundamental and guiding economic philosophy of the New Industrial Policy (NIP 1991)?
To free the Indian economy from administrative controls and make it market-driven, competitive, and globally integrated.

What was the biggest ideological departure of NIP 1991 compared to the Industrial Policy Resolution of 1956 (IPR 1956)?
Accepting private enterprise and the market mechanism—rather than a state-directed command economy—as the primary driver of economic growth.

What were the four major structural pillars of NIP 1991?
1. Delicensing (abolition of industrial licensing), 2. De-reservation (opening public sector monopolies), 3. Foreign investment liberalization, and 4. Removal of MRTP restrictions.
Tap to view answer
How did NIP 1991 alter the 'Commanding Heights' status granted to the public sector under the Nehruvian model?
It ended the monopolistic role of the public sector and confined it only to select areas of strategic and security importance.

Which instrument did NIP 1991 consider best for enhancing efficiency in domestic manufacturing?
Open internal and external competition, so as to eliminate inefficiency and technological backwardness bred by protectionism.
Tap to view answer
What was NIP 1991's approach toward the Incremental Capital-Output Ratio (ICOR) to accelerate growth?
Minimizing ICOR by improving capital efficiency, enabling higher industrial output even with lower capital investment.
Tap to view answer
What changing role of the State in industrial development was defined under NIP 1991?
Transitioning from a direct producer and rigid controller to a catalyst, regulator, and facilitator.
Tap to view answer
How did NIP 1991 promote Supply-Side Economics?
By removing all administrative barriers on production capacity, diversification, and technology imports to clear supply bottlenecks.

What approach was adopted in NIP 1991 to deal with Industrial Sickness?
Emphasizing market discipline, restructuring, or liquidation rather than extending endless budgetary bailouts to inefficient units.

Attracting private investment to backward regions through infrastructure development and financial incentives rather than administrative coercion.

Moving away from the Mahalanobis heavy-industry-based import substitution model toward an open market economy model.
