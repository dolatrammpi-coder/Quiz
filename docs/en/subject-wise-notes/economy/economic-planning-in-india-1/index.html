Economic Planning in India Part 1

Detailed analysis of the historical background of pre-Independence planning, the Gandhian Plan, decentralization, Gram Swaraj, and the Sarvodaya Plan

Key Facts & Contributions
Economic Planning in India Part 1
Plan / IdeaKey FeaturesSignificance in Planning
Sir M. Visvesvaraya Plan (1934)View Answer: 10-year capitalist industrial planning; aimed to double national income by shifting the workforce from agriculture to manufacturing.View Answer: The first well-thought-out and published blueprint for economic planning in India; initiated the first debate on the need for planned development at the national level.
National Planning Committee - NPC (1938)View Answer: Constituted on the initiative of Subhash Chandra Bose and chaired by Jawaharlal Nehru; outlined state control over basic industries and a mixed economy.View Answer: Laid the groundwork for independent India's state-directed planning model, the leading role of the public sector, and the institutional precursor to the Planning Commission.
Bombay Plan (1944)View Answer: Proposed an outlay of ₹10,000 crore over 15 years by 8 leading industrialists (Tata, Birla, etc.); advocated heavy state investment in basic industries.View Answer: A landmark document in which private capitalists themselves acknowledged the necessity of state-directed planning and heavy public capital expenditure.
Gandhian Plan (1944)View Answer: Presented by Shriman Narayan Agarwal; emphasized simplicity, primacy of agriculture, cottage and khadi village industries, self-reliant Gram Swaraj, and decentralization.View Answer: The first blueprint of a labour-intensive, non-violent, and ecologically sustainable Indian development model as an alternative to Western capital-intensive industrialization.
People's Plan (1945)View Answer: Presented by M. N. Roy through the Indian Federation of Labour; proposed ₹15,000 crore over 10 years, nationalization of land, and expansion of consumer goods industries.View Answer: Planning based on Marxist principles; placed special emphasis on agricultural development, state ownership, and meeting the basic needs of the masses.
Sarvodaya Plan (1950)View Answer: Prepared by Jayaprakash Narayan (JP) by synthesizing Gandhian and socialist ideas; inspired by Vinoba Bhave's Bhoodan Movement and Gramdan.View Answer: Philosophy of land redistribution ('Land to the Tiller'), self-governance free from party politics, cooperative farming, and opposition to excessive mechanization.
J. C. Kumarappa's Economy of Permanence ModelView Answer: 'Economy of Permanence'; production based on the cyclic laws of nature, exploitation-free labour, and local self-reliance.View Answer: Ideological foundation of modern green economics, sustainable development, and the decentralized village industries movement.
73rd & 74th Constitutional Amendments (1992)View Answer: Constitutional status to Panchayati Raj and Municipalities; 11th Schedule (29 subjects) and mandatory constitution of District Planning Committees (DPC - Article 243ZD).View Answer: Constitutional establishment of participatory, democratic decentralized planning with public participation—shifting from top-down to bottom-up planning.

One-Liner Questions

Who is credited with presenting the first theoretical and practical blueprint for economic planning in pre-Independence India?
Bharat Ratna Sir Mokshagundam Visvesvaraya (M. Visvesvaraya).

In which year did Sir M. Visvesvaraya publish his famous book 'Planned Economy for India'?
In the year 1934.

How many years of planning were proposed for India's economic development in the Visvesvaraya Plan?
10 years of planning (a Ten-Year Plan).
Tap to view answer
What was set as the primary objective of the Visvesvaraya Plan?
To double the country's national income in 10 years and accelerate industrialization by reducing the excessive dependence of the population on agriculture.
Tap to view answer
To which sector did Visvesvaraya advocate shifting the workforce from agriculture?
To the manufacturing and modern industrial sector, so that labour productivity and per capita income could increase.
Tap to view answer
What was the ideological and structural nature of the Visvesvaraya Plan?
Based on a capitalist democratic model; focusing on free enterprise and state cooperation as seen in Western democracies rather than the communist model of the Soviet Union.
Tap to view answer
Which institution did Visvesvaraya suggest establishing for financing industrial development?
A dedicated Industrial Finance Corporation and a Central Economic Council.
Tap to view answer
According to the Visvesvaraya Plan, which apex body was proposed for the economic administration of the country?
The All India Economic Council.

What official resolution regarding planning did the Federation of Indian Chambers of Commerce and Industry (FICCI) pass in 1934?
It demanded that the state formulate a central economic plan to pull the economy out of depression and provide protection and support to private industries.

According to the National Planning Committee (NPC), who should own the key and basic industries?
Under the ownership and direct control of the State (Government).

What impact did the Second World War (1939–1945) and the Quit India Movement of 1942 have on the functioning of the National Planning Committee?
Due to the arrest of all major leaders including Nehru, the committee's work was disrupted, and its final reports could only be published after Independence in 1948–49.

What preliminary recommendation did the National Planning Committee (NPC) make regarding land reforms?
Abolition of Zamindari and intermediary middlemen, along with security of tenure for tenants and promotion of cooperative farming.
Tap to view answer
Between which two ideological streams did differences emerge in the National Planning Committee regarding the balance between cottage and heavy industries?
Between Gandhian thinkers (who favoured the charkha and village industries) and the Nehru-Saha group (who favoured modern heavy industries).

What was the official title of the famous private plan presented in 1944 by 8 leading industrialists of Bombay?
'A Brief Memorandum Outlining a Plan of Economic Development for India', commonly known as the 'Bombay Plan'.
Tap to view answer
Who were the 8 prominent industrialists who signed the draft of the Bombay Plan (1944)?
J. R. D. Tata, G. D. Birla, Purshotamdas Thakurdas, Kasturbhai Lalbhai, A. D. Shroff, John Mathai, Sir Ardeshir Dalal, and Lala Shri Ram.
Tap to view answer
What were the proposed time period and total budgetary financial outlay of the Bombay Plan?
A total financial outlay of ₹10,000 crore over a period of 15 years.
Tap to view answer
What was the primary output target of the Bombay Plan?
To triple national income and double per capita income within 15 years.
Tap to view answer
What ratio of growth targets between industry and agriculture was proposed in the Bombay Plan?
A target of 500% (five-fold) increase in industrial output and a 130% increase in agricultural output.
Tap to view answer
Despite being presented by private capitalists, what was the most revolutionary recommendation of the Bombay Plan?
Advocating massive investment and active economic intervention by the State (public sector) in infrastructure, heavy industries, and public utilities.
Tap to view answer
Which financial instrument was suggested in the Bombay Plan to bridge its large resource deficit?
Use of Deficit Financing (printing currency), proposed at around ₹3,400 crore.
Tap to view answer
In how many parts was the Bombay Plan published?
In two parts: Part I in January 1944 (targets and finance) and Part II in December 1944 (distribution system and the role of the state).
Tap to view answer
Which new department did the British Government of India establish in 1944 for post-war economic reconstruction?
The Planning and Development Department, with Bombay Plan signatory Sir Ardeshir Dalal appointed as its Member-in-Charge.
Tap to view answer
Who was the chief architect of the 'People's Plan' presented in 1945?
Renowned Marxist thinker and founder of the Radical Democratic Party, M. N. Roy.
Tap to view answer
On behalf of which organization's Post-War Reconstruction Committee was the People's Plan prepared?
The Indian Federation of Labour (IFL).
Tap to view answer
What were the duration and total estimated outlay of M. N. Roy's 'People's Plan' (1945)?
An outlay of ₹15,000 crore over a 10-year period.
Tap to view answer
What was the foremost ideological priority of the People's Plan (1945)?
Giving top priority to the agricultural sector, expanding consumer goods industries, and nationalizing land and the means of production.
Tap to view answer
How did the People's Plan fundamentally differ from the Bombay Plan?
While the Bombay Plan focused on heavy capital goods industries, the People's Plan prioritized agriculture and the consumption needs of the common people (food, clothing, health).
Tap to view answer
What was the most important recommendation of the Advisory Planning Board (1946) chaired by K. C. Neogy?
The establishment of a dedicated, non-political 'Planning Commission' for economic planning in independent India.
Tap to view answer
What was the biggest lesson that the devastating Bengal Famine of 1943 taught pre-Independence planners?
The absolute necessity of complete self-sufficiency in foodgrain production and a robust public distribution system.
Tap to view answer
What was the combined impact of all these pre-Independence plans on India's economic thinking?
A national consensus emerged that India's post-Independence economic development was possible only through state-directed central planning.
Tap to view answer
In which year and by whom was the 'Gandhian Plan' propounded?
In 1944, by Shriman Narayan Agarwal, Principal of Seksaria College of Commerce, Wardha.
Tap to view answer
Did the Gandhian Plan (1944) receive the personal endorsement of Mahatma Gandhi?
Yes, Mahatma Gandhi himself wrote the Foreword to this plan and expressed his agreement with its core principles.
Tap to view answer
What was the initial financial outlay of the Gandhian Plan proposed by Shriman Narayan Agarwal in 1944?
₹3,500 crore (which was revised to ₹5,600 crore in 1946).
Tap to view answer
What was the most fundamental philosophical basis of the Gandhian Plan?
Simplicity, non-violence, ethical values, human-centric development, and the Limitation of Wants.
Tap to view answer
What alternative did Gandhian economic philosophy suggest to Western capitalist 'Mass Production'?
'Production by the Masses'.
Tap to view answer
Which sector was proposed to receive the largest share of total budgetary resources in the Gandhian Plan?
Agriculture and allied activities (around 70% to 80% of the total outlay).
Tap to view answer
According to the Gandhian Plan, what should be the primary medium of industrial development?
Cottage and Village Industries, such as khadi spinning and weaving, oil ghani, jaggery making, and leather craft.
Tap to view answer
What was the Gandhian Plan's attitude toward heavy and large-scale industries (Heavy Machinery)?
Opposition to labour-displacing machines; advocating that large industries be restricted only to indispensable public sectors like defence, power, and basic infrastructure.
Tap to view answer
According to Mahatma Gandhi, what was the greatest social distortion of modern capital-intensive machinery?
It renders a majority of workers unemployed, enslaves human labour, and concentrates wealth in the hands of a few capitalists.
Tap to view answer
Economically, what did Gandhiji's famous 'Charkha' (spinning wheel) symbolize?
A symbol of economic self-reliance, dignity of labour, decentralized production, and an exploitation-free society.
Tap to view answer
What does the 'Theory of Trusteeship' in Gandhian economic thought propound?
A wealthy person is not the absolute owner of surplus wealth, but a 'trustee' of society, who should use the remaining wealth beyond legitimate personal needs for social welfare.
Tap to view answer
What target was set in the Gandhian Plan regarding the provision of Basic Needs?
Guaranteeing every citizen an adequate balanced diet, 20 yards of cloth, decent housing, free primary education, and basic healthcare.
Tap to view answer
What was the Gandhian Plan's firm stance on foreign trade and external debt?
Complete rejection of dependence on foreign aid and foreign loans; strict boycott of luxury imports and promotion of local products (Swadeshi).
Tap to view answer
Which institutional reforms were specially emphasized for agricultural development in the Gandhian Plan?
Complete abolition of Zamindari, ownership rights to tenant cultivators, use of organic/cow-dung manure, and consolidation of landholdings.
Tap to view answer
What alternative was suggested in the Gandhian Plan to eliminate rural indebtedness?
Expansion of cooperative credit societies and strict regulatory control over the moneylending system.
Tap to view answer
How did the Gandhian Plan present a direct theoretical clash with Nehru's industrialization model?
Nehru's model was urbanized, heavy-industry-based, and centralized, whereas the Gandhian Plan was completely rural, agriculture-based, and decentralized.
Tap to view answer
What direct influence did the Gandhian Plan have on the Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP) of the Indian Constitution?
Inclusion of Article 40 (organization of village panchayats), Article 43 (promotion of cottage industries), and Article 47 (prohibition of intoxicating drinks and drugs).
Tap to view answer
Which statutory body was established by the Government of India in 1956 to promote Gandhian village industries?
The Khadi and Village Industries Commission (KVIC).
Tap to view answer
Why is the Gandhian Plan considered relevant for Sustainable Development in the present era?
Because it presents an eco-friendly, carbon-neutral model based on local resources that stands in harmony with nature against excessive consumerism.
Tap to view answer
What is the concept of 'Bread Labour' in Gandhian economics?
Every human being must necessarily perform some physical labour to earn their livelihood and food.
Tap to view answer
According to Mahatma Gandhi, what was the economic principle of 'Swadeshi'?
Giving preference to goods produced in one's immediate neighbourhood and by local artisans over goods manufactured in distant places.
Tap to view answer
What is the fundamental political and economic concept of Mahatma Gandhi's 'Gram Swaraj'?
Every village should be a completely self-reliant and autonomous 'Village Republic' for its essential needs (food, clothing, shelter).
Tap to view answer
Through which geometric metaphor did Gandhiji explain the decentralization of power and the economy?
'Oceanic Circles'; where the individual is at the centre and ever-widening, never-ending cooperative circles expand outward, rather than a hierarchical pyramid.

1. Parasitic, 2. Predatory, 3. Enterprise, 4. Mutual Aid, and 5. Service economy.

According to Kumarappa, what is the core principle of an 'Economy of Permanence'?
Respecting the cyclic laws of nature; taking from nature only as much as can be returned to it (based on recycling and non-violence).

The principle of 'Land to the Tiller', complete abolition of tenancy/sub-letting, and redistribution of land above family-holding size to the landless.

Which three types of agricultural models did the Kumarappa Committee suggest for agrarian reorganization?
1. Family Holdings, 2. Joint Cooperative Farming, and 3. Collective Farming on wastelands.

A three-tier structure: 1. Gram Panchayat (village level), 2. Panchayat Samiti (block level), and 3. Zila Parishad (district level).

Not less than 4/5ths (80%) of the members, elected by and from among the elected members of the Zila Parishad and Municipalities.

What is the fundamental difference between Top-Down planning and Bottom-Up planning?
In top-down planning, policies and budgets are imposed from the Centre downward, whereas in bottom-up planning, needs and plans originate at the Gram Sabha level and move upward.
