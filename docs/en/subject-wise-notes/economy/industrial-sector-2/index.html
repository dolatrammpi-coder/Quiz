Industrial Sector Part 2

The New Industrial Policy of 1991, abolition of the License-Permit Raj, de-reservation of the public sector, MRTP reforms, FDI liberalization, and its historic impact on the Indian economy

🔍
Quick Reference
Key Facts & Contributions
Industrial Sector Part 2
Industrial Policy / ReformKey ProvisionsIndustrial Impact
Abolition of Industrial Licensing (De-licensing)The 1991 policy abolished compulsory licensing for all industries except 18 linked to strategic, health, and environmental concerns (currently limited to just 4-5).Ended the bureaucratic hurdles, artificial scarcities, and corruption of the License-Permit Raj; granted private enterprises full freedom for capacity expansion, diversification, and free market entry.
De-reservation of the Public SectorThe 17 major industries reserved for the government under the 1956 policy were reduced to 8 in 1991, and are currently restricted to just two sectors (Atomic Energy and Railway Operations).Paved the way for massive private and global capital investment in core sectors such as mining, steel, telecom, power, oil refining, heavy machinery, and civil aviation.
MRTP Act Reforms & Competition PolicyAbolition of prior-approval requirements and asset thresholds (₹100 crore threshold) for large firms under the MRTP Act 1969; replaced by the Competition Act in 2002.Freed Indian corporate houses to establish large-scale units and become globally competitive; shifted focus from penalizing size to protecting fair market competition against monopolies.
Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) LiberalizationAutomatic approval for up to 51% foreign equity across 34 high-priority industrial sectors; simplification of procedures for foreign technology agreements.Rapid inflow of foreign capital, advanced manufacturing technologies, and global management practices; integration with global value chains in automobiles, electronics, and pharmaceuticals.
De-reservation of Small Scale Industries (SSI)Phased elimination of the list of over 800 items exclusively reserved for small-scale industries, allowing large enterprises to enter production.Global improvement in product quality, advanced technology adoption, and economies of scale, though low-capital traditional domestic units faced intense competition.
Liberalization of Industrial Location PolicyAbolished central government approval for setting up factories in cities with populations under 10 lakh; restricted the 25 km periphery rule solely to polluting industries.Rapid establishment of industrial units in Tier-2, Tier-3 cities, and peri-urban/rural areas; expansion of industrial clusters and non-metropolitan employment.
Consumer Sovereignty & Market SupplyRemoval of production caps and capacity restrictions permanently ended decades-long waiting lists and artificial shortages in automobiles, scooters, telephones, and consumer goods.Flood of quality choices in the market, price competition, and a transformative improvement in consumers' standard of living and purchasing power.
Modern Regulatory Era (Ease of Doing Business)Transition from a Regulator State to a Facilitator State; National Single Window System (NSWS), Jan Vishwas Act, and decriminalization of minor corporate offences.Reduction in compliance burden, transparent digital approvals, and creation of an enabling environment for domestic and multinational manufacturing investment in India.

On which date was India's historic 'New Industrial Policy' (NIP) announced?

Which Union Minister formally presented the New Industrial Policy of 1991 in Parliament?
Prime Minister P. V. Narasimha Rao (as he concurrently held the portfolio of the Ministry of Industry).

Which severe macroeconomic crises compelled India to introduce the New Industrial Policy in July 1991?
An unprecedented Balance of Payments (BoP) crisis, foreign exchange reserves dropping to cover barely two weeks of imports, fiscal deficit crossing 8%, and severe double-digit inflation.

Prior to the 1991 policy, on which historic policy resolution was the ideological foundation of India's industrial development based?
On the Industrial Policy Resolution 1956 (IPR 1956), which was famously termed the 'Economic Constitution of India'.
Tap to view answer
Which sector was entrusted with leading economic development under the 1956 Industrial Policy?
The Public Sector; under the Nehru-Mahalanobis model, complete state dominance was established over heavy and basic industries.
Tap to view answer
What was the most central and epoch-making philosophy of the New Industrial Policy of 1991?
Dismantling the 'License-Permit-Quota Raj' and transforming the state's role from a 'rigid controller' into a 'development facilitator and catalyst'.
Tap to view answer
Which three foundational reform processes (the LPG Model) did the 1991 policy establish in the Indian economy?
Liberalization, Privatization, and Globalization.
Tap to view answer
What was the practical meaning of 'Liberalization' in the industrial sector under the 1991 policy?
Removing unnecessary administrative controls, licensing, price controls, quota systems, and government restrictions on capacity expansion.
Tap to view answer
What major steps were taken toward 'Privatization' under the 1991 policy?
Opening up industries previously reserved for the public sector to private capital and initiating disinvestment of government equity in Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs).
Tap to view answer
What did 'Globalization' signify in the context of the 1991 Industrial Policy?
Integrating Indian industries with the global economy through Foreign Direct Investment (FDI), foreign technology, and import liberalization.
Tap to view answer
Which Act laid the legal foundation for the 'License-Permit-Quota Raj' in independent India?
The Industries (Development and Regulation) Act, 1951 (IDRA 1951).
Tap to view answer
Under IDRA 1951, for which activities was a private entrepreneur required to obtain a prior government license?
Setting up a new factory, expanding the production capacity of an existing factory, manufacturing a new product, and changing the factory's location.
Tap to view answer
What famous name did Chakravarti Rajagopalachari (Rajaji) give to this regulatory regime in the late 1950s?
'License-Permit-Quota Raj'.
Tap to view answer
Which distorted economic behaviour—known in economics as 'Rent-Seeking'—reached its peak in India due to the License Raj?
Wasting capital and time on political and bureaucratic lobbying to secure government licenses, permits, and quotas instead of focusing on productive innovation and efficiency.
Tap to view answer
What type of manufacturing culture did the License Raj create in India?
An 'Economy of Scarcity' and a 'Seller's Market', where consumers were forced to buy expensive, poor-quality products out of helplessness.
Tap to view answer
What was the most glaring example of artificial scarcity of consumer goods during the License Raj?
Consumers had to endure waiting lists of 8 to 10 years just to get a Chetak scooter, a Fiat/Ambassador car, or a landline telephone connection.
Tap to view answer
What bold decision did the New Industrial Policy of 1991 take regarding 'Industrial Licensing' (De-licensing)?
It abolished compulsory licensing across the entire manufacturing sector in one stroke, retaining it for only 18 industries linked to security, environmental, and social concerns.
Tap to view answer
After the 1991 policy, what simple new procedure must de-licensed industries follow to establish a factory?
Filing an Industrial Entrepreneur Memorandum (IEM), which is merely an informational registration form submitted to the government rather than seeking permission.
Tap to view answer
Following periodic reductions in the licensed list since 1991, how many industries still require compulsory licensing today?
Currently, compulsory licensing is restricted to only 4 to 5 specific industries.

For which specific industries is Compulsory Industrial Licensing still mandatory in India?
1. Aerospace and defence equipment, 2. Industrial explosives, 3. Hazardous chemicals, 4. Cigars, cigarettes & tobacco products, and 5. Distillation of alcoholic beverages.

How many industries were included in Schedule 'A' reserved for the exclusive monopoly of the public sector under the 1956 Industrial Policy?
A total of 17 major strategic and basic industries.

To how many was the number of industries reserved for the public sector reduced from 17 by the policy of 24 July 1991?
Reduced to just 8.
Tap to view answer
How many industries remain exclusively reserved for the central government's public sector at present?
Only 2 industries: 1. Atomic Energy, and 2. Railway Operations (specific core operations).

Which heavy and basic industries saw the historic entry of the private sector following public sector de-reservation?
Telecommunications, civil aviation, power generation & distribution, oil refining & petroleum marketing, coal mining, ports, steel, and heavy machinery.

In which year was the Monopolies and Restrictive Trade Practices Act (MRTP Act) passed?
In 1969 (enforced from 1 June 1970).
Tap to view answer
What was the primary objective behind enacting the MRTP Act, 1969?
To prevent the undue concentration of economic power, curb monopolies, and prohibit unfair trade practices.
Tap to view answer
Prior to 1991, which companies were classified as 'MRTP Companies' and subjected to strict government controls?
Companies whose total assets exceeded ₹20 crore (raised to ₹100 crore in 1985).
Tap to view answer
How did the MRTP law cripple the productive capacity of large Indian corporate houses?
Large firms had to obtain separate prior approval from the government and the MRTP Commission to set up new factories, expand capacity, merge, or launch new products—approvals that were stalled for years.
Tap to view answer
What landmark reform did the New Industrial Policy of 1991 introduce regarding the MRTP Act?
It completely abolished the prior-approval system based on asset threshold limits, granting firms the freedom to achieve economies of scale.
Tap to view answer
Which new law suited to a modern market economy completely repealed and replaced the MRTP Act, 1969?
The Competition Act, 2002.
Tap to view answer
Which quasi-judicial regulatory body was established under the Competition Act, 2002 to replace the MRTP Commission?
The Competition Commission of India (CCI — established in 2003, fully operational since 2009).
Tap to view answer
What is the fundamental philosophical difference between the MRTP regime and the modern competition philosophy of the CCI?
MRTP viewed 'Big Size' itself with suspicion, whereas the CCI does not penalize size, but rather penalizes 'Abuse of Dominant Position' and anti-competitive 'Cartels'.
Tap to view answer
What was the general legal ceiling on Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) in India prior to the 1991 policy?
Generally capped at 40% under FERA 1973, and every foreign investment proposal required lengthy discretionary government approval.
Tap to view answer
What historic initiative did the New Industrial Policy of 1991 take regarding FDI?
It introduced 'Automatic Approval' for up to 51% foreign equity across 34 high-priority industrial sectors.
Tap to view answer
Which high-level body was constituted under the Prime Minister's Office (PMO) in 1991 for the speedy clearance of foreign investment proposals?
The Foreign Investment Promotion Board (FIPB).
Tap to view answer
In which year did the Government of India abolish the FIPB under the 'Minimum Government, Maximum Governance' principle?
In May 2017; thereafter, respective administrative ministries were empowered to directly process government-route proposals.
Tap to view answer
What relaxations did the 1991 policy provide regarding Foreign Technology Agreements?
Automatic government approval was granted for royalty payments up to 5% on domestic sales and 8% on exports, plus lump-sum technology transfer fees up to ₹1 crore in high-priority industries.
Tap to view answer
What was the 'Phased Manufacturing Programme' (PMP) in force before 1991 that was abolished in 1991?
A legal compulsion on foreign companies requiring them to mandatorily source or manufacture a specified percentage of components domestically in India over time.
Tap to view answer
What immediate benefit did the abolition of PMP bring to India's automobile and manufacturing sectors?
Foreign automakers (such as Hyundai, Suzuki, and Toyota) could immediately establish world-class plants in India without rigid local-content mandates.
Tap to view answer
What was the 'Small Scale Industries' (SSI) reservation system prior to the 1991 policy?
A system introduced in 1967 under which the manufacture of over 800 consumer items (footwear, toys, garments, utensils, stationery, etc.) was legally reserved exclusively for small-scale units.
Tap to view answer
What severe negative impact did SSI reservation have on the global competitiveness of Indian manufacturing?
Large Indian and foreign firms could not invest in these labour-intensive sectors, keeping units small and technologically outdated, while China captured global toy, textile, and footwear markets through scale.
Tap to view answer
How was the Small Scale Industries (SSI) reservation list phased out in India?
Based on the recommendations of the Abid Hussain Committee (1997), items were de-reserved in phases, and the reservation list was reduced to zero by April 2015.

Which comprehensive legislation was passed by Parliament in 2006 for the promotion of small industries?
The Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development Act, 2006 (MSMED Act, 2006).

Under the Atmanirbhar Bharat package, what new composite definition of MSMEs came into effect on 1 July 2020?
A dual composite criterion of investment in plant & machinery and annual turnover, completely eliminating the distinction between manufacturing and service enterprises.

Investment in plant & machinery less than ₹1 crore and annual turnover less than ₹5 crore.

Under the 2020 definition, what is the threshold for a 'Small Enterprise'?
Investment less than ₹10 crore and annual turnover less than ₹50 crore.

Investment less than ₹50 crore and annual turnover less than ₹250 crore.

India leaped from a country with just two outdated car models (Ambassador and Fiat) to become the world's third-largest passenger vehicle market and a global hub for auto component exports.

The monopoly of the state Department of Telecommunications (DoT) ended, private mobile operators entered, and India built one of the world's cheapest data markets and a network of 115 crore+ mobile connections.

Establishment of 'Consumer Sovereignty'—an abundance of quality products, competitive price reductions, and top priority to customer service.

Why was 'Jobless Growth' observed in industrial development after the 1991 reforms?

What is the final macroeconomic conclusion of the entire historical and policy analysis of Industrial Sector Part 2?
The 1991 New Industrial Policy broke the chains of the License-Permit Raj and gave wings to Indian entrepreneurship, propelling India to become the world's fifth-largest economic power; the challenge now is to establish India as a self-reliant global manufacturing superpower by coordinating modern infrastructure, semiconductors, green technologies, labour reforms, and strategies like PLI.
