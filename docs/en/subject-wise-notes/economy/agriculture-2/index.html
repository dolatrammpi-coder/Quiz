Key Facts & Contributions
Agriculture Part 2
Agricultural Reform / InitiativeKey FeatureImpact on Agriculture
First Green Revolution (1960s)A combined technological package of dwarf high-yielding variety (HYV) seeds, chemical fertilizers, and controlled water supply.Massive surge in wheat and rice production, attainment of foodgrain self-sufficiency, and an end to dependence on PL-480 foodgrain imports.
Tubewell-Based Groundwater Irrigation ExpansionDense proliferation of private tubewells and pumpsets facilitated by rural electrification and electricity subsidies.Enabled multi-cropping cycles and higher cropping intensity, but led to groundwater exploitation at alarming levels in Northwest India.
Bringing Green Revolution to Eastern India (BGREI)Scientific harnessing of abundant groundwater and fertile alluvial plains in eastern states (Bihar, West Bengal, Odisha, Assam, Eastern UP).Increased paddy productivity in Eastern India, decentralized rice production, and gradually reduced regional agricultural imbalances.
Agricultural Produce Market Committee (APMC) ActEstablishment of state-regulated physical mandis (yards) to protect farmers from exploitation by commission agents (arhatiyas) and middlemen.Provided initial transparency, but over time led to middlemen monopolies, cartelization, and high mandi cesses that hindered marketing.
National Agriculture Market (e-NAM) PlatformLinking APMC mandis nationwide through a common electronic trading portal to ensure transparent price discovery.Eliminated physical barriers, promoted inter-mandi and inter-state online trade, and offered farmers better price realization options.
Model Agricultural Produce and Livestock Marketing (APLM) Act, 2017Ending the APMC monopoly by permitting private wholesale mandis, direct-to-consumer marketing, and declaring warehouses as market sub-yards.Attracted private investment in agricultural marketing, fostered a competitive environment, and reduced farmers' rigid dependence on mandis.
Agriculture Infrastructure Fund (AIF - ₹1 Lakh Crore)Medium-to-long-term debt financing with a 3% interest subvention for post-harvest management, modern silos, cold chains, and primary processing centers.Reduced post-harvest losses, improved agricultural value addition, and curbed distress sales by farmers.
Farmer Producer Organization (FPO) Promotion PolicyOrganizing small and marginal farmers through the formation of 10,000 new FPOs to provide economies of scale.Lowered cultivation costs through collective input procurement, improved access to institutional credit, and strengthened bargaining power with corporate buyers.

What adverse impact did the Green Revolution have on pulses and coarse cereals?
Their acreage and production declined as farmers shifted their irrigated land toward more profitable wheat and paddy.

Soil salinization/alkalinization, depletion of micronutrients, and eutrophication of water bodies.

Why did income inequality in rural areas increase as a socio-economic impact of the Green Revolution?
Large farmers could afford expensive seeds, fertilizers, and tubewells and reaped huge profits, while resource-poor small farmers lagged behind.

As the 'Rise of Rural Capitalism' and class consolidation of rich farmers.

How did the Green Revolution affect the condition of tenant farmers?
With the sharp rise in land values and profits, landlords evicted sharecroppers and began self-cultivation using machines.

'Evergreen Revolution', which emphasizes sustainable increases in productivity without harming the environment.

Eastern and North-Eastern India, rainfed dryland areas, and pulses, oilseeds, coarse cereals, and organic farming.

What type of technical progress is the Green Revolution technology classified as in economics?
Land-saving and capital-intensive technology.

Why are High Yielding Variety (HYV) seeds called 'High Responsive Varieties' by scientists?
Because they deliver high yields only when supplied with precise quantities of chemical fertilizers and controlled water.

What happens to the productivity of HYV seeds if they do not receive adequate irrigation and fertilizers?
Their productivity falls even lower than that of traditional indigenous seeds, creating a risk of complete crop failure.
Tap to view answer
What kind of corporate investment did the Green Revolution accelerate in the agricultural input market?
It created a massive market for multinational and domestic companies manufacturing seeds, chemical fertilizers, pesticides, and tractors.
Tap to view answer
What trend was observed in fertilizer consumption during the Green Revolution?
A multi-fold increase in per-hectare fertilizer consumption, placing India among the world's top chemical fertilizer consumers.

Why is 'irrigation' considered the key catalyst in the success of the Green Revolution?
Because chemical fertilizers scorch plants without adequate and controlled moisture; water dissolves fertilizers and delivers them to the plants.
Tap to view answer
Why is the timing of irrigation critical in the Green Revolution compared to traditional rainfed agriculture?
In HYV crops, precise water availability is mandatory at critical stages like Crown Root Initiation (CRI), tillering, and grain filling.
Tap to view answer
Which mode of irrigation saw the fastest and most widespread expansion in Northwest India during the Green Revolution?
Private tubewells and diesel/electric pumpsets.
Tap to view answer
Why did farmers in Green Revolution states prefer tubewell irrigation over canal irrigation?
Because a farmer has personal control over a tubewell, enabling immediate and precise water supply as needed.
Tap to view answer
What was the main policy reason behind the unchecked expansion of tubewells in the Green Revolution heartlands of Punjab and Haryana?
Free or heavily subsidized flat-rate electricity supply for agriculture.

According to the Central Ground Water Board (CGWB), what is the current status of most development blocks in Northwest India?
Over-exploited (Dark Zones), where the extraction rate exceeds 100% compared to groundwater recharge.
Tap to view answer
What devastating effect did excessive and uncontrolled canal over-irrigation have on the soil?
Waterlogging and soil salinization/alkalinization, turning millions of hectares of fertile land into barren/usar land.

Into which three categories are irrigation projects classified based on their Culturable Command Area (CCA)?
1. Major (more than 10,000 hectares CCA), 2. Medium (2,000 to 10,000 hectares CCA), and 3. Minor (less than 2,000 hectares CCA).
Tap to view answer
How did multipurpose major projects like Bhakra-Nangal and the Indira Gandhi Canal accelerate the Green Revolution?
By ensuring perennial water flow in the arid and semi-arid regions of Punjab, Haryana, and Rajasthan.
Tap to view answer
In which year was the Command Area Development Programme (CADP) launched?
In 1974-75, with the objective of bridging the gap between irrigation potential created and its actual utilization.
Tap to view answer
Which programme was launched in 1996-97 to expedite the completion of stalled major and medium irrigation projects?
Accelerated Irrigation Benefits Programme (AIBP).

In which year was the Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana (PMKSY) launched?
On 1 July 2015, with the motto 'Har Khet Ko Pani' (Water to Every Field) and 'Per Drop More Crop'.
