Poverty and Inequality Part 1

Introduction to poverty and inequality, multidimensional concept of poverty, historical and current poverty line methodologies, absolute vs. relative poverty, and socio-economic impacts

🔍
Quick Reference
Key Facts & Contributions
Poverty and Inequality Part 1
Poverty / Inequality ConceptKey FeatureSocio-Economic Impact
Absolute PovertyView Answer A condition characterized by the deprivation of basic human needs required for subsistence (minimum calories, clean water, clothing, shelter).View Answer Leads to malnutrition, rising child mortality, reduced life expectancy, and the creation of an intergenerational vicious cycle of poverty.
Relative PovertyView Answer Comparative disparity in standard of living, income, and consumption expenditure among different classes or income groups within a society.View Answer Causes social exclusion, a psychological sense of deprivation, inequality of opportunity, and growing socio-economic discontent.
Poverty LineView Answer The threshold level of minimum basic consumption expenditure or income that classifies a person as poor or non-poor.View Answer Guides beneficiary selection for the Targeted Public Distribution System (TPDS), welfare schemes, and allocation of fiscal resources.
Headcount Ratio (HCR)View Answer The percentage proportion of the total population living below the poverty line.View Answer Serves as the primary measure of the incidence of poverty and helps evaluate government poverty alleviation programmes.
Poverty Gap Index (PGI)View Answer Measurement of the average shortfall between the average consumption expenditure of the poor and the prescribed poverty line.View Answer Assesses the depth (intensity) of poverty and determines the total financial resources needed to lift all the poor above the poverty line.
Squared Poverty GapView Answer Weighted average of the squared consumption gaps among the poor, giving greater weight to the most severely deprived individuals.View Answer Identifies internal inequality among the poor and highlights the most severely affected ultra-poor segment.
Income InequalityView Answer Unequal and imbalanced distribution of total national income across different income classes and population groups in an economy.View Answer Concentrates the gains of economic growth, hinders social mobility, contracts aggregate demand, and poses risks of political instability.
Multidimensional PovertyView Answer Simultaneous evaluation of non-monetary deprivations such as health, education, nutrition, sanitation, housing, and drinking water beyond mere income.View Answer Identifies holistic barriers to human capital development and enables the formulation of coordinated multi-sectoral development policies.

One-Liner Questions

What is the standard and multidimensional definition of 'Poverty' in macroeconomics?
A socio-economic condition in which an individual or household is unable to meet the minimum basic necessities of life—such as adequate nutrition, clean drinking water, clothing, safe housing, basic healthcare, and education.

How did Nobel Laureate Prof. Amartya Sen redefine poverty?
As 'Capability Deprivation' (Poverty as Capability Deprivation); meaning not merely a lack of income, but the absence of real opportunities and freedoms in a person's life.
Tap to view answer
What is meant by 'Inequality' in an economy?
The unequal and imbalanced distribution of income, wealth, consumption, and economic opportunities among different individuals, households, or social groups in society.
Tap to view answer
What is the key conceptual difference between poverty and inequality?
Poverty reflects a state of deprivation from a minimum standard of living, whereas inequality measures the comparative gap (disparity) between different classes within society.
Tap to view answer
Can inequality exist in a country even without poverty?
Yes; in developed nations (such as the US or Western Europe), absolute poverty may be nearly eliminated, yet massive income and wealth inequality can persist between the top 1% and the bottom 50%.
Tap to view answer
What is the authoritative definition of the 'Poverty Line'?
The minimum standard level of per capita monthly or daily consumption expenditure (or income) required to maintain a minimally acceptable standard of living, dividing the population into poor and non-poor.
Tap to view answer
Why is the poverty line in India primarily determined on the basis of consumption expenditure rather than income?
Because heavy dependence on the unorganized sector and agriculture causes sharp seasonal fluctuations in income, whereas consumption expenditure is more stable, reliable, and measurable.
Tap to view answer
Which survey forms the basis for collecting official poverty data in India?
The 'Household Consumption Expenditure Survey' (HCES) conducted by the National Statistical Office (NSO, formerly NSSO).
Tap to view answer
What does the 'Headcount Ratio' (HCR) measure?
The percentage proportion of the population living below the prescribed poverty line relative to the country's total population.
Tap to view answer
What is the most prominent statistical limitation of the Headcount Ratio (HCR)?
It only counts the number of poor people; it does not show how far below the poverty line a poor person falls (ignoring the depth of poverty).
Tap to view answer
What does the 'Poverty Gap Index' (PGI) represent in economics?
The ratio of the average gap between the mean consumption level of the poor and the poverty line; it measures the intensity or depth of poverty.
Tap to view answer
If a country's PGI is zero (0), what does it signify?
No person in the country is below the poverty line (zero poverty).
Tap to view answer
What is the utility of the 'Squared Poverty Gap' (Poverty Severity Index)?
It measures consumption inequality among the poor themselves and assigns greater weight to the consumption shortfalls of the poorest (ultra-poor) individuals.
Tap to view answer
Which three variables are integrated into the Sen Index of Poverty?
1. Headcount Ratio (incidence of poverty), 2. Poverty Gap (depth of poverty), and 3. Gini Coefficient of income distribution among the poor (inequality among the poor).
Tap to view answer
What is the standard definition of 'Absolute Poverty'?
The complete absence of basic biological and physical necessities of life measured against a fixed, unchanging minimum physical standard (such as minimum calories or purchasing power).
Tap to view answer
In which countries is the concept of absolute poverty primarily more relevant?
In Developing and Least Developed Countries, where hunger, malnutrition, and homelessness are primary challenges.
Tap to view answer
What is the authoritative definition of 'Relative Poverty'?
A condition where an individual's or group's income and consumption are low compared to the average or median standard of living in society; it inherently reflects income inequality.
Tap to view answer
In the analysis of which countries is the concept of relative poverty considered central?
In developed and high-income nations (such as OECD countries), where a person may not be hungry yet remains deprived of the normal standard of living in society.
Tap to view answer
At what level is the relative poverty line generally set in the European Union (EU) and OECD countries?
At 50% or 60% of the National Median Disposable Income.
Tap to view answer
Can relative poverty increase in an economy even when economic growth occurs?
Yes; if the gains of growth disproportionately accrue to high-income groups and median income rises rapidly while the income of the bottom tier grows slowly.
Tap to view answer
What is the World Bank's current 'Extreme International Poverty Line'?
$2.15 per person per day (at 2017 Purchasing Power Parity - PPP prices; previously $1.90).
Tap to view answer
Which poverty line has the World Bank recommended for Lower-Middle Income countries?
$3.65 per person per day (at 2017 PPP $).
Tap to view answer
What is the standard poverty line set by the World Bank for Upper-Middle Income countries?
$6.85 per person per day (at 2017 PPP $).

Who made the first pre-independence estimate of the 'Poverty Line' in India, and on what concept was it based?
Dadabhai Naoroji; in 1867-68, based on the 'Jail Cost of Living' (₹16 to ₹35 per capita per annum).
Tap to view answer
What statistical adjustment did Dadabhai Naoroji make while converting the jail cost of living into a poverty line for general society?
Accounting for the lower consumption needs of children and the elderly in the population, he took three-fourths (3/4) of the jail ration cost as the general poverty line.
Tap to view answer
What standard for a minimum living wage was set by the 'National Planning Committee' constituted in 1938?
Headed by Pandit Jawaharlal Nehru, the committee set a minimum income target of ₹15 to ₹25 per capita per month.
Tap to view answer
What minimum per capita annual consumption expenditure limit was proposed by leading industrialists in the 'Bombay Plan' of 1944?
₹75 per capita per annum at 1938-39 prices.
Tap to view answer
Which was the first official expert working group constituted by the Government of India after independence to determine the poverty line?
The Planning Commission's Working Group of 1962.
Tap to view answer
What poverty line did the 1962 Planning Commission Working Group recommend for rural and urban areas?
₹20 per capita per month for rural areas and ₹25 per capita per month for urban areas (at 1960-61 prices).
Tap to view answer
Which historic scientific criterion did V. M. Dandekar and Nilakantha Rath introduce in poverty measurement in 1971?
The calorie consumption norm, setting a minimum nutritional level of 2,250 calories per day for both rural and urban areas.
Tap to view answer
According to the Dandekar and Rath study (1971), what percentage of India's rural and urban population was poor in 1960-61?
Approximately 40% of the rural population and 50% of the urban population.
Tap to view answer
What basis did the Dr. Y. K. Alagh Task Force, constituted by the Planning Commission in 1977, establish for the poverty line?
Minimum daily nutritional requirements: 2,400 calories per person per day for rural areas and 2,100 calories per person per day for urban areas.
Tap to view answer
Why did the Alagh Task Force set different norms of 2,400 calories for rural areas and 2,100 calories for urban areas?
Because the rural population requires more energy for agriculture and manual labour, whereas urban lifestyles involve relatively less physical labour.
Tap to view answer
According to the Alagh Committee (1979 report), what were the monetary rural and urban poverty lines at 1973-74 prices?
₹49.09 per capita per month for rural areas and ₹56.64 per capita per month for urban areas.
Tap to view answer
Which committee constituted by the Planning Commission in September 1989 recommended state-specific poverty lines?
The Prof. D. T. Lakdawala Expert Committee (submitted its report in 1993).
Tap to view answer
Which price indices did the Lakdawala Committee suggest using to update the poverty line over time?
'Consumer Price Index for Agricultural Labourers' (CPI-AL) for rural areas and 'Consumer Price Index for Industrial Workers' (CPI-IW) for urban areas.
Tap to view answer
What was the biggest limitation of the Lakdawala methodology that was later rectified?
It was anchored to the outdated 1973-74 consumption basket and assumed that the government would provide health and education free of cost, whereas private household expenditure on these had risen significantly.
Tap to view answer
Which committee constituted by the Planning Commission in December 2005 brought a revolutionary change to poverty measurement methodology in India?
The Prof. Suresh Tendulkar Expert Committee (submitted its report in November 2009).
Tap to view answer
What were the three most significant policy and statistical changes introduced by the Suresh Tendulkar Committee?
1. Moving away from rigid calorie consumption norms, 2. Including private expenditure on health and education, and 3. Adopting a uniform reference consumption basket for both rural and urban areas.
Tap to view answer
Which reference basket did the Tendulkar Committee standardize at the all-India level?
It adopted the 1993-94 Urban Consumption Basket as the standard and applied price adjustments for rural areas.
Tap to view answer
According to the Tendulkar Committee, what were the rural and urban poverty lines at 2011-12 prices?
₹816 per capita per month (approx. ₹27.20 per day) for rural areas and ₹1,000 per capita per month (approx. ₹33.33 per day) for urban areas.
Tap to view answer
Based on the Tendulkar methodology, what was the total poverty headcount ratio (HCR) estimated in India in 2011-12?
21.9% (25.7% in rural areas and 13.7% in urban areas — totaling approximately 26.98 crore poor).
Tap to view answer
Based on the Tendulkar Committee estimates, how much decline in the poverty rate was recorded in India between 2004-05 and 2011-12?
From 37.2% down to 21.9% (a historic drop of about 15.3 percentage points, declining at an annual rate of 2.18%).
Tap to view answer
What major controversy and public criticism arose regarding the Tendulkar Committee report?
That the thresholds of ₹27 and ₹33 per day were excessively low, making it impossible for anyone to live a dignified human life on that amount.
Tap to view answer
Under whose chairmanship was a high-level expert committee formed by the Planning Commission in June 2012 to review the Tendulkar methodology?
Under the chairmanship of Dr. C. Rangarajan (former Governor, RBI).
Tap to view answer
Which three scientific nutritional elements did the C. Rangarajan Committee (Report: June 2014) use as its basis for minimum nutrition?
1. Calories (Rural: 2,155 kcal, Urban: 2,090 kcal), 2. Protein (Rural: 48g, Urban: 50g), and 3. Fat (Rural: 26g, Urban: 28g).
Tap to view answer
According to the Rangarajan Committee, what were the all-India rural and urban poverty lines set at 2011-12 prices?
₹972 per capita per month (approx. ₹32.40 per day) for rural areas and ₹1,407 per capita per month (approx. ₹46.90 per day) for urban areas.
Tap to view answer
According to the Rangarajan methodology, what was the total poverty ratio estimated in India in 2011-12?
29.5% (Rural: 30.9% and Urban: 26.4% — totaling approximately 36.3 crore poor).
Tap to view answer
What was the main difference between the poverty estimates of the Tendulkar Committee and the Rangarajan Committee?
Due to the higher poverty line threshold in the Rangarajan methodology, the number of poor was estimated to be about 9.3 crore higher (29.5%) than under the Tendulkar methodology (21.9%).
Tap to view answer
Did the Government of India officially accept the recommendations of the Rangarajan Committee?
No; in official policy contexts, the Tendulkar Committee's poverty line continued to be treated as the benchmark.
Tap to view answer
Who chaired the 'Task Force on Elimination of Poverty' constituted by NITI Aayog in 2015?
Dr. Arvind Panagariya (the then Vice-Chairperson of NITI Aayog).
Tap to view answer
What was the key recommendation of the Panagariya Task Force on the future determination of the poverty line in India?
That the poverty line is merely a metric to track the progress of welfare programmes; instead, focus should be placed on identifying multidimensional deprivations and targeted social security.
Tap to view answer
What is the 'Uniform Recall Period' (URP) used in household consumption surveys?
When expenditure data for all food and non-food consumption items are collected for a uniform period of the past 30 days preceding the survey date.
Tap to view answer
What is the 'Mixed Recall Period' (MRP) adopted by the Tendulkar Committee?
A 365-day recall period for 5 infrequently purchased non-food items (clothing, footwear, durables, education, institutional health) and a 30-day recall period for all other items.
Tap to view answer
What is the special feature of the 'Modified Mixed Recall Period' (MMRP) adopted by the Rangarajan Committee?
Collecting data over a 7-day recall period for perishable food items (vegetables, fruits, milk, eggs), 365 days for durables, and 30 days for all other items.

Who propounded the theory of the 'Vicious Circle of Poverty' in economics?
Prof. Ragnar Nurkse, in his book 'Problems of Capital Formation in Underdeveloped Countries' (1953).
Tap to view answer
According to Ragnar Nurkse, what is the 'Supply Side' cycle of the vicious circle of poverty?
Low income → Low savings → Low capital formation/investment → Capital deficiency → Low productivity → Back to low income.
Tap to view answer
According to Ragnar Nurkse, what is the 'Demand Side' cycle of the vicious circle of poverty?
Low income → Low purchasing power (limited market demand) → Lack of incentive to invest → Low production capacity → Low productivity → Back to low income.
Tap to view answer
What was Nurkse's famous quote illustrating the nature of poverty?
'A country is poor because it is poor'.
Tap to view answer
Which key policy approach did economists advocate to break the vicious circle of poverty?
Large-scale public capital investment in infrastructure, education, and health (Big Push Theory) alongside human capital development.
Tap to view answer
What is meant by 'Chronic Poverty' in an economy?
Individuals or households that remain persistently below the poverty line across generations and are unable to escape it.
Tap to view answer
What is 'Transient Poverty'?
People who generally hover around the poverty line and temporarily slip below it due to illness, crop failure, or job loss, and then rise back above it.
Tap to view answer
Who are referred to as the 'Churning Poor'?
Workers or small farmers who regularly move in and out of the poverty line (such as seasonal agricultural labourers).
Tap to view answer
Who developed the 'Global Multidimensional Poverty Index' (Global MPI) promoted by the United Nations?
The Oxford Poverty and Human Development Initiative (OPHI) and UNDP in 2010.
Tap to view answer
On which three core dimensions is the Global Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI) based?
1. Health, 2. Education, and 3. Standard of Living.
Tap to view answer
How many total indicators are under the Global MPI, and what is the weightage of each dimension?
A total of 10 indicators; and all three dimensions are given an equal one-third (1/3) weightage.
Tap to view answer
Under the Global MPI, when is a person classified as 'multidimensionally poor'?
When the person is simultaneously deprived in 33.3% (one-third) or more of the total weighted indicators.
Tap to view answer
If a person is deprived in 20% to 33.3% of indicators, how are they classified in the MPI?
'Vulnerable to Poverty'.
Tap to view answer
If a person is deprived in 50% or more of indicators, in which category are they placed?
'Severe Multidimensional Poverty'.
Tap to view answer
Which two new indicators were added to the 10 global indicators in NITI Aayog's 'National Multidimensional Poverty Index' (National MPI)?
1. Antenatal Care (under the Health dimension), and 2. Bank Account (under the Standard of Living dimension).
Tap to view answer
From which national survey is the data for the 12 indicators in NITI Aayog's National MPI sourced?
The National Family Health Survey (NFHS).
Tap to view answer
According to NITI Aayog's report (2024), what historic shift occurred in multidimensional poverty in India between 2013-14 and 2022-23?
Multidimensional poverty fell from 29.17% to 11.28%, and approximately 24.82 crore people escaped poverty.
Tap to view answer
According to NITI Aayog, which Indian state recorded the highest net decline in the number of multidimensionally poor?
Uttar Pradesh (approximately 5.94 crore people escaped multidimensional poverty).
Tap to view answer
What is the structural difference between the root causes of poverty in rural and urban areas of India?
Rural poverty is linked to landlessness, monsoon uncertainty, and disguised unemployment; whereas urban poverty is tied to the informal workforce, slums, lack of skills, and high cost of living.
Tap to view answer
What does the 'Feminization of Poverty' signify?
The disproportionate and excessive share of women in the total poor population, driven by wage discrimination, lack of property rights, and unpaid domestic burdens.

Which is the most famous geometric curve used to measure income inequality in an economy?
The Lorenz Curve (developed by Max O. Lorenz in 1905).
Tap to view answer
Between which two cumulative percentage variables does the Lorenz Curve show a relationship?
Between the cumulative percentage of the population and the cumulative percentage of total income received by them.
Tap to view answer
What is the 45-degree straight diagonal line in a Lorenz Curve called?
The 'Line of Perfect Equality'.
Tap to view answer
What does it indicate when the actual Lorenz Curve bows further away from the 45-degree line?
That income inequality in society is correspondingly higher and more severe.
Tap to view answer
By which coefficient is the numerical mathematical measurement of income inequality based on the Lorenz Curve performed?
The Gini Coefficient (developed by Corrado Gini in 1912).
Tap to view answer
What is the geometric formula for calculating the Gini Coefficient?
$Gini = A / (A + B)$ (where A = area between the line of perfect equality and the Lorenz curve, and B = area under the Lorenz curve).
Tap to view answer
Between what range does the numerical value of the Gini Coefficient lie?
Between 0 and 1 (or 0% to 100% in percentage terms).
Tap to view answer
What does a Gini Coefficient value of zero (0) signify?
'Perfect Income Equality', where every individual in society earns the exact same income.
Tap to view answer
What does a Gini Coefficient value of one (1) signify?
'Perfect Income Inequality', where the entire national income of the country is concentrated in the hands of a single individual and everyone else earns zero.
Tap to view answer
Generally, what does a Gini Coefficient above 0.40 indicate for a country?
Severe and alarming income inequality (High Inequality Alert Line).
Tap to view answer
What is the 'Palma Ratio' for measuring income inequality?
The ratio of the total national income share of the richest top 10% of society to that of the poorest bottom 40%.
Tap to view answer
On which finding by economist Gabriel Palma is the conceptual basis of the Palma Ratio built?
That the middle 50% of the population almost always retains a stable ~50% share of national income; the real disparity lies between the top 10% and the bottom 40%.
Tap to view answer
What relationship between economic growth and inequality does the 'Kuznets Curve', propounded by Simon Kuznets, describe?
In the initial stages of development, income inequality rises with economic growth, and after reaching a certain income level, inequality begins to decline (an inverted U-shaped curve).
Tap to view answer
Why does the Kuznets Curve hypothesis appear valid in the initial stages of developing countries?
Because at the onset of industrialization, the incomes of modern capitalists and skilled urban workers rise rapidly, while rural agricultural workers are left behind.
Tap to view answer
Why did Thomas Piketty refute the Kuznets Curve in his book 'Capital in the Twenty-First Century'?
He proved using historical data that when the rate of return on capital (r) exceeds the rate of economic growth (g) ($r > g$), inequality in modern capitalism does not fall automatically but continues to widen.

According to the 'World Inequality Report 2022' released by the World Inequality Lab, what was the state of income concentration in India?
The top 10% in India hold approximately 57% of total national income, while the bottom 50% hold only 13%.
Tap to view answer
What is meant by 'Inequality of Opportunity'?
When an individual's success is determined not by their talent or hard work, but by the circumstances of their birth (caste, religion, gender, parental wealth).
Tap to view answer
What negative impact does extreme income inequality have on macroeconomic growth?
It erodes the purchasing power of the majority, contracting Aggregate Demand and slowing down industrial growth.
Tap to view answer
Which is the foremost fiscal instrument used to reduce inequality?
'Progressive Taxation', where higher tax rates are levied on high-income earners and the revenue is spent on free education, healthcare, and social security for the poor.
Tap to view answer
Why are indirect taxes (such as GST) considered inherently 'Regressive'?
Because both the rich and the poor pay the exact same tax when buying soap or oil, which represents a much larger percentage of a poor person's total income.

What have been the three components of the 'Three-pronged Strategy' for poverty alleviation in India?
1. Growth-oriented approach (accelerated overall economic growth), 2. Targeted poverty alleviation programmes, and 3. Minimum basic services and social security.
Tap to view answer
What is the role of the 'Public Distribution System' (PDS / NFSA 2013) in poverty alleviation?
Protecting over 81 crore eligible citizens from hunger and nutritional insecurity by guaranteeing free or heavily subsidized foodgrains.

Which is the world's largest legislation providing livelihood security through employment guarantee in rural areas?
The Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (MGNREGA, 2005 — guaranteeing 100 days of unskilled wage employment per household).
Tap to view answer
Which historic Indian scheme freed the poor from the exploitation of moneylenders through financial inclusion?
The Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana (PMJDY, by opening over 50 crore bank accounts).
Tap to view answer
What risk does 'Politics of the Poverty Line' pose in determining poverty thresholds?
Governments artificially setting the poverty line at an excessively low level to claim a rapid reduction in poverty while ground realities remain unchanged.
Tap to view answer
What is the final and concise macroeconomic conclusion of the complete study of Poverty and Inequality Part 1?
Poverty is not merely a lack of basic calories or income, but a contraction of human dignity, choices, and capabilities; while absolute poverty can be eradicated through targeted social security, housing, sanitation, and employment saturation, bridging economic inequality through progressive taxation, quality public education and healthcare, and equality of opportunity is an indispensable prerequisite for building an inclusive and just nation.
