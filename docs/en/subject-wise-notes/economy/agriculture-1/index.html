Agriculture Part 1

In-depth analysis of key features of Indian agriculture, importance in the economy, size and fragmentation of landholdings, and cropping patterns

Quick Reference
Key Facts & Contributions
Agriculture Part 1
Agricultural TopicKey CharacteristicImportance in the Economy
Monsoon Dependence & Rain-Fed AgricultureAbout 50-51% of the country's total net sown area still depends entirely on the South-West monsoon for irrigation.Monsoon failure directly destabilizes rural demand, foodgrain supply, and the overall economic growth rate (GDP).
Overwhelming Dominance of Marginal & Small HoldingsAccording to the Agriculture Census 2015-16, 86.2% of total operational holdings are small and marginal holdings below 2 hectares.The biggest structural barrier to achieving Economies of Scale and spreading modern mechanization in agriculture.
Disguised UnemploymentEngagement of more labour force in farming than required, resulting in near-zero Marginal Productivity of additional workers.Decline in labour productivity and rural per capita income; underscores the urgent need to shift labour to manufacturing and services.
Structural AsymmetryAgriculture's share in GDP (GVA) has fallen to ~16-18%, whereas around 45-46% of the country's workforce depends on it for livelihood.Generates severe income inequality between agricultural and non-agricultural sectors, which is a root cause of rural poverty and indebtedness.
Green Revolution-Driven Wheat-Rice Cropping PatternIntensive monoculture of wheat and paddy in North-West India driven by Minimum Support Price (MSP) and assured government procurement.Made the country self-sufficient in foodgrains, but created groundwater crisis, soil degradation, and a heavy import bill for pulses and oilseeds.
Gradual Shift from Subsistence to Commercial AgricultureMoving away from traditional subsistence farming toward expanding high-value crops (horticulture, fruits & vegetables, dairy, and cash crops).Main foundation for farm income diversification, rural value addition, and rapid expansion of the Food Processing Industry (FPI).
Agricultural Exports & Trade SurplusIndia ranks among the world's top agricultural exporters (rice, marine products, spices, sugar, etc.) and remains a consistent net exporter.Significant contribution to foreign exchange earnings and helps balance the Current Account Deficit (CAD).
Revival of Coarse Cereals (Shree Anna) & DiversificationPolicy push for production and consumption of low-water, climate-resilient, and nutrient-rich nutri-cereals (Bajra, Jowar, Ragi).Mitigates climate change risks, strengthens national nutritional security, and boosts incomes of farmers in dryland regions.

One-Liner Questions

Why is Indian agriculture traditionally called a 'Gamble of Monsoon'?
Because around 50-51% of the country's net sown area still depends on rainfall for irrigation, making agricultural output directly vulnerable to monsoon fluctuations.

What is the concept of 'Disguised Unemployment' in economics?
A situation where more workers are engaged in an activity than needed, such that removing a few workers causes no negative impact on total output (marginal productivity is zero).
Tap to view answer
What is meant by Subsistence Farming in Indian agriculture?
A farming system in which the farmer's primary objective is not to earn profit in the market, but to grow foodgrains to feed their own family.
Tap to view answer
How does Indian agriculture's Yield per Hectare compare with global standards?
It is significantly lower than in China, the USA, and European countries, due to small landholdings, limited mechanization, and imbalanced input use.

It contributes around 30% to agricultural GVA, providing stable and regular income security to farmers in the event of crop failure.

What is the root cause of 'Low Labour Productivity' in Indian agriculture?
