Banking Reforms Part 1

In-depth analysis of the need for banking reforms in India, Narasimham Committees (I & II), Non-Performing Assets (NPAs), and IRAC norms

🔍
Quick Reference
Key Facts & Contributions
Banking Reforms Part 1
Reform MeasureKey ProvisionsImpact on the Banking Sector
Phased Reduction in Reserve Ratios (CRR & SLR)View Answer On the recommendation of the Narasimham Committee-I, reducing CRR from 15% to 3–5% and SLR from 38.5% to 25% (currently 18%).View Answer Reduction in financial pre-emption of banks, availability of substantial liquidity for commercial credit disbursal, and improved profitability.
Deregulation of Interest RatesView Answer Abolition of complex administrative interest controls on deposits and advances; rates became market-driven except for savings bank accounts and select priority sector rates.View Answer Development of healthy price competition among banks, transparent determination of Net Interest Margin (NIM), and market-based returns for depositors.
Income Recognition and Asset Classification (IRAC Norms)View Answer Abandoning the accrual basis to record income only on actual cash realization; scientific classification of assets into 4 categories.View Answer Hidden NPAs came to light, transparency was restored to bank balance sheets, and alignment with international financial reporting standards was achieved.
Capital Adequacy Ratio (CRAR / Basel Norms)View Answer Mandatory maintenance of a minimum 8% Capital to Risk-Weighted Assets Ratio (later raised to 9–10% by Narasimham-II).View Answer Strengthened financial solvency and capital base of banks; developed resilience to absorb economic shocks and heavy loan defaults.
Entry of New Private Sector Banks (1993)View Answer Licensing of new private banks such as HDFC, ICICI, and UTI (Axis) under strict regulatory conditions and a minimum capital requirement of ₹100 crore.View Answer Unprecedented revolution in technology, CBS (Core Banking), ATMs, customer-centric services, and operational efficiency in the banking sector.
Establishment of Debt Recovery Tribunals (DRTs) (1993)View Answer Dedicated tribunals under the RDDBFI Act, 1993 for speedy judicial recovery of banks' stressed loans exceeding ₹10 lakh (currently ₹20 lakh).View Answer Relief from decades-long litigation in traditional civil courts; statutory backing for expeditious NPA recovery.
Liberalization of Branch Licensing PolicyView Answer Autonomy for banks to open new commercial centers and relocate loss-making branches based on operational needs and profitability.View Answer Reduction in banks' operating costs, rationalized branch networks in urban and semi-urban areas, and improved commercial viability.
Rationalization of Priority Sector Lending (PSL)View Answer Retaining the mandatory 40% share of total bank credit for agriculture, MSMEs, and weaker sections, while making sub-targets and eligible categories flexible and practical.View Answer Balance between social banking and commercial profitability; control over the accumulation of bad loans (NPAs) in the priority sector.

One-Liner Questions

What was the primary objective behind the nationalization of 14 major private commercial banks in India in 1969?
To democratize banking services, break the monopoly of large industrial houses, and extend institutional credit to agriculture, small-scale industries, and rural areas.

How many private banks were nationalized in the second phase of bank nationalization in 1980?
6 major commercial banks (whose total Net Demand and Time Liabilities exceeded ₹200 crore).
Tap to view answer
What technical term do economists use to describe the state of the Indian banking system prior to 1991?
'Financial Repression', where the state arbitrarily extracted banking resources through strict administrative controls.
Tap to view answer
According to economists Ronald McKinnon and Edward Shaw, what is the primary impact of 'Financial Repression' on an economy?
Financial intermediation weakens, real interest rates turn negative, and there is a severe shortage of capital for productive investment.
Tap to view answer
What did 'Pre-emption of Funds' of banks mean prior to 1991?
More than half of banks' total deposits (over 53.5%) being blocked by the government at low interest rates through excessively high CRR and SLR to finance its fiscal deficit.
Tap to view answer
What extreme levels had the Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR) and Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) reached in 1990-91?
SLR had reached an all-time peak of around 38.5% and CRR 15% (total pre-emption of 53.5%).
Tap to view answer
What was the status of the yield earned by banks on SLR securities prior to 1991?
Banks received administered interest rates significantly below market rates, forcing them to incur heavy financial losses on mandatory lending to the government.
Tap to view answer
What was the system of interest rate determination in India prior to 1991?
Administered Interest Rate Regime, where interest rates on savings, term deposits, and loans were fixed by the RBI in complex slabs rather than by market demand and supply.
Tap to view answer
What adverse impact did the administered interest rate system have on banks' profitability?
The spread between the cost of deposits and the yield on advances shrank, leaving banks' operational profitability negligible.
Tap to view answer
Which politically pressured events organized in the 1980s severely damaged the asset quality of banks?
'Loan Melas', where loans were distributed on political grounds without creditworthiness checks or collateral, leading to a complete collapse in recovery.
Tap to view answer
Which opaque system was prevalent in Indian banks prior to 1991 to measure asset quality?
The 'Health Code System' (classified into 1 to 8 categories), under which banks concealed bad loans and continued adding notional interest income on them.
Tap to view answer
What was the main flaw of the 'Accrual Basis of Accounting' followed by banks prior to 1991?
Banks recorded uncollected and uncertain interest as income, allowing even loss-making banks to show hefty profits on paper.
Tap to view answer
To what level had the net profitability (Return on Assets - RoA) of public sector banks fallen in 1990-91?
Less than 0.15% (with several major banks suffering heavy real losses).
Tap to view answer
What strict regulatory rule governed the expansion of bank branches prior to 1991?
A rigid branch licensing system under which banks could not open any new branch without prior RBI approval and were mandated to open rural branches in a 4:1 ratio.
Tap to view answer
What was the impact of excessive political and bureaucratic interference on the management of public sector banks?
Commercial autonomy disappeared, crony capitalism flourished in credit allocation, and accountability in credit risk assessment vanished.
Tap to view answer
Why did banks lack price competition to attract customers prior to 1991?
Because deposit and lending rates for all banks were uniformly fixed by the RBI, and the presence of the private sector was negligible.
Tap to view answer
How was the quality of customer service in the Indian banking sector prior to 1991?
Extremely poor and sluggish, characterized by paper ledger entries, weeks of delay in cheque clearing, and a complete absence of modern technology.
Tap to view answer
Which committees' stringent norms governed working capital loans extended by banks to the industrial sector prior to 1991?
The Tandon Committee (1975) and Chore Committee (1979) norms, which imposed excessive administrative restrictions on credit limits.
Tap to view answer
What was the national target fixed for Priority Sector Lending (PSL) prior to 1991?
40% of total bank credit (for agriculture, weaker sections, small exporters, etc.).
Tap to view answer
How did the rigidity of PSL targets and a weak recovery mechanism affect banks' balance sheets?
A massive pile of stressed loans (NPAs) accumulated in the priority sector, for which banks had no capital buffer to absorb losses.
Tap to view answer
What was the capital adequacy status of Indian banks prior to 1991?
The capital base was extremely weak; banks held only 1% to 2% real capital against their risk-weighted assets.
Tap to view answer
What was the biggest legal hurdle in the judicial recovery of loans in India prior to 1991?
Lack of dedicated recovery legislation; banks had to approach traditional civil courts where cases took 15 to 20 years to settle.
Tap to view answer
Did banks have the authority to directly seize pledged collateral prior to 1991?
No; without a court order, banks could neither seize nor sell any mortgaged property.
Tap to view answer
What was the main cause of 'Systemic Risk' in the Indian banking system in the late 1980s?
Most public sector banks were on the verge of insolvency if evaluated under international accounting standards.
Tap to view answer
What was the stance of bank employee trade unions toward computerization of banks prior to 1991?
Stiff opposition; unions feared that computers would lead to massive job cuts, stalling technological modernization of banks for decades.
Tap to view answer
Which committee recommended the phased introduction of computerization and information technology in banks during the 1980s?
Dr. C. Rangarajan Committee (1984 and 1989).
Tap to view answer
What was the financial condition of Regional Rural Banks (RRBs) prior to 1991?
Extremely precarious; almost all RRBs were incurring heavy accumulated losses and their loan recovery rate was below 40%.
Tap to view answer
Why did the 1991 economic crisis make banking reforms inevitable?
Because a crippled, ailing, and undercapitalized banking system was completely incapable of financing an open-market economy and foreign trade.
Tap to view answer
What was set as the primary objective of banking reforms?
To make banks financially viable, operationally efficient, autonomous, and competitive in line with international standards.
Tap to view answer
Who was the Chairman of the historic 'Committee on the Financial System' (CFS) constituted by the Government of India in August 1991?
Maidavolu Narasimham (M. Narasimham), who had served as the 13th Governor of the Reserve Bank of India.
Tap to view answer
When did the Narasimham Committee-I submit its landmark report to the government?
In November 1991.
Tap to view answer
What was the core guiding philosophy of the Narasimham Committee-I?
Freeing the banking system from political and administrative controls to provide commercial autonomy, transparency, and financial discipline.
Tap to view answer
To what percentage did the Narasimham Committee-I recommend reducing the Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR) from 38.5%?
Recommended reducing it in a phased manner to 25%.
Tap to view answer
Approximately what is the current SLR rate prescribed by the Reserve Bank of India?
18.0%.
Tap to view answer
What recommendation did the Narasimham Committee-I make regarding the Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)?
Reducing the excessively high CRR to a level of 3% to 5% so that banks retain liquidity for productive lending.
Tap to view answer
Approximately what is the current CRR rate prescribed by the Reserve Bank of India?
4.50% (in the range of 4% to 4.5%).
Tap to view answer
What revolutionary proposal did the Narasimham Committee-I make regarding interest rates?
Complete deregulation of interest rates; allowing banks to determine deposit and lending rates based on their own risk assessment.
Tap to view answer
What controversial recommendation did the Narasimham Committee-I make regarding the mandatory 40% Priority Sector Lending (PSL) target, which the government rejected?
Recommended reducing the PSL target from 40% to 10% (the government rejected this due to socio-political reasons and retained it at 40%).
Tap to view answer
Which standards of the Bank for International Settlements (BIS) did the Narasimham Committee-I recommend applying to Indian banks?
Basel-I Capital Adequacy Norms, under which a minimum 8% Capital to Risk-Weighted Assets Ratio (CRAR) was mandated.
Tap to view answer
Which special judicial mechanism did the Narasimham Committee-I recommend establishing for loan recovery?
Special Debt Recovery Tribunals (DRTs).
Tap to view answer
What three-tier model did the Narasimham Committee-I propose for the future structure of Indian banking?
1. 3–4 large banks of international stature, 2. 8–10 national-level banks, and 3. local and regional-level banks (RRBs, etc.).
Tap to view answer
When did the Reserve Bank of India issue guidelines for licensing new private sector banks based on the Narasimham Committee-I recommendations?
In January 1993.
Tap to view answer
Which were India's first new-generation private banks established under the 1993 guidelines?
UTI Bank (now Axis Bank), HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, and IndusInd Bank.
Tap to view answer
Which body did the Narasimham Committee-I recommend forming under the RBI to strengthen bank supervision?
Board for Financial Supervision (BFS), which was established under the RBI in 1994.
Tap to view answer
When did the 'Committee on Banking Sector Reforms' (Narasimham Committee-II), constituted in December 1997, submit its report?
In April 1998.
Tap to view answer
What were the primary focus areas of the Narasimham Committee-II?
Strengthening the banking system, structural mergers, drastic reduction in NPAs, IT integration, and regulatory independence.
Tap to view answer
To what percentage did the Narasimham Committee-II recommend raising the Capital Adequacy Ratio (CRAR) from 8%?
Recommended raising it to 9% by the year 2000 and 10% subsequently.
Tap to view answer
Which unique concept did the Narasimham Committee-II advocate to avert crises in weak and ailing banks?
'Narrow Banking', under which weak banks would be barred from risky commercial lending and restricted to investing only in risk-free government securities.
Tap to view answer
To what percentage did the Narasimham Committee-II recommend reducing government equity in public sector banks?
Recommended reducing it to 33%.
Tap to view answer
What clear principle did the Narasimham Committee-II propound regarding bank mergers?
Mergers should take place only between two strong banks; a strong bank should never be forcibly merged with a weak or ailing bank.
Tap to view answer
Which specialized asset restructuring mechanism did the Narasimham Committee-II suggest for the speedy resolution of NPAs?
Establishment of Asset Reconstruction Companies (ARCs).
Tap to view answer
What objection did the Narasimham Committee-II raise regarding the dual role of the Reserve Bank of India (RBI)?
The RBI cannot simultaneously be the regulator of banks and the owner of public banks (such as SBI); ownership should be transferred to the government.
Tap to view answer
When was the RBI's entire shareholding in SBI transferred to the Central Government on the recommendation of the Narasimham Committee-II?
In 2007-08 (RBI sold its 59.7% stake in SBI to the government).
Tap to view answer
What reforms did the Narasimham Committee suggest regarding the Board of Directors of banks?
Complete ban on political appointments, appointment of independent and professional directors, and operational autonomy for management.
Tap to view answer
What did the Narasimham Committee suggest regarding branch licensing controls on banks?
Complete abolition of branch licensing; granting sound banks the freedom to open branches anywhere based on commercial requirements.
Tap to view answer
What policy recommendation did the Narasimham Committee-II make regarding the entry of foreign banks into India?
Allowing foreign banks to operate as Wholly Owned Subsidiaries (WOS) and acquire stakes in Indian private banks.
Tap to view answer
How are the recommendations of the Narasimham Committees known in Indian banking history?
The Magna Carta of Indian Banking, which aligned Indian banks with modern, digital, and global standards.
Tap to view answer
What is the standard definition of a 'Non-Performing Asset' (NPA) according to the Reserve Bank of India?
Any advance or loan on which interest or principal installment remains overdue for a continuous period of 90 days after the due date (90-day overdue norm).
Tap to view answer
When was the 90-day overdue norm for NPA identification formally implemented in India?
From March 31, 2004 (previously it was 180 days in 1993 and 360 days in 1992).
Tap to view answer
When is an agricultural loan declared an NPA?
When it remains overdue for 2 crop seasons in the case of short-duration seasonal crops, and 1 crop season for long-duration crops.
Tap to view answer
What is meant by Gross NPA (GNPA)?
The sum total of all loans disbursed by a bank that have been classified as NPAs, without any provisioning or deductions.
Tap to view answer
What is the formula for calculating the Gross NPA Ratio?
(Total Gross NPA / Total Gross Advances) × 100.
Tap to view answer
How is Net NPA (NNPA) calculated?
By subtracting total provisions set aside by the bank and other admissible deductions from Gross NPA (NNPA = GNPA - Provisions).
Tap to view answer
Why is Net NPA considered a more accurate indicator of a bank's financial health than Gross NPA?
Because it reflects the actual risk of bad loans to the bank's capital for which the bank has not yet created any financial safety buffer.
Tap to view answer
What is the legal definition of a 'Willful Defaulter'?
A borrower who deliberately does not repay a loan despite having the full financial capacity to do so, or diverts/siphons off the loan funds for purposes other than those intended.
Tap to view answer
What punitive restrictions are imposed on Willful Defaulters under RBI rules?
They cannot obtain new loans from any bank or financial institution for 5 years, cannot become a director on the board of any new company, and face criminal prosecution.
Tap to view answer
What is meant by 'Evergreening of Loans' in banking?
Secretly sanctioning a fresh loan to a distressed borrower to save them from defaulting on an old loan, enabling them to pay interest on the old loan and prevent the account from being classified as an NPA.
Tap to view answer
What was meant by the 'Twin Balance Sheet Problem' in the Indian economy?
The simultaneous deterioration of the balance sheets of over-leveraged corporate houses and the crippling of public sector banks' balance sheets due to NPAs arising from bad loans extended to them.
Tap to view answer
What was the objective of the 'Asset Quality Review' (AQR) launched in 2015 by then RBI Governor Dr. Raghuram Rajan?
To conduct a deep forensic audit of stressed loans hidden and restructured in bank balance sheets and force the recognition of true NPAs.
Tap to view answer
What was the immediate impact of the implementation of AQR (2015-16) on the Indian banking sector?
Gross NPAs of public sector banks jumped from ~5% in 2015 to a historic peak of around 11.5% (over ₹10 lakh crore) in March 2018, causing massive losses for banks.
Tap to view answer
How does a rise in NPAs affect banks' 'Net Interest Margin' (NIM)?
NIM falls sharply because interest income on stressed loans drops to zero while the bank must continue paying interest on its deposits.
Tap to view answer
What indirect adverse impact do NPAs have on honest and creditworthy borrowers?
Banks raise lending interest rates (spreads) to recover losses from NPAs, making capital more expensive for good borrowers.
Tap to view answer
When does a 'Credit Freeze' (Risk Aversion) situation arise?
When, out of fear of high NPAs and excessive caution, banks virtually stop lending to new projects and private industry, slowing down economic growth.
Tap to view answer
What was the main external and policy reason behind the massive NPA accumulation in India after the 2008 global financial crisis?
Unbridled lending to infrastructure, steel, and power sectors, followed by stalled projects due to land acquisition disputes, delays in environmental clearances, and coal block cancellations.
Tap to view answer
What was the biggest failure of the Corporate Debt Restructuring (CDR) mechanism?
Banks misused it as a tool to conceal NPAs and evergreen loans, causing most restructured loans to eventually turn into massive NPAs by 2014.
Tap to view answer
What was the name of the seven-pronged reform plan announced by the Central Government in 2015 to tackle the NPA problem?
The 'Indradhanush Plan', covering Appointments, Bank Board Bureau, Capitalization, De-stressing, Empowerment, Framework of Accountability, and Governance Reforms.
Tap to view answer
What are 'Recapitalization Bonds' issued by the government to address NPA pressure?
Special government bonds purchased by banks, with the government using those funds to infuse fresh equity capital into banks to restore their solvency.
Tap to view answer
What is the full form of IRAC?
Income Recognition and Asset Classification.
Tap to view answer
In which year were IRAC guidelines first implemented by the Reserve Bank of India?
In financial year 1992-93 (based on the recommendations of the Narasimham Committee-I).
Tap to view answer
What is the golden rule of Income Recognition under IRAC norms?
Interest income on NPA accounts can be recorded in the books of accounts only when it is actually realized in cash (Cash Realization), never on an accrual basis.
Tap to view answer
If a Standard Asset is declared an NPA during the current financial year, what happens to the uncollected interest already booked?
Interest booked in previous quarters but not actually received must be reversed (written back) by the bank in its current profit and loss account.
Tap to view answer
Into which two broad categories are bank assets (loans) primarily classified under IRAC norms?
1. Performing / Standard Assets, and 2. Non-Performing Assets (NPAs).
Tap to view answer
What is the identifying characteristic of a 'Standard Asset'?
A loan account where principal and interest payments are regular and which does not carry more than normal risk of default.
Tap to view answer
Into which three categories are Non-Performing Assets (NPAs) classified under IRAC norms?
1. Sub-Standard Assets, 2. Doubtful Assets, and 3. Loss Assets.
Tap to view answer
What is a 'Sub-Standard Asset'?
A loan account that has remained an NPA for a continuous period of 12 months or less.
Tap to view answer
What is the definition of a 'Doubtful Asset'?
A loan account that has remained in the 'Sub-Standard' category for a period exceeding 12 months.
Tap to view answer
Into which three sub-categories are Doubtful Assets divided based on risk and duration?
1. D1 (Doubtful up to 1 year), 2. D2 (Doubtful for 1 to 3 years), and 3. D3 (Doubtful for more than 3 years).
Tap to view answer
What is the technical definition of a 'Loss Asset'?
An asset identified as completely uncollectible by the bank, its internal/external auditors, or RBI inspectors, even if it has not yet been formally written off completely from the books.
Tap to view answer
What is the 'Special Mention Accounts' (SMA) system introduced by the RBI to detect early stress in loan accounts?
An early warning system for identifying overdue loans (within 1 to 90 days) before they turn into NPAs.
Tap to view answer
Which loan accounts fall under the SMA-0 category?
Accounts where principal or interest is overdue for 1 to 30 days, or which show other early signs of stress.
Tap to view answer
What is the duration for the SMA-1 category?
Loan accounts where payment remains continuously overdue for 31 to 60 days.
Tap to view answer
What is the duration for the SMA-2 category?
Loan accounts where payment remains continuously overdue for 61 to 90 days (the final stage just before becoming an NPA).
Tap to view answer
What is meant by 'Provisioning' in banking?
Setting aside a specific portion of a bank's operating profit into a separate reserve fund to cover potential or actual loan losses.
Tap to view answer
Do banks have to make general provisioning even on completely regular 'Standard Assets'?
Yes; general provisioning is mandatory at 0.40% on standard advances, 0.25% on agriculture and SMEs, and 1.0% on Commercial Real Estate (CRE) loans.
Tap to view answer
What is the minimum mandatory provisioning for the secured portion of Sub-Standard Assets?
15% of the total outstanding amount.
Tap to view answer
What is the provisioning rate on the unsecured portion of Sub-Standard Assets?
25% of the total unsecured amount (20% for infrastructure loans).
Tap to view answer
How much provisioning is mandatory on the secured portion of D1 category Doubtful Assets (Doubtful up to 1 year)?
25% of the secured portion (and 100% of the unsecured portion).
Tap to view answer
What is the provisioning rate on the secured portion of D2 category Doubtful Assets (Doubtful for 1 to 3 years)?
40% of the secured portion (and 100% of the unsecured portion).
Tap to view answer
How much provisioning is mandatory on D3 category Doubtful Assets (Doubtful for more than 3 years)?
100% provisioning on the entire outstanding amount (both secured and unsecured).
Tap to view answer
How much provisioning is required for Loss Assets as per RBI guidelines?
100% provisioning is mandatory, or the asset must be immediately and completely written off from the books.
Tap to view answer
What is the definition of 'Provisioning Coverage Ratio' (PCR)?
The percentage of total cumulative provisions built by a bank against its total Gross NPAs (Total Provisions / Gross NPA × 100).
Tap to view answer
What is considered a healthy and safe PCR benchmark for commercial banks according to the Reserve Bank of India?
At least 70% or higher (currently, most strong Indian banks have a PCR exceeding 75% to 80%).
Tap to view answer
How does a High PCR enhance a bank's financial safety?
Even if there is zero recovery on stressed loans in the future, the bank's capital and equity remain safe because the entire loss has already been provisioned for.
Tap to view answer
What is the fundamental difference between a 'Write-off' (Technical / Prudential Write-off) and a 'Loan Waiver' in banking?
A write-off is merely an accounting exercise to clean up the balance sheet while legal recovery continues, whereas a loan waiver completely forgives the borrower's debt with no further recovery.

What is the full form of 'CRILC' established by the central bank?
Central Repository of Information on Large Credits.
Tap to view answer
Under the CRILC system, banks must report the loan account status of all large borrowers to the RBI above what exposure threshold?
Accounts with total fund-based and non-fund-based exposure of ₹5 crore and above.

In which year was the SARFAESI Act passed by Parliament?
In 2002 (Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest Act).

What extraordinary power did the SARFAESI Act, 2002 confer upon banks?
The statutory right to directly take possession of and auction secured mortgaged assets upon default without the intervention of any court or tribunal.
Tap to view answer
To which assets does the SARFAESI Act NOT apply?
Agricultural land, loans with outstanding dues below ₹1 lakh, or cases where the remaining due amount is less than 20% of the principal and interest.

How many days' statutory notice must a bank issue to a borrower before taking possession of a mortgaged asset under the SARFAESI Act?
A 60-day Demand Notice (under Section 13(2)).

Which was India's first Asset Reconstruction Company (ARC), and when was it established?
ARCIL (Asset Reconstruction Company of India Limited), established in 2002-03.

In what form do ARCs pay banks when purchasing bad loans?
In a combination of Cash and Security Receipts (SRs).

The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (IBC, 2016).

The National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT).

The IBC 2016 shifted India's credit culture from 'Debtor in Control' to which model?
The 'Creditors in Control' model, where upon default, company management is taken away from the promoter and handed over to a Resolution Professional (RP).

What is the maximum statutory time limit prescribed to complete the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) under IBC 2016?
180 days (extendable by 90 days), and a maximum of 330 days including litigation.

NARCL (National Asset Reconstruction Company Limited) and IDRCL (India Debt Resolution Company Limited).

15% Cash and 85% government-guaranteed Security Receipts (SRs).

A sovereign guarantee of ₹30,600 crore (for a period of 5 years).

What is the 'PCA' framework implemented by the RBI to improve the operations of weak and distressed banks?
Prompt Corrective Action (PCA) Framework, which imposes restrictions on new lending, dividend distribution, and branch expansion when pre-determined triggers on CRAR, Net NPA, and profitability are breached.

1. Capital Adequacy Ratio (CRAR/CET-1), 2. Net NPA Ratio (NNPA), and 3. Leverage Ratio.

Banks Board Bureau (BBB), which was restructured in 2022 as the 'Financial Services Institutions Bureau' (FSIB).

To achieve economies of scale, strengthen balance sheets, and create globally competitive large national banks.
