Impact of Globalization on Trade, Structure & Principles of WTO (MFN, National Treatment), Agricultural Subsidy Boxes, TRIPS, and India's Strategic Interests

Key Facts & Contributions
Foreign Trade Part 2
Trade RegimeKey FeatureSignificance for India
World Trade Organization (WTO - 1995)Rule-based multilateral trading system; consensus-based decision-making and a legally binding dispute settlement mechanism.Protection against unilateral trade sanctions and tariff arbitrariness; a sovereign platform for collective bargaining alongside the developing countries' coalition (G-33).
Most Favoured Nation (MFN Principle)Under Article I of GATT, any tariff concession or policy privilege granted to one country must automatically and equally apply to all member countries.Ensures non-discriminatory, equal competitive market access for Indian products across more than 164 member nations.
National TreatmentUnder Article III of GATT, complete equality between imported goods and domestically produced goods once imported goods clear customs duties and enter the domestic market.Guarantees legal protection for Indian exported goods in foreign markets against unfair internal taxes or local regulatory barriers.
Agreement on Agriculture (AoA) & BoxesClassification of agricultural subsidies: Green Box (permissible), Blue Box (production-limiting), and Amber Box (trade-distorting, 10% de minimis limit).Sovereign protection of Minimum Support Price (MSP) and Public Stockholding (PSH) of foodgrains under the National Food Security Act (NFSA).
Bali Peace Clause (2013)Until a permanent solution on Public Stockholding (PSH) is reached, no WTO dispute can be raised against India if its MSP-linked support breaches the 10% subsidy ceiling.Provides a multilateral legal shield for food security of crores of poor citizens and price support mechanisms for small and marginal farmers.
TRIPS Agreement & Doha DeclarationGlobal protection of patents, copyrights, trademarks, and Geographical Indications (GI); relaxation for Compulsory Licensing during public health crises.Preserves India's status as the 'Pharmacy of the World'; prevents patent evergreening via Section 3(d) and ensures affordable generic medicines for developing nations.
Trade in Services (GATS) & Mode 4Four modes of cross-border trade in services; Mode 4 covers the cross-border free movement of skilled professionals and IT engineers.Facilitates global visa mobility for India's vast software, IT, and professional services sector, generating $350B+ in foreign exchange earnings from service exports.
Trade Facilitation Agreement (TFA - 2017)Simplification of customs procedures at ports and border checkpoints, digitization of documentation, and seamless, rapid clearance of goods.Reduces logistics costs in India, expands 'Turant Customs' and Single Window Interface, and drives a major leap in World Bank LPI rankings.

One-Liner Questions

What is the standard definition of 'Globalization' in an economic context?
The integration of national economies with the global economy, enabling the seamless cross-border flow of goods, services, capital, technology, and labor.
Tap to view answer
What was the biggest institutional transformation brought about by globalization in the structure of international trade?
The dismantling of rigid protectionist tariff and quota regimes and the establishment of an open, rule-based trading system through multilateral trade agreements.
Tap to view answer
What is meant by 'Global Value Chains' (GVCs) in the context of production systems in the era of globalization?
The fragmentation of different production stages of a single product (design, raw materials, assembly, marketing) across various countries based on their cost efficiency.
Tap to view answer
What is a classic contemporary example of a Global Value Chain (GVC)?
The manufacturing of the Apple iPhone, where design takes place in the USA, chips are made in Taiwan, memory in Korea, and final assembly in India and China.
Tap to view answer
How did globalization alter the role of Multinational Corporations (MNCs) in international trade?
Roughly 70% to 80% of global trade began to be driven directly or indirectly through the intra-firm supply chains of multinational corporations.
Tap to view answer
During the era of globalization, what gained unprecedented importance in the nature of trade compared to physical goods?
'Trade in Services' and digital trade (cross-border digital and data flows).
Tap to view answer
What revolutionary impact did the 'Containerization' revolution have on globalization and maritime trade?
The use of standardized shipping containers led to a steep decline of over 90% in logistics costs and time required for loading and unloading cargo.
Tap to view answer
How did the Internet and Information Technology (IT) revolution facilitate international trade?
It completely freed e-commerce, software exports, Business Process Outsourcing (BPO), and financial transactions from geographical distance constraints.
Tap to view answer
What is meant by 'Slowbalization' or 'Deglobalization' in economics?
The slowdown in the pace of global trade, capital flows, and economic integration following the 2008 financial crisis and COVID-19, accompanied by a resurgence of protectionism.
Tap to view answer
In recent shifts in globalization, what is the policy of 'Friend-Shoring' (or Ally-Shoring)?
Relocating supply chains away from strategic rivals (such as China) toward trusted partner nations sharing geopolitical values (such as India and Vietnam).
Tap to view answer
What is the difference between 'Near-Shoring' and 'Re-Shoring'?
In Near-Shoring, factories are set up in geographically neighboring countries, whereas in Re-Shoring, production is brought back entirely from abroad to the home country.
Tap to view answer
Following supply chain disruptions during the COVID-19 pandemic, which trilateral initiative did India, Japan, and Australia launch?
The Supply Chain Resilience Initiative (SCRI, 2021).
Tap to view answer
What is the 'China Plus One' corporate strategy, and what is its significance for India?
A policy by global MNCs to reduce their sole dependence on China and establish an alternative manufacturing base in a country like India.
Tap to view answer
In the intersection of trade and environment during globalization, what new barriers are being erected by developed nations?
Green Protectionism, such as the European Union's 'Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism' (CBAM) and the EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR).
Tap to view answer
What dual impact of globalization was observed on the workforce and labor markets of developing countries?
Millions of modern jobs were created in services and manufacturing, while traditional artisans and unorganized industries faced stiff competition from cheap foreign imports.
Tap to view answer
What is the standard definition of a 'Tariff'?
A financial tax (Customs Duty) imposed by customs authorities on goods crossing international borders.
Tap to view answer
What is the difference between an 'Ad-valorem Tariff' and a 'Specific Tariff'?
An Ad-valorem tariff is levied as a fixed percentage (e.g., 10% or 20%) of the total monetary value of the imported good, whereas a Specific tariff is levied as a fixed amount per physical unit (per tonne, per barrel, or per meter).
Tap to view answer
What is a 'Compound Tariff'?
A combined levy of both an ad-valorem duty and a specific duty on the same imported good.
Tap to view answer
How does a 'Tariff Rate Quota' (TRQ) function?
It applies a low or zero customs duty up to a predetermined import volume (quota), and imposes a substantially higher tariff on imports exceeding that limit.
Tap to view answer
Under Non-Tariff Barriers (NTBs), what are 'Sanitary and Phytosanitary Measures' (SPS Agreement)?
Stringent international standards related to food safety, pesticide residues, and infectious diseases aimed at protecting human, animal, and plant health.
Tap to view answer
What is meant by 'Technical Barriers to Trade' (TBT Agreement) in international trade?
National regulations concerning product packaging, labeling, technical specifications, and quality testing (such as India's mandatory BIS certification).
Tap to view answer
Which is the apex quasi-judicial body in the Government of India that investigates 'Dumping' and recommends anti-dumping duties?
The Directorate General of Trade Remedies (DGTR), under the Ministry of Commerce and Industry.
Tap to view answer
Following an investigation by the DGTR, which ministry officially notifies and enforces anti-dumping or countervailing duties in India?
The Department of Revenue, Union Ministry of Finance.
Tap to view answer
Under what circumstances is a 'Countervailing Duty' (CVD) imposed?
When the exporting country's government provides direct financial subsidies to its domestic producers, artificially lowering the price of goods and causing injury to the importing country's domestic industry.
Tap to view answer
When and why is a 'Safeguard Duty' imposed?
When there is a sudden and unexpected surge in imports of a product that threatens to cause serious injury to the competing domestic industry (temporary emergency protection).
Tap to view answer
In the hierarchy of trade agreements, what is the difference between a 'Preferential Trade Agreement' (PTA) and a 'Free Trade Agreement' (FTA)?
In a PTA, tariffs are reduced only on a Positive List of selected goods, whereas in an FTA, tariffs are eliminated or minimized on almost all goods (except a Negative List).
Tap to view answer
How is a 'Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement' (CEPA) broader than a standard FTA?
A CEPA covers not only trade in goods, but also comprehensively includes trade in services, investment protection, intellectual property, government procurement, and dispute settlement.
Tap to view answer
What is the defining feature of a 'Customs Union'?
Free internal trade among member countries combined with a uniform Common External Tariff (CET) applied against the rest of the non-member world (e.g., the European Union Customs Union).
Tap to view answer
What is a 'Common Market'?
An economic bloc where, in addition to goods and services, there is complete free movement of capital and labor (factors of production) across borders.
Tap to view answer
Under WTO trade terminology, what is an 'Inverted Duty Structure'?
A situation where the import duty on finished goods is lower than the import duty on the raw materials or intermediate components used to manufacture them, thereby discouraging domestic manufacturing.
Tap to view answer
On which date and under which historic agreement was the World Trade Organization (WTO) established?
On 1 January 1995, under the Marrakesh Agreement (signed on 15 April 1994).
Tap to view answer
Which older provisional trade arrangement did the WTO replace in 1995?
The General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade (GATT, 1947).
Tap to view answer
What is the most fundamental institutional difference between GATT (1947) and WTO (1995)?
GATT was merely a provisional legal treaty restricted to trade in physical goods, whereas the WTO is a permanent international organization covering goods (GATT), services (GATS), and intellectual property (TRIPS).
Tap to view answer
Is India a founding member of the WTO?
Yes; India was one of the 23 original founding members of GATT in 1947 and became a founding member of the WTO on 1 January 1995.
Tap to view answer
Why is a rule-based multilateral trading system critically important for a developing country like India?
Because it provides protection against unilateral trade sanctions and tariff coercion by powerful economic superpowers like the USA and the European Union.
Tap to view answer
Which is the highest policy-making body of the WTO, and how often does it meet?
The Ministerial Conference (MC), attended by trade/commerce ministers of all member countries, which generally meets once every two years.
Tap to view answer
Which body handles the day-to-day legislative and administrative functioning of the WTO between Ministerial Conferences?
The General Council, comprising ambassador-level representatives of all member countries stationed in Geneva.
Tap to view answer
What is the primary legal decision-making process in the WTO?
The principle of 'Consensus', where every member country (large or small) holds equal veto power and any single member can block an agreement by objecting.
Tap to view answer
Why is the WTO's Dispute Settlement Mechanism (DSM) considered unique in international law?
Because its rulings are legally binding on member countries, and it authorizes the winning party to impose retaliatory trade sanctions against a non-compliant nation.
Tap to view answer
What are the three main pillars on which the WTO's Agreement on Agriculture (AoA) rests?
1. Market Access (tariff reductions), 2. Domestic Support (control of agricultural subsidies), and 3. Export Subsidies (elimination).
Tap to view answer
What is the 'Green Box' of agricultural subsidies under the WTO?
Government subsidies that do not distort trade or cause minimal distortion (such as agricultural research, pest control, rural infrastructure, disaster relief, and direct income support); there is no financial cap on them.
Tap to view answer
What type of subsidies does the 'Blue Box' in agriculture represent?
Direct payments tied to production-limiting programmes, where farmers are compensated for restricting their cultivated acreage or output (negligible usage in India).
Tap to view answer
What is the 'Amber Box' in agriculture, and why is it the most sensitive issue for India?
Trade-distorting subsidies (such as Minimum Support Price - MSP, fertilizer subsidies, free electricity, and irrigation subsidies), on which the WTO imposes strict quantitative caps.
Tap to view answer
What is the 'De Minimis' limit under the Amber Box?
The permissible percentage of the total value of agricultural production up to which Amber Box subsidies can be granted; it is set at 5% for developed countries and 10% for developing countries (like India).
Tap to view answer
Due to which technical rule does India's Minimum Support Price (MSP) and food security programme face challenges at the WTO?
Because under the AoA, subsidies are calculated using outdated 1986–88 External Reference Prices (ERP) without any adjustment for inflation.
Tap to view answer
What is the 'Peace Clause' secured by India at the 9th Ministerial Conference (Bali, Indonesia) in December 2013?
An interim legal protection stating that until a permanent solution on Public Stockholding (PSH) is found, no country can initiate a WTO legal dispute against India even if its food security support breaches the 10% ceiling.
Tap to view answer
In which year did India invoke the 'Bali Peace Clause' for the first time regarding Public Stockholding for its rice farmers?
In the financial year 2018–19 (and repeatedly in subsequent years), when subsidies given for rice exceeded the prescribed 10% de minimis limit.
Tap to view answer
Which large coalition of developing countries does India lead at the WTO on food security and Public Stockholding (PSH)?
The G-33 group of developing nations.
Tap to view answer
What is India's firmest and non-negotiable demand regarding Public Stockholding (PSH)?
A permanent legal solution replacing the Peace Clause, where the base year for subsidy calculation is updated from 1986–88 to current realistic prices and protection is extended to other crops as well.
Tap to view answer
Which forms of intellectual property are covered under the TRIPS Agreement (Trade-Related Aspects of Intellectual Property Rights)?
Patents (20 years), copyrights, trademarks, industrial designs, Geographical Indications (GI), layout-designs of integrated circuits, and undisclosed information (trade secrets).
Tap to view answer
What historic amendment did India make to its patent law in 2005 to comply with the TRIPS Agreement?
By amending the Indian Patents Act, 1970, India fully implemented the 'Product Patent' regime alongside 'Process Patents' for pharmaceuticals, chemicals, and food products.
Tap to view answer
What does the famous 'Section 3(d)' of the Indian Patents Act, 1970 provide?
It strictly prohibits pharmaceutical companies from extending a drug's 20-year patent monopoly through minor tweaks (Evergreening of Patents), unless the modification demonstrates a significant enhancement in therapeutic efficacy.
Tap to view answer
In which landmark case did the Supreme Court of India uphold the legal validity of Section 3(d) of the Indian Patents Act?
Novartis AG v. Union of India (2013), concerning the anti-cancer drug 'Glivec'.
Tap to view answer
What is meant by 'Compulsory Licensing' (CL) under the TRIPS Agreement?
An authorization granted by the government to a third-party domestic company to manufacture a life-saving patented drug without the patent holder's consent during national emergencies, public health crises, or excessive pricing.
Tap to view answer
In which year and for which drug did the Government of India issue the country's first Compulsory License?
In March 2012, to Hyderabad-based Natco Pharma to manufacture a generic version of Bayer's kidney/liver cancer drug 'Nexavar' (slashing the price from ₹2.8 lakh to ₹8,800 per month).
Tap to view answer
Why was the 2001 'Doha Declaration on TRIPS and Public Health' considered epoch-making for developing countries?
It formally affirmed that the protection of intellectual property rights can never override human lives and access to affordable life-saving medicines for citizens.
Tap to view answer
What are the Four Modes of international trade in services under GATS (General Agreement on Trade in Services)?
1. Mode 1: Cross-border supply, 2. Mode 2: Consumption abroad, 3. Mode 3: Commercial presence, and 4. Mode 4: Presence of natural persons.
Tap to view answer
What is a prime Indian example of 'Mode 1' (Cross-Border Supply) under GATS?
An IT firm based in Bengaluru delivering software coding or back-office services online to a bank located in the United States.
Tap to view answer
What is an example of 'Mode 2' (Consumption Abroad) under GATS?
Foreign nationals traveling to India for medical treatment (Medical Tourism) or enrolling in Indian universities for higher education.
Tap to view answer
What does 'Mode 3' (Commercial Presence) under GATS refer to?
An Indian bank (like SBI) or IT company (like TCS) opening a physical branch or subsidiary abroad to deliver services (FDI-based).
Tap to view answer
Why does India have its highest strategic interest in 'Mode 4' (Presence of Natural Persons) under GATS?
Because it covers the temporary cross-border movement of Indian software engineers, doctors, nurses, and chartered accountants to deliver services abroad (seeking visa and work-permit liberalization).
Tap to view answer
What is the 'Fisheries Subsidies Agreement' concluded at the 12th Ministerial Conference (MC12 - Geneva, 2022)?
A global ban on harmful government subsidies for Illegal, Unreported, and Unregulated (IUU) fishing and for fishing of overfished stocks.
Tap to view answer
How did India protect the interests of millions of its traditional coastal fishermen in the Fisheries Subsidies Agreement?
By securing complete exemption from subsidy bans for boats, nets, and fuel subsidies provided to traditional small-scale artisanal fishermen for livelihood within the 200-nautical-mile Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ).
Tap to view answer
Why is the 'Appellate Body' of the WTO's Dispute Settlement Mechanism currently dysfunctional/paralyzed?
Because the United States has vetoed the appointment of new judges since 2019, leaving the Appellate Body without its required minimum quorum (3 members).
Tap to view answer
What legal anomaly in trade disputes has emerged due to the paralysis of the Appellate Body, known as 'Appeal into the Void'?
When a losing country files an appeal against a panel ruling before a non-functional Appellate Body, the dispute remains suspended indefinitely and the ruling cannot be enforced.
Tap to view answer
In which year did the Trade Facilitation Agreement (TFA) enter into force, and what was its impact on India?
It entered into force in February 2017; India ratified it and digitized 80% of its customs paperwork under the National Trade Facilitation Action Plan (NTFAP).
Tap to view answer
Which country first proposed 'Trade Facilitation in Services' (TFS) at the WTO on behalf of developing nations?
India in 2017, aiming to make visa procedures transparent and streamlined for the cross-border movement of professionals and services, similar to trade in goods.
Tap to view answer
What is India's policy objection to the 'Investment Facilitation for Development' (IFD) agreement under the WTO umbrella?
India argues that it is a non-consensus plurilateral initiative and that the WTO's mandate is strictly limited to trade, not sovereign investment policies.
Tap to view answer
What is India's objection to the 'E-commerce Moratorium' (Moratorium on Customs Duties on Electronic Transmissions)?
Because of this moratorium in place since 1998, developing countries cannot impose customs duties on digitally downloaded software, music, films, and e-books, resulting in massive revenue losses.
Tap to view answer
How can the core principles of the WTO be summarized into four main pillars?
1. Non-discrimination (MFN and National Treatment), 2. Freer trade (progressive tariff cuts), 3. Predictability and transparency (bound tariffs), and 4. Promoting development (special exemptions for developing countries).
Tap to view answer
What is the simple meaning of the WTO's first non-discrimination principle, 'Most Favoured Nation' (MFN)?
'Treating all foreign trading partners equally'; a country cannot grant a special concession to one nation while discriminating against another.
Tap to view answer
What is the practical meaning of the WTO's second non-discrimination principle, 'National Treatment'?
'Treating foreign products and domestic products equally'; once customs duties are paid, no additional internal local taxes can be imposed on imported goods.
Tap to view answer
In the WTO system, what is the difference between a 'Bound Tariff' and an 'Applied Tariff'?
A Bound Tariff is the legal maximum ceiling above which a country cannot raise its import duty (WTO commitment), whereas an Applied Tariff is the actual tariff rate currently charged by the government at ports.
Tap to view answer
Why is India's Applied Tariff significantly lower than its Bound Tariff?
So that the government retains policy space to raise tariffs during emergencies or import surges to protect domestic industries without violating WTO rules.
Tap to view answer
What is the provision of 'Special and Differential Treatment' (S&DT) in the WTO?
Special legal flexibilities granted to developing and Least Developed Countries (LDCs), including longer implementation timelines, higher subsidy ceilings, and preferential market access.
Tap to view answer
How does a country obtain 'Developing Country' status in the WTO?
Through the principle of 'Self-Declaration'; any member nation can declare itself a developing country, although other members may challenge its eligibility.
Tap to view answer
What major objection has the United States raised regarding the developing country status of India and China at the WTO?
That nations with large economic size and high growth rates should no longer receive S&DT exemptions (which India firmly rejected by citing its low per capita income and widespread poverty).
Tap to view answer
What is the function of the 'Trade Policy Review Mechanism' (TPRM)?
To conduct impartial, periodic reviews of each member country's national trade policies and laws to maintain transparency in global trade.
Tap to view answer
In which year was India's latest 'Trade Policy Review' (7th Trade Policy Review) held at the WTO?
In January 2021.
Tap to view answer
Under the WTO's TRIMs Agreement (Trade-Related Investment Measures), which type of investment restrictions are illegal?
'Local Content Requirements' (LCR), meaning foreign companies cannot be forced to mandatorily source domestic components (as challenged by the US in India's National Solar Mission case).
Tap to view answer
What is the fundamental difference between 'Multilateral' and 'Plurilateral' agreements in the WTO?
A multilateral agreement is binding on all 164+ members by consensus, whereas a plurilateral agreement applies only to those select countries that voluntarily sign it (such as the Government Procurement Agreement - GPA).

Where was the 13th Ministerial Conference (MC13) held in February–March 2024?
In Abu Dhabi (United Arab Emirates - UAE).

What was India's clear negotiating stance on agriculture and food security at MC13 Abu Dhabi?
India made it clear that no new multilateral agreement on agriculture would be accepted without a permanent solution on Public Stockholding (PSH).

Where is the headquarters of the WTO located, and who is its current Director-General?
In Geneva (Switzerland); and the current Director-General is Dr. Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala (Nigeria).

Who holds the distinction of being the first woman and the first African Director-General of the WTO?
Dr. Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala (since March 2021).

Who was the then Director-General of GATT who prepared the final draft of the Uruguay Round negotiations (1986–1994)?
Arthur Dunkel (author of the 'Dunkel Draft').

Why did the 'Dunkel Draft' and the WTO face massive political and public protests in India during the early 1990s?
Due to fears over loss of farmers' seed sovereignty from seed patenting, steep price hikes in life-saving medicines, and forced cuts in agricultural subsidies.

Which unique legislation did India enact within its intellectual property framework to protect the rights of farmers and plant breeders?
The Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers' Rights Act, 2001 (PPV&FR Act, 2001), which grants farmers the inalienable right to save, use, sow, resow, exchange, share, and sell their farm-saved seeds.

Which database did India create to prevent foreign companies from patenting traditional Indian knowledge, yoga, and Ayurveda (Biopiracy)?
The Traditional Knowledge Digital Library (TKDL, 2001).

On what legal basis did India win the cancellation of US patents on Indian Basmati rice and turmeric?
By presenting irrefutable evidence of 'Prior Art' and documented traditional knowledge.

Under which international agreement does a Geographical Indication (GI Tag) grant global legal protection to a product?
Under Articles 22 to 24 of the WTO's TRIPS Agreement (enforced in India via the GI Act of 1999, effective from 2003).

Darjeeling Tea.

What is the standard timeline for resolving a trade dispute under the WTO's Dispute Settlement Mechanism?
Approximately 1 year from panel establishment to adoption of the final report (and around 15 to 18 months in case of an appeal).

What is the mandatory first step in WTO dispute resolution?
A mandatory 60-day bilateral 'Consultations' process; a dispute settlement panel is constituted only if consultations fail.

In the US-India Solar Panel Dispute (2016), which Indian provision did the WTO rule illegal?
The Domestic Content Requirement (DCR) mandating the use of locally manufactured solar cells and modules under the National Solar Mission, on grounds of violating GATT and TRIMs.

On what grounds did India lose the India-US Poultry Dispute at the WTO?
India's ban on US chicken leg imports citing Avian Influenza (bird flu) failed to pass the scientific risk assessment standard under the SPS Agreement.

How did India and the United States resolve all seven of their pending bilateral trade disputes at the WTO in 2023?
By mutually agreeing to withdraw the disputes through diplomatic settlements and reciprocal tariff concessions.

Which countries form the 'Cairns Group' coalition at the WTO?
A coalition of major agricultural exporting nations (such as Australia, Brazil, Argentina, and Canada) that advocates for the complete elimination of all agricultural subsidies and trade barriers globally.

Why do the demands of the Cairns Group clash with India's agricultural priorities?
Because they exert intense pressure to classify India's Minimum Support Price (MSP) and food security buffer stocks as trade-distorting and restrict them.

Can the WTO stop a sovereign nation from restricting imports on grounds of environmental protection or national security?
No; Article XX of GATT (General Exceptions) permits restrictions for public morals, health, and environment, while Article XXI permits restrictions for essential national security interests.

Why has the WTO's Doha Development Agenda (DDA, 2001) remained stalled for decades?
Due to a deadlock caused by developed countries (USA/EU) refusing to dismantle their massive agricultural subsidies and developing countries refusing to fully open their industrial markets.

What are the three most prominent causes of the current crisis of the WTO?
1. Paralysis of the Appellate Body, 2. US-China geopolitical trade wars and rising protectionism, and 3. A growing shift toward bilateral FTAs due to the failure of consensus-based decision-making.
Tap to view answer
Is the proliferation of bilateral and regional Free Trade Agreements (FTAs) a threat to the WTO's multilateral system?
Yes; economist Jagdish Bhagwati termed this the 'Spaghetti Bowl Effect', where complex and overlapping bilateral rules fragment global trade.

Why does the WTO's multilateral system remain indispensable and vital for India even today?
Because India's economic clout is still smaller than that of developed Western economies; in a weakened WTO, superpowers would dictate unilateral terms, whereas inside the WTO, developing nations can unite to defend their sovereign interests.

What is the ultimate macroeconomic conclusion from the comprehensive study of Foreign Trade Part 2?
Globalization has transformed international trade into a vast dynamic network of Global Value Chains and digital services; in this landscape, the WTO serves as India's strongest sovereign shield for rule-based fair market access, enabling India to emerge as a responsible global trade powerhouse while steadfastly protecting food security for its 1.4 billion citizens, sovereign policy space, and affordable healthcare.
