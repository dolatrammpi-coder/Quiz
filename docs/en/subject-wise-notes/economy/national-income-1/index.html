National Income Part 1

Definition of National Income, historical background, need for measurement, concept of Gross Domestic Product (GDP), key features, and macroeconomic significance

🔍
Quick Reference
Key Facts & Contributions
National Income Part 1
National Income ConceptDefinition / Key FeatureEconomic Significance
Gross Domestic Product (GDP at Market Prices)Total monetary market value of all final goods and services produced within the domestic territory of a country during a financial year (without deducting depreciation).The primary and universal macroeconomic indicator of an economy's overall size, productive capacity, employment generation, and international economic standing.
Gross National Product (GNP at Market Prices)Total market value of final goods and services produced by the normal residents of a country in one year [GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA)].Measures the actual national earning capacity and global economic footprint of a country's residents; captures the impact of foreign income and remittances.
Net Domestic Product (NDP at Market Prices)Value obtained by subtracting wear-and-tear expenses (Depreciation) of fixed assets incurred during the production process from Gross Domestic Product (GDP).A realistic reflection of capital asset maintenance and net productive capacity; represents net output after accounting for wear and tear of machinery in the economy.
Net National Product / National Income (NNP at Factor Cost)Net National Product at Factor Cost (NNP_FC); the sum of net factor incomes earned by the four factors of production (land, labour, capital, entrepreneurship) for their productive contribution.Theoretically and technically the country's true National Income; the ultimate benchmark of citizens' real purchasing power and overall economic welfare.
Factor Cost vs Market Price (FC vs MP)Factor Cost is the cost received by the producer; adding Net Indirect Taxes (Indirect Taxes - Government Subsidies) to Factor Cost yields Market Price.Clarifies the distorting impact of government fiscal policies (taxes and subsidies) on price levels; since January 2015, India has adopted GDP_MP as its headline measure.
Real GDP vs Nominal GDPNominal GDP is measured at the prevailing current prices of the financial year, whereas Real GDP is measured at the constant prices of a fixed base year (2011-12).Eliminates the distorting effect of inflation (price rise) to accurately determine the real net growth rate in physical output (Real Economic Growth).
GDP DeflatorPercentage ratio of Nominal GDP to Real GDP [(Nominal GDP / Real GDP) * 100]; a comprehensive price index for the entire economy.A broader measure of inflation than the Consumer Price Index (CPI); reflects price changes across all domestically produced goods and services in the country.
Gross Value Added (GVA at Basic Prices)Net value added obtained by subtracting intermediate consumption from the value of output; includes production taxes and excludes production subsidies.Provides an accurate assessment of the supply side of the economy and the separate sectoral performance and productivity of the primary, secondary, and tertiary sectors.

One-Liner Questions

What is the simplest and standard definition of 'National Income' in macroeconomics?
The aggregate sum of total factor income (NNP at Factor Cost) earned by the normal residents of a country from all final goods and services produced during a financial year.

From which perspective did the renowned neo-classical economist Prof. Alfred Marshall define National Income?
From the Production Approach: the net aggregate of physical and non-physical goods produced annually by the labour and capital of a country acting on its natural resources.
Tap to view answer
What monetary definition of National Income did Prof. A. C. Pigou put forward?
The welfare-based monetary approach: that part of the objective income of the community, including income derived from abroad, which can be measured directly or indirectly in money.
Tap to view answer
On what basis does the famous American economist Irving Fisher's definition of National Income rest?
On the Consumption Approach: the total value of goods and services directly consumed by final consumers during a year.
Tap to view answer
Among the definitions of Marshall, Pigou, and Fisher, which is considered theoretically superior and which is practically difficult?
Fisher's definition is theoretically the most scientific and logical, but statistically measuring the annual consumption value of durable goods (such as cars and houses) is extremely difficult.

What does the 'Circular Flow of Income' represent in National Income Accounting?
The continuous, unending cycle of production, income generation, and consumption expenditure, wherein production creates income and that income is spent on goods to generate demand for further production.
Tap to view answer
Between which two primary economic units does the circular flow take place in a simple Two-Sector Closed Economy?
The Household Sector (owners of factors of production and consumers) and the Producing Firm Sector (producers of goods and services).
Tap to view answer
What is the difference between 'Real Flow' and 'Money Flow' in the circular flow of income?
Real Flow involves the flow of factors of production and physical goods/services, whereas Money Flow involves the flow of money in the form of factor payments (wages, rent) and consumption expenditure.
Tap to view answer
What do 'Leakages' and 'Injections' mean in the circular flow of income?
Leakages (savings, taxes, imports) withdraw money from the circular flow, whereas Injections (investment, government expenditure, exports) add new demand and income to the cycle.
Tap to view answer
What are the four basic factors of production and what are their respective factor payments called?
1. Land (Rent), 2. Labour (Wages/Salaries), 3. Capital (Interest), and 4. Entrepreneurship (Profit).
Tap to view answer
What is the fundamental difference between 'Factor Income' and a 'Transfer Payment'?
Factor Income is earned in return for rendering productive services in the production process (earned income), whereas a Transfer Payment is received unilaterally without any corresponding current production of goods or services (e.g., donations, scholarships).
Tap to view answer
Why are transfer payments such as old-age pensions, unemployment allowances, and scholarships excluded from National Income calculations?
Because no new physical goods or services are produced in the economy in exchange for them; they merely represent a redistribution of income from one group to another.
Tap to view answer
Is the 'Retirement Pension' received by government employees included in National Income?
Yes; because retirement pension is part of the deferred wage for productive services rendered earlier by the employee, not a transfer payment.
Tap to view answer
What is meant by 'Domestic Territory' or 'Economic Territory' in National Income Accounting?
The geographical territory administered by a government within which persons, goods, and capital circulate freely.
Tap to view answer
Indian embassies, high commissions, and military establishments located abroad are considered part of which country's domestic territory?
India's domestic territory (even though they are physically situated on the geographical land of Washington, London, or Tokyo).

Production carried out by ships, aircraft, and oil rigs operated by Indian residents in international waters is counted in whose domestic territory?
In India's domestic territory and India's Gross Domestic Product (GDP).

Why is regular and scientific measurement of National Income essential for a modern nation?
To gauge the direction and pace of economic growth, formulate budgetary policies, assess living standards, and determine international creditworthiness.

How do National Income figures help measure a country's 'Economic Growth Rate'?
The annual percentage growth rate of Real GDP at constant prices accurately indicates whether the economy is in recession, stagnant, or expanding rapidly.

What is the significance of National Income data in 'Macroeconomic Policymaking and Budgetary Planning'?

How does the sectoral classification (Sectoral Composition) of National Income reveal the 'structural transformation' of an economy?
By tracking changes over time in the percentage shares of the primary (agriculture), secondary (industry), and tertiary (services) sectors.

Which key indicator is derived from National Income to assess the average standard of living of citizens?

Is Per Capita Income a complete and flawless indicator of citizens' standard of living and well-being in a country?
No; because it is an arithmetic mean that does not reveal severe income inequality, concentration of wealth, or quality of life (health/education) in society.

How do National Income figures assist in 'International Economic Comparisons' between different countries?
In comparing the economic size, market potential, and debt-repayment capacity of different countries on the basis of US Dollars and Purchasing Power Parity (PPP).

What is the importance of National Income in determining contributions and voting quotas in the International Monetary Fund (IMF) and the United Nations (UN)?

Why do National Income figures form the basis for evaluating financial indicators like the 'Tax-to-GDP Ratio' and 'Debt-to-GDP Ratio'?
To measure how much tax revenue the government is able to mobilize relative to the actual size of the national economy and what burden of sovereign public debt it carries.

How does measuring National Income make it possible to assess 'inequality in the distribution of income and wealth' in a country?

What is the practical significance of National Income forecasts for businesses, industrialists, and the corporate sector?
In assessing future market demand, consumer purchasing power, and overall economic trends to plan new factories and capital investments.

How are a country's 'Savings and Investment Rates' determined from National Income measurement?
Subtracting total consumption expenditure from National Income yields the national savings rate and the exact percentage of Gross Fixed Capital Formation (GFCF).

What is the direct relationship between Capital Formation and National Income?
The higher the rate of gross capital formation, the faster the growth in the economy's future productive capacity, labour productivity, and National Income.

Why does the central bank (RBI) analyze the 'Output Gap' of National Income while deciding monetary policy and interest rates?
To determine whether actual output exceeds potential output (risk of inflation) or falls short of it (risk of recession and unemployment), so that interest rates can be adjusted accordingly.

What is the United Nations 'System of National Accounts' (SNA)?
A standardized and universal global accounting framework for compiling National Income, jointly developed by the UN, IMF, World Bank, and OECD (current version: SNA 2008).

What is the standard and authoritative definition of 'Gross Domestic Product' (GDP)?
The total monetary market value of all final goods and services produced within the domestic/geographical territory of a country during a specified period (usually one financial year).
Tap to view answer
What does the word 'Gross' mean in the definition of GDP?
Total value calculated without deducting the wear-and-tear expenses (Depreciation) of machinery, buildings, and equipment used in the production process.
Tap to view answer
Which geographical boundary does the word 'Domestic' highlight in the definition of GDP?
All production taking place within the political and economic domestic territory of the country, whether carried out by its own citizens or by foreign companies/nationals.
Tap to view answer
What is meant by 'Product' in the definition of GDP?
The total real output of physical goods (cars, foodgrains, textiles) and intangible services (banking, education, healthcare, IT) produced in a financial year.
Tap to view answer
Why are only 'Final Goods and Services' included in the calculation of GDP?
To avoid the serious statistical error of 'Double Counting' caused by repeatedly adding the value of intermediate goods.
Tap to view answer
What are 'Final Goods' in economics?
Goods that have crossed the boundary line of production and are completely ready for final consumption (by consumers) or final capital investment (by firms).
Tap to view answer
What is the 'Problem of Double Counting' in National Income Accounting?
When the value of an intermediate good is counted repeatedly—both at the raw material stage and within the value of the final finished product—making National Income appear artificially inflated.
Tap to view answer
Which two main methods do economists use to avoid double counting?
1. Final Output Method (adding only the value of final consumer goods), and 2. Value Added Method (adding only the net value added at each stage of production).
Tap to view answer
What is the concept of 'Value Added' and what is its standard formula?
The net addition made to the value of a good at a specific stage of the production process [Value Added = Total Value of Output - Cost of Intermediate Consumption].
Tap to view answer
If a farmer sells wheat worth ₹1,000 to a miller, the miller sells flour worth ₹1,500 to a baker, and the baker sells bread worth ₹2,200 to the final consumer, how much value will be added to GDP?
A total of ₹2,200 (Farmer's value added ₹1,000 + Miller's ₹500 + Baker's ₹700 = ₹2,200).
Tap to view answer
Is the buying and selling of old houses, used cars, or second-hand goods added to the current year's GDP?
No; because they were produced in earlier years and were already counted in that year's GDP; adding them again would cause double counting.
Tap to view answer
Is the 'commission or brokerage' charged by brokers or dealers on the sale and purchase of old assets or real estate included in GDP?
Yes; because the commission or brokerage represents the value of a new productive service rendered by the broker in the current year.
Tap to view answer
Why are unpaid domestic chores performed by homemakers, such as cooking meals and caring for children at home, excluded from GDP?
Because these are non-market activities with no official market price, valuation, or formal financial transaction record.
Tap to view answer
Is agricultural output retained by farmers for their own family consumption (Self-Consumption in Agriculture) included in GDP or not?
Yes; it is included in GDP because an objective market value can be imputed to it and it is a primary productive activity.
Tap to view answer
Is the 'Imputed Rent of Owner-Occupied Dwellings' included in GDP?
Yes; if a person lives in their own house, the estimated market rent of that house is added to GDP as the output of housing services.
Tap to view answer
Why is income earned from illegal activities (smuggling, gambling, drug trafficking, black marketing) excluded from GDP?
Because they have no authentic accounting records and are unlawful activities considered to make a negative contribution to social welfare.
Tap to view answer
Why is the buying and selling of shares, bonds, and debentures in the stock market not counted in GDP?
Because it is merely a transfer of financial claims and paper ownership of assets from one person to another; it does not create any new physical good or service.

Production carried out by a foreign multinational company (such as Samsung or Apple) within the geographical territory of India is counted in whose GDP?
In India's GDP; because the production took place within India's domestic geographical territory.

Production carried out by an Indian company (such as Tata Motors or Infosys) at its plant located in the UK or the USA is counted in whose GDP?

What is the most fundamental difference between 'Gross Domestic Product' (GDP) and 'Gross National Product' (GNP)?
GDP is a concept based on 'geographical territory' (where production occurred), whereas GNP is a concept based on 'citizenship / residency' (who produced it).
Tap to view answer
What is the standard mathematical formula to derive GNP from GDP?
GNP = GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA).
Tap to view answer
What are the three main components of 'Net Factor Income from Abroad' (NFIA)?
1. Net compensation of employees, 2. Net income from property and entrepreneurship (interest, dividends, profits), and 3. Net retained earnings of foreign companies.
Tap to view answer
Why is India's Gross National Product (GNP) generally lower than its Gross Domestic Product (GDP)?
Because factor income flowing out of India to foreign investors (interest, dividends, royalties) exceeds the factor income brought into India by Indians from abroad (negative NFIA).

Which economic variable determines the difference between 'Gross' and 'Net' National Income indicators?
'Depreciation' (Consumption of Fixed Capital - CFC).

What is the definition of 'Depreciation' (Consumption of Fixed Capital) in economics?
The continuous decline in the monetary value of fixed assets (machines, equipment, plants) used in the production process due to normal wear and tear, passage of time, and obsolescence.
Tap to view answer
What is the formula for calculating Net Domestic Product (NDP)?
NDP = GDP - Depreciation.
Tap to view answer
How is Net National Product (NNP) obtained?
NNP = GNP - Depreciation, or NDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA).

What official designation is given to Net National Product when calculated at 'Factor Cost' (NNP at Factor Cost)?
True National Income (National Income - NI).

What is the difference between 'Factor Cost' and 'Market Price'?
Factor Cost is the total remuneration paid by producers to the factors of production, whereas Market Price includes Net Indirect Taxes (Indirect Taxes - Government Subsidies) added to Factor Cost.
Tap to view answer
What is the formula for calculating 'Net Indirect Taxes' (NIT)?
Net Indirect Taxes = Indirect Taxes (GST, excise duty, etc.) - Government Subsidies.
Tap to view answer
When indirect taxes collected by the government in an economy exceed the subsidies given, which is larger between MP and FC?
Market Price is greater than Factor Cost (Market Price > Factor Cost).

Before January 2015, on what basis was India's official headline GDP growth rate measured?

Under the international methodology adopted by the NSO in January 2015, on what basis is India's official GDP being measured?
Gross Domestic Product at Market Prices (GDP at Market Prices / GDP_MP).
Tap to view answer
What is the concept of 'Basic Price' adopted in the January 2015 reforms?
The price receivable by the producer from the purchaser that includes production taxes and excludes production subsidies, but excludes product taxes (such as GST).
Tap to view answer
What is the difference between 'Product Taxes' and 'Production Taxes'?
Product taxes are levied directly per unit of product produced or sold (e.g., GST, excise duty, customs duty), whereas production taxes are levied on the enterprise independent of the volume of production (e.g., land revenue, professional tax, stamp duty).
Tap to view answer
What are examples of 'Product Subsidies' and 'Production Subsidies'?
Product subsidies are paid per unit of sale (food, fertilizer, and LPG subsidies), whereas production subsidies are given to the enterprise as a whole (such as MSME interest subsidies and handloom subsidies).
Tap to view answer
What is the exact mathematical relationship among Factor Cost, Basic Price, and Market Price?
Basic Price = Factor Cost + Net Production Taxes; and Market Price = Basic Price + Net Product Taxes.
Tap to view answer
What is the relationship between 'Gross Value Added' (GVA) and Gross Domestic Product (GDP)?
GDP (at Market Prices) = Total GVA of all sectors (at Basic Prices) + Product Taxes - Product Subsidies.

What is the definition of 'Nominal GDP' (GDP at Current Prices)?
The total monetary value of final goods and services produced in the current financial year, calculated at the prevailing market prices of that same current year.
Tap to view answer
What is 'Real GDP' (GDP at Constant Prices)?
The total monetary value of final goods and services produced in the current year, calculated at the constant prices of a pre-determined fixed 'Base Year'.
Tap to view answer
Why is Real GDP considered more reliable than Nominal GDP for knowing economic development and the true growth rate of productive capacity?
Because Real GDP neutralizes the artificial and misleading effect of inflation (price rise) and reflects only the real net increase in physical output.
Tap to view answer
What is the mathematical formula for the 'GDP Deflator', which measures price changes across the entire economy?
GDP Deflator = (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) * 100.
Tap to view answer
What is the most prominent structural difference between the GDP Deflator and the Consumer Price Index (CPI)?
The GDP Deflator covers all domestically produced goods and services in the country and has flexible weights, whereas the CPI measures only a fixed basket of goods consumed by ordinary consumers.

Are price changes of goods imported from abroad included in the GDP Deflator?
No; the GDP Deflator includes only domestically produced goods, while imported goods remain completely excluded.

What are the three major established methods for measuring National Income / GDP?
1. Output / Value Added Method, 2. Income Method, and 3. Expenditure Method.

How is GDP calculated under the Output Method?
By summing the Gross Value Added (GVA) generated by all producing units across the primary, secondary, and tertiary sectors.

Under the Income Method, National Income is calculated by summing which components?
The sum of factor payments received by factors of production [Compensation of Employees + Operating Surplus (Rent, Interest, Profit) + Mixed Income of the Self-Employed].

Does a country's high GDP automatically prove that every citizen there is prosperous and happy?
Not at all; because GDP only reflects the total physical volume of production and does not show whether that output is distributed equitably among all sections of society.

How does the concept of 'Green GDP' differ from traditional GDP?

What is the final, concise macroeconomic conclusion of the entire study of National Income Part 1?
Gross Domestic Product (GDP) and National Income are the foremost thermometers for measuring the health, size, and pace of growth of any modern economy; while accurate valuation of final goods and avoidance of double counting present a realistic picture of production, true economic progress lies not merely in high GDP numbers, but in the equitable distribution of output and the qualitative upliftment of citizens' standard of living.
