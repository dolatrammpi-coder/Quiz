Poverty estimation committees (Alagh, Lakdawala, Tendulkar, Rangarajan), methodology, consumption baskets, poverty lines, comparative analysis, and policy impact

Quick Reference
Key Facts and Contributions
Poverty and Inequality Part 2
Poverty Committee / MethodologyKey FeatureRole in Poverty Measurement
Dr. Y. K. Alagh Task Force (1979)Minimum nutritional requirement: 2,400 kcal per person per day for rural areas and 2,100 kcal per person per day for urban areas.First official determination in India of a scientific poverty line (rural ₹49.09, urban ₹56.64) using a nutrition-based consumption expenditure basket.
Prof. D. T. Lakdawala Committee (1993)State-specific poverty lines based on the 1973-74 consumption basket; used CPI-AL for rural areas and CPI-IW for urban areas.First to incorporate inter-state differences in price levels and cost of living into the poverty line, revealing regional realities instead of a single national ratio.
Prof. Suresh Tendulkar Committee (2009)Moved away from the calorie norm, included private expenditure on health and education in the basket, and adopted the 1993-94 urban consumption basket as the all-India benchmark.Used the Mixed Recall Period (MRP); fixed the 2011-12 poverty line at ₹816/month (₹27.20/day) for rural and ₹1,000/month (₹33.33/day) for urban areas, estimating the national poverty rate at 21.9%.
Dr. C. Rangarajan Committee (2014)Independent normative survey covering three nutritional components (calories + protein + fat) alongside essential non-food expenses (rent, transport, education, clothing).Used the Modified Mixed Recall Period (MMRP); fixed the 2011-12 poverty line at ₹972/month (₹32.40/day) for rural and ₹1,407/month (₹46.90/day) for urban areas, estimating 29.5% (36.3 crore) of the population as poor.
N. C. Saxena Committee (2009)Constituted by the Ministry of Rural Development to identify rural Below Poverty Line (BPL) households through automatic inclusion, automatic exclusion, and a scoring/grading system.Formulated an administrative framework to identify the genuine rural poor for the Targeted Public Distribution System (TPDS) and welfare schemes.
S. R. Hashim Committee (2012)Constituted by the Planning Commission to identify the urban poor by classifying them across three dimensions of deprivation: residential, occupational, and social.Enabled targeted urban poverty alleviation schemes by identifying the urban homeless, informal workers, and socially excluded households.
Arvind Panagariya Task Force (2015)Constituted by NITI Aayog to review the poverty line and elimination strategies; emphasized multidimensional deprivations rather than an income-only threshold.Viewed the traditional poverty line as a tracker of welfare progress and advocated aspirational growth policies and saturation-based welfare drives.
National Multidimensional Poverty Index (NITI Aayog)Comprehensive measurement of simultaneous household deprivations across 12 indicators (health, education, standard of living) based on the Oxford OPHI methodology.Moved beyond the monetary poverty line to highlight actual physical deprivations; documented that 24.8 crore people escaped multidimensional poverty between 2013-14 and 2022-23.

The Planning Commission, which periodically constituted expert committees and task forces.

What is the correct chronological order of the major official expert committees constituted for poverty estimation in India?
1. Y. K. Alagh Task Force (1977-1979), 2. D. T. Lakdawala Committee (1989-1993), 3. Suresh Tendulkar Committee (2005-2009), and 4. C. Rangarajan Committee (2012-2014).

What was the official name of the task force constituted by the Planning Commission in 1977 under the chairmanship of Dr. Y. K. Alagh?
Task Force on Projections of Minimum Needs and Effective Consumption Demand.
Tap to view answer
In which year did the Dr. Y. K. Alagh Task Force submit its landmark report to the Planning Commission?
In 1979.
Tap to view answer
What was the minimum daily nutritional energy requirement recommended by the Alagh Task Force (1979)?
2,400 kcal per person per day for rural areas and 2,100 kcal per person per day for urban areas.
Tap to view answer
Why did the Alagh Task Force set a differential norm of 2,400 kcal for rural areas and 2,100 kcal for urban areas?
Because the rural population is engaged in agriculture and heavy manual labour requiring higher biological energy, whereas urban occupations generally involve less physical exertion.
Tap to view answer
What Monthly Per Capita Consumption Expenditure (MPCE) at 1973-74 prices did the Alagh Task Force fix to meet the required calorie intake?
₹49.09 per month for rural areas and ₹56.64 per month for urban areas.
Tap to view answer
From which year's National Sample Survey (NSSO) was the consumption expenditure data drawn in the Alagh Task Force methodology?
From the 28th Round of the NSSO consumption survey (1973-74).
Tap to view answer
Who chaired the 'Expert Group on Estimation of Proportion and Number of Poor' constituted by the Planning Commission in September 1989?
Prof. D. T. Lakdawala (former Deputy Chairman, Planning Commission).
Tap to view answer
When did the D. T. Lakdawala Committee submit its official report to the Planning Commission?
In July 1993 (formally accepted by the Government in 1997).
Tap to view answer
What was the most path-breaking recommendation of the Lakdawala Committee (1993)?
Determining 'State-Specific Poverty Lines' in place of a single uniform all-India poverty line.
Tap to view answer
Why did the Lakdawala Committee recommend state-specific poverty lines?
Because price levels, inflation rates, and the cost of living vary widely across different states (for example, Punjab versus Bihar).
Tap to view answer
Which price index was used in the Lakdawala Committee model to update the rural poverty line over time?
Consumer Price Index for Agricultural Labourers (CPI-AL).
Tap to view answer
Which price index was used in the Lakdawala Committee model to update the urban poverty line over time?
Consumer Price Index for Industrial Workers (CPI-IW).
Tap to view answer
Which basic nutritional standard did the Lakdawala Committee retain?
The calorie norm of 2,400 kcal (rural) and 2,100 kcal (urban) fixed by the Alagh Task Force.
Tap to view answer
How did the Lakdawala Committee calculate the national-level poverty line?
As a weighted average of the poverty lines and the number of poor across all states.
Tap to view answer
According to the Lakdawala methodology, what was India's total Head Count Ratio (HCR) of poverty recorded in FY 1993-94?
36.0% (37.3% in rural areas and 32.4% in urban areas).
Tap to view answer
What was considered the biggest limitation or weakness of the Lakdawala Committee methodology?
It relied on a static 20-year-old consumption basket from 1973-74 and ignored rising private out-of-pocket expenditure on health and education.
Tap to view answer
Who chaired the expert committee constituted by the Planning Commission in December 2005 to review the methodology for estimating poverty?
Prof. Suresh D. Tendulkar (former Chairman, National Statistical Commission).
Tap to view answer
In which year did the Suresh Tendulkar Committee submit its landmark report to the Planning Commission?
In November 2009.
Tap to view answer
What were the three most significant conceptual and statistical shifts adopted by the Suresh Tendulkar Committee?
1. Moving away entirely from the rigid calorie consumption norm, 2. Incorporating private expenditure on health and education, and 3. Adopting a uniform reference consumption basket for both rural and urban areas.
Tap to view answer
Why did the Tendulkar Committee reject the old calorie consumption norm in poverty measurement?
Because no direct correlation was found between calorie intake and nutritional/health outcomes, and actual consumption patterns were shifting from cereals toward non-food items.
Tap to view answer
Which consumption basket did the Tendulkar Committee adopt as the all-India reference basket?
The official 1993-94 Urban Consumption Basket.
Tap to view answer
Which statistical method did the Tendulkar Committee use to align the rural poverty line with the urban consumption basket?
The Fisher Price Index, to adjust for differences in the cost of living between rural and urban areas.
Tap to view answer
Which NSSO consumption measurement approach was mandatorily adopted by the Tendulkar Committee?
The Mixed Recall Period (MRP).
Tap to view answer
According to the Tendulkar Committee, what was India's total national poverty ratio estimated for FY 2004-05?
37.2% (41.8% in rural areas and 25.7% in urban areas).
Tap to view answer
Why was the Tendulkar Committee's 2004-05 estimate of 37.2% higher than the old Lakdawala methodology's estimate (27.5%)?
Because the Tendulkar methodology included essential spending on health, education, and clothing, which raised the rural poverty line threshold.
Tap to view answer
Under the Tendulkar Committee methodology, what were the rural and urban poverty lines fixed at 2011-12 prices?
₹816 per month (approx. ₹27.20 per day) for rural areas and ₹1,000 per month (approx. ₹33.33 per day) for urban areas.
Tap to view answer
According to the Tendulkar Committee, what was the monthly poverty line expenditure for an average five-member household in 2011-12?
₹4,080 per family per month in rural areas and ₹5,000 per family per month in urban areas.
Tap to view answer
Based on the Tendulkar methodology, what was the total poverty Head Count Ratio (HCR) recorded in India in FY 2011-12?
21.9% (25.7% in rural areas and 13.7% in urban areas).
Tap to view answer
According to the Tendulkar Committee data, what was the total number of poor people in India in 2011-12?
Approximately 26.98 crore (269.8 million poor; 21.67 crore rural and 5.31 crore urban).

According to the Tendulkar methodology, by how many percentage points did the poverty rate decline between 2004-05 (37.2%) and 2011-12 (21.9%)?

Which state recorded the highest poverty Head Count Ratio (HCR) in the Tendulkar Committee's 2011-12 poverty estimates?

What was the main controversy among civil society and economists regarding the Tendulkar Committee report?
The daily thresholds of ₹27.20 (rural) and ₹33.33 (urban) were criticized as unacceptably low, making a life of basic human dignity impossible at those levels.

What minimum daily nutritional requirements were fixed by the Rangarajan Committee?

On what basis did the Rangarajan Committee determine non-food expenses beyond nutrition?
By conducting a separate statistical assessment of actual consumption levels for essential non-food items such as clothing, housing, education, and transport.

What final recommendation did the Arvind Panagariya Task Force make regarding the determination of the poverty line in India?
That a separate expert committee should be formed to fix a new official poverty line, while SECC deprivation data should be used for delivering welfare schemes.
