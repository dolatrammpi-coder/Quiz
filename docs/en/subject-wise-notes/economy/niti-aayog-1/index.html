NITI Aayog Part 1

Comprehensive analysis of the formation of NITI Aayog, institutional structure, ideological transition from the Planning Commission to NITI Aayog, key objectives, and cooperative and competitive federalism

Key Facts & Contributions
NITI Aayog Part 1
NITI Aayog AspectKey FeatureRole in Governance / Development
Institutional Formation & NatureView Answer Non-constitutional and non-statutory apex policy think-tank established on 1 January 2015 through an executive resolution of the Union Cabinet.View Answer Providing strategic, technical, and policy advice to the government; transforming the old centralized control mechanism into a modern knowledge-based system.
Governing CouncilView Answer Chaired by the Prime Minister, comprising Chief Ministers of all States and Lieutenant Governors/Administrators of Union Territories; the supreme policy forum.View Answer Ensuring direct participation of states in policy formulation; shaping national development priorities in the spirit of 'Team India'.
Cooperative FederalismView Answer Equal partnership between the Centre and the States; complete abandonment of the old 'One size fits all' approach.View Answer Formulating policies aligned with the specific geographical and social priorities of states and institutionally strengthening Centre-State coordination.
Competitive FederalismView Answer Transparent, data-driven ranking of States and Union Territories across various socio-economic indicators (SDGs, innovation, health, exports).View Answer Fostering healthy developmental competition among states, incentivizing better performance, and promoting the exchange of best practices.
Bottom-Up ApproachView Answer Initiating credible development planning at the village level and progressively integrating plans at higher administrative tiers.View Answer Making grassroots realities the foundation of national development policies and giving practical shape to decentralized democratic planning.
Development Partner & Knowledge HubView Answer Two core hubs: 'Team India Hub' (Centre-State interface) and 'Knowledge and Innovation Hub' (think-tank research capability).View Answer Integrating cutting-edge research, domain expertise, global best practices, and technological knowledge into India's development agenda.
Separation of Financial PowersView Answer Completely divested of financial powers to allocate central plan grants and funds to states like the Planning Commission (powers now vest with the Ministry of Finance).View Answer Eliminating economic bias and financial dependency to serve as an impartial intellectual and strategic advisor to the states.
Strategic & Long-Term Vision FrameworkView Answer Replacing traditional 5-year plans with a 15-year 'Vision', 7-year 'Strategy', and 3-year 'Action Agenda'.View Answer Adapting the economy to dynamic global conditions and charting a clear roadmap toward the national goals of 'Viksit Bharat @2047'.

In which year, through which instrument, and under whose chairmanship was the Planning Commission established?
On 15 March 1950, by a resolution of the Union Cabinet, under the chairmanship of India's first Prime Minister, Jawaharlal Nehru.
Tap to view answer
On which model was the foundational theoretical framework of the Planning Commission's development philosophy based?
On the socialist centralized planning system of the Soviet Union (USSR) (the Nehru-Mahalanobis model).
Tap to view answer
As which approach was the planning methodology of the Planning Commission known?
The 'Top-Down Approach' (command and imperative planning).
Tap to view answer
What was the biggest structural weakness of the Planning Commission's 'Top-Down Approach'?
The 'One-Size-Fits-All' policy, where uniform policies for all states were decided centrally at Yojana Bhavan in New Delhi.
Tap to view answer
Which new administrative approach did NITI Aayog adopt in place of the Planning Commission's top-down model?
The 'Bottom-Up Approach'.
Tap to view answer
How does NITI Aayog's 'Bottom-Up Approach' operate?
By developing mechanisms to formulate credible plans at the village level and progressively aggregating them at higher levels (block, district, state, and national).
Tap to view answer
Which immensely powerful financial authority did the Planning Commission possess that NITI Aayog does not have?
The power to allocate public financial funds (plan grants) to states and central ministries for development schemes.
Tap to view answer
Under which formula was the financial allocation to states by the Planning Commission regulated?
The Gadgil-Mukherjee Formula.
Tap to view answer
During the Planning Commission era, what situation did State Chief Ministers face while seeking financial assistance?
They had to appear before the Planning Commission like supplicants to seek financial approval for their state plans.
Tap to view answer
After the abolition of the Planning Commission, where is the responsibility for transferring financial resources from the Centre to the States now centralized?
With the Union Ministry of Finance and the statutory recommendations of the Constitutional Finance Commission (Article 280).
Tap to view answer
What has been the biggest theoretical advantage of depriving NITI Aayog of financial allocation powers?
Freed from financial leverage or bias, NITI Aayog has emerged as an impartial, independent, and intellectual strategic advisor (a pure think-tank) to both the Centre and the States.
Tap to view answer
During the Planning Commission era, which was the only formal forum for dialogue with State Chief Ministers?
The National Development Council (NDC, constituted in 1952).
Tap to view answer
How was the functioning of the National Development Council (NDC) different from and weaker than NITI Aayog's Governing Council?
The NDC met only once a year or sporadically and had been reduced to a mere rubber-stamp forum for approving pre-drafted Five-Year Plans.
Tap to view answer
How did NITI Aayog's Governing Council overcome this weakness of the NDC?
By making State Chief Ministers permanent and regular partners from the initial stages of policy formulation, involving them directly in decision-making.
Tap to view answer
What change occurred in NITI Aayog regarding the role of part-time members and domain experts compared to the Planning Commission?
The Planning Commission had no regular provision for part-time members, whereas NITI Aayog has an institutional provision for 2 part-time members from premier research institutions and global experts.

Which was India's last Five-Year Plan, and what was its tenure?
The 12th Five-Year Plan (tenure: 2012 to 2017).

After the conclusion of the 12th Five-Year Plan (31 March 2017), which new policy planning framework did NITI Aayog adopt?
A three-tier planning framework: 15-Year Vision, 7-Year Strategy, and 3-Year Action Agenda.

What is the objective of the '15-Year Vision Document' in NITI Aayog's three-tier planning framework?
To prepare a comprehensive roadmap for achieving long-term national goals, social aspirations, and international Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).
Tap to view answer
What is the role of the '7-Year Strategy Document' in NITI Aayog's framework?
To translate the 15-year long-term vision into medium-term strategic policy actions and sectoral priorities.
Tap to view answer
What does the '3-Year Action Agenda' ensure in NITI Aayog's framework?
It outlines immediate policy reforms, government expenditure allocations, and concrete actions for near-term priorities (first agenda: 2017-18 to 2019-20).

Why was NITI Aayog's three-tier framework considered more effective than the rigid 5-year cycle of the Planning Commission?
Because technology and markets evolve rapidly in the 21st-century global economy; the three-tier framework offers greater flexibility and scope for periodic review.

What is the standard constitutional and administrative definition of 'Cooperative Federalism' in Indian polity?
A system where the Centre and the States work together as equal partners through mutual cooperation and consensus on national priorities, rather than in a superior-subordinate hierarchy.

How has NITI Aayog become the primary institutional platform for realizing Cooperative Federalism?
By placing State Chief Ministers at the centre of policy formulation through the Governing Council and directly addressing their regional challenges.

What is the concept of 'Competitive Federalism' championed by NITI Aayog?

What is the final and concise macroeconomic conclusion of the complete study of NITI Aayog Part 1?
NITI Aayog represents a transformative paradigm shift in 21st-century Indian administrative thinking; by dismantling the Planning Commission's centralized 'command-and-control' model and institutionalizing the 'Team India' spirit on the foundation of 'Cooperative Federalism', it is paving the way for an Atmanirbhar and developed global power through competitive rankings, innovation missions, and bottom-up planning.
