One-Liner Questions

What is the standard and authoritative definition of 'Inclusive Growth' in macroeconomics?
An economic growth process that not only accelerates economic output (GDP) rapidly, but also creates equitable opportunities for development and ensures fair distribution of growth benefits across all social sections, especially the poor and marginalized.

How is inclusive growth fundamentally different from the traditional 'Trickle-Down Theory'?
The trickle-down theory assumed that the benefits of high GDP growth would automatically filter down to the bottom, whereas inclusive growth holds that the poor must be made direct participants and drivers of the growth process rather than passive beneficiaries.

What are the two interconnected, inseparable dimensions of inclusive growth?
1. Process Dimension (participation—ensuring employment and opportunities for everyone in the economic process), and 2. Outcome Dimension (equitable distribution of development benefits and income).

What function does the pillar of 'Social Security' perform in inclusive growth?
Providing a universal minimum safety net to protect vulnerable sections from economic ruin during unforeseen adversities such as old age, illness, disability, unemployment, or natural disasters.

Why is 'Environmental Sustainability' an indispensable pillar in the process of inclusive growth?

According to the World Bank, how does the concept of 'Shared Prosperity' measure inclusive growth?
The income or consumption growth rate of the poorest 40% (Bottom 40%) of a country's population consistently exceeding the national average growth rate.
