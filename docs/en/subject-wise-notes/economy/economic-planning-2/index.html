Economic Planning Part 2

Shifting priorities of Five-Year Plans (1st to 12th FYP), regional balance, the journey toward self-reliance, and policy impact

🔍
Quick Reference
Key Facts & Contributions
Economic Planning Part 2
Planning AspectKey PriorityImpact on Development
First Five-Year Plan (1951-56)View Answer: Supreme emphasis on agriculture, multipurpose major irrigation (Bhakra Nangal, DVC, Hirakud), and price stability (Harrod-Domar Model).View Answer: Historic 36% increase in foodgrain production, effective control over inflation, and a strong growth rate of 3.6% against a target of 2.1%.
Second Five-Year Plan (1956-61)View Answer: Rapid development of basic and heavy capital industries and minerals under the Nehru-Mahalanobis strategy.View Answer: Establishment of Bhilai, Durgapur, and Rourkela steel plants; creation of a modern industrial backbone, though neglect of agriculture sowed the seeds of a food crisis.
Third Five-Year Plan (1961-66)View Answer: Achieving self-sustaining growth and self-reliance in both foodgrains and industry.View Answer: Plan failed severely due to the 1962 China War, 1965 Pakistan War, and severe drought of 1965-66 (target 5.6%, achieved only 2.8%); devaluation of the Rupee.
Fourth Five-Year Plan (1969-74)View Answer: Growth with stability and progressive achievement of self-reliance (Gadgil Formula and Ashok Rudra-Alan Manne Model).View Answer: Nationalization of 14 major banks, intensive spread of the Green Revolution, though inflation peaked due to the 1971 Bangladesh War and 1973 oil shock.
Fifth & Sixth Five-Year Plans (1974-85)View Answer: Poverty eradication ('Garibi Hatao'), Minimum Needs Programme (MNP), and direct poverty alleviation programmes (IRDP, NREP, TRYSEM).View Answer: First notable decline in the population below the poverty line, expansion of agricultural growth, and growth rate crossing 5.5% in the 1980s.
Eighth Five-Year Plan (1992-97)View Answer: Indicative Planning after the 1991 reforms, human resource development, and making the private sector the economic engine.View Answer: High growth rate of 6.8% against a target of 5.6%, restoration of foreign exchange reserves, and global integration of the Indian economy.
Eleventh & Twelfth Plans (2007-17)View Answer: Faster, Sustainable and More Inclusive Growth; education, health, and infrastructure.View Answer: India recorded high average growth of 7-8%, massive expansion of infrastructure (roads, power, telecom); end of the Planning Commission era.
Regional Balance & Self-Reliance FrameworkView Answer: Special area programmes for backward regions (DPAP, DDP, HADP), Gadgil-Mukherjee Formula, and modern 'Atmanirbhar Bharat' and PLI campaigns.View Answer: Gradual reduction in regional disparities, indigenization of manufacturing capacity, and self-reliance in strategic sectors (defence, space, renewable energy).

One-Liner Questions

In which year and from what date were Five-Year Plans formally launched in India?
From April 1, 1951 (First Five-Year Plan: 1951-1956).
Tap to view answer
How many Five-Year Plans in total were implemented in India between 1951 and 2017?
A total of 12 Five-Year Plans; the 12th Plan (2012-2017) was the final Five-Year Plan.
Tap to view answer
What major role was assigned to the state in the Indian economy through the Five-Year Plans?
A multifaceted role as a direct producer, major investor, economic director, and regulator.
Tap to view answer
What was the division of labour between the Planning Commission and the National Development Council (NDC) in formulating Five-Year Plans?
The Planning Commission prepared the technical Draft Plan, while the NDC, comprising Chief Ministers and Union Ministers, gave it final national approval.
Tap to view answer
On which famous economic model was the First Five-Year Plan (1951-56) based?
On the Harrod-Domar Growth Model.
Tap to view answer
What was the primary reason for keeping the central focus of the First Five-Year Plan on agriculture and irrigation?
To tackle the severe foodgrain crisis caused by Partition and World War II, rehabilitate refugees, and control war-induced inflation.
Tap to view answer
Which were the three historic multipurpose river valley projects launched during the First Five-Year Plan?
Bhakra Nangal Project, Damodar Valley Corporation (DVC), and Hirakud Dam Project.
Tap to view answer
What were the targeted growth rate and actual achieved growth rate in the First Five-Year Plan?
The target rate was 2.1%, while actual achievement was 3.6%, making the plan unexpectedly highly successful.
Tap to view answer
Which historic programme for rural reconstruction was launched on October 2, 1952 during the First Plan period?
The Community Development Programme (CDP).
Tap to view answer
Which institutions were established at the end of the First Five-Year Plan in 1956 for higher technical education?
Five Indian Institutes of Technology (IITs) and the University Grants Commission (UGC).
Tap to view answer
On the model of which eminent Indian statistician and economist was the Second Five-Year Plan (1956-61) based?
Prof. Prasanta Chandra Mahalanobis (P. C. Mahalanobis Model / 4-Sector Model).
Tap to view answer
What was the most central economic philosophy of the Nehru-Mahalanobis strategy?
Accelerated public investment in basic and heavy industries (Heavy Capital Goods) to ensure domestic production of machines to make machines.
Tap to view answer
Which three major public sector steel plants were established with foreign collaboration during the Second Five-Year Plan?
1. Bhilai (with Soviet Union collaboration), 2. Rourkela (with West Germany collaboration), and 3. Durgapur (with British collaboration).
Tap to view answer
What growth target was set in the Second Five-Year Plan and what was achieved?
The target was 4.5%, while actual achievement was 4.27% (about 4.3%), which was considered satisfactory.
Tap to view answer
What serious negative impact did the excessive emphasis on heavy industries in the Second Plan have on the country's external balance?
Heavy imports of capital machinery rapidly depleted foreign exchange reserves, and in 1957-58 India faced its first severe Balance of Payments (BoP) pressure after Independence.
Tap to view answer
What was the primary declared goal of the Third Five-Year Plan (1961-66)?
Making the Indian economy 'Self-Reliant and Self-Generating'.
Tap to view answer
On which model or framework was the Third Five-Year Plan based?
On the John Sandee and Sukhamoy Chakravarty model (extending the ideas of Prof. P. C. Mahalanobis).
Tap to view answer
Which famous steel plant located in Jharkhand was established in 1964 during the Third Five-Year Plan with Soviet Union collaboration?
Bokaro Steel Plant.
Tap to view answer
Why is the Third Five-Year Plan considered the most unsuccessful plan in the history of Indian economic planning?
Because against a growth target of 5.6%, only 2.8% could be achieved, owing to the 1962 China War, the 1965 Pakistan War, and the worst drought of the century in 1965-66.
Tap to view answer
By what name is the period from 1966 to 1969 following the failure of the Third Plan known?
'Plan Holiday,' during which three separate Annual Plans were implemented.
Tap to view answer
By what percentage did the Government of India devalue the Rupee in June 1966 during the Plan Holiday (1966-69)?
A sharp devaluation of 36.5% (to restore foreign aid and under IMF/World Bank conditions).
Tap to view answer
Which epoch-making technological revolution began in the agriculture sector during the Plan Holiday (1966-69)?
The Green Revolution, under which an intensive package of High-Yielding Variety (HYV) seeds, chemical fertilizers, and assured irrigation was implemented.
Tap to view answer
What was the official objective of the Fourth Five-Year Plan (1969-74)?
'Growth with Stability' and 'Progressive Achievement of Self-Reliance'.
Tap to view answer
On which model was the Fourth Five-Year Plan based?
On the Open Consistency Model of Ashok Rudra and Alan S. Manne (and under the guidance of Planning Commission Deputy Chairman D. R. Gadgil).
Tap to view answer
Which historic financial step was taken in the very first year (July 1969) of the Fourth Five-Year Plan?
Nationalization of 14 major private commercial banks.
Tap to view answer
Which external events severely derailed the financial targets of the Fourth Plan?
The 1971 India-Pakistan War, the massive influx of Bangladeshi refugees, and the 1973 first global oil crisis (OPEC Oil Shock).
Tap to view answer
What were the targeted and actual growth rates of the Fourth Five-Year Plan?
The target was 5.7%, but actual achievement was only 3.3%.
Tap to view answer
What were the two main central objectives of the Fifth Five-Year Plan (1974-79)?
1. Poverty Eradication ('Garibi Hatao') and 2. Attainment of Self-Reliance.
Tap to view answer
Who prepared the draft of the Fifth Five-Year Plan?
D. P. Dhar.
Tap to view answer
Which programme was launched in 1974 during the Fifth Plan period for citizens at the lowest rung of society?
The Minimum Needs Programme (MNP).
Tap to view answer
Which 20-point programme was launched in 1975 during the Fifth Five-Year Plan for the upliftment of the rural poor?
The Twenty Point Programme (TPP).
Tap to view answer
Who terminated the Fifth Five-Year Plan one year before its completion (in 1978)?
The newly formed Janata Party government led by Morarji Desai.
Tap to view answer
What is the plan run by the Janata Party government between 1978 and 1980 called?
The 'Rolling Plan,' whose framework was prepared by Prof. D. T. Lakdawala.
Tap to view answer
By which government was the Sixth Five-Year Plan (1980-85) formally relaunched?
By Indira Gandhi's Congress government, which returned to power in 1980 (1980-85).
Tap to view answer
On which strategies was the primary thrust of the Sixth Five-Year Plan placed?
On direct poverty alleviation programmes (such as IRDP, NREP, RLEGP, TRYSEM) and economic and technological modernization.
Tap to view answer
Which apex institution for agriculture and rural credit was established on July 12, 1982 during the Sixth Five-Year Plan?
The National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development (NABARD).
Tap to view answer
Which bank was established in 1982 during the Sixth Five-Year Plan to finance foreign trade?
The Export-Import Bank of India (EXIM Bank).
Tap to view answer
What was the actual growth achievement in the Sixth Five-Year Plan?
A growth rate of 5.66% was achieved against a target of 5.2%, breaking the economy out of the traditional 'Hindu Rate of Growth' (3.5%).
Tap to view answer
What was the central guiding slogan of the Seventh Five-Year Plan (1985-90)?
'Food, Work and Productivity'.
Tap to view answer
On which model was the Seventh Five-Year Plan based?
The 'Wage-Goods Model' (a refined version of the Vakil and Brahmananda model) under the guidance of Pranab Mukherjee and Manmohan Singh.
Tap to view answer
Which famous integrated employment scheme for rural youth was launched in 1989 during the Seventh Plan?
Jawahar Rozgar Yojana (JRY).
Tap to view answer
In which plan, for the first time in the history of Indian planning, was private sector capital investment given greater priority than the public sector?
In the Seventh Five-Year Plan (1985-90).
Tap to view answer
What were the targeted and actual growth rates of the Seventh Plan?
The target was 5.0%, while the actual growth rate was 6.01% (a highly successful plan).
Tap to view answer
Why could the Eighth Five-Year Plan not be launched on time between 1990 and 1992?
Due to political instability of rapidly changing coalition governments at the Centre and the severe 1991 Balance of Payments (BoP) crisis (two Annual Plans were run).
Tap to view answer
Under what historic circumstances was the Eighth Five-Year Plan (1992-97) launched?
Immediately after the July 1991 LPG (Liberalization, Privatization, Globalization) economic reforms, under the leadership of Prime Minister P. V. Narasimha Rao and Finance Minister Dr. Manmohan Singh.
Tap to view answer
On which model was the Eighth Five-Year Plan (1992-97) based?
On the John W. Miller Model and the Rao-Manmohan Model.
Tap to view answer
What was set as the top priority of the Eighth Five-Year Plan?
Human Resource Development, encompassing employment generation, universalization of primary education, and public health.
Tap to view answer
Which new methodology of planning did India formally adopt in the Eighth Five-Year Plan?
Indicative Planning, where the state became a guide and facilitator of the private sector rather than issuing commands.
Tap to view answer
Of which global trade organization did India become a founding member on January 1, 1995 during the Eighth Five-Year Plan?
The World Trade Organization (WTO).
Tap to view answer
How did economic growth perform in the Eighth Five-Year Plan?
An impressive growth rate of 6.68% (around 6.8%) was achieved against a target of 5.6%.
Tap to view answer
On what historic occasion was the Ninth Five-Year Plan (1997-2002) launched, and what was its main motto?
On the 50th anniversary of India's Independence; its motto was 'Growth with Social Justice and Equity'.
Tap to view answer
Why did the Ninth Five-Year Plan remain confined to 5.35% against its growth target of 6.5%?
Due to the 1997 East Asian financial crisis, international sanctions following the 1998 Pokhran nuclear tests, and the 1999 Kargil War.
Tap to view answer
Which historic scheme was launched on December 25, 2000 during the Ninth Plan to improve rural infrastructure?
Pradhan Mantri Gram Sadak Yojana (PMGSY).
Tap to view answer
What was the most unique methodology of the Tenth Five-Year Plan (2002-2007)?
For the first time, setting 11 specific 'Monitorable Targets' such as poverty, literacy, infant mortality rate, and forest cover, rather than focusing solely on GDP growth.
Tap to view answer
Within what timeframe was the ambitious target of doubling per capita income set in the Tenth Plan?
A target of doubling India's per capita income over the next 10 years.
Tap to view answer
What was the actual growth achievement in the Tenth Five-Year Plan?
A high growth rate of 7.6% to 7.8% was recorded against the ambitious target of 8.0%.
Tap to view answer
What was the main title/theme of the Eleventh Five-Year Plan (2007-2012)?
'Towards Faster and More Inclusive Growth'.
Tap to view answer
Under the guidance of which renowned economist was the draft of the Eleventh Five-Year Plan prepared?
Dr. C. Rangarajan (then Chairman of the Prime Minister's Economic Advisory Council).
Tap to view answer
Despite the 2008 Global Financial Crisis, what average annual growth did the Eleventh Plan record?
A high growth rate of about 8.0% (though the target was 9%), which was the highest average growth rate of any plan in India's history.
Tap to view answer
What was the official guiding motto of the Twelfth Five-Year Plan (2012-2017)?
'Faster, Sustainable and More Inclusive Growth'.
Tap to view answer
How much investment was estimated for infrastructure development in the Twelfth Five-Year Plan?
About 1 trillion US dollars (₹50-55 lakh crore), with a 50% share targeted from the private sector (PPP model).
Tap to view answer
Why did the Twelfth Five-Year Plan become redundant even before its scheduled end (March 31, 2017)?
Because the Planning Commission was dissolved on January 1, 2015, and the NDA government decided to end Soviet-style five-year planning.

What is meant by 'Regional Imbalance' in economic geography and development economics?
Sharp disparities in per capita income, industrialization, infrastructure, and human development levels across different geographical regions, states, or sub-regions of a country.
Tap to view answer
According to Gunnar Myrdal, what is the 'Backwash Effect' that widens regional inequality?
The draining of skilled human labour, capital, and natural resources from backward regions toward developed, prosperous regions driven by market forces.
Tap to view answer
What is Gunnar Myrdal's 'Spread Effect'?
The outward trickle of economic prosperity, technology, demand, and capital from developed centres into surrounding backward rural areas.
Tap to view answer
Which two similar concepts did Albert Hirschman propound in the context of regional development?
The 'Polarization Effect' and the 'Trickling-Down Effect'.
Tap to view answer
What was the colonial historical background of regional disparity in India at the time of Independence?
British rule had developed only port Presidency cities (Calcutta, Bombay, Madras) and raw-material supply hubs, while the interior hinterlands remained completely backward.
Tap to view answer
Which public policy did planners in India adopt in the 1950s to achieve regional balance?
Direct establishment of massive Central Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs), mines, and steel townships in backward and mineral-rich states.
Tap to view answer
What was the main objective of the 'Freight Equalization Policy' implemented in 1952?
To make basic industrial raw materials like coal, iron, and steel available at uniform freight rates across the entire country regardless of distance from the factory location.
Tap to view answer
What devastating economic impact did the Freight Equalization Policy have on mineral-rich states like Bihar, West Bengal, Odisha, and Madhya Pradesh?
These states were stripped of their natural local geographical comparative advantage; industrialists transported raw materials cheaply from here to set up factories in western and southern states, causing the de-industrialization of eastern India.
Tap to view answer
In which year and on the recommendation of which committee was the Freight Equalization Policy abolished?
In the year 1993, on the recommendation of the Pranab Mukherjee Committee.
Tap to view answer
Which formula was adopted by the Planning Commission in 1969 to allocate Central Plan Assistance to the states?
The Gadgil Formula, which was approved by the National Development Council (NDC) at the beginning of the Fourth Plan.
Tap to view answer
In the Gadgil-Mukherjee Formula (revised 1991), what were the weightages of various criteria in the 100% distribution of central assistance?
1. Population (1971 Census) = 60%, 2. Per capita income / distance = 25%, 3. Fiscal and tax performance = 7.5%, and 4. Special regional problems = 7.5%.
Tap to view answer
Which states were first granted 'Special Category Status' (SCS) under the Gadgil Formula?
Assam, Nagaland, and Jammu & Kashmir in 1969 (due to difficult hilly terrain, strategic border location, and low economic base).
Tap to view answer
What extraordinary financial benefit did states with Special Category Status (SCS) receive in Central Plan Assistance?
90% of central assistance was received as a Grant (non-repayable) and only 10% as a loan (whereas normal states received 30% grant and 70% loan).
Tap to view answer
What impact did the recommendations of the 14th Finance Commission (2015) have on the 'Special Category Status' arrangement?
After increasing tax devolution from 32% to 42%, this formal distinction between general and special category states was virtually ended (except for the North-Eastern and 3 Himalayan states).
Tap to view answer
In which year was the Drought Prone Areas Programme (DPAP) launched?
In 1973-74 (at the end of the Fourth Plan), to restore water, soil, and vegetation balance in drought-prone areas and reduce regional disparities.
Tap to view answer
In which year and for which regions was the Desert Development Programme (DDP) launched?
In 1977-78, to check desertification in the hot deserts of Rajasthan, Gujarat, and Haryana as well as the cold deserts of Ladakh and Lahaul-Spiti.
Tap to view answer
What was the main objective of the Hill Area Development Programme (HADP)?
To develop horticulture, forestry, and infrastructure while protecting the local environment in ecologically fragile hill regions.
Tap to view answer
In which Five-Year Plan was the Tribal Sub-Plan (TSP) strategy introduced?
In the Fifth Five-Year Plan (1974-79), under which states had to mandatorily earmark a portion of their total plan budget in proportion to the tribal population.
Tap to view answer
In which year was the Special Component Plan for Scheduled Castes (SCs) launched?
In the year 1979-80 (at the beginning of the Sixth Plan).
Tap to view answer
What was the 'Backward Regions Grant Fund' (BRGF) launched by the Central Government in 2006 to bridge regional disparities?
Special financial assistance to bridge infrastructure gaps through local governance (Panchayats) in the 250 most backward districts of the country.
Tap to view answer
What is the 'Aspirational Districts Programme' (ADP) launched by NITI Aayog in January 2018?
Rapid transformation of health, education, agriculture, water, and infrastructure in the 112 most socio-economically backward districts of the country through data-driven ranking.
Tap to view answer
Which are the three main operational principles (3 Cs) of the Aspirational Districts Programme (ADP)?
1. Convergence (of central and state schemes), 2. Collaboration (of citizens and officials), and 3. Competition (monthly delta ranking among districts).

Which new programme did NITI Aayog launch at the local level in 2023 on the lines of the Aspirational Districts Programme?
The 'Aspirational Blocks Programme' (ABP), which focuses on the 500 most backward blocks in the country.

Which is the statutory apex body constituted for the balanced development of the North-Eastern states?
The North Eastern Council (NEC), constituted under the North Eastern Council Act, 1971 (headquarters: Shillong).

What minimum percentage of the budget of central ministries is mandatorily required to be spent on the development of the North-Eastern Region (NER)?
At least 10% of the total budgetary support (Non-Lapsable Central Pool of Resources - NLCPR).

Which central scheme was launched in 2022 for infrastructure and social development in the North-East?

Tap to view answer
Why did regional inequality among states in India widen further after the 1991 economic liberalization?
Because private and foreign investment flowed only to those states (western and southern) that already had better ports, power, skilled labour, and strong law and order.

Which major statistical indices are used in economics to measure regional imbalance?
The Coefficient of Variation and the Gini Coefficient of Gross State Domestic Product (GSDP).

Currently in India, how wide is the gap in per capita income between the top state and the bottom-most state?
The per capita income of top states (such as Goa, Sikkim) is about 7 to 8 times higher than that of the lowest state (Bihar).

For studying which region did the Dr. Vijay Kelkar Committee (2013) submit a detailed report in the context of regional imbalance in India?
For studying the regional backwardness and development imbalance of Vidarbha and Marathwada in Maharashtra.

What is the true concept of 'Self-Reliance' in development economics?
Depending on one's own domestic resources to meet essential economic needs and development priorities, rather than on foreign aid or concessional loans.
Tap to view answer
In economics, what is the difference between 'Self-Reliance' and 'Self-Sufficiency / Autarky'?
Self-sufficiency means producing all goods domestically and reducing foreign trade to zero (which is unrealistic), whereas self-reliance means being capable of paying for essential imports out of one's own competitive exports.

Why was economic self-reliance indispensable in the 1950s and 1960s to safeguard India's foreign policy (Non-Alignment)?
Because excessive foreign food aid (such as US PL-480) exerted severe pressure on India's diplomatic sovereignty and foreign policy autonomy.

1. Food Corporation of India (FCI - 1965), 2. Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices (CACP / then APC - 1965), and 3. Minimum Support Price (MSP) system.
