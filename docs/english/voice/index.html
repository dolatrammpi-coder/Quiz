What is Voice in English Grammar?

Voice shows whether the subject does the action or receives the action. There are two types of voice: Active Voice and Passive Voice. In active voice, the subject performs the action, as in She writes a letter. In passive voice, the subject receives the action, as in A letter is written by her. Voice change is an important topic for SSC CGL, CHSL, CPO, Railway, and Banking exams, where active to passive and passive to active conversion is frequently asked.

Important Rule: Only transitive verbs can be changed into passive voice because they have an object that becomes the subject in passive. Intransitive verbs like sleep, laugh, arrive, and die cannot be changed into passive voice as they do not have an object. The passive structure is Object + be verb + past participle + by + subject.

Rules of Voice Change in Different Tenses

Voice Change in Present Tense

In present indefinite, active structure Subject + V1 + Object becomes Object + is/am/are + V3 + by + Subject in passive. For example, He writes a letter becomes A letter is written by him. Present continuous active Subject + is/am/are + Ving + Object becomes Object + is/am/are + being + V3 + by + Subject. For example, She is cooking food becomes Food is being cooked by her. Present perfect active Subject + has/have + V3 + Object becomes Object + has/have + been + V3 + by + Subject.

Voice Change in Past Tense

In past indefinite, active Subject + V2 + Object becomes Object + was/were + V3 + by + Subject. For example, She wrote a novel becomes A novel was written by her. Past continuous active Subject + was/were + Ving becomes Object + was/were + being + V3. Past perfect active Subject + had + V3 becomes Object + had + been + V3 + by + Subject.

Voice Change in Future Tense and Modals

In future indefinite, active Subject + will/shall + V1 + Object becomes Object + will/shall + be + V3 + by + Subject. For example, She will write a letter becomes A letter will be written by her. With modals like can, could, may, might, should, must, the structure is active Subject + modal + V1 + Object becomes Object + modal + be + V3 + by + Subject. For example, You must obey the rules becomes The rules must be obeyed by you.

Tense / TypeActive VoicePassive Voice
Present IndefiniteShe writes a letter.A letter is written by her.
Past IndefiniteShe wrote a letter.A letter was written by her.
Future IndefiniteShe will write a letter.A letter will be written by her.
Present ContinuousShe is writing a letter.A letter is being written by her.
Modal VerbYou must do it.It must be done by you.
ImperativeClose the door.Let the door be closed.

Voice Change in Imperative and Interrogative Sentences

Imperative sentences give orders, requests, or advice. Active Close the door becomes passive Let the door be closed. For request, Please help me becomes You are requested to help me. Interrogative sentences starting with wh-words or helping verbs also change. Active Who wrote this book becomes passive By whom was this book written. Active Did she write a letter becomes Was a letter written by her. The question form is maintained in passive by changing helping verb position according to tense.

Exam Tip: In SSC exams, remember that present perfect continuous, past perfect continuous, future continuous, and future perfect continuous tenses do not have passive forms. Also, by + agent can be omitted in passive when agent is unknown, obvious, or unimportant, such as The thief was arrested, where by police is understood and not needed.
