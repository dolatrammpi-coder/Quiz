What is Direct and Indirect Speech?

Direct speech shows the exact words of the speaker within quotation marks, while indirect speech reports the speaker's words without quoting exactly. For example, Direct: He said, 'I am busy.' Indirect: He said that he was busy. Narration change is an important topic for SSC CGL, CHSL, CPO, Railway, Banking, and CTET exams, where conversion of direct to indirect and vice versa is frequently tested. Understanding rules of narration helps in avoiding errors in tense, pronoun, and time word changes.

Important Rule: If the reporting verb is in present or future tense, there is no change in tense of reported speech. If reporting verb is in past tense, tense changes as present to past, past to past perfect, and future to conditional. Pronouns change according to speaker and listener, and words showing nearness change to distance words like this to that, here to there, now to then.

Rules of Narration Change

Change of Tense, Pronouns and Time Words

When reporting verb is past, present indefinite changes to past indefinite, present continuous to past continuous, present perfect to past perfect, and will to would. For example, Direct: She said, 'I am reading.' Indirect: She said that she was reading. Pronouns change as first person according to subject, second person according to object, and third person remains unchanged. Time and place words change as now to then, today to that day, tomorrow to the next day, yesterday to the previous day, here to there, and this to that. These changes are essential for correct indirect speech.

Narration of Assertive and Interrogative Sentences

Assertive sentences use that as connector in indirect speech. Direct: He said, 'I will come.' Indirect: He said that he would come. Interrogative sentences are of two types. Wh-questions use wh-word as connector, and yes/no questions use if or whether. Direct: She asked, 'Where do you live?' Indirect: She asked where I lived. Direct: He asked, 'Are you coming?' Indirect: He asked if I was coming. Reporting verbs like asked, enquired, wanted to know are used instead of said.

AssertiveHe said, 'I am busy.'He said that he was busy.
Interrogative (Wh-)She asked, 'Where are you going?'She asked where I was going.

Imperative, Exclamatory and Optative Sentences

Imperative sentences express order, request, advice, or suggestion. In indirect speech, reporting verbs like ordered, requested, advised, suggested are used with to + verb. Direct: Teacher said, 'Sit down.' Indirect: Teacher ordered to sit down. Negative imperative uses not to. Exclamatory sentences express strong feelings and use exclaimed with joy, sorrow, surprise. Direct: He said, 'Hurray! We won.' Indirect: He exclaimed with joy that they had won. Optative sentences express wish or prayer and use wished, prayed. Direct: He said, 'May God bless you!' Indirect: He prayed that God might bless me.

Exam Tip: Remember that universal truths, habitual facts, and proverbs do not change tense in indirect speech even if reporting verb is past. For example, He said, 'The sun rises in the east.' becomes He said that the sun rises in the east, not rose. Also, said to changes to told in indirect assertive, but say to does not change to tell to in all cases; it becomes told, asked, ordered depending on sentence type.
