What is Reading Comprehension?

Reading Comprehension means understanding a passage and answering questions from it. In SSC CGL, CHSL, CPO, Railway, Banking, CTET, reading comprehension is very important. One passage comes with 5 to 10 questions. Passage can be story, article, essay, biography, science, history. Example: Passage about Mahatma Gandhi life, his struggle, his honesty, his non-violence. Questions ask about main idea, title, facts, inference, vocabulary, tone, purpose. Reading Comprehension Basics means how to read passage and answer questions. First read passage fully, understand story, main idea, tone. Then read questions one by one and find answer from passage. Do not add your own knowledge, answer only from passage. Example: Passage says Gandhi was born in Porbandar in 1869, question Where was Gandhi born? Answer Porbandar from passage, not from outside knowledge. Factual Questions ask direct facts from passage. Example: When was Gandhi born? 1869 direct fact. Inference-Based Questions ask conclusion from passage, not direct fact, you have to understand and conclude. Example: Passage says Gandhi left school and went to England, inference he was educated in England. Vocabulary in Context asks word meaning from passage context. Example: Word honest in passage means truthful, not liar.

Important Exam Tip: 5 golden rules for Reading Comprehension in SSC. Rule 1: Read passage fully first, at least 2 times, understand main idea, theme, tone, purpose. Do not read questions first, read passage first. Rule 2: For factual questions, scan passage for keywords, find exact line where fact is mentioned, answer from that line. Example: Question about date, find date in passage. Rule 3: For vocabulary in context, read sentence before and after word, understand context, positive negative tone. Example: Word benevolent in passage about kind king, benevolent means kind, not cruel. Rule 4: For inference questions, answer is not directly written, you have to conclude logically from passage, but conclusion must be based on passage, not your own imagination. Rule 5: For title, main idea, central theme, choose title that covers full passage, not only one part, title should be general, not too specific, not too narrow.

Types of Reading Comprehension Questions

Reading Comprehension Basics, Main Idea, Central Theme, Title of the Passage

Reading Comprehension Basics: How to attempt comprehension. Read passage 2 times, understand story. Example passage: Once there was a king who was very kind, honest, brave, he helped poor, he was loved by all. Basics means understanding what is passage about, king and his qualities. Main Idea is main point of passage, what passage mainly tells. Example: Passage about king kind, honest, brave, main idea is The qualities of a good king, main idea covers whole passage. Central Theme is central message, theme of passage, similar to main idea but theme is deeper message. Example: Theme is Kindness and honesty are qualities of good king. Title of the Passage is heading that suits passage, title should cover full passage, not just one part. Example: Title for king passage can be A Kind King, or Qualities of a Good King, or An Ideal King. Title should be short, general, covering full passage, not too long, not only about honesty, but about all qualities.

Factual Questions, Inference-Based Questions, Vocabulary in Context

Factual Questions ask direct facts from passage. Example: Passage says king ruled in 15th century, question When did king rule? Answer 15th century direct fact. Factual questions keyword scanning, find exact words. Example: Passage says Gandhi born 1869 in Porbandar, question Where born? Porbandar fact. Inference-Based Questions ask conclusion, not direct fact, you have to infer. Example: Passage says Gandhi studied in England and became lawyer, inference he was educated, inference he was lawyer, not directly said he was lawyer? Actually says studied law so inference he became lawyer. Example: Passage says king helped poor daily, inference king was kind, inference king loved poor. Vocabulary in Context asks word meaning as used in passage, not dictionary meaning, context meaning. Example: Passage says The benevolent king helped poor, benevolent in context means kind, generous, not cruel. Example: Word abandoned in passage The king abandoned his palace and went to forest, abandoned means left, not kept. Example: Word brave in passage The brave soldier fought enemy, brave means courageous, not coward. For vocabulary, check sentence before and after, tone positive negative.

Tone of the Passage, Author's Purpose, Reference-Based, Conclusion-Based Questions

Tone of the Passage is feeling of passage, how author writes, tone can be positive, negative, neutral, optimistic, pessimistic, sarcastic, critical, appreciative, sympathetic, humorous, serious, formal, informal. Example: Passage praising king kind, honest, brave, tone appreciative, positive, admiring. Example: Passage criticizing corruption, tone critical, negative, angry. Example: Passage about pollution dangers, tone serious, concerned, worried. Author's Purpose is why author wrote passage, purpose can be to inform, to entertain, to persuade, to criticize, to appreciate, to describe, to explain. Example: Passage about Gandhi life, purpose to inform about Gandhi life, to describe his qualities. Example: Passage about importance of honesty, purpose to persuade readers to be honest, to teach moral. Reference-Based Questions ask what does pronoun refer to. Example: Passage He was very kind, who is He? He refers to king, previous noun king. Example: This was his greatest quality, This refers to honesty, previous sentence honesty. Conclusion-Based Questions ask final conclusion, what can be concluded from passage. Example: Passage about king kind, honest, brave, conclusion A good king should be kind, honest, brave. Example: Passage about hard work leads to success, conclusion Hard work is key to success. Conclusion must be based on passage, not outside knowledge.

Tips for Reading Comprehension in SSC Exams

1) Read passage fully 2 times before reading questions, understand main idea, theme, tone, purpose, central message. 2) For factual questions, scan passage for keywords like names, dates, places, find exact line, answer from passage only, not from your own knowledge. 3) For vocabulary in context, read one sentence before and after word, understand positive negative tone, context meaning not dictionary meaning, if passage positive, positive meaning. 4) For inference, conclusion questions, answer must be logical conclusion from passage, not direct fact, but must be based on passage, not your imagination, conclusion should be supported by passage lines. 5) For main idea, central theme, title, choose option that covers full passage, not only one paragraph, title should be general, short, covering whole passage, not too specific, not too narrow. 6) For tone, check adjectives used by author, if praising words like kind, honest, brave, generous, tone appreciative, positive, if criticizing words like corrupt, dishonest, cruel, tone critical, negative, if worried words like dangerous, serious, alarming, tone concerned, serious. 7) For reference questions, find pronoun he, she, it, this, that, they, find nearest noun before pronoun that makes sense, pronoun refers to nearest logical noun. Practice daily 2-3 passages, improve reading speed and understanding.

Exam Tip: SSC most asked reading comprehension patterns: Main idea and title - read full passage, choose title covering full passage. Factual - scan for keyword date, name, place. Inference - logical conclusion based on passage. Vocabulary in context - context meaning, positive negative tone, read before after sentence. Tone - appreciative if praising, critical if criticizing, concerned if worried, humorous if funny, sarcastic if taunting. Purpose - to inform if giving information, to persuade if convincing, to entertain if story, to describe if describing. Reference - pronoun refers to nearest noun. Conclusion - final message based on passage. Do not add outside knowledge, answer only from passage, do not assume beyond passage.
