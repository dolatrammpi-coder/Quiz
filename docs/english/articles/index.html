What are Articles in English Grammar?

Articles are words that define a noun as specific or unspecific. There are two types of articles: indefinite articles A and An, and definite article The. Indefinite articles are used for general or unspecific nouns, while definite article The is used for specific or particular nouns. For example, A book means any book, An apple means any apple, and The book means a particular book we already know. Articles are very important for SSC CGL, CHSL, CPO, Railway, Banking, and CTET exams because error detection and fill in the blanks questions frequently test article rules.

Important Rule: Use A before consonant sound and An before vowel sound. It is the sound that matters, not the spelling. For example, A university (yu sound consonant), An hour (our sound vowel), An MBA (em sound vowel), A European (yu sound). Many students make mistake with A and An based on letter, but exam tests sound-based rule. Remember: A one-eyed man, An honest person, A useful book.

Use of A, An and The

Indefinite Articles A and An

A is used before words beginning with consonant sound like a boy, a car, a house, a university, a European, a one-rupee note. An is used before words beginning with vowel sound a, e, i, o, u sound like an apple, an egg, an umbrella, an hour, an heir, an honest man, an MBA, an MLA. A and An are used with singular countable nouns for general meaning, to express profession like He is a teacher, to show price or rate like 10 rupees a kilo, and with exclamatory what a like What a beautiful day!

Definite Article The and Its Uses

The is used when noun is specific or already known, with superlatives like the best, the tallest, with ordinals like the first, the second, with unique objects like the sun, the moon, the earth, with rivers, seas, oceans, mountain ranges, deserts, with musical instruments like the guitar, the piano, with newspapers, holy books, and with adjectives to represent whole class like the rich, the poor. For example, The Ganga is a holy river, The Himalayas are in the north, The rich should help the poor.

Article Rule / TypeExample
A + Consonant SoundA boy, a car, a university, a one-eyed man
An + Vowel SoundAn apple, an hour, an honest man, an MBA
The + Specific / Known NounThe book you gave me, the man in blue shirt
The + Rivers, Seas, Mountain RangesThe Ganga, The Arabian Sea, The Himalayas
The + Superlative / OrdinalThe best student, the first prize
Omission (No Article)Go to school, at home, by bus, in winter

Omission of Articles and Common Expressions

No article is used before proper nouns like Ram, Delhi, before abstract nouns like honesty, love when used generally, before material nouns like gold, silver, before names of games like cricket, football, before names of meals like breakfast, lunch, dinner when general, before man, woman when general like Man is mortal, and in many common phrases. Common expressions without article include go to school, go to bed, at home, at work, by bus, by train, on foot, in winter, in summer, last night, next week. Articles are not used before names of languages, but The is used before The English language when referring to language as subject.

Exam Tip: Use The with geographical names: rivers (The Ganga), seas (The Red Sea), oceans (The Pacific Ocean), mountain ranges (The Himalayas), deserts (The Thar Desert), island groups (The Andamans), holy books (The Gita), newspapers (The Times of India), but no article with single mountain (Mount Everest), single island (Sri Lanka), lakes (Lake Chilika), continents (Asia), countries (India) except The USA, The UK, The Netherlands. Also, The is used with institutions when specific building is meant: He is in the hospital (as patient), but He is in hospital is wrong in Indian exams, both He is in the hospital and He goes to school are tested.
