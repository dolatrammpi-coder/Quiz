What is Fill in the Blanks in English Grammar?

Fill in the Blanks means completing a sentence with correct word. Example: She goes to school daily. He has been waiting for two hours. In SSC CGL, CHSL, CPO, Railway, Banking, CTET fill in the blanks questions are very common. 4-5 questions come every year. Two types: Single filler one blank, Double filler two blanks. Example single: The boy is playing football. Example double: He is not only intelligent but also hardworking. Grammar-Based Fill in the Blanks tests grammar rules like tense, subject verb agreement, article, preposition, modal, conjunction. Vocabulary-Based tests word meaning like synonym, antonym, context. Preposition-Based tests correct preposition like in, on, at, by, with, for, of. Article-Based tests a, an, the. Conjunction-Based tests and, but, or, although, because. Tense-Based tests past present future. Subject-Verb Agreement tests singular plural verb. Modal-Based tests can, could, may, might, shall, will, should, must. Context-Based tests understanding full sentence meaning. Double Fillers tests two blanks together.

Important Exam Tip: 5 golden rules for Fill in Blanks in SSC. Rule 1: Read full sentence before filling, understand context. Example: He is an honest man. Context honest starts with vowel sound, so an honest man. Rule 2: Check tense clue words - yesterday past tense, tomorrow future, daily present. Example: Yesterday he went, Tomorrow he will go, Daily he goes. Rule 3: Subject-Verb Agreement - singular subject singular verb, plural subject plural verb. Example: The boy is, The boys are. Rule 4: Preposition fixed phrases - good at, interested in, afraid of, depend on. Rule 5: For double fillers, check both blanks together, not single. Example: He is not only honest but also hardworking, both blanks should match.

Types of Fill in the Blanks

Grammar-Based, Vocabulary-Based, Preposition-Based, Article-Based

Grammar-Based Fill in the Blanks tests grammar rules. Example: Each of the boys is present. Each singular so is present, not are. Example: Neither of them is coming. Neither singular so is coming. Vocabulary-Based tests word meaning. Example: The movie was very boring, audience was bored, so boring. Example: He is very kind person, always helps others, so kind, generous. Preposition-Based tests correct preposition. Example: He is good at English. Good at English, not good in. Example: She is interested in music. Interested in music. Example: He depends on his parents. Depends on parents. Article-Based tests a, an, the. Example: She is an honest woman. An honest because honest starts with vowel sound h silent, honest pronounced onest vowel sound. Example: He is a university student. A university because university starts with consonant sound yoo. Example: The sun rises in east. The sun because unique object, the sun, the moon, the earth.

Conjunction-Based, Tense-Based, Subject-Verb Agreement Based Blanks

Conjunction-Based tests joining words. Example: He is poor but honest. He is poor but honest, contrast so but. Example: Although he is poor, he is honest. Although he is poor, he is honest, contrast although. Example: I will wait until he comes. I will wait until he comes, time conjunction until. Tense-Based tests past present future. Example: Yesterday she went to market. Yesterday past clue so went, not go or will go. Example: She has been waiting for two hours, present perfect continuous clue for two hours, has been waiting. Example: Tomorrow he will come. Tomorrow future clue will come. Subject-Verb Agreement Based tests singular plural matching. Example: The boys are playing. Boys plural so are playing, not is. Example: Each boy is playing. Each singular so is playing, not are. Example: The news is good. News singular uncountable so is good, not are. Example: Mathematics is difficult subject. Mathematics singular subject so is difficult.

Modal-Based, Context-Based, Double Fillers

Modal-Based tests can could may might shall will should must would. Example: You should respect your elders. Should respect, advice, should. Example: It may rain today, sky cloudy. May rain, possibility, may. Example: May I come in? May I come in, permission, may. Example: He can lift 100 kg, strong. Can lift, ability, can. Context-Based tests full sentence understanding. Example: The thief ran away before the police came. Before police came, thief ran before police. Example: Despite working hard, he failed. Despite working hard, he failed, although hard work. Double Fillers tests two blanks together, need both correct. Example: He is not only honest but also hardworking. He is not only honest but also hardworking, double filler not only but also. Example: Unless you work hard, you will fail even though you are intelligent. Double filler unless and even though. Example: The book is both interesting and useful. The book is both interesting and useful, both and.

Tips for Fill in the Blanks in SSC Exams

1) Always read full sentence twice, understand meaning before filling. Example: He is an honest man, read full, honest vowel sound so an. 2) Look for clue words for tense - yesterday past, tomorrow future, always present, for two hours perfect continuous, since morning perfect. 3) For subject verb agreement, find subject singular plural, Each, Every, Neither, Either singular, Boys, Girls plural. 4) For preposition, remember fixed phrases - good at, bad at, interested in, keen on, afraid of, proud of, depend on, rely on, insist on. 5) For double fillers, check both blanks together, eliminate options where one blank wrong, both should be correct. 6) For article, remember vowel sound not vowel letter, an honest vowel sound, an hour vowel sound, a university consonant sound yoo, a one-eyed man consonant sound wun. 7) For modal, can ability, may possibility permission, should advice, must compulsion, might less possibility.

Although but contrast, Because reason, Until time, Unless condition. For double fillers check pair not only but also, both and, either or, neither nor, although yet, unless else.
